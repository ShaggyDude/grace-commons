## Summary

Audit Trail answers, all at once, the four questions a regulator or investigator asks about any consequential action: what happened, who authorized it, has the record been altered, and was it kept long enough?

It does this by combining four simpler patterns into one queryable record: an add-only event log (what happened), cryptographic attribution tying each event to the actor who performed it (who), tamper-evident sealing that makes any after-the-fact change detectable (has it been altered), and a retention policy that fixes how long records must be kept (kept long enough).

None of the four answers the full question alone — the event log does not name the actor, the attribution does not protect integrity, the retention does not detect rewriting, the sealing does not name the actor — but stacked together they produce a record that is observable, attributable, tamper-evident, and lifetime-bounded.

Combining them also produces guarantees none has alone: every event is simultaneously logged, attributed, retention-tracked, and sealed; when retention expires, one coordinated cascade retires all four together, so no dangling references remain; and a query on any kept event returns a definite, verifiable answer that distinguishes a lawfully destroyed record from a missing one.

Two of the four patterns deliberately offer no way to delete anything, which is what makes them trustworthy. So the cascade does not delete: it records the lawful end of the retention period, marks on the covering seal exactly which of the records it commits to were destroyed, and hands the actual destruction of the stored content to whatever erasure mechanism the deployment has declared for that purpose. What survives is the proof that the record existed and was destroyed lawfully — which is exactly what the regulator asks for.

This is the canonical audit substrate (a *substrate* being the shared foundation other patterns are built on top of rather than beside) — a composition, not a new primitive (a *primitive* being a new irreducible concept of its own; this introduces none, it only wires four existing ones together) — behind financial-controls, healthcare-access, cardholder-data, and broker-dealer audit requirements, and it is reused as a building block by other compositions.

## Intent

Every regulated system carries the same obligation: when the auditor arrives, the system must be able to answer four questions about any action of consequence — *what happened, who authorized it, has the record been altered, and was the retention obligation honored?* Each question maps cleanly onto one of the four constituent atoms; the auditor's actual ask is all four at once, for any record, on demand, over the regulatory horizon.

The composition addresses what the four atoms cannot answer alone. Event Log records the fact but does not bind it to an actor. Actor Identity binds the actor but does not commit to the records' integrity. Retention Window bounds the lifetime but does not detect rewriting. Tamper Evidence detects rewriting but does not name the actor. Stacked correctly, the four answer the regulator's question in one structure: an [Audit Record] that is observable, attributable, integrity-protected, and lifetime-bounded.

This is a composition, not a new primitive. The four atoms are unchanged; the composition is the wiring that makes them coherent — one consolidated audit surface rather than four parallel record stores the auditor has to correlate by hand. The construction is the same one that runs in every audit-grade system in production today: signed events appended to an immutable (unchangeable once written) log, sealed periodically against a tamper-evident structure, governed by a retention policy with a structural no-early-purge guarantee. Different vocabularies; identical mechanic.

## Composes

- **[Event Log](../atoms/event-log.md)** — provides the append-only, totally-ordered sequence the audit's *what happened* answer is read from. The composition maintains exactly one Event Log instance (the audit log). **Instance capability requirement:** Event Log declares that a query may name a sequence-number range but leaves the exact query shape to implementation policy, so the wired instance must expose its declared sequence-range query **with an open upper bound** — a range beginning at a given `sequence_number` with no upper limit. **Eight procedures issue it and none has a substitute** (one — [Record Action] step 5's read-back — admits an instance optimization that skips the read in the healthy case, but its declared miss-fallback is the full open-upper-bound enumeration, so the requirement stands for it too)**:** [Seal Now]'s head read; [Record Action] step 5's `sequence_number` read-back, which runs from the composition's last known sequence high-water mark to the open end (the bound is open because concurrent records interleave and the new event may sit behind several others); the rebuild procedures for `event_to_sequence`, for `event_to_attestation`'s **live** entries, and for `compensated_attestations` (Composition state) — those three being full log enumerations by definition; the reconciliation scan's **binding-set construction**, which enumerates the log in full to collect the `attestation_id`s live payloads carry, and its **third half**, which enumerates the log to find events carrying no retention record (the cascade-on-purge rule); and the **auditor's own enumeration** at Generation-acceptance check 2, which rebuilds that same binding set from outside the implementation. That last user is why the requirement is a *deployment* obligation and not merely an internal one: an instance that cannot serve the open upper bound cannot be audited for orphan-freedom at all, whatever its own code does. `seal_coverage`'s ranges are deliberately *not* on the list: that rebuild enumerates the **seal** store and never touches `EventLog.read` at all. `EventLog.read`'s `invalid-query` on any of these paths is a **deployment-configuration fault** (the wired instance does not meet a declared capability requirement), alerted on by the deployment rather than surfaced as an outcome of this composition's contract.
- **[Actor Identity](../atoms/actor-identity.md)** — provides the verifiable attribution the audit's *who authorized it* answer is read from. The composition maintains exactly one Actor Identity instance (the attestation store). **How the composition uses the surface:** it calls `attest` at [Record Action] step 2 and `verify` at [Verify Record] step 3, hands the attestation's `proof` to the erasure mechanism at [Purge Event] step 3, and reads the attestation record's **surviving stored fields through Actor Identity's declared read surface** — `attestation_id`, `action_ref`, `actor_ref`, `attested_at`, per that atom's Outputs — at [Read Record] step 4 for a purged event; it deletes nothing, because the atom declares no deletion surface (its Invariant 9).
- **[Retention Window](../atoms/retention-window.md)** — provides the policy-bounded lifetime the audit's *was the retention honored* answer is read from. The composition maintains **exactly one** Retention Window instance configured with the host's regulatory policy (or a policy selector for content-derived rules). One retention record governs the audit event and its attestation jointly (Configuration), so there is no second instance anywhere in this composition. **How the composition uses the surface:** it calls `place_under_retention` at [Record Action] step 4 and on the reconciliation path's compensating placement, calls `purge` at [Purge Event] step 1, and delegates wholesale to the atom's own read-time `purge_eligible` projection at [Purge Eligible]; it evaluates no eligibility clock of its own.
- **[Tamper Evidence](../atoms/tamper-evidence.md)** — provides the integrity proof the audit's *has the record been altered* answer is read from. The composition maintains exactly one Tamper Evidence instance (the seal store) sealing over ranges of the audit log on a configured cadence. **How the composition uses the surface:** it calls `seal` from [Seal Now] alone — reached by the cadence firing at [Record Action] step 6, by a host call, or by [Purge Event] step 0's precondition — and `verify` at [Verify Record] step 5; it writes no other kind of entry into the seal store, disposes of none, and never seals a range some seal already covers.

## Composition logic

### Composition state

The composition owns emergent state that wires the four constituent atoms into one queryable audit surface. Every element carries its Contract classification per [`execution-contract.md`](../execution-contract.md) §Composition state — *derived index*, with the named rebuild procedure that regenerates it from constituent stores, or *extraction-pending*, with the named proposed atom that will own the truth. Four elements — `event_to_retention`, `event_to_sequence`, `sealed_through`, and `compensated_attestations` — are wholly derived indexes. Two elements split: `event_to_attestation` is a derived index for live events and extraction-pending for purged ones, and `seal_coverage` is a derived index over contiguous sequence-number ranges carrying one extraction-pending per-entry marker. One element — `erasure_outcomes` — is wholly extraction-pending: the erasure mechanism's per-event outcome record, truth no constituent store carries.

- **`event_to_attestation`** — map from `event_id` to the `attestation_id` produced by Actor Identity at record time. Lets the auditor traverse from any event to its attribution without scanning. **The element's classification splits by the event's retention state, because the source the rebuild reads from does not survive the cascade.**
  - *Entries for **live** events* — **derived index.** *Rebuild procedure:* enumerate the audit log by a **sequence-range read with an open upper bound** — `EventLog.read` over the range beginning at 1 with no upper bound, the declared instance capability requirement in *Composes* — and for each returned event take its `event_id` field as the key and the `attestation_id` its own payload carries as the value — [Record Action] step 3 writes `attestation_id` into the appended payload, so the binding fact is already immutable Event Log content. The Contract's three obligations follow: the map sits **outside the action's atomicity surface** (its population is *evidence* that the truth-bearing writes committed, never a peer write the compensation protocol must handle); a missing or lost entry is a **rebuild trigger, not data loss** — reads consult it with rebuild-on-miss semantics; and it claims no cross-constituent transactional consistency. Never modified after insertion.
  - *Entries for **purged** events* — **extraction-pending.** The rebuild above reads the binding out of the payload, and [Purge Event]'s cascade has the payload's recoverability destroyed — so for a purged event the binding fact is destroyed with the thing that carried it, and no enumeration of any constituent store replays it. The pair `(event_id, attestation_id)` is therefore **captured at purge time, before the delegation runs, as part of the destruction record** written with the step-2 marker (see [Purge Event] step 2), and it carries the same durability obligation `purged_events` does — it rides the cascade's write, and losing it loses the auditor's traversal from a lawfully destroyed event to the attestation that was destroyed with it. The named proposed atom is the same **Erasure Tombstone** *(forthcoming)* that `purged_events` rides; until that extraction lands the pair is flagged debt, named rather than normalized.

- **`event_to_retention`** — map from `event_id` to the `retention_id` produced by Retention Window at record time. Lets the auditor read the policy under which the event is held. **Contract classification: derived index.** *Rebuild procedure:* enumerate the Retention Window store and re-key each retention record by its `record_ref`, which [Record Action] step 4 sets to the `event_id`. The same three obligations attach — outside the atomicity surface, rebuild-on-miss, no consistency claim. Never modified after insertion.

- **`event_to_sequence`** — map from `event_id` to the `sequence_number` Event Log assigned that event at append. This is the index that makes id-addressed reads possible at all: **Event Log declares no read-by-id surface** — its `read` takes a query that may name a sequence-number range, a wall-time range, or a payload predicate, and its own *Reverse lookup / indexing* edge case routes lookup by payload field to a separate Reverse Index pattern *(forthcoming)*. So [Read Record], [Verify Record], and [Purge Event] resolve their caller-supplied `event_id` to a `sequence_number` through this index and then issue Event Log's **declared sequence-range query** for the singleton range `[n .. n]`. Populated at [Record Action] step 5. **The primary population path is the declared read-back**: a sequence-range read over the new tail through `EventLog.read` — from the composition's last known sequence high-water mark to the **open end**, the same declared query shape the capability requirement in *Composes* obliges — matching the returned `event_id` by equality, with a miss falling back to the rebuild procedure below ([Record Action] step 5 states the bound rule in full). It is a declared Q-surface call available on every conforming Event Log instance. Where an instance's append result *also* surfaces the assigned `sequence_number` (Event Log's Feedback fixes it at the append seam), the composition may take the value from there instead and skip the read; that is an **instance optimization**, not the contract, and a specification that made it the contract would be requiring a Feedback detail no Event Log invariant obliges an implementation to surface. **Contract classification: derived index.** *Rebuild procedure:* enumerate the audit log by a **sequence-range read with an open upper bound** — `EventLog.read` over the range beginning at 1 with no upper bound, the same declared query shape [Seal Now] uses to find the log's tail, and the shape the wired instance must expose per the capability requirement in *Composes* — and re-key each returned event by its own `event_id` field; Event Log's Outputs declare that every returned event carries its `event_id`, `sequence_number`, `recorded_at`, and `data`, so both sides of the mapping are immutable Event Log content and the rebuild is total. *Relation:* `event_id` ↔ `sequence_number` — **one-to-one, mandatory on both sides at quiescence**: every event in the log has exactly one sequence number (Event Log Invariant 2, event immutability) and no two events share either (Invariants 3 and 6). Both sides are fields of the same Event Log event, so the relation is Event Log's own and this index only reads it back; a lost entry is a rebuild trigger, never a relation violation. The same three obligations attach — outside the atomicity surface, rebuild-on-miss, no consistency claim. Never modified after insertion.

- **`seal_coverage`** — for each `evidence_id` in the seal store, the **contiguous sequence-number range** the seal commits to as cut by [Seal Now], plus a per-entry `purged_events` set written by the cascade. Lets the verifier present the correct record set to Tamper Evidence's `verify`. The element splits into two parts with two classifications:
  - *The ranges* — **derived index.** *Rebuild procedure:* enumerate the seal store and read each evidence record's `record_set_ref`, which is exactly the sequence-number slice presented at [Seal Now] time. Ranges are keyed on `sequence_number` only, never on `event_id`: Event Log's `event_id` supports equality but carries no ordering semantics (Event Log's Identity model), so a "range of `event_id`s" would not be a well-defined interval. Ranges are **contiguous and disjoint by construction**, because [Seal Now] cuts strictly advancing slices (`[sealed_through + 1 .. tail]`, then `sealed_through` advances past them) and the canonical composition has no other way to write a coverage entry — a hole in a range, or two entries overlapping, is not a state this composition can reach. Tamper Evidence Invariant 1 makes `record_set_ref` immutable and Invariant 9 makes the seal store non-decreasing, so both sides of the derivation are stable constituent content. Same three obligations as the maps above.
  - *The `purged_events` set* — **extraction-pending.** The records-purged fact is recorded at **event granularity, not entry granularity**: `purged_events` is the set of `sequence_number`s *within* that seal's range whose content the cascade has had destroyed. It is not a flag over the whole entry, because a seal covering a thousand events is very often partly purged and partly live — a policy selector can give two events in one range two different retention periods — and a whole-entry flag would report a live event as destroyed. Nothing in the seal store, the attestation store, or the retention record says *this member of the seal's record set was later destroyed*; the set is truth no constituent replays, so no rebuild procedure can regenerate it. The named proposed atom is an **Erasure Tombstone** *(forthcoming)* — a write-once record of what was destroyed, when, and under whose authority — the same atom [Purge Event] delegates content destruction to, and the same one Event Log's *Right-to-be-forgotten erasure* edge case already names. Until that extraction lands the set is flagged debt, named rather than normalized, and it is one of the **three** extraction-pending facts in Composition state carrying a durability obligation — the others being `event_to_attestation`'s entries for purged events, which the same destruction record carries, and `erasure_outcomes`, which lands on that record when the delegation returns. All three ride the cascade's write (see *Durability across crashes* in Edge cases).

- **`erasure_outcomes`** — for each event over which [Purge Event] step 3 has issued its destruction delegation, the outcome records the configured `erasure_mechanism` reported for it: **`destroyed`** or **`destruction-failed(reason)`** (Configuration, `erasure_mechanism` — the two-value taxonomy is fixed there), **addressable per `event_id`** — the reconciliation scan's first-half predicate resolves the outcome for one specific *Purged* retention, so an outcome that could not be traced to the event it reports on could close nothing. An event's entry is *closed* exactly when a `destroyed` outcome is among its records; a `destruction-failed(reason)` record closes nothing and the entry stays open for re-driving. The element is load-bearing on four surfaces: the scan's half-completed-cascade predicate tests it for a **successful** (`destroyed`) value (the cascade-on-purge rule, *First half*); [Purge Event] step 4's surviving-record inventory lists it; Invariant 8 names it as the fourth proving record of a lawful destruction; and Generation-acceptance check 5(e) audits it. **Contract classification: extraction-pending.** No constituent witnessed the mechanism's report — the mechanism is a declared deployment capability, not a constituent, and nothing in the log, attestation, retention, or seal stores replays what it answered — so no rebuild procedure exists. The named proposed atom is the same **Erasure Tombstone** *(forthcoming)* the destruction record's other two facts ride: a write-once record of what was destroyed, when, under whose authority — and with what reported outcome. It carries the same **durability obligation** as those facts, and for a sharper reason than symmetry: a lost outcome record is not safely re-derivable the way a lost derived-index entry is — re-driving the delegation over already-destroyed content is not guaranteed to return `destroyed` again (a mechanism may only answer *target already unreadable in a way it cannot confirm*, a named `destruction-failed` reason), so losing a `destroyed` outcome can leave a completed cascade permanently unclosable rather than merely re-checked. It rides the cascade's durable write: the destruction record step 2 opens carries the `(event_id, attestation_id)` pair before the delegation runs, and the outcome lands on that same record when the delegation returns at step 3 (see *Durability across crashes* in Edge cases). Until the extraction lands the element is flagged debt, named rather than normalized.

- **`sealed_through`** — the most recent `sequence_number` covered by any seal. Events with `sequence_number > sealed_through` are in the *unsealed tail*; the next seal cadence covers them. **Contract classification: derived index.** *Rebuild procedure:* the maximum sequence number over all `seal_coverage` entries' ranges, themselves rebuilt from the seal store (zero for an empty seal store). Outside the atomicity surface; a lost value is recomputed, never recovered.

- **`compensated_attestations`** — the set of `attestation_id`s for which an `audit.compensation` event has been recorded. It is the composition's **closed-state marker for orphan reconciliation**: the reconciliation scan reads it before writing a compensation, and Invariant 1's liveness arm reads membership in it as the observable condition that an orphan has been *reconciled*. It exists because the orphan itself never goes away — Actor Identity's **Invariant 9** forecloses deletion, so an unbound attestation stays unbound in the store forever, and the store therefore cannot carry the closure. The marker does. **Contract classification: derived index.** *Rebuild procedure:* enumerate the audit log by a **sequence-range read with an open upper bound** — `EventLog.read` over the range beginning at 1 with no upper bound, the same declared capability requirement *Composes* names — filter the returned events to those whose payload `action_ref` equals the composition-owned `audit.compensation` reference **and whose payload carries `subject = attestation`**, then read the orphan `attestation_id` that payload names (the payload requirement, including the subject-kind discriminator, is stated where `audit.compensation` is defined — the cascade-on-purge rule, *Where the compensation lands*). **The subject filter is not optional bookkeeping:** one action reference covers both compensating writes the reconciliation path makes — the one naming an unbound attestation and the one naming an event whose retention was placed — and only the first kind belongs in this set. A rebuild that took every `audit.compensation` event would read an `event_id` where it expected an `attestation_id` and populate the set with values that are not attestation ids at all. **The filtering happens in composition code, not in the query**: selecting on a payload field is exactly the lookup Event Log routes to a forthcoming Reverse Index pattern (Action wiring), so the composition enumerates and filters rather than asking `EventLog.read` for a payload predicate — and a deployment that has composed Reverse Index may use it as an instance optimization without changing the contract. *Relation:* `attestation_id` → *compensated* — a **set, not a map**, and membership is monotone: an `attestation_id` enters it once and no action of this composition removes it. *Bound on the rebuild's totality, stated rather than assumed:* compensation events are ordinary audit events under the configured `retention_policy` and are themselves eventually purged, and a purged compensation event's payload is unreadable, so the rebuilt set covers the compensation events still live in the log. That is sufficient for the marker's one job — a scan cannot re-compensate inside a window the compensation event outlives by its whole retention period — and the residual, a second `audit.compensation` event naming an attestation compensated many years earlier, is a duplicate record rather than a false one. The same three obligations as the maps above attach — outside the atomicity surface, rebuild-on-miss, no consistency claim. Never removed after insertion.

  **The reserved `audit.*` namespace is what makes this rebuild trustworthy rather than merely convenient, and it belongs in the classification rather than in a footnote.** [Record Action] step 1 rejects any `action_ref` carrying the `audit.` prefix except from a call carrying the configured `reconciliation_operator` identity (*Primitive policies*; Configuration) — and step 2 attests that identity's credential before anything is appended — so the only writer of an `audit.compensation` event anywhere in a conforming deployment is this composition's own reconciliation path, under the operator identity the deployment declared. The rebuild is therefore reading records **only the composition could have written** — which is precisely what makes Invariant 1's closure non-forgeable. Without the reservation, any caller could record an `audit.compensation` event naming any `attestation_id` it liked, and the orphan-freedom arm of Invariant 1 would close on an assertion an outsider made rather than on evidence a compensation was actually performed; the marker would report *someone said so* while reading as *the composition did so*.

### Configuration

Twelve knobs. Each carries its type, its default, and the rule the deployment uses to set it.

- **`retention_policy`** — *Type:* a Retention Window `policy_ref`, or a policy selector function `(action_ref, actor_ref, data) → policy_ref` for content-derived rules (medical records under HIPAA — the US Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act — cardholder data under PCI DSS — the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard). *Default:* **none — deployment-required.** There is no safe default: a retention period is a legal obligation, and a composition that silently picked one would be asserting a legal conclusion it cannot make. An instance with no `retention_policy` set does not start. *Setting rule:* the deployment sets the reconciled policy for the record class — the longest applicable retention across every regulation in force (a Policy Reconciliation pattern *(forthcoming)* produces the reconciled value; this composition consumes it). Applied at [Record Action] time. One retention record governs the audit event **and its attestation jointly**: the attestation's lifetime rides the event's own retention, and the two are destroyed in one cascade ([Purge Event]). There is therefore no second Retention Window instance and no separate attestation meta-retention policy — the *Composes* declaration of exactly one Retention Window instance for audit events is literally accurate. The seal outlives both and is **retained indefinitely** (the cascade-on-purge rule).
- **`seal_cadence`** — *Type:* one of *per-event* (strongest, most expensive), *interval-based* (every N events or every T seconds), or *on-demand* (the host calls [Seal Now]). *Default:* **none — deployment-required.** The cadence directly bounds the forensic window for any detected tampering, so a defaulted cadence would be the composition choosing the deployment's tamper-detection resolution for it; an instance with no cadence set does not start. *Setting rule:* the deployment sets the cadence from the forensic window its regime tolerates — tighter cadence narrows the window at the cost of seal-store growth and verify-time work (Edge cases — *Seal cadence vs. write rate*). Deployments whose auditor requires a bounded per-event tampering window set *per-event*; deployments that only need a bounded batch window set *interval-based* and monitor tail depth.
- **`seal_mechanism`** — *Type:* a Tamper Evidence mechanism reference (hash chain, Merkle tree, RFC 3161-anchored timestamp — RFC — Request for Comments — is the Internet Engineering Task Force's standards-document series, and RFC 3161 is its trusted time-stamping standard). *Default:* **none — deployment-required.** Tamper Evidence is deliberately mechanism-neutral (its Invariant 8, mechanism opacity), so the atom supplies no default and neither does this composition; an instance with no mechanism set does not start. *Setting rule:* the deployment selects a mechanism that is cryptographically sound for the full audit horizon (Tamper Evidence Invariant 2's soundness qualification; a Mechanism Registry pattern *(forthcoming)* owns mechanism health), and selects an **anchored or chained** mechanism where the forensic-window bound of Generation acceptance check 4 is required. **And the knob must agree with the wired instance, under the same rule `payload_cap` carries:** the mechanism this knob names must be the mechanism the wired Tamper Evidence instance actually runs — that atom is mechanism-neutral and records the choice outside itself (implementation policy, a Mechanism Registry, or deployment configuration, per its own Behavior), so this knob is the composition's *declaration* of that choice, never a selector that configures it. The two must be equal: a disagreement is a **deployment fault**, and the one place it can surface from the records is [Seal Now]'s `mechanism-failure(reason)` arm — keying material that fails the actually-running mechanism's preconditions, which Tamper Evidence routes there (reserving its `invalid-request` for an absent credential or an empty record-set reference). Every other disagreement — a chaining posture different from the one check 4's forensic-window reading assumes, a rendering commitment different from the one declared below — is invisible from the records by mechanism opacity (Tamper Evidence Invariant 8), which is exactly why the agreement is audited externally rather than from the trail (*Externally-clearable checks*). **The setting rule carries one further obligation, and it is the one this knob exists to pick up:** the record rendering the selected mechanism commits to at **seal time** MUST be the same rendering *Primitive policies* fixes for **verify-time** presentation — the payloads of every event in the sealed range, **byte-exact, in ascending `sequence_number` order**, with no re-serialization, key reordering, or encoding fixup on either side. Tamper Evidence's *Record-set definition* edge case routes exactly this agreement to the host — the atom takes `record_set_ref` as opaque, observes that different mechanisms make different assumptions (Merkle trees expect a defined leaf order, hash chains a defined sequence), and requires the host to present the record set consistently at seal time and at verify time — and this composition is that host. Nothing in the seal store records *which* rendering a seal committed to, so the two sides are never obliged to agree by any record: a mechanism that hashed the range in a different order, or over a re-serialized form, would produce proofs that fail every conforming verify-time presentation while nothing in the trail said why. The deployment declares the agreement, and it is audited externally rather than from the records (*Externally-clearable checks*). Mechanism choice is otherwise implementation policy; the Tamper Evidence atom is neutral.
- **`mechanism_credential`** — *Type:* opaque credential material passed to `TamperEvidence.seal` on every seal, whatever the cadence. Its nature is the mechanism's business, not the composition's: a signing key for a keyed mechanism, a Time-Stamp Authority (TSA — a trusted third party that signs proofs of when data existed) client credential for an anchored mechanism. *Default:* **empty (unkeyed)** — a bare hash chain needs no credential, and Tamper Evidence's own rule permits an empty credential, so the empty value is the safe default and the precondition check lives in the constituent, not here. *Setting rule:* the deployment sets it if and only if the configured `seal_mechanism` is keyed or anchored; deployments requiring the seal to carry non-repudiation must set a credential whose nature supplies it, since non-repudiation depends on the credential, not on this composition's surface. The composition passes it through unchanged and never inspects or logs it.
- **`unsealed_tail_mode`** — *Type:* one of `strict` or `lenient`, governing what [Verify Record] returns for an event still in the unsealed tail. *Default:* **`strict`** — the fail-closed value: an uncovered event returns `failed-verification(unsealed)`, because integrity really is unverified until a seal covers it, and defaulting the other way would let a deployment report unverified events as `verified` by omission. *Setting rule:* regulated deployments keep `strict`. A deployment may set `lenient` — accepting Event Log's per-event immutability as sufficient until the next cadence firing, so a tail event can still return `verified` — only where the log substrate is independently trusted (WORM — Write-Once-Read-Many — storage, an external replica) and the `seal_cadence` is coarse enough that a `strict` tail would be a standing false negative. The knob is deployment policy; the composition names the choice rather than assuming one.
- **`erasure_mechanism`** — *Type:* a reference to the deployment's composed content-destruction mechanism, invoked at [Purge Event] step 3, which **must be shredding-class** — it destroys recoverability rather than stored bytes, and never mutates, blanks, or truncates a stored field; the full argument for why only that class conforms lives in the cascade-on-purge rule, *Boundary one*, and is not restated here. **What the mechanism destroys is exact, and the exactness is the point:** it destroys the readability of (a) **Event Log's `data` field in its entirety** and (b) the attestation's **`proof`** field — the cryptographic artifact Actor Identity records as binding its `actor_ref` to its `action_ref`. *In its entirety* is the load-bearing phrase, because the name `data` does double duty on this page: Event Log's `data` field is the **whole constructed object** `{action_ref, actor_ref, attestation_id, data}` that [Record Action] step 3 appended, and the caller-supplied `data` (payload sub-field) is one member inside it. The mechanism takes the outer object, so the payload's own copies of `action_ref`, `actor_ref`, and `attestation_id` lose readability along with the sub-field — which is why the purged-event answers at [Read Record] step 4 and in Invariant 1 are read out of the attestation store and the destruction record instead. The attestation's other stored fields — its `attestation_id`, `action_ref`, `actor_ref`, and `attested_at` (Actor Identity's own five-field record, less the `proof`) — **survive readable**. The regulator's *who / what / when* therefore outlives the purge; what the cascade destroys is the payload content and the cryptographic verifiability of the binding, not the record of who did what and when. *Default:* **none — deployment-required.** There is no safe default and no fallback: the composition destroys no content itself (Event Log declares no delete surface; Actor Identity never deletes an attestation), so an instance with no `erasure_mechanism` set cannot complete a cascade and does not start. Defaulting to *no destruction* would leave retention records reading *Purged* over readable content — the exact silent gap Invariant 8 exists to foreclose — and defaulting to any concrete mechanism would be the composition choosing a deployment's erasure posture. **The mechanism's outcome vocabulary is fixed here, because the cascade has to be able to tell success from failure and a mechanism that only reported *I was called* would make that undecidable.** The mechanism's outcome record reports exactly one of two values for the event it was asked about: **`destroyed`** — recoverability of both targets is gone — or **`destruction-failed(reason)`**, naming why (key store unreachable, shred operation refused, target already unreadable in a way the mechanism cannot confirm). Both are recorded; the negative one is not swallowed. **The recorded outcome is composition state**: the `erasure_outcomes` element (Composition state) — extraction-pending against the same forthcoming Erasure Tombstone atom, addressable per `event_id`, and riding the cascade's durable write alongside the step-2 pair (*Durability across crashes*). [Purge Event] step 3 maps a reported `destruction-failed` to `rejected(cascade-failure(step-3))`, and the reconciliation scan's half-completed-cascade predicate tests for a **successful** outcome record — the presence of an outcome record saying `destruction-failed` closes nothing. *Setting rule:* the deployment names a mechanism whose class it can evidence as shredding-class to its auditor (Generation acceptance, *Externally-clearable checks* — the composition's records cannot show the key really went away), and whose outcome surface reports the two-value taxonomy above **per event asked about** — the outcome must be traceable to the `event_id` it reports on, which is the addressability `erasure_outcomes` and check 5(e) resolve by; **tombstone-by-mutation mechanisms are non-conforming** and a deployment that wires one has left this composition's contract.
- **`compensation_window`** — *Type:* a duration. It is the bound within which the liveness arms of Invariants 1, 2, and 8 must close: an orphan attestation, an event in the log without a retention record, or a half-completed cascade must be surfaced *and* reconciled inside it, and outside it the same state is a violation rather than a bounded transient. *Default:* **none — deployment-required.** There is no safe default: the window is the deployment's declared tolerance for a surfaced compliance finding standing open, which is a regulatory judgment about its own regime, and a composition that picked one would be asserting how long an audit surface may be inconsistent. An instance with no `compensation_window` set does not start. *Setting rule:* the deployment sets it from the tightest reconciliation deadline its regime imposes, and it is **the value Generation-acceptance check 2 audits against** — the auditor enumerates at quiescence, which this knob defines as *no [Record Action] in flight and this duration elapsed*. **The bound against `reconciliation_cadence` is strict, not loose:** the window must be **strictly greater than** one `reconciliation_cadence` interval, **with headroom for the compensating write itself**. A window merely *equal* to the cadence is unmeetable by construction — a finding detected at the very end of one scan interval would have zero elapsed time in which to be compensated — and the compensating write is not free: it is an ordinary [Record Action], attesting, appending, and placing a retention across three stores. The deployment sets the window to at least one cadence interval plus the observed worst-case latency of that write. **The bound has a surfacing arm rather than being advisory:** the pair is checked at instance start, and an instance whose `compensation_window` is not strictly greater than its resolved `reconciliation_cadence` **does not start** — the same refusal every mis-set deployment-required knob gets, and for the same reason: a window the scan cannot close inside is not a tolerance, it is a standing violation declared in advance. The start check enforces the strict inequality; the write-latency headroom remains the deployment's own setting-rule obligation, unverifiable at start because the latency is observed, not declared.
- **`reconciliation_cadence`** — *Type:* a duration: how often the reconciliation scan runs. **The scan has three halves, and this knob governs all three** — over *Purged* retentions, checking each for a `purged_events` membership, a destruction-record pair, and a **successful** (`destroyed`) mechanism outcome record; over the attestation store, for unbound attestations; and over the audit log, for events carrying no retention record at all (the cascade-on-purge rule, which specifies all three predicates — the binding-set predicate the unbound-attestation half turns on, and the `event_to_retention` test the third half turns on — and the *Partial attestation on step failure* edge case). The scan also runs at restart, unconditionally, independent of this knob. *Default:* the **time arm** of the deployment's `seal_cadence` where that cadence is *interval-based and carries one* (*every T seconds*), and **deployment-required otherwise**. The restriction to the time arm is load-bearing rather than fussy: `seal_cadence`'s interval form may be *every N events*, *every T seconds*, or both, and an **events-only** cadence yields no duration to derive from — a rate measured in appends says nothing about how long an unreconciled compliance finding may stand, and a quiet write period would stretch the reconciliation interval without bound while the `compensation_window` kept running. So events-only interval cadence joins *per-event* and *on-demand* on the deployment-required side; where the cadence carries both arms (*every N events or every T seconds, whichever first*), the time arm is the one the default takes. The seal cadence's time arm is the safe derived default because it is already the rate at which this deployment has declared it wants the audit surface brought up to date. *Setting rule:* the deployment sets it **strictly shorter** than the `compensation_window` it declared, by at least the latency of the compensating write — the mirror of that knob's own rule, and for the same reason: a scan no faster than the window cannot close findings inside it — and tightens it where the purge volume is high enough that a full sweep would otherwise carry many unreconciled entries at once. The strict bound is enforced at instance start — a pair that violates it does not start (the `compensation_window` entry names the arm and its scope).
- **`reconciliation_operator`** — *Type:* an `actor_ref`: the deployment's maintenance actor authorized to record under the reserved `audit.*` namespace. **This knob is the discriminator [Record Action] step 1's namespace gate turns on:** a call supplying an `action_ref` whose bytes begin with `audit.` is the reconciliation path's exactly when its `actor_ref` equals this value — byte-identity, like every comparison in *Primitive policies* — and is `rejected(invalid-request)` otherwise. Without a declared discriminator the gate would be undecidable: "external caller" names no observable property of a call, so an implementer could conformingly refuse `audit.*` from everyone (the reconciliation scan could not write, and the liveness arms of Invariants 1, 2, and 8 could never close) or accept it from everyone (the closure marker would be forgeable). *Default:* **none — deployment-required.** The reconciliation scan is not optional — the three liveness arms close through its compensating writes — so an instance with no `reconciliation_operator` set does not start. *Setting rule:* the deployment provisions a real Actor Identity actor in the wired attestation store with usable credential material (the cascade-on-purge rule, *Where the compensation lands* — [Record Action] step 2 attests the operator like any other caller) and sets this knob to that actor's `actor_ref`. **The knob does not weaken the non-forgeability claim:** knowing the operator's `actor_ref` admits a caller past step 1 only; step 2's `ActorIdentity.attest` still demands the operator's credential, and a caller without it is refused `invalid-credential` with nothing recorded — so an `audit.*` event cannot exist in the log unless it was attested under the operator identity, which is what the rebuild's unforgeability reading (Generation-acceptance check 7) rests on.
- **`payload_cap`** — *Type:* a byte size: the ceiling [Record Action] step 1 checks the **full constructed payload** `{action_ref, actor_ref, attestation_id, data}` against, before step 2's `attest` commits. **What is measured is the serialized envelope** — the byte length of the object *exactly as it is handed to* `EventLog.append`, in exactly the encoding the wired instance sizes it in, framing and field names included — and **not** the sum of the four members' own lengths. The distinction is load-bearing rather than pedantic, and it points in one direction: a check that added up the members would **under-count** by whatever the serialization's structure costs, and under-counting is precisely the direction that lets an oversized payload past step 1 and into the `invalid-payload` at step 3 that this check exists to prevent — stranding a committed, immutable attestation. Step 1 states the same rule. *Default:* **none — deployment-required.** Event Log fixes no payload cap in its own invariants — the cap is a per-instance configuration of the wired Event Log instance — so the composition can neither derive one nor safely assume the atom's declared 64 KB default — a per-instance configuration the wired instance may have changed; and the value is not optional bookkeeping, because step 1's check is what keeps an oversized payload from stranding a committed, immutable attestation, and a check with no ceiling is no check. An instance with no `payload_cap` set does not start. *Setting rule:* the deployment reads the cap off the Event Log instance it wired and sets this knob to **exactly that value**. The two must be equal: set higher, step 1 passes payloads `EventLog.append` will reject, and the rejection arrives after the attestation has committed — the orphan the ordering exists to prevent; set lower, the composition refuses payloads its own log would have accepted. A disagreement between the two is a **deployment fault**, and the arm it surfaces on is `invalid-payload` at [Record Action] step 3 — reaching that arm after step 1 has passed *means* the two caps disagree, which is why that step names it a deployment fault rather than an ordinary caller rejection.
- **`reference_length_cap`** — *Type:* a byte length, applied independently to `action_ref` and to `actor_ref` at [Record Action] step 1 (*Primitive policies*). *Default:* **1 KB each** — generous for a reference token, and safe because it is a *ceiling* rather than a semantic choice: it constrains nothing a well-formed caller does, and it exists so a caller cannot push the constructed payload past Event Log's cap through the reference fields alone. *Setting rule:* the deployment lowers it where its reference vocabulary is known to be short and it wants the rejection to arrive at the reference rather than at the payload cap, and raises it only where a genuine reference scheme exceeds 1 KB. The cap must leave headroom under the configured `payload_cap` for `attestation_id_width` plus the largest `data` the deployment intends to accept, or a well-formed caller's reference fields alone can push the constructed payload past the payload ceiling.
- **`attestation_id_width`** — *Type:* a byte length: the width of the `attestation_id` the configured Actor Identity instance allocates. *Default:* **none — deployment-required.** Actor Identity declares the `attestation_id` opaque and host-allocated at its own seam and fixes no width, so the composition cannot derive one; and the value is not optional bookkeeping — [Record Action] step 1's full-payload size check needs it to size `{action_ref, actor_ref, attestation_id, data}` *before* step 2's `attest` commits, which is the whole reason that check sits ahead of the attestation. *Setting rule:* the deployment reads the width from the Actor Identity instance it wired and sets it to that instance's **maximum** allocated width, never its typical one — an under-declared width makes step 1's check optimistic and reopens the oversized-payload-strands-a-committed-attestation path the ordering exists to close.

### Primitive policies

The composition takes four caller-supplied inputs at [Record Action], one at each id-addressed surface, and one more at [Verify Record]. Each is validated at this layer or by a named constituent; nothing is normalized anywhere.

- **`action_ref`** — opaque reference to the action being recorded. Must contain at least one non-whitespace character. **Length cap: the configured `reference_length_cap`** (Configuration; default 1 KB) — generous for a reference token, and present so that a caller cannot push the constructed payload past Event Log's cap through the reference fields alone. Validated **at this layer**, before any constituent is called; a failure (empty, all-whitespace, or over cap) returns `rejected(invalid-request)` with nothing recorded. No normalization: no trimming, no case-folding, no Unicode normalization. Equality is opaque byte-identity, which is what makes Invariant 1's `action_ref`-match check mechanical. (Actor Identity applies the same non-empty minimum at `attest`; validating here first means the rejection is clean rather than post-attestation.) **The `audit.*` namespace is reserved, and the reservation is a validation rule rather than a convention.** An `action_ref` whose bytes begin with the prefix `audit.` is rejected `rejected(invalid-request)` at [Record Action] step 1 unless the call's `actor_ref` is the configured **`reconciliation_operator`** (Configuration — the declared discriminator for the composition's own reconciliation path), with nothing recorded — the same locus and the same arm as the emptiness and length rules. The prefix is byte-identity like every other comparison here; no normalization, no case-folding, so `Audit.` is a different reference and is not reserved. Only the composition's **own internal recording paths** — the reconciliation scan's compensation writes, identified by that discriminator — may use the namespace, under the two references it defines there (`audit.compensation` and `audit.reconciliation`; the cascade-on-purge rule, *Where the compensation lands*), and on that path step 1 additionally enforces the `audit.compensation` payload requirement. The reservation exists because two composition-owned surfaces read *inside* payloads written under these references — `compensated_attestations`' rebuild (Composition state) and, through it, Invariant 1's orphan-freedom closure — and a caller able to write under them could close a compliance finding the composition never compensated. Reserving the namespace is what makes those records evidence rather than assertion.
- **`actor_ref`** — opaque reference to the actor performing the action. Same rule, same locus, same reasons: non-empty and non-whitespace, **the same configured `reference_length_cap`**, applied independently to this field, no normalization, byte-identity equality.
- **`credential`** — opaque credential material consumed **only** by `ActorIdentity.attest`. The composition never inspects it, never normalizes it, never stores it, and never writes it into the event payload or any log line. Its validity is Actor Identity's judgment alone, surfaced as `invalid-credential`. No length cap is applied at this layer: the credential never enters the event payload, so it cannot contribute to the payload-cap check, and its shape is the mechanism's business.
- **`data`** — the opaque audit payload. **A naming note first, because the word does double duty on this page:** *this* `data` is the caller-supplied **payload sub-field**, while **Event Log's `data` field** is the whole constructed object `{action_ref, actor_ref, attestation_id, data}` the composition appends around it. Where the two could be confused the spec writes *Event Log's `data` field* for the outer, appended object and *`data` (payload sub-field)* for this one; the distinction is load-bearing at [Purge Event] step 3, where the erasure mechanism takes the outer object **in its entirety** rather than the sub-field alone. This entry governs the sub-field. It is **opaque to the composition's core logic** — no step of [Record Action], [Read Record], [Verify Record], or the cascade reads inside it, and neither does Event Log — with **two** declared exceptions. The first is at the configuration seam: where the deployment's `retention_policy` is a **policy selector** rather than a fixed `policy_ref`, the selector is handed `data` and **may inspect it** to derive the applicable policy (Configuration; content-derived rules like HIPAA-vs-PCI classification are exactly the case that knob exists for). The exception is narrow and worth naming precisely: the inspecting code is the deployment's selector, not this composition, the inspection happens once at [Record Action] step 4's policy resolution, and nothing the selector reads is retained, normalized, or logged by the composition. Saying flatly *the composition never interprets `data`* would be false at that seam, and a reader who took it literally would be surprised by a selector that reads a field. **The second exception is the composition reading its own writes:** `compensated_attestations`' rebuild procedure (Composition state) enumerates the log and reads the subject-kind discriminator and the orphan `attestation_id` out of the payload of each live `audit.compensation` event — a payload this composition wrote itself, under an action reference it owns, to a shape it fixed for exactly that purpose (the cascade-on-purge rule, *Where the compensation lands*). That is still not an interpretation of a **caller's** `data`: the only payloads any path on this page reads inside are those whose `action_ref` is one of the two composition-owned references, and the `audit.*` namespace reservation above is what guarantees no caller wrote them — a caller's payload stays opaque throughout. The size check at this layer is over the **full constructed payload** `{action_ref, actor_ref, attestation_id, data}` — the exact object step 3 hands to `EventLog.append` — against the configured **`payload_cap`** (Configuration — the knob the deployment sets to exactly the wired Event Log instance's own configured cap, which is per-instance configuration rather than anything Event Log's invariants fix). Checking `data` alone would be the wrong measurement: the reference fields and the `attestation_id` ride in the same payload, so a `data` value just inside the cap can still produce an oversized append. What is measured is the **serialized envelope** — the object as `EventLog.append` receives it, framing included — rather than the sum of the members' own lengths, for the reason the knob gives: summing under-counts, and under-counting is the direction that strands a committed attestation. The check is enforced **at this layer, before step 2's `attest` runs**, using the configured `attestation_id_width` (Configuration — the maximum width the wired Actor Identity instance allocates); oversize returns `rejected(invalid-request)` with nothing recorded. That ordering is load-bearing rather than stylistic: checking the size only when Event Log sees it would let an oversized payload strand a committed, immutable attestation, producing exactly the orphan the *Partial attestation on step failure* edge case calls an anomaly. Empty `data` is valid (Event Log's rule — rejecting meaningless events is the composing pattern's job); a deployment that wants a richer payload schema wires a Schema Evolution pattern *(forthcoming)* above this composition.
- **`event_id`** — opaque, system-generated by Event Log, supplied by callers to address an existing event at [Read Record], [Verify Record], and [Purge Event]. Equality-only: it carries **no ordering semantics** (Event Log's Identity model), which is why `seal_coverage` ranges over `sequence_number` and never over `event_id`, and why an id-addressed read must go through `event_to_sequence` rather than asking Event Log for the id directly. Never normalized; an unknown id yields `not-known` from the addressed action.
- **`original_event_payload`** — the record set the caller re-presents at [Verify Record], passed through unchanged to `TamperEvidence.verify`. **The authoritative rule is one sentence and does not vary: the argument is the payloads of every event in the covering seal's coverage, in ascending `sequence_number` order.** The cadence only moves that coverage's extent, and the per-event case is the shortcut it produces rather than a second rule: under *per-event* `seal_cadence` the covering seal is **typically** a single event's payload, because a per-event seal is cut over a singleton range. Typically, not always — `seal_cadence` is deployment-settable and a deployment that tightened it from interval to per-event leaves older events covered by the ranges its earlier cadence cut, and those events still require their whole covering range presented. Under *interval-based* or *on-demand* cadence the covering seal commits to a range of many events, and the argument is all of their payloads. The verifier never has to reason about the cadence at all — [Read Record] returns the covering seal's range, so the composition tells the caller exactly what to present (Invariant 7), and a caller that follows the returned range is correct under every cadence and every cadence change. Must be non-empty; an empty presentation is a caller error and surfaces from Tamper Evidence as `failed-verification(seal-record-set-mismatch)`, never as the composition's own `not-known`. **Presented byte-exact, with no normalization of any kind** — no re-serialization, no key reordering, no whitespace or encoding fixups. The proof commits to content, so a semantically-equal but byte-different presentation is a mismatch and must read as one; a layer that quietly canonicalized the presentation would be manufacturing agreement the seal never certified.

No input is case-sensitivity-normalized at this layer. Deployments wanting normalization wire it at the calling layer, before invoking a composition action.

### Action wiring

The composition exposes one *record* action that wraps all four constituents, plus *seal*, *read*, *verify*, and *purge* actions over the composed surface. The consolidated per-event join is [Read Record].

**List-shaped queries split by what they select on.** A query **by sequence-number range or by wall-time range** passes through to Event Log's `read` unchanged, without orchestration — both are declared query shapes on that surface. A query **by payload field** — every event referencing action X, every event by actor Y, every event carrying a given business id inside `data` — does **not** pass through: Event Log's own *Reverse lookup / indexing* edge case routes lookup by payload field ("find all events of type X, find all events touching id Y") to a separate **Reverse Index** pattern *(forthcoming)*, and this composition does not absorb it. A deployment that needs `action_ref`-shaped or `actor_ref`-shaped audit queries composes Reverse Index over the audit log and joins its results back through [Read Record] one `event_id` at a time. The one payload-field lookup this composition *does* own — resolving an `event_id` to its `sequence_number` — it owns because it maintains the `event_to_sequence` index for exactly that purpose (Composition state), which is why the id-addressed actions never need a payload predicate.

Each step below names every rejection its constituent call can return and where that rejection lands at this composition's boundary. Silent rejection-code drift is a Pass 1 reference-graph finding, so the enumeration is exhaustive rather than illustrative.

- **[Record Action]** — (Projected contract: `record_action(action_ref, actor_ref, credential, data) → event_id | rejected(invalid-credential | invalid-request | recording-failure(step))`) — the `recording-failure` arm **carries the step that refused**, the same declared-payload shape as `cascade-failure(step)` and `mechanism-failure(reason)`: a bare token would satisfy the projection while defeating the three surfaces that read the step off the outcome (step 7's operator diagnosis, Invariant 1's surfaced-orphan requirement, and the rejection walkthrough).
  1. **Validate the primitives at this layer, before any constituent is called** (see *Primitive policies*): `action_ref` and `actor_ref` each non-empty, non-whitespace, and within the configured reference cap (default 1 KB each); `action_ref` **not in the reserved `audit.*` namespace** unless the call is the reconciliation path's own — the discriminator is mechanical: the supplied `actor_ref` equals the configured `reconciliation_operator` (Configuration), byte-identity; and the **full constructed payload** `{action_ref, actor_ref, attestation_id, data}` — the exact object step 3 will hand to `EventLog.append`, measured as the **serialized envelope** that call receives (framing and field names included, not the sum of the members' own lengths — Configuration, `payload_cap`), sized with the `attestation_id` width the configured Actor Identity instance allocates — within the configured **`payload_cap`** (Configuration). Any failure → `rejected(invalid-request)`; nothing is recorded anywhere. The size check belongs *here*, ahead of step 2, precisely so that an oversized payload can never strand a committed, immutable attestation, and it is taken over the whole serialized payload rather than over `data` alone because the reference fields ride in the same object and the framing rides with them.

     **The namespace check has two sides and both are this step's work, split by one declared discriminator: whether the call's `actor_ref` is the configured `reconciliation_operator` (Configuration).** On the **external** side — any call whose `actor_ref` is *not* that value; there is no other marker of origin — it is a plain refusal: an `action_ref` whose bytes begin with `audit.` returns `rejected(invalid-request)` with nothing recorded, because the two references in that namespace are the ones `compensated_attestations`' rebuild and Invariant 1's orphan-freedom closure read inside (*Primitive policies*, `action_ref`), and a forgeable compensation record is a closable compliance finding. (A caller that supplies the operator's `actor_ref` without the operator's credential passes this step and is refused at step 2's `attest` with nothing recorded — the gate is the pair: discriminator here, credential there.) On the **internal** side — a call supplying the configured operator identity, the only calls permitted the prefix — this step additionally enforces the **`audit.compensation` payload requirement**: the payload must carry the subject-kind discriminator and the corresponding id (`subject = attestation` with an `attestation_id`, or `subject = event` with an `event_id`; the cascade-on-purge rule, *Where the compensation lands*), and a compensating write that omits either is refused here rather than recorded as a compensation naming nothing. This is the one place the composition validates the *shape* of a payload, and it validates only payloads it wrote itself.
  2. `ActorIdentity.attest(action_ref, actor_ref, credential)` → `attestation_id`. Rejection mapping: `invalid-credential` → propagated as `rejected(invalid-credential)`; `invalid-request` → propagated as `rejected(invalid-request)`; `storage-failure` → [Recording Failure]. In all three arms nothing further is recorded and no attestation exists — Actor Identity's `storage-failure` guarantees no partial record was written.
  3. `EventLog.append({action_ref, actor_ref, attestation_id, data})` → `event_id`. The composition does not supply `recorded_at`; Event Log stamps it at its own seam from the host-injected clock (per Event Log's Identity model / Inputs and the Logic Confinement Principle). Where the audit event's timestamp is read back later, it is Event Log's stamped `recorded_at`. If a business event-time is needed it lives inside the opaque `data`, distinct from Event Log's `recorded_at`. Rejection mapping: `storage-failure` → [Recording Failure]; `invalid-payload` → `rejected(invalid-request)`. That second arm is **reachable** — `data` is caller-supplied and Event Log enforces a payload cap — which is why step 1 checks the size first against the configured **`payload_cap`**; reaching it after step 1 has passed means the configured `payload_cap` and the wired Event Log instance's own cap disagree, which the knob's setting rule forbids. It is a **deployment fault** the deployment must alert on, not an ordinary caller rejection, and `invalid-payload` at this step is the arm it surfaces on.
  4. `RetentionWindow.place_under_retention(event_id, retention_policy)` → `retention_id`. The `record_ref` is the `event_id`; the policy is the instance's configured `retention_policy` (or the value its selector returns). Rejection mapping: `invalid-request` → propagated as `rejected(invalid-request)`; `invalid-policy` and `policy-not-found` → `rejected(invalid-request)`, and **this is a deployment fault, not a caller fault** — the policy reference came from this composition's own Configuration, not from the caller's arguments, so the deployment must alert on it rather than surface it as ordinary input rejection; `storage-failure` → [Recording Failure].
  5. Record `event_to_attestation[event_id] = attestation_id`, `event_to_retention[event_id] = retention_id`, and `event_to_sequence[event_id] = sequence_number`. The `sequence_number` comes from **the declared read-back**: a sequence-range query over the new tail through `EventLog.read`, matching the returned `event_id` by equality (Event Log Invariants 4 and 6). **The read's bound is stated rather than left to the implementation, because "the new tail" is not self-defining:** the read runs from **the composition's last known sequence high-water mark** — the greater of `sealed_through` and the highest `sequence_number` this instance has itself recorded into `event_to_sequence` — **to the open end**, using the same open-upper-bound query shape the capability requirement in *Composes* obliges. The lower bound is a high-water mark rather than a fixed window because concurrent [Record Action] calls interleave freely (*Concurrency*, obligation 3) and the event this call appended may sit behind several others by the time the read issues; an open upper bound is what keeps that from being a miss. **If the read comes back without this `event_id` anyway** — a stale high-water mark, a read replica behind the append, an instance whose read consistency lags — the composition does **not** guess: it falls back to the element's declared **rebuild procedure**, the full open-upper-bound enumeration re-keyed on each returned event's own `event_id` (Composition state), which is total by construction. That fallback costs a full enumeration and is expected to be rare; what it buys is that step 5 never writes a wrong `sequence_number` and never leaves the index silently short. That is the primary path because it uses only Event Log's declared Q surface, which every conforming instance has. Where an instance's append result *also* surfaces the assigned `sequence_number` — Event Log's Feedback declares the appended event receives `sequence_number` = the log's prior `next_sequence_number`, so the value is determined at the append seam — the implementation may take it from there and skip the read; that is an **instance optimization**, and an implementation that has it should use it. What the composition must not do is *depend* on it: no Event Log invariant obliges an instance to surface the value at the append seam, so a specification that made the append-result capture the contract would be pinning correctness on a Feedback detail rather than on a declared read. All three writes are derived-index writes, outside the action's atomicity surface (Composition state): a failure here is a rebuild trigger, not a [Recording Failure].
  6. Under per-event `seal_cadence`, immediately seal through [Seal Now]'s wiring; under interval or on-demand cadence, defer to the next batch. **What gets sealed is the range the cadence firing actually cuts** — `[sealed_through + 1 .. tail]`, which under per-event cadence is *typically* the singleton range holding just this new event, but is wider whenever a prior firing's seal failed and left events stranded in the tail. The firing seals the tail it finds, not the event that triggered it; a step that promised "the singleton range" would be describing the healthy case as if it were the contract. **A seal failure at this step does not reject [Record Action].** The load-bearing writes have committed — the actor is attested, the event is appended, the retention is placed — and the event simply remains in the unsealed tail, which Invariant 3 already states the coverage claim modulo. `TamperEvidence.seal`'s `invalid-request`, `mechanism-failure`, and `storage-failure` are surfaced here as a **deployment alerting obligation** (an unsealed-tail alarm naming the cause), and the seal is retried at the next cadence firing. Rejecting the call would be a lie about the record: it exists, it is attributed, and it is under retention.
  7. Return `event_id`. If step 3 or step 4 fails **on its `storage-failure` arm** after step 2 has succeeded, the composition returns `rejected(recording-failure(step))` — the arm's declared payload naming the step that refused — and the implementation must address the resulting partial state. The clause is scoped to the storage-failure arms deliberately: steps 3 and 4 also reject on `invalid-payload` / `invalid-request` / `invalid-policy` / `policy-not-found`, and those map to `rejected(invalid-request)` per their own step, not to [Recording Failure] — they leave the same partial state and the same compensation obligation, but they are input or deployment faults, not store failures, and collapsing them into `recording-failure` would misname the cause the operator has to fix.

     **Which partial state is left depends on where the sequence stopped, and the two are different orphans with different owners.** *Step 3 fails after step 2 succeeded* — the attestation committed and the append did not — leaves an **orphan attestation**: an immutable Actor Identity record bound to no event, owned by the *Partial attestation on step failure* edge case, closed by Invariant 1's liveness arm, and found by the reconciliation scan's **binding-set** half. *Step 4 fails after step 3 succeeded* — the append committed and the retention placement did not — leaves an **unretained event**: an immutable log entry governed by no retention record, owned by Invariant 2's liveness arm, closed by the compensating `place_under_retention` under the per-`event_id` serialization, and found by the reconciliation scan's **third half** (the cascade-on-purge rule). The two are not interchangeable: the first is an attestation nothing points at, the second is an event nothing bounds the lifetime of, and a reconciliation path that looked only for the first would carry the second forever. Both are surfaced on the same arm; which one the operator is looking at is read off the step the outcome carries — which is why the `(step)` payload is declared in the projected contract rather than left to logging.

- **[Seal Now]** — (Projected contract: `seal_now() → evidence_id | rejected(nothing-to-seal | mechanism-failure(reason) | invalid-request | recording-failure)`) — under interval or on-demand cadence, seals the current unsealed tail.

  **The tail is a declared read, not a reach into Event Log's state.** `tail` is defined as **the highest `sequence_number` returned by `EventLog.read` over a sequence-range query with an open upper bound** — the range beginning at `sealed_through + 1` with no upper bound — and the empty result of that read *is* the empty-tail condition. This matters because the obvious alternative phrasing, "the log's `next_sequence_number` minus one," would be reading an Event Log **internal state field** that the atom exposes on no declared surface: `next_sequence_number` is the allocator high-water mark, it counts allocations rather than successful appends (Event Log's own storage-failure gap edge case leaves the two apart), and Event Log's own Invariant 5 was re-scoped off it for exactly that reason. The open-upper-bound read returns successfully-appended events only, which is the set a seal must commit to. The open upper bound is a **declared instance capability requirement** (*Composes*): if this read returns `EventLog.read`'s `invalid-query`, the wired instance does not support the query shape the composition requires, and that is a **deployment-configuration fault** the deployment alerts on — not a refusal [Seal Now] surfaces to a caller, since no caller input reaches the query.

  Returns [Nothing To Seal] if that read comes back empty (no events since `sealed_through`). Otherwise the presented record set is the sequence-number slice `[sealed_through + 1 .. tail]`; the call delegates to `TamperEvidence.seal(slice_ref, mechanism_credential)`, records `seal_coverage[evidence_id]` as that range, and advances `sealed_through` to `tail`. Rejection mapping — **each constituent arm gets its own outcome, because they name three different things an operator has to fix**:
  - Tamper Evidence's `mechanism-failure(reason)` → [Mechanism Failure] **carrying the constituent's `(reason)` through unchanged** — the mechanism could not compute a proof, and that atom's own contract routes **two operationally different worlds** onto this one arm, which is why the carried reason must be read rather than the arm alone. One is **transient outage**: signing hardware down, TSA unreachable under anchored mode, HSM — Hardware Security Module, the tamper-resistant device that holds signing keys and performs the signing — session lost; the next cadence firing may well clear it, and the operator restores the mechanism. The other is **standing misconfiguration**: keying material that fails the mechanism's preconditions — an empty credential against a keyed mechanism, a TSA client credential against a bare hash chain — which Tamper Evidence's own Decision points route to `mechanism-failure`, *not* to `invalid-request`, and which every subsequent firing reproduces identically until a human changes Configuration. In both worlds the seal did not land, `sealed_through` does not advance, and the tail is retried at the next firing; which world the operator is in is read off the `(reason)`, and treating a preconditions reason as an outage to wait out is the misdiagnosis this mapping exists to prevent.
  - Tamper Evidence's `invalid-request` → `rejected(invalid-request)` **as its own arm** — the *request itself* was malformed at that atom's guard, which reserves this arm for exactly two things: a record-set reference with no non-whitespace character, or a `mechanism_credential` **absent entirely** (an *empty* credential is valid for unkeyed mechanisms, and wrong-*shape* keying material is `mechanism-failure`, above — the constituent's own split). Neither input comes from a caller — the composition builds the slice reference itself and passes the configured credential through — so this arm reaching an operator means a defect in the composition's own call construction or a configuration plumbing fault that passed no credential at all: standing, not transient, and a page for a human rather than something to retry.
  - Tamper Evidence's `storage-failure` → [Recording Failure] — the mechanism computed a proof and the **seal store** refused to persist it. `sealed_through` does not advance and no `seal_coverage` entry is written, so the tail is intact and the next cadence firing re-seals the same slice; Tamper Evidence's own `storage-failure` guarantee is that no partial evidence record was written, so there is no half-seal to reconcile. It is named `recording-failure` rather than [Mechanism Failure] because the mechanism did its job — the failing surface is a store, which is the same distinction [Record Action] draws when a constituent store refuses a write.

  Under every rejection arm the events stay in the unsealed tail and `unsealed_tail_mode` governs what [Verify Record] says about them; a tail that stops draining is the operational alarm the *Verification of the unsealed tail* edge case names.

  **There is no re-sealing surface, and its absence is declared rather than accidental.** [Seal Now] cuts new coverage over the unsealed tail and nothing else: the canonical composition has no action that writes a second seal over a range some seal already covers. Re-sealing a partly-purged seal's surviving members, and rotating a seal onto a fresh mechanism ahead of that mechanism's deprecation, both belong to a forthcoming **Seal Lifecycle** composing pattern (Edge cases — *Seal re-sealing and mechanism deprecation*), which owns them together with the supersession bookkeeping they both require.

- **[Read Record]** — (Projected contract: `read_record(event_id) → audit_record | not-known`) — the consolidated read surface. It is a **pure projection**: it changes no state, produces no audit event (reading the trail is not itself an audited action at this layer), and carries no Permissions layer at this composition — callers gate access to it. It is the surface Invariant 6 rests on and the one [Audit Record] is produced by.
  1. Read the retention record: look up `event_to_retention[event_id]` (rebuild-on-miss) and read the retention.
  2. Read the event. Resolve `event_to_sequence[event_id]` → `n` (rebuild-on-miss), then call `EventLog.read(query)` with the **declared sequence-range query** for the singleton range `[n .. n]`. The composition never asks Event Log to select on `event_id`: no read-by-id surface is declared there, and payload-predicate lookup is routed to Reverse Index (above). A well-formed query matching nothing returns an empty sequence rather than a rejection. **`EventLog.read`'s `invalid-query` rejection is unreachable by construction here**: the composition builds the query itself from its own index, so the only inputs are a well-formed singleton sequence range over a value Event Log itself assigned; there is no caller-supplied query shape to be malformed. An implementation that nonetheless observes `invalid-query` has a defective index or a query builder that disagrees with its Event Log instance — a deployment fault to alert on, not an outcome this action's contract exposes. That is why `read_record` carries a `not-known` arm and no rejection arm at all.
  3. If there is **neither** a retention record **nor** a log entry → `not-known`. That conjunction is the definition of a fabricated id: no constituent has ever heard of it. The fourth cell of the two-by-two — a retention record whose `event_id` resolves to **no log entry** — is **unreachable by construction, and stated rather than silently unenumerated**: [Record Action] step 4 places a retention only after step 3's append has committed, Event Log's append-only invariant means a committed event never leaves the log, and the cascade transitions a retention to *Purged* without ever removing the log entry — so no conforming history produces a retention over a log-absent id. An implementation that observes one anyway is looking at a defective store or an out-of-band write into the retention store — a deployment fault to alert on, not an outcome this contract enumerates. ([Verify Record] step 2 and [Purge Event]'s `not-known` arm read the same two-by-two and inherit the same reasoning.)
  4. Otherwise assemble and return the [Audit Record], joining: the event's `action_ref`, `actor_ref`, `sequence_number`, `recorded_at`, and `data` where the content is still present; the `attestation_id` from `event_to_attestation`; the retention record's `retention_id`, policy reference, state (*Retained* or *Purged*), `retention_until`, `purge_deadline`, and `purged_at` where set; and the seal-coverage status — the covering `evidence_id` (the one whose `seal_coverage` entry contains this `sequence_number`) with **its full sequence-number range** and its `sealed_at` (and `anchored_at` where the mechanism anchors), or *unsealed tail* where `sequence_number > sealed_through`, or *records-purged* where this event's own `sequence_number` is a member of that seal's `purged_events` set. Four things about that line are load-bearing.

     First, the *records-purged* status is reported **per event, not per seal**: a seal covering a thousand events reports records-purged for exactly those of its members whose sequence numbers are in `purged_events`, and reports ordinary coverage for the rest.

     Second, returning the covering seal's **range** is what makes [Verify Record]'s asymmetry usable — under interval cadence the verifier must present the payloads of the whole covering set, and this is where the composition tells them which set that is (Invariant 7; *Primitive policies*, `original_event_payload`). Presenting exactly what is returned is the whole of the caller's obligation.

     Third, for a **live** event whose covering seal is **partly purged** — this event's `sequence_number` is *not* in `purged_events`, but some other member's is — the returned status names the coverage as **partially purged**, and it is the honest signal that [Verify Record] on this event will answer `unverifiable(partially-purged-coverage)` rather than `verified` or a failure: the record set the seal commits to can no longer be re-presented, because part of it was lawfully destroyed. That is the canonical composition's standing answer for such an event, not a transient — it has no re-sealing surface with which to make the coverage presentable again, and the deployment's remedy is the forthcoming **Seal Lifecycle** pattern (Edge cases).

     Fourth — and this is the branch that keeps the action answerable at all after a cascade — **for an event whose retention state is *Purged*, the `action_ref`, `actor_ref`, and `attested_at` are read from the attestation record, not from the event payload.** The purge destroys the readability of **Event Log's `data` field in its entirety** (Configuration, `erasure_mechanism`), and that field is the whole constructed object `{action_ref, actor_ref, attestation_id, data}` — so the payload's copies of the *who* and the *what*, and the payload's copy of the `attestation_id` that would have led to them, are all unreadable together. The surviving carrier is the **destruction record's `(event_id, attestation_id)` pair**, captured at [Purge Event] step 2 before the delegation ran; the pair names an attestation, and Actor Identity's Outputs declare that each attestation carries `attestation_id`, `action_ref`, `actor_ref`, `attested_at`, and the `proof` — of which the first four survive readable and only the `proof` does not. **That this branch may look an attestation up *by its id* is derivable from the declared surface rather than assumed, and the argument is stated here because this is where the branch first needs it.** Actor Identity's Outputs enumerate *the current set of attestations*, and declare that each one carries its own `attestation_id` among its fields — so an enumeration of that set, re-keyed on the id each member carries, is an id-addressed lookup, built out of nothing the atom does not already declare. This is the one-sentence mirror of the argument `event_to_sequence` makes on the Event Log side, and it lands differently for a good reason: Event Log's Outputs support the same enumerate-and-re-key move but its `read` takes a *query*, and its own *Reverse lookup / indexing* edge case routes id-shaped lookup to a forthcoming Reverse Index, which is why that side needs a composition-owned index and this side does not. No composition-owned index stands between the pair and the attestation; the composition asks Actor Identity's declared read surface for the record the pair names. So the purged [Audit Record] joins: `attestation_id`, `action_ref`, `actor_ref`, and `attested_at` **from the attestation store, reached through the destruction record's pair**; `sequence_number`, `recorded_at`, and the seal-coverage status from Event Log's and the seal store's own untouched stored fields; the retention record in *Purged* with `purged_at`; and **no** `data` — which is the one thing the *where the content is still present* hedge above is hedging. For a **live** event both sources carry the same `action_ref` and `actor_ref` (that agreement is what Invariant 1's live-case field match compares), and reading either is correct; after a purge only the attestation store answers, which is why the pair is the durability obligation *Durability across crashes* names.
  5. **Orphan window.** If a log entry exists but no retention record does, the event is inside the compensation window of Invariant 2's liveness arm. [Read Record] does not return `not-known` — the event is real and the log proves it — and does not fabricate a retention. It returns the [Audit Record] with everything it can join and retention status `unresolved (compensation window)`, which is the honest reading: the retention side has not landed yet and reconciliation owes it. The status is a surfaced compliance finding, not a steady state (Invariant 8).
  6. No verification is performed. [Read Record] presents; [Verify Record] proves. The split is forced by Invariant 7: verification needs the original payload re-presented, and a read surface that quietly fetched it internally would destroy exactly the asymmetry the composition exists to surface.

- **[Verify Record]** — (Projected contract: `verify_record(event_id, original_event_payload) → outcome | not-known`, where `outcome` is `verified | failed-verification(reason) | unverifiable(reason)`, each of which may additionally carry the qualifier `(compensation-window)`)

  **The qualifier is a separate channel, not a fourth outcome and not a reason.** Step 2 can establish that the event is inside Invariant 2's compensation window — a log entry with no retention record yet — and that fact is orthogonal to every integrity finding the remaining steps produce: the attestation and the seal are checkable and their answer is worth having whether or not the retention side has landed. So the qualifier rides *alongside* whichever of the three outcomes the checks produce, and the three combinations are all reachable and all meaningful: `verified (compensation-window)` (integrity is sound, retention is owed), `failed-verification(seal-proof-invalid) (compensation-window)` (integrity is not sound *and* retention is owed — two findings, not one), `unverifiable(attestation-registry-unavailable) (compensation-window)` (integrity is unknown, retention is owed). Collapsing the qualifier into a reason under `failed-verification(...)` would accuse a record that has nothing wrong with its integrity; promoting it to its own outcome would suppress the integrity answer the caller asked for. `not-known` never carries it — the qualifier presupposes a log entry, and `not-known` is the finding that there is none.
  1. **Retention state first.** Read the retention record via `event_to_retention[event_id]` (rebuild-on-miss, like every derived-index consultation — Composition state). If it exists and is in *Purged* → `failed-verification(purged)`: the content was lawfully destroyed and no integrity claim over it is possible. This check runs **before** the log-presence check deliberately, and the reason is the **conforming** case rather than an exotic one. Under a conforming shredding-class mechanism the log entry is emphatically still there — its `event_id`, `sequence_number`, and `recorded_at` are stored fields the cascade never touches, so step 2 would find it and `not-known` was never the risk. What the cascade does destroy is **Event Log's `data` field in its entirety** (Configuration, `erasure_mechanism`), and every downstream check on this action is payload-dependent: step 5 cannot be presented a conforming record set for a seal whose members include this event's now-unreadable payload, and step 3 re-checks a `proof` the same cascade destroyed. Running the retention read first is what lets the composition answer out of the record it still **has** — the *Purged* retention with its `purged_at` — instead of reporting a lawful destruction as a downstream integrity finding, which is precisely the confusion Invariant 8 exists to prevent. **It is also what keeps a purged event's attestation out of step 3, and that is load-bearing rather than incidental.** The cascade destroys the readability of the attestation's `proof` (Configuration, `erasure_mechanism`), so `ActorIdentity.verify` on that attestation can only return one of its non-`verified` arms from here on — the atom re-checks a stored `proof` it can no longer read. The `purged` outcome **anticipates exactly that**: because step 1 short-circuits, step 3 never runs for a purged event, and the composition reports the lawful destruction it has a record of rather than an attestation failure it would be manufacturing out of its own cascade's compliant work. (The attestation's surviving fields — `attestation_id`, `action_ref`, `actor_ref`, `attested_at` — remain readable and remain the auditor's *who/what/when*; what is gone is the cryptographic verifiability, which is the one thing `verify` reports on.)
  2. **Log presence.** Resolve `event_to_sequence[event_id]` → `n` (rebuild-on-miss) and read the singleton sequence range `[n .. n]` through `EventLog.read`, exactly as [Read Record] step 2 does and for the same reason — Event Log declares no read-by-id surface, and `invalid-query` is unreachable by construction because the composition builds the query from its own index rather than from caller input. If there is no retention record **and** `event_id` is not present in the audit log → `not-known`. Fabricated id; nothing further runs. If the log entry is present but the retention record is absent, the event is in the **orphan window** of Invariant 2's liveness arm: verification **proceeds** — the attestation and the seal are checkable and their answers are worth having — and whichever outcome steps 3–7 reach carries the qualifier `(compensation-window)` on the separate channel the contract declares above. An event in the compensation window is *verifiable but not yet retention-covered*, and reporting the two facts separately is the honest form; folding the missing retention into a `failed-verification(...)` would accuse a record that has nothing wrong with its integrity. ([Read Record] step 5 reports the same condition as the [Audit Record]'s retention status `unresolved (compensation window)`; the two are the same finding surfaced on the two surfaces' own shapes.)
  3. `ActorIdentity.verify(event_to_attestation[event_id])` — the index lookup rebuild-on-miss, as everywhere — propagates any `failed-verification(reason)` with the reason prefixed `attestation-` (e.g. `attestation-proof-invalid`, `attestation-actor-unknown-in-registry`). Two of Actor Identity's results do **not** propagate that way, and they sit at different levels of that atom's contract — one is a *reason*, the other an *arm*. `registry-unavailable` is a **reason under Actor Identity's own `failed-verification(...)` arm**, not an arm of its own, and this composition **re-levels** it: it routes to step 6's availability arm rather than being prefixed, because *the registry was unreachable* is not a finding about the record. Actor Identity's `not-known` **is** an arm of its contract (the `attestation_id` does not resolve in the attestation store), and it surfaces here as `failed-verification(attestation-not-known)` — the event exists, so this is a broken link inside a known record, not a fabricated id, and it must not collapse into the composition's own `not-known`.

     **A step-3 result short-circuits steps 4–6, and the precedence belongs here rather than in an example.** Where step 3 yields a prefixed `failed-verification(attestation-...)`, the composition returns it without locating the covering seal or calling `TamperEvidence.verify`: the attestation is the binding the seal's content claim is *about*, and a record whose attribution has already failed gains nothing from a second, subordinate finding against its seal. Where step 3 re-levels `registry-unavailable`, the same short-circuit holds for the opposite reason — verification could not be performed at all, so the seal check is not run and **no integrity claim is made either way**, which is what makes step 6's answer *unknown* rather than *bad*. Precisely: a prefixed `failed-verification(attestation-...)` skips steps 4, 5, **and** 6 and returns directly; a re-levelled `registry-unavailable` skips steps 4 and 5 and is answered by step 6. Steps 4 and 5 run only when step 3 returned `verified`. (The `unverifiable(attestation-registry-unavailable)` run in *Selected rejection and outcome runs* walks it.)
  4. Locate the covering seal: the `evidence_id` whose `seal_coverage` entry contains the event's `sequence_number` (Invariant 3 guarantees there is at most one, because [Seal Now] cuts disjoint slices and nothing else writes a coverage entry).

     **Partly-purged coverage.** If that seal's `purged_events` set is non-empty while this event's own `sequence_number` is not in it, the record set the seal commits to **cannot be re-presented**: some of its members were lawfully destroyed and no conforming presentation of the seal's record set exists any more. Return [Unverifiable] as `unverifiable(partially-purged-coverage)` and run no further checks against the seal. This is *verification is impossible*, not *this record failed* — nothing is known to be wrong with the event, its attestation, or the proof, and the only thing that changed is that the evidence's counterpart was destroyed under a lawful retention. Answering `failed-verification(seal-record-set-mismatch)` here would be the composition manufacturing a finding out of its own cascade's lawful work, which is the same false accusation the [Unverifiable] arm exists to prevent for registry outages. **This is the canonical outcome, and it is standing rather than transient:** the composition has no re-sealing surface with which to give the survivors a presentable seal, so the answer holds for the rest of those events' retained lifetimes. A deployment whose auditor cannot live with it composes the forthcoming **Seal Lifecycle** pattern (Edge cases — *Seal re-sealing and mechanism deprecation*), which owns re-sealing the survivors and the supersession bookkeeping that follows; the remedy is named, and it is named outside this composition rather than hidden inside it.

     If no entry covers the `sequence_number` at all, the event is in the unsealed tail, and `unsealed_tail_mode` (Configuration) decides. Under `strict`: return `failed-verification(unsealed)`. Under `lenient`: treat coverage as satisfied, **skip step 5 and proceed directly to step 6** — there is no `evidence_id`, so there is nothing to present a record set to, and step 5 has no meaning here. The `lenient` path therefore reaches step 7's `verified` on the strength of the attestation check plus Event Log's per-event immutability, which is exactly what the deployment declared when it set the knob.
  5. `TamperEvidence.verify(evidence_id, original_event_payload)` — propagates with the reason prefixed `seal-`. The verifier must present **the record set the covering seal commits to**, and the composition passes `original_event_payload` through unchanged. What that record set *is* follows the cadence, because the seal is what it must match: under *per-event* `seal_cadence` the covering seal commits to this one event, so the argument is this event's payload; under *interval-based* or *on-demand* cadence the covering seal commits to many events, so the argument is the payloads of every event in the seal's range, in ascending `sequence_number` order, obtained through Event Log's declared range read over the range [Read Record] reported. An absent, short, or wrong presented record set surfaces as `failed-verification(seal-record-set-mismatch)`, propagated from Tamper Evidence; this is distinct from the composition's own `not-known`, which is reserved for the fabricated id of step 2. As at step 3, two of Tamper Evidence's results are handled rather than prefixed, and they sit at the same two levels: `mechanism-verification-unavailable` is a **reason under that atom's `failed-verification(...)` arm** and is re-levelled to step 6's availability arm; Tamper Evidence's `not-known` **is** an arm of its contract (the `evidence_id` does not resolve in the seal store) and surfaces as `failed-verification(seal-not-known)`.
  6. **Availability arm.** If either constituent reported that verification could not be performed — Actor Identity's `registry-unavailable`, Tamper Evidence's `mechanism-verification-unavailable` — return [Unverifiable] as `unverifiable(attestation-registry-unavailable)` or `unverifiable(seal-mechanism-verification-unavailable)`, naming which surface was down. This is a distinct outcome arm, not a reason under `failed-verification(...)`: a transient registry outage is *verification could not be performed*, which is emphatically not *this record failed*. Retrying when the surface returns is the correct response; treating it as a failed record is a false accusation against the trail. Step 4's `partially-purged-coverage` is the **third** reason on this same arm and the one that is *not* transient: the two availability reasons resolve when a surface comes back up, while partly-purged coverage does not resolve at all within this composition — it resolves only if the deployment composes the forthcoming **Seal Lifecycle** pattern to re-seal the survivors. Both are the same kind of answer — *verification could not be performed* — which is why they share the arm rather than being split by durability.
  7. All checks pass → `verified`. Where step 2 established the compensation-window condition, the returned outcome — this `verified`, or whichever earlier arm was taken — carries the `(compensation-window)` qualifier on the contract's separate channel.

- **[Purge Eligible]** — (Projected contract: `purge_eligible() → list of event_ids`) — the composition does **not** re-derive `now ≥ retention_until` at this layer. It delegates to Retention Window's own read-time `purge_eligible` projection (Retention Window Invariant 11 — eligibility is derived at read time and never stored) and maps each eligible `retention_id` back to its `event_id` through the retention record's `record_ref`. The eligibility clock is therefore the Retention Window instance's injected `now` — one reading per [Purge Eligible] call. **That reading is not shared with a later `RetentionWindow.purge`, and does not need to be.** The projection and the purge are two independent readings at two different moments: the projection reads `now` at the moment the list is built, and `RetentionWindow.purge` reads its own `now` at the moment the cascade runs, where Retention Window's Invariant 8 single-reading discipline applies *within that Purge action* (its eligibility guard and its `purged_at` stamp read the same value). The gap between the two readings is harmless because eligibility is **monotone in `now`**: `now ≥ retention_until` once true stays true under a non-decreasing clock, so an event on the list cannot become ineligible before the cascade reaches it, and the only reachable disagreement is the benign one Retention Window's own *Clock semantics* edge case names — a record listed just before its boundary and purged just after, or missed by one sweep and caught by the next. The composition's own `now` (see *Clock source for cadence and purge* in Edge cases) governs `seal_cadence` and nothing else. [Purge Eligible] **has no rejection arm**: it is a pure projection over the retention store with no caller-supplied input to be invalid and no store to write, so there is nothing for it to refuse.

- **[Purge Event]** — (Projected contract: `purge_event(event_id) → ok | rejected(not-known | not-eligible | retention-unresolved | cascade-failure(step) | under-legal-hold†)`) — for any event whose retention has elapsed, the composition **coordinates** a cascade across the four stores. Returns `rejected(not-known)` if the `event_id` resolves to neither a retention record nor a log entry; [Not Eligible] when Retention Window refuses the purge because the period has not elapsed; [Retention Unresolved] when the `event_id` resolves to a log entry but to **no retention record at all** — the compensation-window state Invariant 2's liveness arm admits, where there is no retention to purge and therefore no cascade to run. The argument list is unchanged; `retention-unresolved` is an additive arm.

  **[Cascade Failure] is the store-failure arm, and it carries the step so the retention state it left behind is named rather than inferred.** A cascade crosses stores, so a store can refuse mid-cascade, and the halves of that leave *materially different* worlds an operator has to tell apart:
  - `cascade-failure(seal)` — the step-0 precondition seal did not land. **Nothing changed anywhere:** the retention is still *Retained*, the event is still in the unsealed tail, no marker was written and no delegation was issued. The event remains on [Purge Eligible]'s list and the next sweep re-offers it — by which time the ordinary `seal_cadence` firing has usually drained the tail on its own. It is named separately from `step-1` because the surface that refused is the **seal mechanism or seal store**, not the retention store, and the operator's remedy is [Seal Now]'s own three-way diagnosis rather than a retention retry.
  - `cascade-failure(step-1)` — `RetentionWindow.purge` returned `storage-failure`. **Nothing changed anywhere:** the retention is still *Retained*, no marker was written, no destruction record exists, and no delegation was issued. The event remains on [Purge Eligible]'s list, so the ordinary nightly sweep re-offers it and a plain retry is the whole remedy.
  - `cascade-failure(step-2)` / `cascade-failure(step-3)` — the retention moved to *Purged* and then the marker write, the destruction-record write, or the delegation failed. **`step-3` covers two distinct failures and deliberately does not split them**: the delegation could not be issued or its outcome could not be recorded, *and* the delegation was issued and the mechanism reported **`destruction-failed(reason)`** (Configuration, `erasure_mechanism`). Both leave the same world and take the same remedy, which is why one arm carries both; the mechanism's reason, where there is one, rides in its own outcome record for the operator to read. **The retention state is Purged and the cascade is incomplete**, which is the dangerous half: the record now says the content was lawfully destroyed while the evidence of that destruction is missing or the content is still readable. It is also *invisible to [Purge Eligible]*, whose projection is false for a *Purged* retention — so the sweep will never re-offer it. **The reconciliation scan is the retry trigger** (the cascade-on-purge rule; *Cross-store consistency under failure*), it runs at restart and on the `reconciliation_cadence`, and until it closes the entry the state is a surfaced compliance alert bounded by the `compensation_window`.

  Absorbing either into a bare `ok` or an unnamed rejection would hide precisely the gap this composition exists to make unhideable, and collapsing the two into one arm would tell an operator to retry when the retry path is a scan they have not run, or to run a scan when a retry would do. **† [Under Legal Hold] is a conditional arm**: it is present in the contract exactly when the deployment composes a [Legal Hold](../atoms/legal-hold.md) pattern (a legally mandated preservation order suspending normal deletion), and absent otherwise. When present, a hold over this record intercepts the cascade before step 1 runs and the action returns `rejected(under-legal-hold)` with **no step of the cascade executed** — the hold is a preservation order, so a partial cascade under a hold would be the exact destruction the order forbids. The arm is conditional rather than always-present because a composition that declared it unconditionally would be promising a hold check it does not itself perform; Legal Hold owns the gate, and this composition names where the gate lands.
  0. **Seal before purge.** Resolve `event_to_sequence[event_id]` → `n` (rebuild-on-miss). If `n > sealed_through` the event is still in the **unsealed tail**, and the cascade **invokes [Seal Now] first**, before anything else runs. The reason is structural: step 2's destruction record lives *on a covering `seal_coverage` entry*, and an event with no covering entry has nowhere to record that it was destroyed — the cascade would move the retention to *Purged* and then have no place to write the evidence, producing the `cascade-failure(step-2)` world by construction on every single tail purge. Sealing first is what makes the entry exist. If [Seal Now] refuses on any of its arms, the cascade **rejects `cascade-failure(seal)` and nothing changes** — no retention transition, no marker, no delegation. Where `n ≤ sealed_through` the covering entry already exists and this step is a no-op. (Under *per-event* `seal_cadence` the step is almost always a no-op, since [Record Action] step 6 sealed the event at record time; it is reachable there only where that seal failed and the tail has not drained since.)

     **Step 0½ — retention must be resolved before the cascade proper begins.** (Numbered as a half-step deliberately: it is a second precondition alongside step 0, not a store-touching step of the cascade, and folding it into step 1 would put the check behind a `retention_id` the caller does not have when it fails.) Resolve `event_to_retention[event_id]` (rebuild-on-miss). If it resolves to no retention record while the log entry from step 0 exists, the event is in the **compensation window** of Invariant 2's liveness arm — an appended event whose retention placement has not landed — and the action returns **`rejected(retention-unresolved)`**. There is nothing to purge: `RetentionWindow.purge` takes a `retention_id` and there is none, and inventing a retention in order to expire it would be the composition asserting a lifetime no policy ever placed. **The remedy is the reconciliation path, not a retry of this action:** the compensating `place_under_retention` must land first (Invariant 2's liveness arm, under the `event_to_retention` pre-check and the per-`event_id` serialization the *Concurrency* edge case obliges), after which the event acquires an ordinary retention with an ordinary `retention_until` and becomes purgeable — or not — on the same terms as every other event. **No step of the cascade proper has run:** no retention transition, no destruction record, no delegation. The one thing that may have happened is step 0's precondition seal, which is not a cascade step and is not undone — a tail event that got sealed is in a state the composition wanted regardless, the next attempt finds it already covered, and a seal destroys nothing. *Placement:* the check sits **after** step 0 rather than before it because step 0 is itself a precondition over the log side and its `event_to_sequence` resolution is what establishes that a log entry exists at all — which is exactly the antecedent this arm needs in order to distinguish *log entry, no retention* (`retention-unresolved`) from *neither* (`not-known`).
  1. `RetentionWindow.purge(retention_id)` — moves the retention Retained → Purged with `purged_at`. This is **the one constituent with a purge surface — and even it deletes no record**: `purge` is a state transition, the retention record survives in *Purged* state with `purged_at`, and it is that surviving record that is the evidence the destruction was lawful. No constituent of this composition deletes anything, which is exactly why step 3 has to be a delegation. Rejection mapping: `retention-period-not-elapsed` → [Not Eligible]; `not-known` → `rejected(not-known)`; `not-retained` (the retention is already *Purged*) → the cascade resumes from step 2 rather than rejecting, so a re-driven or retried cascade is idempotent; `storage-failure` → [Cascade Failure] as `cascade-failure(step-1)` — the cascade does not proceed, nothing downstream runs, nothing changed, and the event stays on [Purge Eligible]'s list for an ordinary retry.
  2. **Write the destruction record, before anything is destroyed.** Two facts are captured here, together, in one durable write:
     - Add this event's `sequence_number` to the `purged_events` set on the covering `seal_coverage` entry — which exists by construction, because step 0 sealed the event first if it did not. The marker is written at **event granularity**: the entry is not flagged wholesale, because the seal's other members are typically still live and marking them destroyed would be false. The seal record itself is **retained indefinitely** as audit evidence of the destruction — a seal is a compact commitment, and a seal whose record set is partly or wholly destroyed remains proof that those records existed and were sealed before they were destroyed. Nothing in this composition disposes of seals; see the cascade-on-purge rule for why, and for the externally-composed variant that does.
     - Record the pair **`(event_id, attestation_id)`** — read from `event_to_attestation`, or rebuilt from the still-readable payload if the index has been lost. **The ordering is load-bearing:** the binding lives inside the payload, and step 3 destroys the payload's recoverability, so a cascade that captured the pair after the delegation would be reading a binding that no longer exists. Capturing it here is what keeps the auditor's traversal *purged event → the attestation destroyed with it* possible at all.

     Both facts are **extraction-pending** truth against the forthcoming *Erasure Tombstone* atom (Composition state), and this write is the part of the cascade carrying a durability obligation — an implementation that lets it be lost has lost the evidence that a particular sealed record was destroyed and what attested it. A failure here → [Cascade Failure] as `cascade-failure(step-2)`: the retention is already *Purged*, the cascade is incomplete and invisible to [Purge Eligible], and the reconciliation scan is the retry trigger.
  3. **Content destruction is delegated, never performed here, and only to a shredding-class mechanism.** What is destroyed is named exactly: **(a)** **Event Log's `data` field in its entirety** — the whole constructed object `{action_ref, actor_ref, attestation_id, data}` that [Record Action] step 3 appended, not merely the caller-supplied `data` (payload sub-field) inside it, so the payload's own copies of `action_ref`, `actor_ref`, and `attestation_id` lose readability along with it — and **(b)** the attestation's **`proof`** field, the cryptographic artifact Actor Identity records as binding its `actor_ref` to its `action_ref`. Those two, and nothing else. The attestation's remaining stored fields — its **`attestation_id`**, **`action_ref`**, **`actor_ref`**, and **`attested_at`** — **survive readable**, which is the whole reason the cascade is a lawful destruction rather than an erasure of the audit answer: the regulator's *who* (`actor_ref`), *what* (`action_ref`), and *when* (`attested_at`) outlive the purge — and from here on they are read **out of the attestation store, reached through step 2's `(event_id, attestation_id)` pair**, because the payload's copies went with the field ([Read Record] step 4's purged branch; Invariant 1's purged-case clause). Both are destroyed by the deployment's **composed erasure mechanism** (Configuration, `erasure_mechanism`), which must be **shredding-class**: it destroys recoverability rather than stored bytes, so every stored field of the Event Log entry and of the attestation stays exactly as written and Event Log's and Actor Identity's immutability invariants hold verbatim. The full argument for why only that class conforms — why tombstone-by-mutation does not, and why the forthcoming *Erasure Tombstone* pattern is a recorder rather than a mutator — lives in the cascade-on-purge rule, *Boundary one*, and is not restated here. Until that pattern lands, the destruction surface is a **declared deployment obligation**: the deployment names a shredding-class mechanism in the `erasure_mechanism` knob (Configuration), the mechanism performs the destruction, and the cascade records its outcome.

     **What this step is, in the execution contract's own vocabulary, is a mechanism-capability invocation** — [`execution-contract.md`](../execution-contract.md) §Substrate composition invocation, *Mechanism capability invocation is the named residual*: a composition-introduced surface consuming a **deployment-declared mechanism capability**, which is neither an action call on a constituent nor a query against one, and is permitted under exactly the capability-provenance discipline. The three conditions that section requires all hold here: the capability is **declared** (the `erasure_mechanism` knob, a named deployment obligation verified by an externally-clearable check rather than from these records); the consuming surface is **composition-introduced** and specified at the composition layer (this step, and the cascade-on-purge rule that defends it); and the invocation is **logic-confinement-clean** — the mechanism is configured material invoked at a named step, never destruction improvised inside core logic. That section also requires the construct carry a **named exit**, because a recurring mechanism-capability surface is a not-yet-extracted-atom signal; this one's exit is named throughout the pattern: the forthcoming **Erasure Tombstone** atom, which is the retirement path, not a decoration. The construct is a bridge with a named exit, and both ends are declared.

     **The mechanism reports one of two outcomes and the cascade branches on which.** A **`destroyed`** outcome record completes the cascade and step 4's surviving-record inventory holds. A **`destruction-failed(reason)`** outcome record does not: the composition maps it to [Cascade Failure] as **`rejected(cascade-failure(step-3))`**, and the world it leaves is named exactly — **the retention is *Purged*, the content is still readable, and the two disagree**. That is the same shape as a lost delegation and takes the same remedy: the **reconciliation scan is the retry trigger**, and because the scan's half-completed-cascade predicate tests for a **successful** outcome record rather than for the mere presence of one, an entry carrying a `destruction-failed` record stays open and is re-driven on every cycle until a `destroyed` one lands. The recorded outcome is the destruction record's third fact — the **`erasure_outcomes`** element (Composition state), extraction-pending against the same Erasure Tombstone atom and durable with the step-2 write it completes (*Durability across crashes*). Swallowing the negative outcome — recording that the mechanism was called and treating that as completion — would produce exactly the state Invariant 8 forecloses: a retention record asserting lawful destruction over content anyone can still read, with the composition's own records agreeing it was done. A failure to issue the delegation, or to record its outcome at all, lands on the same arm with the same reading as `step-2`: retention *Purged*, cascade incomplete, reconciliation scan as the retry trigger.

     **After the delegation returns — what the composition does not do.** The event's destruction has just left its covering seal partly purged, and the seal's surviving members can no longer be verified against it: the record set the proof commits to is no longer presentable in full. **The canonical composition attempts no repair**, and the resulting behavior is stated rather than left implicit — the partly-purged seal **remains the one seal covering its range**, and its surviving live members verify as **`unverifiable(partially-purged-coverage)`** from here on ([Verify Record] step 4). That is *verification is impossible*, not *verification failed*: nothing is wrong with those events, and the composition says so rather than manufacturing a finding against them out of its own lawful work. It is a standing answer for the rest of those events' retained lifetimes, not a transient. A deployment that needs the survivors verifiable again composes the forthcoming **Seal Lifecycle** pattern (Edge cases — *Seal re-sealing and mechanism deprecation*), which owns re-sealing them and the supersession bookkeeping that follows.
  4. What survives a **completed** cascade — one whose step 3 returned a `destroyed` outcome — by design: the Retention Window record in *Purged* state with `purged_at` (the evidence the destruction was lawful), the seal record with this event's `sequence_number` in its `purged_events` set (the evidence a record set existed and which of its members were destroyed), the destruction record's `(event_id, attestation_id)` pair (the traversal from the destroyed event to the attestation destroyed with it, which the payload can no longer supply), the mechanism's own `destroyed` outcome record in `erasure_outcomes` (the evidence the delegation actually completed, which is what the reconciliation scan tests for), the attestation's own surviving `attestation_id`, `action_ref`, `actor_ref`, and `attested_at` fields (the regulator's *who / what / when*, still readable — only the `proof` lost its readability), and — once the Erasure Tombstone pattern lands — the tombstone naming what was destroyed and under whose authority. The attestation's lifetime **rides the event's own retention record** (Configuration): one retention governs event and attestation jointly, so step 3's delegation covers both, in one cascade, under one `retention_id`. There is no second Retention Window instance and no separate attestation meta-retention policy.

### The cascade-on-purge rule

The composition's load-bearing wiring decision: **when an event's retention elapses, the composition first ensures the event is covered by a seal (sealing the tail if it is not), then purges the one record it is permitted to purge — the Retention Window record — writes a destruction record adding the event's sequence number to the covering seal's `purged_events` set and capturing that event's `(event_id, attestation_id)` binding before it is destroyed, and delegates destruction of the event's `data` payload and the attestation's `proof` to the deployment's composed shredding-class erasure mechanism. It coordinates the cascade and records the outcome; it destroys nothing itself, it disposes of no seal, and it re-seals nothing.**

*Principle.* When a regulated record reaches the end of its lifetime, all four stores must reach a coherent end state together: no retained event without attribution or integrity coverage, and no destroyed event leaving a dangling attestation or a live content claim on a seal. Coordinating that across four independent stores is work no constituent can do — each knows only its own record — so it belongs at the composition layer, together with the honest record of what the coordination achieved.

*Likely objection.* Why not have the composition delete the content directly? It already knows which event, which attestation, and which seal are involved. A cascade that ends in a delegation looks like a cascade that does not finish.

*Mechanism that resolves it.* Two of the four constituents forbid it in their own invariant text, and a composition that overrode them would not be composing those atoms — it would be silently substituting weaker ones. Event Log declares only `append` and `read`, states plainly that there is no delete or edit surface, holds Invariant 1 (append-only), and routes true deletion to a composing pattern (Erasure Tombstone, Cryptographic Shredding) in its *Right-to-be-forgotten erasure* edge case. Actor Identity's Invariant 9 (attestation durability) states that an attestation is never deleted by the atom, that the store's record count is monotonically non-decreasing, and that cascading deletion under a retention policy is the composing pattern's responsibility. So the destruction surface is named where the atoms already say it belongs — an *Erasure Tombstone* *(forthcoming)* composing pattern, or cryptographic shredding at the storage layer, declared per deployment — and this composition wires the coordination around it: the retention purge, the seal's `purged_events` marker, the delegation, and the surviving evidence of what was coordinated.

Two boundaries make that delegation honest rather than a loophole.

*Boundary one — only shredding-class mechanisms conform.* **This is the full argument's home; the four other sites that touch it — Configuration's `erasure_mechanism` knob, [Purge Event] step 3, Invariant 5, and the [Purge Event] Terms card — state the conclusion in one sentence and cite here.** A conforming mechanism destroys **recoverability**, not stored bytes: it destroys the key material under which **Event Log's `data` field in its entirety** — the whole constructed `{action_ref, actor_ref, attestation_id, data}` object, not merely the caller's `data` (payload sub-field) — and the attestation's `proof` were stored, leaving every stored field of the Event Log entry and of the attestation exactly as written — and leaving the attestation's `attestation_id`, `action_ref`, `actor_ref`, and `attested_at` still *readable*, so the regulator's *who / what / when* survives the destruction of the payload and of the binding's verifiability. That is why Event Log Invariant 2 and Actor Identity Invariant 1 (both *immutability* claims over stored fields) survive the cascade verbatim — nothing is rewritten, and neither invariant claims readability. **Tombstone-by-mutation — overwriting, blanking, or truncating a stored field in place — is non-conforming**, because it breaks exactly those two invariants; a deployment that wires one has left this composition's contract. The *Erasure Tombstone* pattern's role is unambiguous in the other direction: it **records** destruction as a new write-once record, and never mutates the record it describes.

**One constituent prescription is deliberately declined, and the declination is named rather than left silent.** Retention Window's own *Divergence between retention state and underlying record* edge case prescribes the opposite coordination to this cascade's: in that atom's own frame, its `purge` signals the storage layer to destroy the governed record, and if the storage layer cannot confirm destruction, `purge` should return `storage-failure` rather than `ok` — so the retention never reads *Purged* over surviving content. This composition does not take that prescription, and structurally cannot: the destruction record must be written **after** the retention transition and **before** the delegation ([Purge Event] steps 1–3 — the `(event_id, attestation_id)` pair must be captured while the payload is still readable, and the covering entry must exist to write it on), and the destruction itself is performed by a composed mechanism `RetentionWindow.purge` has no view of, so there is no moment at which that atom could conditionally refuse on the mechanism's answer. What replaces the prescription is the same guarantee by other means, and it is the shape of Invariant 8: the divergence window the atom warns about — *Purged* over readable content — is reachable here, but it is **recorded** (`cascade-failure(step-3)`; the `destruction-failed` outcome in `erasure_outcomes`), **surfaced** (a compliance alert, never quietly carried), **bounded** (the `compensation_window`), and **re-driven** (the reconciliation scan's first half, until a `destroyed` outcome lands). The atom's edge case asks the coordination to *prevent* the state; this composition's design makes it impossible to *hide* instead — the trade that buys the durable pre-destruction record the atom's ordering could not.

*Boundary two — the composition disposes of no seal, and it writes no replacement for one either.* **Seals are retained indefinitely by default**, and each one written stays the only seal over its range for good. A seal is a compact commitment — a proof, not a copy — and its indefinite retention is precisely what keeps two claims unconditionally true: this composition's Invariant 3 (every event at or below `sealed_through` is covered by exactly one seal), which a seal disposal would silently falsify for every event the disposed seal covered — and Tamper Evidence's Invariant 9 (the evidence set is monotonically non-decreasing, and a seal that survives its originating records is the audit evidence that those records existed). Tamper Evidence's *retention coupling* edge case observes that a seal over a destroyed record set is structurally meaningless *for content verification* and suggests purging it in step; this composition takes the other reading **Tamper Evidence's own Invariant 9** supports, because the seal remains meaningful as existence evidence even when it is useless as a content check, and because the canonical composition declares no seal-store retention owner and will not assert a disposal surface it has not wired. A deployment that genuinely must dispose of seals — a jurisdiction that treats a commitment over erased personal data as residual personal data, for instance — composes the forthcoming *Erasure Tombstone* pattern, which then **owns the seal-disposal surface and the coverage bookkeeping that follows from it** (which events lose their cover, and what Invariant 3 reads as thereafter). That is an externally-composed variant, named here and deliberately out of the canonical composition's scope.

**The cost of writing no replacement seal is named on the same terms.** A seal the cascade has partly purged stays the seal covering its range; its surviving members answer `unverifiable(partially-purged-coverage)` for the rest of their retained lifetimes; and a seal produced under a mechanism heading for deprecation cannot be rotated onto a sound one — which is the remedy Tamper Evidence's own **Invariant 2** prescribes ("seals produced under deprecated mechanisms should be re-sealed under a sound mechanism before the old one is deprecated"). Both belong to a forthcoming **Seal Lifecycle** composing pattern, and they belong there for the same reason seal disposal belongs to Erasure Tombstone: each needs a *which seal is current* fact — new truth **no constituent store carries and no rebuild replays**, since Tamper Evidence's own *Concurrent seals on the same record set* edge case treats two evidence records over one record set as two valid independent proofs and takes no view on which supersedes which. A composition that absorbed that bookkeeping would be holding unclassifiable truth on behalf of a concept it has not specified. Naming the owner is the honest move; carrying the state is not.

*Result.* Four constituent invariants hold **verbatim** over their instances — Event Log's Invariants 1 and 2, Actor Identity's Invariants 9 and 1 — which is what makes Invariant 5 a literal claim rather than an aspiration, and the audit outcome is unchanged: a lawfully destroyed event still answers `failed-verification(purged)` from its *Purged* retention record, still leaves a seal whose `purged_events` set names it as evidence that the record existed and was destroyed, and still distinguishes lawful destruction from a missing record (Invariant 8). The seal outlives the cascade indefinitely — the *fact* that a record existed and was lawfully destroyed is itself audit evidence the regulator queries.

**The reconciliation scan has three halves, and all three are specified here rather than left to the implementation.** The composition's three liveness arms fail in three structurally different ways — a cascade that stopped partway, an attestation nothing points at, and an event nothing bounds — and each needs its own detector, its own predicate, and its own compensating write. A scan that ran only the first two would carry the third state forever, unsurfaced, because no other surface looks for it. They share a trigger (restart, unconditionally, plus the declared **`reconciliation_cadence`**), a surfacing discipline (any unreconciled finding is a compliance alert, never silently carried), and a deadline (the declared **`compensation_window`**, per finding, from its detection).

*First half — the half-completed cascade must be reachable for retry.* The cascade's steps are not all in one store, so a crash between them is reachable: a retention can be in *Purged* while its `purged_events` marker or its delegation record never landed. That state is **invisible to [Purge Eligible]**, whose projection is false for a *Purged* retention — so the ordinary sweep will never re-offer it and the retry can never be driven from the eligible list. The scan therefore enumerates *Purged* retentions and checks each for a matching `purged_events` membership, a matching `(event_id, attestation_id)` destruction-record entry, and a matching erasure-mechanism outcome record (`erasure_outcomes`, Composition state) — and uses its output as the retry trigger. **The outcome-record test is for a *successful* outcome, not for presence.** The mechanism reports `destroyed` or `destruction-failed(reason)` (Configuration, `erasure_mechanism`), both are recorded, and only `destroyed` closes the entry; an entry carrying a `destruction-failed` record is an entry whose retention says *lawfully destroyed* over content still readable, which is exactly the state that must be re-driven, and a predicate that accepted any outcome record would close it on the evidence of its own failure. So the scan re-drives such an entry on every cycle until a `destroyed` outcome lands. An unreconciled entry means a record whose retention says it was lawfully destroyed and whose destruction cannot be evidenced, which is the one thing this composition exists to make impossible to hide.

*Second half — the orphan-attestation predicate.* "An attestation with no event" is not a fact any single store holds: Actor Identity knows only its own records, and Event Log carries the binding inside a payload the cascade eventually makes unreadable. The scan therefore builds a **binding set** and tests membership against it. The binding set is the union of two enumerations:

- **the `attestation_id`s carried in live event payloads** — obtained by enumerating the audit log in full through a **sequence-range read with an open upper bound** (the declared instance capability requirement in *Composes*) and reading each returned event's payload `attestation_id`; and
- **the `attestation_id`s in destruction records** — the second member of each `(event_id, attestation_id)` pair written at [Purge Event] step 2, which is where the binding for a purged event went when the payload that used to carry it stopped being readable.

**An attestation in the store that appears in neither enumeration is an orphan**, and is the compensation target. Both halves are needed and neither is optional: the first alone would report every lawfully purged event's attestation as an orphan the day its payload was shredded, and the second alone would see nothing but purges. **This is the same live/purged split of the same map that Generation-acceptance check 7 audits `event_to_attestation` for** — *live entries rebuildable from the payload, purged entries readable from the destruction record* — read in the reverse direction: check 7 walks events and asks whether each has its binding, the scan walks attestations and asks whether each is bound. An implementation that gets one right and the other wrong has two views of one relation.

*Whether a found orphan still needs compensating is a separate question, and it has its own state.* The scan **pre-checks `compensated_attestations`** (Composition state) before it writes anything: an orphan whose `attestation_id` is already in that set has been compensated on a previous pass and the scan takes no action. The pre-check is not optional bookkeeping, because the orphan itself is **permanent** — Actor Identity's Invariant 9 forecloses deletion, so the unbound attestation is still unbound on every subsequent pass, forever, and a scan without the pre-check would write a fresh `audit.compensation` event for the same orphan at every `reconciliation_cadence` interval for the life of the deployment. The check-then-record pair runs **under per-`attestation_id` serialization** (Edge cases — *Concurrency*, obligation 4), which is what makes it a decision rather than a race: two passes over one orphan cannot both observe *not yet compensated* and both record. This is the exact shape of the third half's compensating retention placement, on the other relation and under the other lock.

*Third half — the unretained-event predicate, which is the detector Invariant 2's liveness arm needs and the one nothing else supplies.* [Record Action] step 7 names the state: step 4 failing after step 3 succeeded leaves an **event in the log governed by no retention record**. That state is reachable, durable (Event Log is append-only; the event cannot be withdrawn), and — this is the trap — **invisible to every other surface the composition has**. [Purge Eligible] enumerates *retentions*, so an event with none never appears on it. The first half enumerates *Purged retentions*, so an event with no retention at all is outside its domain. The second half enumerates *attestations*, and an unretained event's attestation is perfectly bound — it is in a live event payload, so the binding set contains it and the orphan predicate is correctly false. [Read Record] and [Verify Record] surface the condition, but only for an `event_id` a caller already thought to ask about. Nothing sweeps for it. The third half is what sweeps for it, and its procedure is:

- **Enumerate the audit log** through the **declared open-upper-bound read** — `EventLog.read` over the range beginning at 1 with no upper bound, the instance capability requirement *Composes* names, which this half joins as a user.
- **For each returned event, test `event_to_retention[event_id]`, and rebuild on miss *first*.** The ordering is load-bearing and it is the same trap the *Durability across crashes* edge case names from the other side: `event_to_retention` is a derived index, a lost entry is an ordinary rebuild trigger, and a scan that read a lost entry as *no retention exists* would misdiagnose an index gap as a compliance finding — and then compensate it, placing a **second** retention over an event that already had one, which Retention Window's Invariant 5 records as a new retention with a new id and which quietly falsifies Invariant 2's *exactly one* claim. So the scan rebuilds from the retention store's own `record_ref` before it concludes anything, and only a miss that survives the rebuild is a miss.
- **For a true miss, run the compensating `place_under_retention`** under the **per-`event_id` serialization** the *Concurrency* edge case obliges, behind the same `event_to_retention` pre-check Invariant 2's liveness arm already specifies (obligation 2) — pre-check and placement inside one lock, so two passes over one event cannot both observe *absent* and both place. **The pair's two writes are ordered, and the order is truth first:** the placement commits, *then* the compensating write is recorded through [Record Action] under `audit.compensation` with `subject = event` and the `event_id` (*Where the compensation lands*, below). Placement-first is the direction that closes the compliance gap even when the second write fails — the reverse order would record a compensation that had not yet happened, a false record in exactly the register the `audit.*` reservation exists to keep honest. **The recording half's own failure is named rather than assumed away:** a refusal of the compensating [Record Action] inside the scan cycle is retried like any other [Record Action]; a crash *between* the two writes leaves the gap itself closed — the event is retention-governed, which is the state Invariant 2 requires — but the closure unrecorded, and **no detector exists for that residual**: nothing in the retention store distinguishes a compensating placement from an ordinary step-4 one, so no scan half can sweep for it, and an implementer should not invent a detector the records cannot support. The residual is safe in the direction that matters, which is why it is carried rather than fixed: what can be lost is the first-class audit narration of a repair — never a false closure, and never an unclosed gap — the mirror of `compensated_attestations`' loss asymmetry (*Durability across crashes*).

**The auditor runs this same procedure from outside, and that is Generation-acceptance check 2.** The check's enumeration of the log against the retention store at quiescence is the mirror of this half — same open-upper-bound read, same per-event test, same reading of a residual as a surfaced compliance finding rather than a silent violation. The two must agree for the same reason check 7's live/purged split and the second half's binding set must: an implementation whose scan does not look where the auditor looks will be told about the gap by its regulator rather than by its own reconciliation.

*Where the compensation lands — the composition is its own event log.* The scan's findings and the compensating writes are not narration and not a side channel: **they are audit events, recorded through this composition's own [Record Action]**, under composition-owned action references — `audit.reconciliation` for each scan's findings and `audit.compensation` for each compensating write (the compensating record naming an unbound attestation, the compensating retention placed for an event that got past step 3 without one) — attributed to the deployment's **operator identity** — the configured `reconciliation_operator` (Configuration).

**Both references live in the reserved `audit.*` namespace, and the reservation is what makes them composition-owned rather than merely composition-preferred.** [Record Action] step 1 rejects an `action_ref` carrying the `audit.` prefix from any **external** caller as `rejected(invalid-request)` (*Primitive policies*, `action_ref`); only the reconciliation path — the scan's own writes, described here — may use it. That matters because these are the only payloads any surface on this page reads *inside*: `compensated_attestations`' rebuild reads them, and Invariant 1's orphan-freedom arm closes on what that rebuild finds. Without the reservation an ordinary caller could record an `audit.compensation` event naming any `attestation_id`, and a compliance finding the composition never compensated would read as closed. The reservation is the difference between a marker that is *evidence of a compensation* and one that is *anybody's claim that there was one*.

**`audit.compensation` carries a payload requirement, and it is a requirement rather than a convention:** a compensation event's `data` (payload sub-field) **MUST name both the kind of subject the compensation is about and that subject's id** — a **subject-kind discriminator** `subject: attestation | event`, plus the corresponding id: `subject = attestation` with the orphan's `attestation_id` for an unbound attestation, `subject = event` with the `event_id` whose retention was placed for a missing retention. [Record Action] step 1 enforces it on the internal path, so a compensating write missing either field is refused rather than recorded. **The discriminator is load-bearing and not decoration:** one action reference covers both compensating writes the reconciliation path makes, and `compensated_attestations`' rebuild (Composition state) filters on `subject = attestation` precisely so that it reads `attestation_id`s and not `event_id`s. Without the discriminator the rebuild would have to guess which id kind a payload carried, and a set of orphan-attestation markers silently seeded with event ids would report attestations as compensated that never were. Without the id at all the event would record *that* a compensation happened and not *what* it was for, leaving the composition with no way to tell a reconciled orphan from an unreconciled one. This is the one place the composition fixes a payload shape, and it fixes it only for payloads it writes itself under its own reserved references; a caller's `data` stays opaque (*Primitive policies*). The scan is implementation-owned (it is the implementation that "must run" it), so it is an ordinary *caller* of [Record Action] and supplies its `actor_ref` and `credential` at that call the way every other caller does — with one declared difference: the `actor_ref` it must supply is the configured **`reconciliation_operator`** (Configuration), because that is the discriminator [Record Action] step 1 admits the reserved namespace by. The operator identity is the deployment's own maintenance actor, provisioned by the deployment; the composition declares *which* actor may write in the namespace, never the actor itself. This is not a new action and not a new surface, and it grows no second store: the one Composition-state element the discipline adds, `compensated_attestations`, is a **derived index over these very events** — rebuilt by enumerating the log for live `audit.compensation` events — so it holds no truth the audit log does not already carry. `execution-contract.md` is explicit that a composition needing to record that a multi-step sequence occurred **does so by composing Event Log**, and never by growing a second store to hold what an atom already owns; this composition *is* an Event Log composition, so the rule is satisfied by it recording its own coordination history through the same [Record Action] every other caller uses. The compensation events are therefore attested, sequenced, retention-governed, and sealed exactly like the events they are about — an auditor asking *"what did this system do about the gap it found?"* gets a first-class audit answer from the same traversal, rather than a log line in an operational system the audit has no reach into. Two consequences worth stating: the operator identity must be a real Actor Identity actor with usable credential material, because [Record Action] step 2 will attest it like any other — a deployment that has not provisioned one cannot record its own compensations, which is why the `reconciliation_operator` knob is deployment-required with no default (Configuration); and compensation events are themselves under `retention_policy` and will themselves eventually be purged, which is correct — they are records of what happened, and they age like records of what happened.

## Composition-level invariants

These invariants (conditions that must always hold) emerge from the composition. None belongs to a single constituent atom; each requires the four atoms working together to hold. Each carries a *Rests on:* line naming the wiring step that establishes it and the constituent invariants it stands on, so an auditor can trace every claim back to a declared capability rather than to this page's confidence.

**The composition's three structural relations.** Per [`spec-format.md`](../spec-format.md) §Structural-relation invariant templates, a declared relation carries its cardinality, its modality, and the template invariants that keep the pointing honest:

- **event ↔ attestation — one-to-one, mandatory on both sides at quiescence.** Every event has exactly one attestation, and every attestation made through this composition belongs to exactly one event. Both sides mandatory, so both *referential integrity* and *orphan-freedom* apply; Invariant 1 states them in the orphan-freedom template's safety-plus-liveness form.
- **event ↔ retention — one-to-one, mandatory on both sides at quiescence.** Every event has exactly one retention record, and every retention record placed through this composition governs exactly one event — and, jointly, that event's attestation. Invariant 2 states them in the same form.
- **seal → events — one-to-many, optional on the event side while the event is in the unsealed tail, mandatory once `sequence_number ≤ sealed_through`.** One seal covers a contiguous range of sequence numbers, as [Seal Now] cuts it; an event is covered by at most one seal, because [Seal Now]'s slices are disjoint and strictly advancing and the canonical composition writes coverage entries no other way. Invariant 3 states it, and the optional side *is* the unsealed tail the cadence bounds. (A composed **Seal Lifecycle** pattern *(forthcoming)* that re-seals an existing range would make the relation many-to-one — two evidence records over one record set, which is Tamper Evidence's own accumulating-evidence model and not a violation of anything — and would own the *which seal is current* bookkeeping that reading requires. This composition declares neither.)

The inverse directions of all three are read through the composition's derived indexes, classified there per the *inverse consistency* template (Composition state); a lost index entry is a rebuild trigger, never a relation violation.

- **Invariant 1 — Attribution coverage (safety + liveness at quiescence).** *Safety:* no action of this composition leaves an event in the audit log with its attestation side unbound and unsurfaced. [Record Action] attests before it appends (step 2 before step 3), so any appended event carries a committed `attestation_id` inside its own immutable payload; the reverse partial — an attestation whose append failed — returns `rejected(recording-failure)` in the same outcome that surfaces the orphan. *Liveness:* that reverse orphan is reachable and durable (Actor Identity's records are immutable once committed, so synchronous rollback is unavailable), so the honest claim is that every orphan is surfaced and reconciled **within the deployment's configured `compensation_window`** (Configuration) per the *Partial attestation on step failure* edge case — a surfaced transient under compensation, never a quiet inconsistency and never a steady state of a conforming implementation. **What *reconciled* means here is observable rather than a disposition: an orphan is reconciled exactly when its `attestation_id` is a member of `compensated_attestations`** (Composition state) — the set of attestations for which an `audit.compensation` event has been recorded through [Record Action]. **The closure has to be carried by that marker rather than by the attestation store, and the reason is the orphan's permanence:** Actor Identity's **Invariant 9** forecloses deletion, so the unbound attestation is still unbound on every subsequent enumeration, for good. Nothing about the attestation itself ever changes to say *this has been dealt with*, so nothing in that store can close the arm; the marker is the only thing that can. The reconciliation scan pre-checks the set before it writes a compensation, under the per-`attestation_id` serialization the *Concurrency* edge case obliges (obligation 4), so a permanent orphan produces exactly one compensation rather than one per scan interval forever. **And the marker is not forgeable, which is what keeps the closure meaningful:** the `audit.compensation` events the set is rebuilt from are written under a **reserved `audit.*` action reference** no caller but the configured `reconciliation_operator` may supply ([Record Action] step 1; *Primitive policies*; Configuration) — with the operator's credential attested at step 2 before anything is appended — so the rebuild reads records only the composition's own reconciliation path could have written. An arm that closed on a marker anyone could write would close on an assertion rather than on a compensation.

  **One case in this arm is self-referential and is named rather than left to be discovered: the compensating write can itself fail at step 3.** A compensation is an ordinary [Record Action] — it attests, then appends — so `EventLog.append` refusing after `ActorIdentity.attest` committed leaves a **new orphan attestation**, created by the very write that was closing the old one. Nothing about that is pathological; it is the same reachable partial the arm already admits, arriving through the compensation path instead of the caller path. **Convergence does not rest on the compensating write succeeding, and it must not:** it rests on the reconciliation scan's *next* cycle, which enumerates the attestation store afresh, finds **both** the original orphan (still absent from `compensated_attestations`, since its compensation never landed) and the new one (absent for the same reason), and retries both. Each is bounded by `compensation_window` **from its own detection**, not from the first orphan's — the new orphan is a new finding with its own clock, and a deployment reading one window across a chain of retries would be auditing against the wrong deadline. The chain terminates for the ordinary reason retries terminate: each cycle's compensations succeed or fail independently, and a store healthy enough to accept an append clears the whole backlog in one pass. What the composition guarantees is that no link in such a chain is ever *unsurfaced* — every orphan in it is in the attestation store, in no binding set, and in no compensation marker, which is exactly what the scan looks for. **The liveness arm is conditioned on the constituent stores being available**, on the same terms Invariant 6 conditions its determinism claim: reconciliation is itself a [Record Action] against the attestation, log, and retention stores, so a store that cannot be reached suspends the window's closure rather than falsifying the claim, and the outstanding finding stays surfaced until it can be written. *At quiescence* — no [Record Action] in flight, `compensation_window` elapsed, constituent stores reachable — for every `event_id` in the log **recorded through [Record Action]** (the quantifier the *Pre-attestation legacy events* non-goal fixes: this composition declares exactly one way in, and an event written around it is not one this invariant covers), `event_to_attestation[event_id]` (rebuilt on miss for a live event; read from the destruction record for a purged one, whose role is that traversal and nothing else) references a recorded attestation carrying a readable `action_ref` and `actor_ref`. **And on the other side of the relation** — the side orphan-freedom is actually about — every attestation in the store is either in the **binding set** (the cascade-on-purge rule's scan predicate: `attestation_id`s in live event payloads ∪ `attestation_id`s in destruction records) or in `compensated_attestations`. The two together are the checkable form of *mandatory on both sides*. **What the clause compares differs by retention state, because the cascade destroys one side of the comparison and not the other.** For a **live** event it is a cross-store field match: the attestation's `action_ref` and `actor_ref` equal the event payload's, byte for byte. For a **purged** event the payload's copy is gone — `erasure_mechanism` destroys the readability of the event's `data` and the attestation's `proof`, and nothing else — so the clause is evaluated **against the attestation store's surviving fields alone**: the attestation the pair names still exists and still carries a readable `attestation_id`, `action_ref`, `actor_ref`, and `attested_at`. That is the strongest claim the records support after a lawful destruction, and it is the one the regulator actually asks: *who*, *what*, and *when* outlive the purge; what does not is the ability to re-check them against a payload destroyed on purpose. The destruction record is never read for those fields — it carries `(event_id, attestation_id)` and no more. *Rests on:* [Record Action] steps 2, 3, and 5 establish it; **the liveness arm rests on [Record Action] itself** — the compensating record naming an unbound attestation is written through [Record Action] under the composition-owned `audit.compensation` action reference, and the scan that finds it writes its findings under `audit.reconciliation` (the cascade-on-purge rule, *Where the compensation lands*), so the compensation is an audit event with the same attestation, retention, and seal coverage as any other, not an out-of-band note — **and the arm's observable closure rests on `compensated_attestations`** (Composition state), the derived index over those very `audit.compensation` events, pre-checked under the per-`attestation_id` serialization of *Concurrency* obligation 4; Actor Identity Invariants 1 (attestation immutability), 2 (action binding), 3 (actor binding), and 9 (attestation durability — which is *why* the closure needs a marker at all, since the orphan it forecloses deleting is permanent); Event Log Invariants 1 (append-only) and 2 (event immutability).

- **Invariant 2 — Retention coverage (safety + liveness at quiescence).** *Safety:* no successful [Record Action] returns an `event_id` whose retention side is unbound — step 4 places the retention after step 3's append and before the return. *Liveness:* a failure between step 3 and step 4 leaves an event in the log with no retention record, and that state is reachable because Event Log is append-only and an appended event cannot be withdrawn; it is surfaced as `rejected(recording-failure)` in the same outcome and reconciled within the configured `compensation_window` (Configuration) by placing the missing retention. **The idempotence of that compensating write is the composition's own mechanism, not something Retention Window supplies, and the distinction is load-bearing.** Retention Window's **Invariant 5** says a retention's `record_ref` and `policy_ref` are fixed at placement and that re-retaining the same record **under a different policy** produces a **new retention with a new id** — and it declares nothing at all about re-retaining under the *same* policy, so `place_under_retention` is nowhere declared idempotent on `record_ref`, and a reconciliation path that simply re-placed on every pass would accumulate a second, third, and fourth retention governing one event, each with its own deadlines, quietly falsifying this invariant's *exactly one* claim in the direction the atom cannot refuse. The composition therefore **pre-checks**: before issuing the compensating `place_under_retention`, the reconciliation path reads `event_to_retention` for the `event_id` (rebuild-on-miss, so a lost index entry regenerates from the retention store's own `record_ref` rather than reading as absent) and **places only if there is none**. **What finds the event in the first place is the reconciliation scan's third half** (the cascade-on-purge rule): a full open-upper-bound enumeration of the log testing each event against that same map, which is the only surface in the composition that sweeps for this state — [Purge Eligible] enumerates retentions and so cannot see an event that has none, and the scan's other two halves enumerate *Purged* retentions and attestations. Without that half this arm would have a compensation and no detector. The check-then-place pair runs **under the per-`event_id` serialization the *Concurrency* edge case already obliges**, which is what makes it a decision rather than a race: two reconciliation passes over one event cannot both observe *absent* and both place. *At quiescence,* for every `event_id` in the log **recorded through [Record Action]** — the same quantifier Invariant 1 carries, fixed by the *Pre-attestation legacy events* non-goal: this composition declares exactly one way in, and an event written around it is not one this invariant covers — `event_to_retention[event_id]` (rebuilt on miss) references **exactly one** recorded retention, in either *Retained* or *Purged*. **The liveness arm is conditioned on the constituent stores being available** — the pre-check reads the retention store and the placement writes it, so a store that cannot be reached suspends the window's closure rather than falsifying the claim, and the finding stays surfaced until the write can land. *Rests on:* [Record Action] steps 3, 4, and 5 establish it; **the liveness arm rests on [Record Action] itself** — the compensating retention placement and the scan that triggers it are recorded through [Record Action] under the composition-owned `audit.compensation` and `audit.reconciliation` action references (the cascade-on-purge rule, *Where the compensation lands*), so *that the gap was closed* is itself an auditable record rather than an operational claim — **and on the composition's own `event_to_retention` pre-check under per-`event_id` serialization (Edge cases — *Concurrency*), which is what supplies the idempotence Retention Window's Invariant 5 nowhere declares** — **and on the reconciliation scan's third half as the arm's detector** (the cascade-on-purge rule), without which the compensating write would have no path that finds the events needing it; **and [Purge Event] step 0½ is the arm that keeps the cascade out of this window** — an event whose retention has not yet landed rejects `retention-unresolved` rather than being purged around the gap; Retention Window Invariants 1 (membership exclusivity), 5 (`record_ref` and `policy_ref` immutability, and the new-retention-on-re-retention-under-a-different-policy rule that makes the pre-check necessary), and 10 (retention store durability); Event Log Invariant 1 (append-only).

- **Invariant 3 — Integrity coverage (modulo unsealed tail).** Every event in the audit log with `sequence_number ≤ sealed_through` is covered by **exactly one seal** in the seal store — the one `evidence_id` whose `seal_coverage` entry contains that `sequence_number`. The unsealed tail is observable as a bounded gap; tighter `seal_cadence` shrinks it, and `unsealed_tail_mode` names what a verifier makes of it.

  **The claim is exact because there is exactly one way a coverage entry gets written.** [Seal Now] cuts the slice `[sealed_through + 1 .. tail]` and then advances `sealed_through` past it, so the slices are **contiguous, disjoint, and strictly advancing**; no other action of this composition writes a `seal_coverage` entry, and none re-covers a range some entry already holds. *At most one* follows from disjointness and *at least one* from the `sealed_through` bound, and neither half is qualified: the claim is over seals, full stop, with no second class of seal anywhere in the composition to quantify around.

  A seal that carries purged members in its `purged_events` set still satisfies this coverage claim for those purged events — the seal record persists as audit evidence of lawful destruction even when the content it committed to is gone; what those events lose is verifiability, not coverage, and [Verify Record] reports that honestly (`failed-verification(purged)` for the purged event itself, `unverifiable(partially-purged-coverage)` for its surviving seal-mates). The claim is **unconditional** rather than modulo a seal lifetime, and that is exactly why the canonical composition disposes of no seal: **seals are retained indefinitely** (Configuration; the cascade-on-purge rule), so no event that was once covered ever stops being covered. Two composed patterns would each put this invariant modulo their own bookkeeping, and both are named and out of scope: the forthcoming *Erasure Tombstone* pattern, if a deployment composes it to **dispose** of seals, and the forthcoming *Seal Lifecycle* pattern, if a deployment composes it to **re-seal** a range some seal already covers (Edge cases). *Rests on:* [Seal Now] (and [Record Action] step 6 under per-event cadence) establishes it, and [Purge Event] step 0's seal-before-purge precondition keeps it true of every purged event by construction; Tamper Evidence Invariants 1 (evidence immutability), 3 (record-set binding), and 9 (seal store durability, which is why a seal outlives the records it committed to); Event Log Invariants 3 (total order) and 4 (sequence-number monotonicity), without which a contiguous range would not be a well-defined cover.

- **Invariant 4 — Cascade coordination on purge.** When an event's retention elapses and [Purge Event] runs, the composition drives all four stores to a coherent end state: the event is covered by a seal (step 0 seals the tail first if it is not), the retention record moves to *Purged* with `purged_at`, the event's `sequence_number` joins the covering `seal_coverage` entry's `purged_events` set together with the destruction record's `(event_id, attestation_id)` pair captured before the binding is destroyed, and destruction of the event's `data` payload and the attestation's `proof` is **delegated to the deployment's declared `erasure_mechanism`**, whose outcome the cascade records — a `destroyed` outcome completing the coordination, a `destruction-failed(reason)` one leaving it incomplete under `cascade-failure(step-3)` and re-driven by the reconciliation scan until it completes. The invariant is over the coordination, not over a destruction this composition performs: no retained event is left without attestation or integrity coverage, and no purged event leaves a dangling attestation reference or a live seal *content* claim. The coordination completes either in a single transaction or via a compensating record that acknowledges and resolves any partial state; the transactional boundary is implementation-owned (see *Cross-store consistency under failure* in Edge cases), and the delegated destruction is a declared deployment obligation whose completion is evidenced externally (Generation acceptance, *Externally-clearable checks*). *Rests on:* [Purge Event] steps 0–4 establish it, and the cascade-on-purge rule defends it; Retention Window Invariants 3 (terminal absorption), 7 (no early purge), and 8 (purge timestamp consistency); Tamper Evidence Invariant 1 (evidence immutability); Event Log Invariant 1 and Actor Identity Invariant 9, which are what make the delegation necessary rather than optional.

- **Invariant 5 — Constituent invariants preserved.** All invariants from the four constituent atoms hold over their respective instances: Event Log's append-only and total order, all Actor Identity's invariants (immutability, action binding, actor binding, non-repudiation contract, attestation durability, etc.), all Retention Window's invariants (membership exclusivity, no early purge, retention window monotonicity, retention store durability, etc.), and all Tamper Evidence's invariants (evidence immutability, detectability of tampering, record-set binding, seal store durability, etc.). **One sentence carries the whole of what makes that literal, and it is argued in one place rather than here:** the cascade delegates content destruction rather than performing it, and only to a **shredding-class** mechanism that destroys recoverability while leaving every stored field as written — tombstone-by-mutation being non-conforming for exactly that reason (the cascade-on-purge rule, *Boundary one*, which is the argument's home). Granting it, this invariant is **literally true of all four invariants the cascade could otherwise threaten**: Event Log **Invariant 1** (append-only) holds because this composition never removes a log entry; Event Log **Invariant 2** (event immutability) holds because no stored `event_id`, `sequence_number`, `recorded_at`, or `data` field is ever rewritten — shredding destroys the key, not the bytes; Actor Identity **Invariant 9** (attestation durability) holds because this composition never deletes an attestation; and Actor Identity **Invariant 1** (attestation immutability) holds because no stored attestation field is ever rewritten either. **The composition's only writes into the seal store are `TamperEvidence.seal` calls from [Seal Now]**: it writes new evidence records, mutates no existing one (Tamper Evidence Invariant 1), and deletes none (Invariant 9). *Rests on:* the whole action wiring — every constituent call this composition makes is a declared surface of that constituent, and no step reaches around one; the cascade-on-purge rule is where the claim would otherwise break.

- **Invariant 6 — Forensic completability** (an investigator can reconstruct the full history from records alone). For any `event_id`, [Read Record] returns the [Audit Record] — the action, the actor, the attestation reference, the payload **where content is still present**, the retention policy with state and deadlines, and the seal-coverage status including `sealed_at` of the covering seal — or `not-known` for a fabricated id; the hedge is the cascade's, not a softening: for a purged event the `data` payload is no longer readable and the [Audit Record] is returned without it, which is why *completability* is a claim about the record set rather than about the content ([Read Record] step 4 carries the same qualification, and the attestation's `action_ref`, `actor_ref`, and `attested_at` — read out of the attestation store, reached through the destruction record's `(event_id, attestation_id)` pair, since the payload's copies went unreadable with Event Log's `data` field — are what still answer *who / what / when*); and [Verify Record] returns one of `verified`, `failed-verification(reason)`, `unverifiable(reason)`, or `not-known` — the first three optionally carrying the `(compensation-window)` qualifier on the separate channel its contract declares — deterministically **given that the constituent verification surfaces are available**. That condition is not a hedge but the reason the [Unverifiable] arm exists: when the actor registry or the seal mechanism cannot be reached, the honest answer is that verification could not be performed, and determinism is claimed over the outcomes reachable with the surfaces up. *Rests on:* [Read Record] and [Verify Record] establish it — **and for a purged event the *who / what / when* half of the join rests on two things the cascade deliberately leaves standing: the destruction record's `(event_id, attestation_id)` pair, captured at [Purge Event] step 2 before the delegation ran, and the attestation store's surviving fields it names** (`attestation_id`, `action_ref`, `actor_ref`, `attested_at`, per Actor Identity's Outputs), which is the branch [Read Record] step 4 declares and the only route left once Event Log's `data` field is unreadable in its entirety; Actor Identity Invariants 1 (attestation immutability) and 9 (attestation durability), without which that record would not still be there to read, 6 (self-containment) and 7 (verification consistency under fixed registry state); Tamper Evidence Invariants 4 (verification self-containment given the originating records) and 7 (verification consistency under a fixed record set); Invariants 1–3 above for the completeness of what is joined.

- **Invariant 7 — Verification asymmetry preserved.** [Verify Record] requires the original record set to be re-presented (inheriting Tamper Evidence's *verification self-containment given the originating records*); it does not require the actor's credential beyond the registry's public material (inheriting Actor Identity's *verification self-containment*). The asymmetry is intentional and surfaces at the composition's caller boundary — which is also why [Read Record] does not verify: a read surface that fetched the payload internally would hide exactly this asymmetry. **The asymmetry extends to cadence rather than being narrowed by it.** The rule is one sentence and does not vary: *the verifier presents what the covering seal commits to.* Under *per-event* cadence that is typically this one event's payload; under *interval-based* or *on-demand* cadence it is the payloads of the seal's whole sequence-number range. `verify_record`'s second argument is the same argument in every case — the record set the covering seal commits to — and only its extent moves. What the composition owes the verifier in exchange is knowing *which* record set that is, and it discharges that through [Read Record], which returns the covering seal's range; the composition tells the caller what to present and the caller presents it. Fetching the coverage internally would collapse the asymmetry exactly as fetching a single payload would. *Rests on:* [Verify Record] steps 3 and 5 establish it, [Read Record] step 4 supplies the covering range, and *Primitive policies* (`original_event_payload`) fixes both shapes; Tamper Evidence Invariants 3 (record-set binding) and 4; Actor Identity Invariant 6.

- **Invariant 8 — Honest representation of destruction (at quiescence).** *Missing without record* does not occur through this composition. **At quiescence** — no [Record Action] in flight and the configured `compensation_window` (Configuration) elapsed, the same condition Invariants 1 and 2 carry and for the same reason — every event it recorded is in exactly one of two states with records to prove it: **retained** — a retention record in *Retained*, against which [Verify Record] checks attestation and seal — or **lawfully destroyed** — a retention record in *Purged* with `purged_at`, plus the event's `sequence_number` in the covering `seal_coverage` entry's `purged_events` set, plus the destruction record's `(event_id, attestation_id)` pair, plus the mechanism's own **`destroyed`** outcome record in `erasure_outcomes` (Composition state). **That fourth record is why the foreclosed state stays foreclosed, and the mechanism is the scan rather than the cascade.** A cascade whose delegation reported `destruction-failed(reason)` leaves the exact state this invariant is about — a *Purged* retention over content still readable — and the composition neither hides it nor treats it as terminal: [Purge Event] step 3 rejects `cascade-failure(step-3)`, and the reconciliation scan's half-completed-cascade predicate tests for a **successful** outcome record, not for the presence of one, so the entry is re-driven on every cycle **until a `destroyed` outcome lands**. The state is therefore a surfaced, bounded transient under the `compensation_window` rather than a third steady state, and it is foreclosed *because the scan retries*, not because failure was assumed away. An implementation whose predicate accepted any outcome record would close the entry on the evidence of its own failure and reach exactly the state this invariant forbids, with its own records agreeing the destruction was lawful. **Every purged event has a covering `seal_coverage` entry by construction**, because [Purge Event] step 0 refuses to cascade over an event in the unsealed tail until [Seal Now] has covered it — so the second of those four records can never be missing for the structural reason that there was nowhere to write it. The quiescence qualifier is not decorative: without it this invariant would contradict Invariant 2's liveness arm, which admits a reachable window in which an appended event has no retention record yet. **Inside that compensation window** the composition still refuses to be silent about the event. [Read Record] returns the [Audit Record] with retention status `unresolved (compensation window)` — never `not-known`, because the log entry proves the event is real — and [Verify Record] proceeds and returns its ordinary integrity outcome carrying the `(compensation-window)` qualifier: an event in the compensation window is **verifiable but not yet retention-covered**, which is a surfaced compliance finding with a bounded lifetime, not a third steady state. [Verify Record]'s `failed-verification(purged)` is read off the *Purged* retention record, not off the absence of content, which is what makes the distinction survive every conforming erasure mechanism: whether the payload's key was shredded or the payload is still readable pending the mechanism's run, the answer is the same and it is backed by a record. `not-known` is reserved for the id no constituent has ever heard of. **The liveness arm is conditioned on the constituent stores being available**, on the same terms Invariants 1, 2, and 6 carry: the reconciliation scan reads the retention, seal, and attestation stores and writes its findings through [Record Action], so a store that cannot be reached suspends the window's closure rather than falsifying the claim, and the entry stands as a surfaced compliance alert until the write can land. *Rests on:* [Purge Event] steps 0, 1, 2, and 4 and [Verify Record] steps 1–2 establish it, and [Read Record] step 5 and [Verify Record] step 2 define the compensation-window reading; **the liveness arm — that the compensation-window state is bounded and closes — rests on [Record Action]**, through which the reconciliation scan records its findings (`audit.reconciliation`) and the compensating retention placement is recorded (`audit.compensation`), so the closure of the window is itself an audit record rather than an assertion (the cascade-on-purge rule, *Where the compensation lands*); Retention Window Invariants 1 (membership exclusivity), 3 (terminal absorption), and 10 (retention store durability); Tamper Evidence Invariant 1 (evidence immutability); Invariant 2 above for the compensation window it is conditioned on, and Invariant 4 for the coordination that produces the surviving records.

Attribution coverage and retention coverage together give the *complete-record* property — every event in the audit log has the metadata the regulator's first three questions need. Integrity coverage modulo unsealed tail names the cadence trade-off honestly. Cascade coordination on purge is the wiring contract that prevents dangling state across the four stores without pretending to a destruction surface two constituents forbid. Honest representation of destruction is the property that distinguishes a *complete* audit trail from a *suspicious* one.

## Examples

### Walkthrough

A regulated bank deploys this composition as the canonical audit trail for its core ledger. The composition is configured with `retention_policy = sox_7_year`, `seal_cadence = every 1000 events or 60 seconds, whichever first`, `seal_mechanism = SHA-256 hash chain, chain tail anchored synchronously at seal time to an RFC 3161 TSA` (SHA-256 is a standard cryptographic hash function), `erasure_mechanism = per-event content-key shredding at the storage layer`, `compensation_window = 24 hours`, `reconciliation_cadence = 60 seconds` (the derived default: the **time arm** of its interval seal cadence — the *every 1000 events* arm is not a duration and could not have supplied one), and `payload_cap = 64 KB` (matching the wired Event Log instance's own configured cap). The chain is **linked across seals** — each seal commits to its predecessor — which is what makes the forensic-window bound of step 3's audit and of Generation acceptance check 4 available to this deployment.

1. **A wire-transfer authorization arrives.** The system calls `record_action(wire_w91, supervisor_s12, supervisor_credential, {amount: 50000, counterparty: ...})`. The composition invokes Actor Identity → `attestation_a44`; Event Log → `event_e9301`; Retention Window → `retention_r9301` with `retention_until = 2033-05-10`; the event lands in the unsealed tail. Returns `event_e9301`.
2. **The cadence fires.** The thousand-event arm trips first: [Seal Now] runs over the sequence-number slice `[8302 .. 9301]`, whose last member is `e9301`. The mechanism computes a hash-chain proof over the whole slice, the chain tail is anchored to the TSA **synchronously, within the seal call** — which is what entitles the seal record to carry `anchored_at` at all: Tamper Evidence sets that field only for seal-time anchoring, and later, batched anchoring is a separate External Anchoring composing pattern with its own records — and `evidence_s127` is recorded with `anchored_at = 2026-05-10T14:33:00Z`. `seal_coverage[s127] = [8302 .. 9301]`; `sealed_through` advances to 9301.
3. **Six years later, a SOX §404 audit** (SOX — the US Sarbanes-Oxley Act of 2002, the financial-reporting controls statute). The auditor asks *"show me the supervisor authorization on wire w91, and prove it hasn't been altered."* The audit team first calls `read_record(e9301)`, which returns the [Audit Record] in one shot: action `wire_w91`, actor `supervisor_s12`, attestation `a44`, retention `r9301` in *Retained* with `retention_until = 2033-05-10`, and seal coverage `s127` **over the sequence-number range `[8302 .. 9301]`** — `e9301` sits at `sequence_number 9301`, the last member of that seal's thousand-event range. That range is the instruction for the next call. This deployment runs interval cadence, so `s127` commits to a thousand events, not to one: the auditor pulls **all thousand payloads for `[8302 .. 9301]`** from cold storage — through Event Log's declared sequence-range read where the log is still online, and from the archive otherwise — and calls `verify_record(e9301, payloads_8302_through_9301)`. The composition reads retention first (*Retained*, so no `purged` short-circuit), confirms the event is present, verifies the attestation against `s12`'s public material, locates `s127` as the covering seal, and passes the presented range through to `TamperEvidence.verify`. Returns `verified`. The auditor sees one structural answer to all four questions — and never has to guess what to present, because [Read Record] named the range (Invariant 7). Under a *per-event* cadence the same call would carry `e9301`'s single payload and nothing else; the argument is the same argument, and only its extent moves with the cadence.
4. **Seven years and one month later.** The retention has elapsed. The composition runs [Purge Eligible] nightly — delegating to Retention Window's own `purge_eligible` projection — and `e9301` is on the list. `purge_event(e9301)` runs the cascade. Step 0 is a no-op — `9301 ≤ sealed_through`, so `s127` already covers it and there is somewhere to write the destruction record. Then `RetentionWindow.purge(r9301)` moves the retention to *Purged* with `purged_at = 2033-06-14`; `9301` joins `seal_coverage[s127].purged_events` — **just that one sequence number**, since the other 999 members of `[8302 .. 9301]` are still under their own retentions and marking them destroyed would be false — and the same write captures the pair `(e9301, a44)` into the destruction record, **before** anything is destroyed, because after step 3 the payload that carried that binding is unreadable and nothing else replays it; destruction of **Event Log's `data` field in its entirety** for `e9301` — the whole appended `{action_ref, actor_ref, attestation_id, data}` object, not merely the caller's `data` sub-field — and of `a44`'s `proof` is then handed to the deployment's declared `erasure_mechanism` — here, shredding of the per-event content key at the storage layer, a shredding-class mechanism — which reports `destroyed` back to the cascade, completing it. Had it reported `destruction-failed(...)` instead, the cascade would have rejected `cascade-failure(step-3)` and the 60-second reconciliation scan would have re-driven the entry until a `destroyed` outcome landed, `r9301` standing as a surfaced compliance alert in the meantime. The composition itself deletes nothing and rewrites nothing: the Event Log entry's stored fields and the attestation record's stored fields are byte-for-byte what they were, exactly as Event Log's Invariants 1 and 2 and Actor Identity's Invariants 9 and 1 require. What changed is that the key is gone, so the payload and the proof can no longer be read — while `a44`'s `action_ref` (`wire_w91`), `actor_ref` (`supervisor_s12`), and `attested_at` stay readable, so *who authorized what, and when* survives the purge; what does not survive is the ability to re-check it cryptographically. `verify_record(e9301, ...)` now returns `failed-verification(purged)`, read off `r9301`'s *Purged* state rather than off the absence of content — and read there **before** any `ActorIdentity.verify` call is made, which is what keeps the composition from reporting its own lawful shredding of `a44`'s proof as an attestation failure. No re-seal follows, because this composition has no re-sealing surface: `s127` stays the one seal over `[8302 .. 9301]`, and its 999 surviving members now answer `unverifiable(partially-purged-coverage)` — the thousand-payload record set `s127` commits to can no longer be presented in full, so their integrity is *unknown*, not *bad*, and it stays unknown for the rest of their retained lifetimes. A bank that could not accept that standing answer would compose the forthcoming **Seal Lifecycle** pattern, which would cut a fresh seal over the 999 survivors and carry the record of which of the two seals over that range a verifier should now present against — at the cost of one mechanism round-trip per purge, which for a nightly sweep over an expired cohort is a real bill, and at the cost of a pattern this deployment would have to wire.
5. **A subsequent regulator inquiry.** *"What happened to wire w91?"* The auditor queries the retention store, finds `r9301` in *Purged* with `purged_at` within the lawful window, and the inquiry resolves to *lawfully destroyed under SOX §802 retention policy*. The seal store retains `s127` — now carrying `9301` in its `purged_events` set and still the cover for its other 999 members — **indefinitely**. Nothing in this composition disposes of it: the seal is a compact commitment, and it is the standing evidence that `e9301` existed and was sealed before it was destroyed. The attestation store likewise retains `a44` with its `action_ref`, `actor_ref`, and `attested_at` still readable — and `read_record(e9301)` reaches them the only way left: it sees retention *Purged*, takes the `(e9301, a44)` pair out of the destruction record, and reads the *who / what / when* off `a44` itself rather than off a payload that no longer reads ([Read Record] step 4's purged branch). So the answer is not merely *something was destroyed* but *`supervisor_s12`'s authorization of `wire_w91`, attested at that moment, was destroyed lawfully on 2033-06-14* — which is the answer the regulator is actually asking for.

### Walkthrough — rejection paths

The same bank, the same configuration, two runs that do not reach `verified`. Both exercise the load-bearing cascade in its refusing mode.

**Orphan attestation — `attest` succeeds, `append` fails.** A second wire authorization arrives: `record_action(wire_w92, supervisor_s12, supervisor_credential, {amount: 75000, counterparty: ...})`.

1. Step 1 validates the primitives: `wire_w92` and `supervisor_s12` are non-empty, non-whitespace, and far inside the 1 KB reference cap, and the **full constructed payload** — the two references, the forthcoming `attestation_id`, and the 2 KB `data` — totals about 2 KB, well inside the Event Log instance's 64 KB cap. Pass — nothing is recorded yet.
2. Step 2 calls `ActorIdentity.attest(wire_w92, supervisor_s12, supervisor_credential)` → `attestation_a45`. The attestation is now committed and immutable.
3. Step 3 calls `EventLog.append({...})`; the log's underlying store is mid-failover and returns `storage-failure`. Per Event Log's contract the caller must treat that as definitive: `event_e9302` does not exist and never will.
4. The composition returns `rejected(recording-failure(step-3))` to the caller and, **in the same outcome**, surfaces `attestation_a45` as an orphan: an immutable attestation with no corresponding event. Actor Identity's Invariant 9 forecloses rollback, so nothing deletes it. The deployment's compensation discipline runs — a compensating record naming `a45` as an unbound attestation, recorded **through this composition's own [Record Action]** under `audit.compensation` and attributed to the bank's operator identity, raised as a high-priority compliance finding, plus the reconciliation scan (here on a 60-second `reconciliation_cadence`) that catches the same orphan if the process had crashed before it could return and records its findings under `audit.reconciliation`. So the compensation is itself an attested, sealed, retention-governed audit event: an examiner asking *"what did the bank do about `a45`?"* reads the answer out of the same trail rather than out of an operations ticket. The scan finds `a45` by the binding-set test — it is in the attestation store, in no live event payload, and in no destruction record — and, finding `a45` absent from `compensated_attestations`, writes the one compensation and adds it to the set, so the next scan sixty seconds later leaves it alone. Until the compensation lands, Invariant 1's liveness arm is what is being honored: the orphan is surfaced, bounded by the configured `compensation_window` (here 24 hours), never a quiet inconsistency.
5. The caller retries. `record_action(wire_w92, supervisor_s12, supervisor_credential, {...})` after the store recovers produces a *fresh* attestation `attestation_a46` and `event_e9303`. `a45` remains in the store forever as a surfaced, reconciled orphan — permanently unbound, permanently in `compensated_attestations`, which is the honest record of what happened rather than a defect to be hidden.

Note what step 1 prevents. Had `data` been 80 KB, the rejection would have arrived at step 1 as `rejected(invalid-request)` with *nothing* recorded — no attestation to orphan. Validating the size after `attest` would have produced this same orphan for an ordinary oversized-payload caller error, which is why the check sits where it does.

**`not-eligible` — a purge attempted before the window elapses.** In 2030, four years into `e9301`'s seven-year retention, a records-management job is misconfigured with a five-year policy and calls `purge_event(e9301)`.

1. The composition resolves `event_to_retention[e9301]` → `r9301` and calls `RetentionWindow.purge(r9301)`.
2. Retention Window evaluates its own no-early-purge guard against its injected `now`: `2030-06-14 < retention_until = 2033-05-10`. It refuses with `retention-period-not-elapsed`, writing nothing (Retention Window Invariant 7).
3. The composition maps that to [Not Eligible] and returns `rejected(not-eligible)`. **No step of the cascade runs**: nothing joins `s127`'s `purged_events` set, no erasure delegation is issued, and `r9301` stays in *Retained*. `verify_record(e9301, payloads_8302_through_9301)` still returns `verified`.
4. The gate is structural, not advisory. The misconfigured job cannot talk the composition past it, because the composition does not evaluate eligibility itself — it asks the atom that owns the retention record, and that atom's guard is a precondition on its own state.

### Selected rejection and outcome runs

The same bank, three one-paragraph runs over arms the two walkthroughs above do not reach. **Selected, not exhaustive** — [Record Action], [Seal Now], [Verify Record], and [Purge Event] between them declare more arms than any example section should try to walk, and several ([Seal Now]'s `invalid-request` construction fault, [Purge Event]'s `cascade-failure(seal)` and `cascade-failure(step-2)`) are defined at their wiring steps with the state they leave behind named there. The three below were chosen because each makes a *distinction* the composition is built on legible: unverifiable-versus-failed, the declared tail posture, and rejection-before-any-write.

**`unverifiable(attestation-registry-unavailable)` — the registry is down when the auditor asks.** During the same SOX audit, the team calls `verify_record(e9308, payloads_9302_through_10301)`. Retention reads *Retained*; the log entry is present; the composition calls `ActorIdentity.verify(a51)` and the actor registry is unreachable — a network partition to the identity provider. Actor Identity returns `registry-unavailable`. Step 3 does **not** prefix it as `failed-verification(attestation-registry-unavailable)`; it routes it to step 6, which returns `unverifiable(attestation-registry-unavailable)`. Steps 4 and 5 do not run — that is step 3's declared short-circuit, stated in the wiring — so the seal check is skipped and no integrity claim is made either way. The auditor records the outage, not a finding: nothing is known to be wrong with `e9308`, and the correct next step is to retry when the registry returns. Had this collapsed into `failed-verification(...)`, the audit would have manufactured a finding against a record that is, as far as anyone can tell, intact.

**A `lenient`-mode tail verify.** A second deployment of the same composition — an internal operations trail over a WORM log substrate, `seal_cadence = every 6 hours`, `unsealed_tail_mode = lenient` — records `e440` at 09:12 and is asked to verify it at 09:20, four and a half hours before the next cadence firing. `read_record(e440)` reports seal coverage as *unsealed tail* (`sequence_number 440 > sealed_through = 431`). `verify_record(e440, e440_payload)` runs: retention *Retained*, log entry present, attestation verifies. Step 4 finds no covering `evidence_id` — and under `lenient` it does **not** return `failed-verification(unsealed)`. It treats coverage as satisfied, skips step 5 entirely (there is no `evidence_id` to present anything to), proceeds to step 6, finds no availability failure, and returns `verified` at step 7. The deployment has accepted Event Log's per-event immutability plus its WORM substrate as sufficient for the tail. The same call against the bank's `strict` instance returns `failed-verification(unsealed)` — same records, different declared posture, and the difference is visible in Configuration rather than buried in an implementation.

**`invalid-credential` — a record rejected before anything is written.** A terminated supervisor's smart card is used to authorize a wire: `record_action(wire_w95, supervisor_s12, revoked_credential, {amount: 12000, ...})`. Step 1 passes — the references are well-formed and the payload is inside the cap. Step 2 calls `ActorIdentity.attest(wire_w95, supervisor_s12, revoked_credential)`, and Actor Identity refuses with `invalid-credential`. The composition propagates it unchanged as `rejected(invalid-credential)` and stops: no attestation exists (Actor Identity wrote nothing), no event is appended, no retention is placed, no seal is attempted, and **no orphan is created** — the failure is at the first constituent, so there is nothing committed to compensate. The audit log carries no trace of the attempt, which is the deliberate scope line of the *Failed attribution attempts* edge case: this composition's surface is committed actions, and a deployment that must audit the *attempt* composes a Failed-Attempt Log pattern *(forthcoming)* beside it.

The composition is what *"adequate internal controls"* operationally means.

### Healthcare — PHI (Protected Health Information — individually identifiable health data) access audit under HIPAA §164.312(b)

Every read of, write to, or amendment of protected health information is recorded with the accessing clinician's attestation (smart-card-bound credential under EPCS — Electronic Prescriptions for Controlled Substances — for controlled substances, regular EHR — Electronic Health Record — credential otherwise), retained for the longer of HIPAA's 6-year baseline or state law, and sealed in a per-patient Merkle tree. ### Payments — cardholder-data access audit under PCI DSS Requirement 10

The annual QSA (Qualified Security Assessor — the PCI-council-certified auditor who signs off on a merchant's compliance) assessment runs [Verify Record] over the prior year's high-risk actions; any tampering — to hide an exfiltration or to forge access for a fraudulent dispute — is detected from the trail itself.

The CFR is the codified body of US federal agency rules, and Part 11 of its Title 21 is the electronic-records rule. The SEC is the US securities regulator. ### Regulated adversarial scenarios

Three scenarios the composition must survive in regulated contexts:

- **Regulator audit — "show me the complete, verifiable history of action X over the retention horizon."** *Enumerating the events is the composed dependency, not a passthrough.* "Every event referencing action X" is a query **by payload field**, and Event Log's own *Reverse lookup / indexing* edge case routes that to a separate **Reverse Index** pattern *(forthcoming)*; a deployment that has to answer this ask wires Reverse Index over the audit log and gets back the set of `event_id`s. (Had the ask been "every event in this sequence-number window" or "every event last quarter," it would pass straight through to Event Log's `read` — those are declared query shapes.) From there the composition answers per event: for each retained event, [Verify Record] returns `verified` (with attestation and seal both checked); for each purged event, the retention record proves lawful destruction. Invariants 1, 2, 3, and 8 are the structural answer. The auditor does not consult source code, runbooks, or developer narration — every claim is verified from the records, and the one thing this composition does **not** claim to supply is the payload-field enumeration that starts the walk.
- **Disputed action — "I didn't do that."** The investigator retrieves the event, calls [Verify Record]. If `verified`, the attestation binds the named actor to the named action at `attested_at` (Actor Identity's non-repudiation contract, propagated through Invariant 5). The actor cannot plausibly deny it without claiming credential compromise — and a Compromise Disclosure composing pattern *(forthcoming)* handles that reinterpretation, never by mutating the trail.
- **Breach forensics — "when was the trail compromised?"** An incident responder walks the seals in `sealed_at` order — every entry in the seal store, since this composition writes exactly one seal per range and never a replacement — running [Verify Record] against representative events in each seal's range. The most recent seal that returns `verified` end-to-end and the next seal that returns `failed-verification(seal-proof-invalid)` bound the forensic window. **That bound has a precondition the deployment owns: the seal mechanism must be chained or linked across seals — each seal committing to its predecessor.** Only then does a verified seal say anything about the seals before it, which is what turns two adjacent results into a window. Under **independent per-range seals** — each proof standing alone over its own range, committing to nothing outside it — a failed seal narrows the tampering only to *within that seal's range*, and says nothing about the intervals between seals; the responder gets a set of compromised ranges rather than a bounded window. Which of the two a given `seal_mechanism` is, is the mechanism's business and not visible through Tamper Evidence's surface (its Invariant 8, mechanism opacity), so the deployment must declare it rather than infer it. An `unverifiable(...)` result does **not** bound anything — under the two availability reasons it means the mechanism or registry was down when the responder asked, and the responder must retry rather than read the outage as the tampering boundary; under `partially-purged-coverage` it means a lawful purge has made that seal's record set unpresentable, and the responder must step over it to the nearest presentable seal rather than read a retention expiry as a tampering boundary — a step that widens the window rather than falsifying it, and one a deployment can narrow by composing the forthcoming **Seal Lifecycle** pattern to keep purged ranges presentable. Collapsing that outcome into `failed-verification(...)` would move the apparent boundary to wherever the mechanism happened to be unreachable, which is why the arm is distinct (Invariant 6). Where the seals carry `anchored_at` from a TSA outside the adversary's reach, the upper bound on the time of tampering is independently established. The cadence governs the resolution; the four-atom stack governs the certainty.

## Generation acceptance

A derived implementation of Audit Trail is *acceptable* — in the regulator-acceptance sense — when an external auditor, given the composition's emergent state plus the four constituent stores, can clear the checks below without recourse to source code, runbooks, or developer narration. The bar splits in two, because two structurally different things are being asked. The first list is what the composition's own records answer. The second is what arises *around* the composition and needs evidence the composition does not hold — the audit-gap questions, important to surface and not this composition's contract to satisfy.

### Audit-Trail-traversal-clearable checks

1. **Answer all four audit questions for any event.** From an `event_id`, one [Read Record] call returns the joined [Audit Record]: what (the event's payload), who (the attestation reference, verifiable via [Verify Record]), integrity (the covering seal and its coverage status), retention (the retention record with state and deadlines). Invariant 6 is the contract; this check verifies the join is complete and that `not-known` is returned only for an id no constituent knows.
2. **Verify all eight composition-level invariants over the record set.** Attribution coverage, retention coverage, integrity coverage modulo unsealed tail, cascade coordination on purge, constituent invariants preserved, forensic completability, verification asymmetry preserved, honest representation of destruction. Invariants 1 and 2 are verified **at quiescence**, and the auditor reads the quiescence condition off a declared number rather than off the spec's confidence: **the deployment's configured `compensation_window`** (Configuration) is the value this check audits against. The auditor enumerates the log against the attestation and retention stores with no [Record Action] in flight and that duration elapsed, and reads any residual orphan as a surfaced compliance finding rather than a silent violation. **The retention side of that enumeration is deliberately the mirror of the reconciliation scan's third half** (the cascade-on-purge rule): same open-upper-bound read of the log, same per-event test of `event_to_retention` with rebuild-on-miss first, same reading of a true miss past the window as a finding. The auditor runs from outside what the implementation is obliged to run from inside, and the two answers must agree — an instance whose scan does not sweep for unretained events will have this check find them for it. An instance that cannot produce a `compensation_window` value fails this check before the enumeration starts — there is no window to have elapsed, so "at quiescence" is not a checkable condition, which is why the knob is deployment-required with no default.

   **The orphan-freedom half of Invariant 1 is audited against a marker rather than against the attestation store's shape, and the procedure is exact.** The auditor builds the **binding set** the reconciliation scan builds — the `attestation_id`s carried in live event payloads, from a full open-upper-bound enumeration of the log, unioned with the `attestation_id`s in destruction records (the cascade-on-purge rule specifies the predicate) — and takes every attestation in neither as an orphan. For each orphan the auditor then checks membership in **`compensated_attestations`** (Composition state): an orphan **in** the set has been reconciled and its `audit.compensation` event is readable from the trail as proof of it; an orphan **not** in the set, with the `compensation_window` elapsed, is the residual compliance finding this check surfaces. The reason the closure is read off a marker and not off the store is Actor Identity's **Invariant 9** — the orphan is permanent, so "no orphans remain" is not a state a conforming instance can ever reach, and an auditor who expected it would be auditing against an impossibility. What a conforming instance reaches is *every orphan compensated*.
3. **Verify each constituent atom's invariants or acceptance bar over its own instance.** Event Log carries **no Generation-acceptance bar** — it is an unregulated foundational atom — so what is verified over the audit log instance is its *invariants*, all seven: append-only, event immutability, total order, sequence-number monotonicity, read consistency, no id reuse, and wall-time best-effort monotonicity (Event Log Invariant 7 — under a non-decreasing clock, `recorded_at` is non-decreasing in append order; this one is best-effort by its own terms and the auditor reads a violation as a clock finding, not a log finding, which is why it belongs on this list rather than the external one). Actor Identity's, Retention Window's, and Tamper Evidence's own Generation-acceptance bars are verified over their respective instances, each satisfied independently and observable from that constituent's records.
4. **Bound the forensic window of any detected tampering.** Using Tamper Evidence's per-seal verification cascade, the auditor identifies the latest verified seal and the first failed seal; the cadence and the seals' `sealed_at` (and `anchored_at` where present) give the window. The walk runs over **every** entry in the seal store, because this composition writes one seal per range and never a replacement for one; an entry whose `purged_events` set makes its record set unpresentable returns `unverifiable(partially-purged-coverage)` and the auditor steps over it to the nearest presentable seal, which widens the window rather than bounding it falsely. **Deployment note:** this check clears as a *window* only where the configured `seal_mechanism` is chained or linked across seals, each seal committing to its predecessor. Under independent per-range seals the same walk clears only to "within the failed seal's range" and says nothing about the intervals between seals, so the auditor must read the mechanism's chaining posture from the deployment's declaration — Tamper Evidence's Invariant 8 (mechanism opacity) means it is not readable from the seal records themselves.
5. **Distinguish lawfully destroyed from missing.** [Verify Record] returning `failed-verification(purged)` is backed by a Retention Window record in *Purged* state with `purged_at`. The check reads the *records*, not the absence of content: for every purged event the auditor confirms (a) the retention record is in *Purged*, (b) the event's own `sequence_number` is a member of the covering `seal_coverage` entry's `purged_events` set — membership, not a flag on the entry, so a partly-purged seal is read correctly rather than condemning its live members, and **a covering entry exists for every purged event by construction**, since [Purge Event] step 0 seals an unsealed-tail event before it will cascade over it, so a purged event with no covering entry is a conformance failure rather than an expected shape — (c) the destruction record carries that event's `(event_id, attestation_id)` pair, which is the only surviving traversal from a destroyed event to the attestation destroyed with it, (d) the attestation that pair names still exists with its `action_ref`, `actor_ref`, and `attested_at` **readable** — the cascade destroys the `proof` and the event's `data`, and a deployment whose mechanism took more than that has wired a non-conforming one — and (e) the deployment's `erasure_mechanism` has a **successful (`destroyed`) outcome recorded** for that event in `erasure_outcomes` (Composition state). The success qualifier is the whole of (e): the mechanism's taxonomy has two values (Configuration, `erasure_mechanism`), and an outcome record reading `destruction-failed(reason)` is evidence the cascade did **not** complete — a *Purged* retention over readable content, which is a live compliance finding the reconciliation scan should be re-driving, not a cleared check. An auditor who read mere presence of an outcome record as completion would clear exactly the state Invariant 8 forbids. **And for the live members of a partly-purged seal**, the auditor confirms the composition reports them honestly: they must read as `unverifiable(partially-purged-coverage)` — never as `verified` (a claim the seal can no longer support) and never as any `failed-verification(...)` (an accusation nothing supports). That is the canonical composition's standing outcome for those events; an instance that instead returns `verified` for them has a re-sealing surface this composition does not declare, which the auditor reads through check 8 as a composed **Seal Lifecycle** *(forthcoming)* pattern and audits against that pattern's own bar. *Missing* — an event with no retention record at all — does not occur at quiescence, and the auditor sees the absence as a structural guarantee (Invariants 4 and 8). An event found without a retention record *inside* the configured `compensation_window` is read as the surfaced, bounded finding Invariant 8 names, and the auditor confirms it carries the `unresolved (compensation window)` status rather than being silently absent. **The auditor also confirms that such an event is not purgeable while it is in that state:** [Purge Event] over it must return `rejected(retention-unresolved)` ([Purge Event] step 0½) — not `ok`, and not `not-known`. An instance that lets the cascade run there has destroyed content under no policy at all and left a destruction with no *Purged* retention record behind it, which is exactly the *missing without record* shape Invariant 8 forbids; an instance that answers `not-known` has reported a real, logged event as a fabricated id.
6. **Distinguish a failed record from an unverifiable one.** For every [Verify Record] result in the audit's sample, confirm that *verification could not be performed* surfaces as `unverifiable(...)` and never as `failed-verification(...)`. Three reasons are in scope and all three must land on the `unverifiable` arm: `attestation-registry-unavailable`, `seal-mechanism-verification-unavailable`, and — the one that is not an outage — `partially-purged-coverage`, returned for a live event whose covering seal has had other members lawfully destroyed. That third reason is the sharpest form of this check, because the composition's *own cascade* created the condition: an implementation that reports its own lawful purge as a failed record for the purged event's innocent seal-mates is manufacturing findings against itself out of compliant work. A trail whose registry outages or partly-purged seals read as failed records is not a conforming implementation. Invariant 6 is the contract.
7. **Rebuild the derived indexes and get the same answers.** `event_to_retention`, `event_to_sequence`, `sealed_through`, `seal_coverage`'s ranges, and `compensated_attestations` are derived indexes; the auditor can discard them, run the named rebuild procedures (Composition state) against the constituent stores, and reproduce every traversal answer. `compensated_attestations` rebuilds off the audit log itself — the live `audit.compensation` events carrying `subject = attestation`, each naming the orphan `attestation_id` in its payload, the discriminator being what keeps the other compensation kind's `event_id`s out of the set — and its reproduction is what makes check 2's orphan-freedom audit repeatable rather than a one-time reading of a set the instance happens to hold. The auditor can also confirm the set is **unforgeable**: every event in it carries an `action_ref` in the reserved `audit.*` namespace, which [Record Action] step 1 refuses to every caller but the configured `reconciliation_operator` (Configuration) — and each such event's own payload `actor_ref`, attested at step 2 under the operator's credential, must equal that configured value, which the auditor checks directly — so nothing outside the composition's reconciliation path could have put a member there. `event_to_sequence` is the one to discard first, since every id-addressed traversal runs through it: rebuilt by an open-upper-bound sequence-range enumeration of the log re-keyed on each event's own `event_id`, it must reproduce the same `sequence_number` for every id or the traversal answers are not reproducible. An index that cannot be rebuilt is carrying truth it should not, and is a conformance failure.

   **`event_to_attestation` is checked for totality in two halves, because its source differs by retention state.** For every **live** event the entry must be regenerable from the constituent stores — the rebuild reads the `attestation_id` out of the event's own payload — and the auditor discards and regenerates it like the others. For every **purged** event no rebuild is possible or expected: the cascade destroyed the payload's recoverability, and with it the binding fact the rebuild would have read, so the entry must instead be **present in the destruction record**, captured at [Purge Event] step 2 before the delegation ran. Totality is the conjunction: *live entries rebuildable, purged entries readable from the destruction record*, with no event falling in neither half. A purged event with no destruction-record pair is a conformance failure and a real audit gap — the traversal from a lawfully destroyed event to the attestation destroyed with it is simply gone, and no rebuild will bring it back.

   **The reconciliation scan's orphan predicate reads these same two halves in the other direction, and the auditor should check that the two agree.** The scan's **binding set** is the `attestation_id`s in live event payloads unioned with the `attestation_id`s in destruction records (the cascade-on-purge rule specifies it) — this check's live/purged split walked from the attestation side rather than the event side. An implementation whose scan consults only one half while its index maintains both holds two views of one relation, and the disagreement surfaces as phantom orphans in check 2's enumeration: every purged event's attestation reported unbound the day its payload was shredded.

   `seal_coverage`'s `purged_events` sets, `event_to_attestation`'s purged-event entries, and `erasure_outcomes` are the composition's **three extraction-pending facts** and are **not** in scope for the rebuild half of this check; no rebuild regenerates them (check 5 verifies all three against the destruction record and the erasure mechanism's own records instead).
8. **Identify the composing patterns active in this deployment.** Whether [Legal Hold](../atoms/legal-hold.md) and [Defensible Retention](./defensible-retention.md) are wired in at this deployment — and likewise, each of them *(forthcoming)*: Trusted Timestamping, Storage Tier, Compromise Disclosure, Erasure Coordination, Policy Reconciliation, Mechanism Registry, Reverse Index, Legacy Import, Failed-Attempt Log, Schema Evolution, Erasure Tombstone, Seal Lifecycle, and the declared shredding-class erasure mechanism — and with what configuration. Four of these change what the other checks mean and must be identified before them: Reverse Index (without it, the payload-field enumeration that starts a "history of action X" audit is unavailable), Legal Hold (adds the conditional `under-legal-hold` arm to [Purge Event]), Erasure Tombstone composed for seal disposal (which takes check 4's and Invariant 3's coverage bookkeeping out of this composition's hands), and Seal Lifecycle (which does the same from the other direction — it re-seals ranges this composition sealed once, so check 4's walk and check 5's `partially-purged-coverage` expectation both read against that pattern's bar instead of this one's).

### Externally-clearable checks

These questions arise around this composition but cannot be answered from its records alone:

- **Whether the erasure mechanism was authorized and actually completed.** The cascade *delegates* content destruction (the cascade-on-purge rule); the composition records that it delegated and what the mechanism reported. Verifying that the deployment's mechanism was authorized to destroy, that it is **shredding-class** rather than a non-conforming tombstone-by-mutation, and that the destruction genuinely completed — the key material was actually destroyed — requires the mechanism's own evidence, external to these four stores. (The composition's own records *do* answer the tombstone-by-mutation half in the negative direction: a stored field that changed is visible against Event Log's and Actor Identity's immutability invariants. What they cannot show is that the key really went away.) This check is the direct cost of the delegation and is named here rather than papered over. Once the *Erasure Tombstone* *(forthcoming)* atom lands — the atom that **records** a destruction as a new write-once record, never mutating the record it describes — part of this check migrates to the traversal-clearable list.
- **Whether the actor registry retained its historical public material across key rotation.** Actor Identity's verification depends on the registry's public material for the attesting actor. If the registry drops superseded material on rotation, old attestations begin returning `failed-verification(attestation-actor-unknown-in-registry)` for a reason that has nothing to do with the trail's integrity. The registry's retention policy is external evidence; the composition cannot produce it.
- **Whether the seal mechanism is cryptographically sound.** Tamper Evidence is deliberately mechanism-neutral (its Invariant 8, mechanism opacity). That a SHA-256 hash chain, a Merkle tree, or a given TSA's signature scheme is still fit for the audit horizon is a question for cryptographic review and the deployment's Mechanism Registry *(forthcoming)*, not for the records.
- **Whether the mechanism's seal-time rendering agrees with the verify-time presentation.** The `seal_mechanism` setting rule (Configuration) obliges the two to be the same byte-exact, ascending-`sequence_number` rendering — Tamper Evidence's *Record-set definition* edge case routes that agreement to the host, and this composition is the host. **The auditor cannot verify it from the records alone.** Nothing in the seal store says which rendering a given seal committed to: `record_set_ref` names the sequence-number slice, not the bytes hashed over it, and mechanism opacity (Tamper Evidence Invariant 8) means the proof does not disclose it either. A mechanism that sealed over a differently-ordered or re-serialized form would produce proofs that fail every conforming presentation, and the failure would surface as `failed-verification(seal-proof-invalid)` — indistinguishable, from the records, from actual tampering. The agreement is attested by **deployment declaration** and evidenced the way the erasure mechanism's class is: from the mechanism's own documentation and configuration, external to these four stores. A deployment that cannot produce that declaration cannot distinguish a rendering mismatch from a compromised trail, which is the worst possible ambiguity for the one answer this composition exists to give.
- **Whether the clock was honest.** Retention deadlines, `purged_at`, `sealed_at`, and `recorded_at` are all only as truthful as the injected clock (*Clock source for cadence and purge* in Edge cases). Clock authority — that the deployment's clock was monotonic and not manipulated over the audit horizon — needs external evidence: a Trusted Timestamping composition's *(forthcoming)* anchors, time-service audit logs, or both.
- **Whether the retention policy was the correct one.** The composition applies the configured `retention_policy`. Whether that policy is the right one for this record class under the deployment's overlapping obligations is a Policy Reconciliation *(forthcoming)* question answered against the applicable regulations and the deployment's Policy Registry.

This is the generator's contract: any code generated from this composition must produce records and a runtime surface that clear the eight traversal checks above, and must make the six externally-clearable questions *askable* — naming the external evidence each needs rather than implying the records answer it. The bar is the regulator's question — *"can you prove what happened, who did it, when, that it hasn't been altered, and that the retention obligation was honored?"* — answered structurally from the records and the proofs, not procedurally from runtime claims.

## Edge cases and explicit non-goals

What this composition does not cover:

- **Multi-instance Audit Trails.** A real system may have many audit trails (one per audited subsystem, one per jurisdictional scope). The composition specifies one instance; multi-instance configuration, cross-instance query, and federation are handled at the deployment layer. A higher-order Audit Federation pattern *(forthcoming)* composes naturally.
- **Pre-attestation legacy events.** Events imported from a legacy system without verifiable attribution have no usable Actor Identity binding, and **this composition has no path for them.** It declares exactly one way for an event to enter the audit log — [Record Action], which attests before it appends — so every event it records is an attested event, and Invariant 1's attribution coverage quantifies over **events recorded through [Record Action] only**. Bulk import of pre-attestation history is a composing **Legacy Import** pattern *(forthcoming)*, and it has two honest shapes: either it attests each imported record under a **declared import identity** (an actor reference naming the migration itself, so the resulting attestation is truthful about who asserted the record and when — it binds the importer, not the original actor), and then records the result through [Record Action] like any other event; or it maintains a **separate unattested store** that the auditor reads as a distinct, explicitly-unattributed body of history. What it must not do is write into this composition's log around [Record Action], because that would put events in the audit log that Invariant 1 does not cover while the invariant still reads as if it did. Whether the attribution gap is acceptable for the audit horizon is a legal question the deployment answers about the imported body of records, not one this composition can absorb.
- **Seal cadence vs. write rate.** Tighter cadence (per-event) narrows the forensic window but increases the seal-store growth rate and verify-time cost; coarser cadence is cheaper but widens the window. Selection is handled at the deployment layer. The composition surfaces the trade-off; the deployment owns the choice. **A coarse cadence has a second cost that only shows up years later, at purge time**, and it is worth naming beside the first: the coarser the cadence, the more live seal-mates each purge strands. Those survivors answer `unverifiable(partially-purged-coverage)` until their own retentions elapse, and the canonical composition offers no way to shorten that — the deployment either accepts the standing answer or composes the forthcoming **Seal Lifecycle** pattern and pays a mechanism round-trip per purge. Neither is wrong, but the choice is made years before the bill arrives, which is exactly the kind of coupling a deployment should see stated rather than discover.
- **Compromised credential mid-window.** If an actor's credential is compromised and the discovery is later than the compromise, attestations made during the compromise window verify but should be reinterpreted. The composition does *not* retroactively invalidate (inheriting Actor Identity's contract); a Compromise Disclosure composing pattern *(forthcoming)* produces *new* records that reframe the previously-verified attestations as untrustworthy. Required reading for breach response runbooks.
- **Payload-field queries.** Enumerating events by what is *inside* them — every event referencing action X, every event by actor Y, every event carrying a business id in `data` — is not this composition's surface and is not a passthrough to Event Log either: Event Log's own *Reverse lookup / indexing* edge case routes payload-field lookup to a separate **Reverse Index** pattern *(forthcoming)*. A deployment that needs those queries composes Reverse Index over the audit log and joins its `event_id` results back through [Read Record]. Queries by sequence-number range or wall-time range are different and *do* pass through unchanged; both are declared query shapes on Event Log's `read` (Action wiring).
- **Policy disagreement across overlapping rules.** When multiple regulations apply to one event (HIPAA + state law + GDPR (EU General Data Protection Regulation) + GLBA — the US Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act, governing financial-data privacy), the `retention_policy` must reconcile to the longest applicable retention, the strictest data-minimization posture, and any conflicting destruction rules. A Policy Reconciliation composing pattern *(forthcoming)* owns this; the composition takes the reconciled `policy_ref` as input.
- **Right-to-be-forgotten vs. retention obligation.** A GDPR Article 17 erasure request can collide with a regulatory retention obligation (HIPAA, SOX). The composition does not adjudicate; an Erasure Coordination composing pattern *(forthcoming)* (with legal counsel in the loop) decides whether to honor the request, cryptographically shred the personal-data fields while preserving the structural audit record, or document the retention override. The *destruction mechanism itself* is separately out of scope for the same structural reason the cascade delegates it (the cascade-on-purge rule): Event Log exposes no delete surface and Actor Identity never deletes an attestation, so content destruction — whether triggered by an erasure request or by an elapsed retention — is performed by the deployment's declared **shredding-class** mechanism (an *Erasure Tombstone* *(forthcoming)* composing pattern recording the destruction, over cryptographic shredding at the storage layer performing it), never by this composition, and never by mutating a stored field.
- **Seal disposal.** The canonical composition retains seals **indefinitely** and declares no seal-store retention owner (Configuration; the cascade-on-purge rule). A deployment whose jurisdiction treats a commitment over erased data as residual data of that data must dispose of seals, and that is an externally-composed variant: the forthcoming *Erasure Tombstone* pattern owns the seal-disposal surface and the coverage bookkeeping that follows it — which events lose their cover, and what Invariant 3 reads as once they have. This composition does not carry that surface, because carrying it would mean asserting a seal lifetime it has no knob for and would put Invariant 3 and Tamper Evidence's Invariant 9 permanently modulo an unstated policy.
- **Seal re-sealing and mechanism deprecation.** The canonical composition writes **one seal per range and never a replacement for one**. [Seal Now] cuts new coverage over the unsealed tail and that is its whole surface; there is no action that seals a range some seal already covers. Two consequences follow, and both are non-goals rather than gaps:

  *A partly-purged seal is not repaired.* When the cascade destroys one member of a seal's record set, the seal **remains the one seal covering its range**, and its surviving live members answer `unverifiable(partially-purged-coverage)` from that point on ([Verify Record] step 4) — *verification is impossible*, not *this record failed*, and standing rather than transient.

  *A seal cannot be rotated onto a new mechanism.* Tamper Evidence's own **Invariant 2** qualifies its detectability guarantee on the mechanism being cryptographically sound and prescribes the remedy — *"seals produced under deprecated mechanisms should be re-sealed under a sound mechanism before the old one is deprecated"* — and this composition cannot take it. `seal_mechanism` governs seals cut *from now on*; seals already written stay under the mechanism that produced them, and a deployment approaching a deprecation must plan for that rather than assume a rotation surface exists here.

  **Both belong to a forthcoming *Seal Lifecycle* composing pattern**, which owns re-sealing a partly-purged seal's surviving members, mechanism rotation ahead of a deprecation, and the **supersession bookkeeping both require** — *which seal is current* over a range two seals now cover. That third item is why the concept is routed out rather than absorbed: it is **new truth no constituent carries**. Tamper Evidence's *Concurrent seals on the same record set* edge case treats two evidence records over one record set as two valid independent proofs and takes no view on which supersedes which, so no enumeration of the seal store replays the fact, and no rebuild procedure can regenerate it. A composition that held it would be carrying unclassifiable state on behalf of a concept it had not specified — which is precisely the extraction test, and this concept clears it. Naming the owner is the honest move; the canonical composition's behavior without that owner is stated plainly wherever it shows (Invariant 3, [Verify Record] step 4, [Read Record] step 4, [Purge Event] step 3, Generation acceptance checks 4 and 5).
- **Legal hold suspension of purge.** Pending litigation or investigation must stop destruction within the affected scope, and **where the composed hold lands is [Purge Event], not [Purge Eligible]**. A [Legal Hold](../atoms/legal-hold.md) composing pattern intercepts the cascade before its first step and rejects it while the hold is active, which is what [Purge Event]'s conditional [Under Legal Hold] arm names, for exactly as long as that pattern is composed; the cascade defers until the hold is released. **[Purge Eligible] is hold-unaware and stays that way**: it is a pure projection that delegates wholesale to Retention Window's own read-time `purge_eligible` (Retention Window Invariant 11), which evaluates `now ≥ retention_until` and knows nothing of holds, so a held event keeps appearing on the eligible list and is refused at the gate rather than filtered out ahead of it. That is the honest shape — the list means *retention has elapsed*, which is true of a held record, and folding a hold filter into it would have this composition asserting a suppression on behalf of a pattern that owns the gate. A deployment that wants a hold-filtered worklist builds it by asking the composed Legal Hold pattern, not by expecting this projection to have done it. [Defensible Retention](./defensible-retention.md) is the composition that already wires this gate over a business record set.
- **Storage tier (active vs. cold).** SEC Rule 17a-4's *first two years immediately accessible* is orthogonal to retention obligation and to integrity. A Storage Tier composing pattern *(forthcoming)* owns the active-to-cold transition; the composition's [Verify Record] accepts records retrieved from either tier identically.
- **Concurrency.** Four serialization obligations, all implementation-owned, mirroring the corpus's other serialization edge cases. **(1) Sealing must be serialized per instance.** [Seal Now], the cadence-fired sealing, and [Purge Event]'s step-0 precondition seal all read `sealed_through`, seal the slice above it, and advance it; two concurrent sealings that read the same `sealed_through` would each seal the same range and produce two `evidence_id`s covering it, violating Invariant 3's *exactly one seal* claim. Tamper Evidence will not stop them — its own *Concurrent seals on the same record set* edge case says two seals over one record set are two valid independent proofs and that *one seal per record set per cadence* is the composing pattern's coordination job, which is this obligation. The implementation serializes the read-seal-advance sequence per Audit Trail instance, and step 0's seal takes the same lock as any other.

  **(2) [Purge Event] must be serialized per `event_id` for the cascade, and the destruction-record write must additionally serialize per `evidence_id`.** Both are needed, because the cascade and its second step contend on **different resources**. The per-`event_id` obligation is over the cascade: two concurrent cascades over one event would both attempt the retention purge (Retention Window's terminal absorption makes the loser observe `not-retained`, which step 1 already resumes from) and both issue the delegation, so serializing on the `event_id` keeps the cascade's steps in one order and keeps the reconciliation scan's reading of "landed or not" meaningful. **The same per-`event_id` serialization is what makes the compensating retention placement of Invariant 2's liveness arm a decision rather than a race**: the reconciliation path checks `event_to_retention` for the event and places only if absent, and Retention Window's Invariant 5 (re-retention creates a *new* retention with a new id) means two passes that both observed *absent* would leave two retentions governing one event. Under this lock they cannot both observe it. But the step-2 write — adding a `sequence_number` to `purged_events` and appending the `(event_id, attestation_id)` pair — contends on **the covering seal's `seal_coverage` entry**, which is shared by every member of that seal's range. Two cascades over *different* events under one seal hold different `event_id` locks and race freely on that one entry, and a read-modify-write of the set under that race silently loses a membership: the last writer's set overwrites the other's addition, and the lost membership is exactly the evidence that a particular sealed record was destroyed — extraction-pending truth no rebuild will bring back. **So the destruction-record write serializes per `evidence_id`** — the contended resource — **or the implementation uses an atomic set-add** (a set-union primitive, a conditional write, an append-only membership record) that is correct without a lock. Either discharges it; what does not discharge it is the per-`event_id` lock alone, which is the natural mistake because it is the lock the surrounding cascade already holds.

  **(3) [Record Action] steps 1–5 need no composition-level serialization** — their ordering is governed by Event Log's own append serialization, which that atom names as the load-bearing precondition for its Invariants 3 and 4. Concurrent [Record Action] calls interleave freely through validation, attestation, append, retention placement, and index writes, and each gets its own sequence number; the index writes are per-`event_id` and contend with nothing. **The scope stops at step 5 deliberately.** Step 6's cadence-fired seal is not covered by this obligation and must not be read as if it were: it reads `sealed_through`, seals the slice above it, and advances it — the exact read-seal-advance sequence **obligation (1)** governs — so the seal a [Record Action] fires under *per-event* cadence takes the same per-instance sealing lock as [Seal Now] and [Purge Event]'s step-0 precondition seal. Two concurrent per-event-cadence records would otherwise double-seal a range and violate Invariant 3's *exactly one seal* claim, which is obligation (1)'s whole subject. Step 6 rides obligation (1); steps 1–5 need nothing. The read surfaces ([Read Record], [Purge Eligible]) are pure projections and are excluded from every one of these obligations.

  **(4) The reconciliation scan's orphan compensation must be serialized per `attestation_id`.** The scan pre-checks `compensated_attestations` (Composition state) and records an `audit.compensation` event only if the orphan's `attestation_id` is absent from it (the cascade-on-purge rule). Unserialized, two passes — a restart scan and a cadence scan, or two nodes running the cadence — can both read *absent* for one orphan and both record, producing two compensation events for one attestation. The contended resource is the `attestation_id`, so **that is what the lock is over**: the per-`event_id` lock of obligation (2) does not cover it, because an orphan attestation has no event — being unbound is the whole reason it is an orphan — so there is no `event_id` to take a lock on. Either the implementation serializes the check-and-record pair per `attestation_id`, or it makes the pair atomic by other means (a conditional insert on the marker, keyed on the `attestation_id`). The consequence of getting it wrong is bounded but real: duplicate compensation events are not false — each truthfully records that a compensation was recorded — but they inflate the trail and make *"was this orphan compensated once, or is the scan looping?"* a question an auditor has to answer by reading timestamps. This is the exact shape of obligation (2)'s compensating-retention pre-check, over the other relation and under the other key.
- **Durability across crashes.** The composition's emergent state is classified per element in Composition state, and the durability obligation follows the classification rather than the element list. `event_to_attestation`'s entries **for live events**, `event_to_retention`, `event_to_sequence`, `sealed_through`, `seal_coverage`'s ranges, and `compensated_attestations` are **derived indexes**: they sit outside the atomicity surface of [Record Action], and a crash that records the event in Event Log but loses a map entry is a **rebuild trigger, not a defect** — reads consult the index with rebuild-on-miss semantics, and the named rebuild procedures regenerate every entry from the constituent stores (the event payload carries `attestation_id`; the retention record's `record_ref` is the `event_id`; the returned event carries its own `event_id` and `sequence_number`; the seal's `record_set_ref` is the sequence-number range; `sealed_through` is the maximum of those ranges; and each live `audit.compensation` event's payload carries its subject-kind discriminator, the `subject = attestation` ones naming the `attestation_id` compensated). **`event_to_retention`'s rebuild-on-miss path is load-bearing beyond ordinary index hygiene**, and it is worth naming here: Invariant 2's compensating retention placement pre-checks that map before it places, so an implementation that read a lost entry as *absent* rather than rebuilding it would place a second retention over an event that already had one — which Retention Window's Invariant 5 would happily record as a new retention with a new id, quietly breaking Invariant 2's *exactly one* claim. `event_to_sequence` is worth naming separately here because losing it makes every id-addressed action miss rather than fail: [Read Record], [Verify Record], and [Purge Event] all resolve through it, so its rebuild-on-miss path is the hottest of the six and an implementation that treats a miss as `not-known` instead of rebuilding would report real events as fabricated ids. **`compensated_attestations` earns a line for the opposite reason — a lost value there is safe in the direction that matters.** A rebuild that comes back short makes the scan re-compensate an orphan it had already closed, which writes a duplicate audit event and never a false one; the failure mode it does *not* have is silently reporting an uncompensated orphan as closed, because nothing enters the set except a compensation event that was actually recorded. That asymmetry is why it can be an ordinary derived index rather than a durability obligation. An `event_id` that still has no attestation or no retention *after* rebuild is the real orphan, and it is Invariants 1 and 2's liveness arm that owns it, not this edge case. **Three facts carry a durability obligation, and they land on the same durable record.** [Purge Event] step 2's destruction record opens with two of them: `seal_coverage`'s `purged_events` membership, and `event_to_attestation`'s entry **for that now-purged event** as the captured `(event_id, attestation_id)` pair. Both are extraction-pending truth no rebuild reproduces, and for the same underlying reason — the cascade destroys the payload's recoverability, and the payload is what the rebuild would have read. Losing the membership loses the evidence that a particular sealed record was destroyed; losing the pair loses the traversal from a lawfully destroyed event to the attestation destroyed with it, which is the *who* half of the regulator's question for every purged record. They must persist with the cascade's write, and they must be written **before** step 3's delegation runs, since after it the source is gone. The third is **`erasure_outcomes`** — the mechanism's outcome record, which lands on the same destruction record when step 3's delegation returns. It is unreproducible for its own reason rather than the payload's: re-driving a delegation over already-destroyed content is not guaranteed to report `destroyed` again (the mechanism may only answer *target already unreadable in a way it cannot confirm*, a named `destruction-failed` reason — Configuration, `erasure_mechanism`), so a lost `destroyed` outcome can leave a completed cascade permanently unclosable by the reconciliation scan — which is why it is durable truth like the pair, and not the safe-to-lose marker `compensated_attestations` is. **The asymmetry inside `event_to_attestation` is the thing to hold onto:** the same map is a discardable, rebuildable index for a live event and durable, unreproducible truth for a purged one, and an implementation that treats the whole map uniformly gets one of the two halves wrong — either persisting what it could regenerate, or discarding what it cannot. Until the *Erasure Tombstone* *(forthcoming)* extraction lands and gives all three facts an atom with its own durability contract, the deployment owns persisting them. The implementor owns the transactional boundary for the truth-bearing writes; the spec assumes it.
- **Cross-store consistency under failure.** If `EventLog.append` succeeds but `RetentionWindow.place_under_retention` fails, the composition is in a state Invariant 2's safety arm forbids at quiescence (an event in the log without retention) and Event Log's append-only forecloses withdrawing the event. The implementation must order operations so that either all constituent calls succeed atomically or the composition surfaces the failure — returning `rejected(recording-failure)` and recording the partial state as a compliance finding — and reconciles it within the configured `compensation_window` (Configuration). Two-phase commit, saga compensation, or single-transaction storage all satisfy; the spec names the requirement, the implementation chooses the mechanism.

  **Where the compliance finding and the compensating write are recorded is not left open, and it is not an operational log.** Both are **audit events recorded through this composition's own [Record Action]** — the scan's findings under the composition-owned action reference `audit.reconciliation`, each compensating write under `audit.compensation`, attributed to the deployment's operator identity — the configured `reconciliation_operator` (Configuration) — with the implementation-owned scan acting as an ordinary caller supplying that `actor_ref` and its `credential`. This is the `execution-contract.md` rule that a composition needing to record that a multi-step sequence occurred does so **by composing Event Log** rather than growing a second store, discharged in the one way available to a composition that *is* an Event Log composition: it records its own coordination history through the same surface every other caller uses. The consequence is the point of it — a compensating write is attested, sequenced, retention-governed, and sealed like any other event, so *"the gap was found and closed"* is a first-class audit answer readable from the same traversal, rather than a claim an auditor has to take from a runbook or an operational system the audit has no reach into. It adds no action and no second store; the one Composition-state element it does add, `compensated_attestations`, is a derived index over these very compensation events (*Durability across crashes*). The reconciliation scan runs at restart and on the configured `reconciliation_cadence`. The derived indexes are explicitly *not* part of this obligation (see *Durability across crashes*).

  **The scan has three halves and each has a stated predicate; none is left to the implementation to invent.** The *unbound-attestation* half tests each attestation against the **binding set** — the `attestation_id`s carried in live event payloads, from a full open-upper-bound enumeration of the log, unioned with the `attestation_id`s in destruction records — and treats an attestation in neither as an orphan, then pre-checks `compensated_attestations` before writing anything, under per-`attestation_id` serialization (*Concurrency*, obligation 4). The *unretained-event* half enumerates the log through the same open-upper-bound read, tests each event against `event_to_retention` **rebuilding on miss first** (so a lost index entry is not misread as a missing retention and compensated into a second retention over an event that already had one), and places the compensating retention for a true miss under the per-`event_id` serialization of obligation (2) — placement first, then the `audit.compensation` record; the cascade-on-purge rule states the order and the recording-half residual — — it is the detector for the state [Record Action] step 7 leaves when step 4 fails after step 3, and no other surface sweeps for it, since [Purge Eligible] enumerates retentions and the other two halves enumerate *Purged* retentions and attestations respectively. Its auditor-side mirror is Generation-acceptance check 2. The *half-completed-cascade* half is the one below. The full specification of all three, and the reason the binding set needs both of its enumerations, is in the cascade-on-purge rule; the first mirrors Generation-acceptance check 7's live/purged split of `event_to_attestation`, read from the attestation side instead of the event side. **The cascade's own half-completed state needs a different trigger, and this is the trap:** a retention left in *Purged* whose `purged_events` membership or whose erasure-delegation record never landed is **invisible to [Purge Eligible]**, because that projection is false for a *Purged* retention — the ordinary sweep will never re-offer it, so the retry can never be driven off the eligible list the way a not-yet-purged event can. The implementation must run a **reconciliation scan** over *Purged* retentions, checking each for a matching `purged_events` membership, a matching `(event_id, attestation_id)` destruction-record pair, and a **successful (`destroyed`) mechanism outcome record** — presence alone does not close it, since a recorded `destruction-failed(reason)` is evidence the cascade did *not* complete (Configuration, `erasure_mechanism`) — and use its output as the retry trigger. It is exactly parallel to the orphan-attestation discipline (*Partial attestation on step failure*): a scan at restart and on the configured `reconciliation_cadence`, and any unreconciled entry surfaced as a **compliance alert** rather than quietly carried — an entry whose retention record says the content was lawfully destroyed while nothing evidences the destruction is precisely the gap this composition exists to make unhideable.
- **Verification of the unsealed tail.** Events in the tail (between `sealed_through` and the current append point) are not yet covered by a seal. [Verify Record] returns `failed-verification(unsealed)` for these under `unsealed_tail_mode = strict`, or continues to the remaining checks under `lenient` (where the deployment accepts Event Log's per-event immutability as sufficient until the next seal cadence). The choice is deployment policy, declared in Configuration rather than assumed. Two things put an event in the tail: the cadence has not fired yet, or a seal attempt failed. **A seal failure at [Record Action] step 6 does not reject the record** — the attestation, event, and retention all committed, and reporting a failure would misdescribe a record that exists and is fully attributed. The event stays in the tail, the underlying `TamperEvidence.seal` rejection is raised as a deployment alerting obligation naming its cause, and the next cadence firing retries the seal. A tail that stops draining is therefore an operational alarm, not a silent gap: the deployment is expected to monitor tail depth and age against the configured `seal_cadence`. **Which alarm it is matters, and the three of [Seal Now]'s four rejection arms that report a *failure* are what tell them apart** ([Nothing To Seal], the fourth, is not an alarm — it means there was nothing in the tail to seal)**:** [Mechanism Failure]'s carried `(reason)` splits its arm in two — an outage reason (hardware down, anchor service unreachable) is transient and the next firing may well clear it, while a preconditions reason (keying material the mechanism rejects — Tamper Evidence routes wrong-shape credentials here, not to `invalid-request`) is a **standing misconfiguration** every firing reproduces until Configuration changes; [Recording Failure] means the seal store refused the write and the next firing re-seals the same slice; `rejected(invalid-request)` — a whitespace-only slice reference or an absent credential, both built by the composition rather than any caller — is a standing construction or plumbing defect. A tail draining-alarm under a preconditions reason or under `invalid-request` is a page for a human, not something to wait out.
- **Failed attribution attempts.** A [Record Action] call rejected at step 2 (Actor Identity rejects the credential) leaves no trace in the audit log — the attempt is not recorded. Nor is a step-1 primitive-validation rejection. For high-assurance deployments where failed attribution attempts are themselves auditable events (an insider retrying with forged credentials, for example), a Failed-Attempt Log composing pattern *(forthcoming)* records the rejected attempt as its own event. This composition does not absorb that concept: its audit surface is committed actions, not attempted actions.
- **Partial attestation on step failure.** If step 2 (`ActorIdentity.attest`) succeeds but step 3 (`EventLog.append`) fails, an attestation record exists in the Actor Identity store with no corresponding event in the audit log — an orphan attestation. Actor Identity's records are immutable once committed and Invariant 9 forecloses deletion, so synchronous rollback is not available. The implementation must flag the orphan, return `rejected(recording-failure)` to the caller **in the same outcome**, and reconcile it within the configured `compensation_window` (Configuration) — a compensating record naming the unbound attestation, plus a reconciliation scan run at restart and on the configured `reconciliation_cadence` to catch the case where the process crashed before it could return. **The compensating record is itself an audit event**, recorded through [Record Action] under the composition-owned `audit.compensation` action reference and attributed to the deployment's operator identity, with the scan's own findings recorded under `audit.reconciliation` (the cascade-on-purge rule, *Where the compensation lands*) — so an unbound attestation and its resolution are both readable from the trail rather than only from an operations system. This is the liveness arm of Invariant 1: the orphan is reachable and durable, but never unsurfaced and never a steady state. **How the scan finds it, and how it knows when to stop, are both specified rather than left open:** it tests each attestation against the **binding set** — `attestation_id`s in live event payloads unioned with `attestation_id`s in destruction records — and treats one in neither as an orphan; and it pre-checks **`compensated_attestations`** (Composition state) before recording, so a given orphan produces exactly one compensation (the cascade-on-purge rule specifies both; *Concurrency* obligation 4 supplies the lock). **Reconciled therefore has an observable form: the orphan's `attestation_id` is in `compensated_attestations`** — which is the only form available, since the orphan itself never stops being one (Actor Identity Invariant 9 forecloses deleting it), so the marker and not the attestation store is what closes the finding. High-assurance deployments should treat any orphan not in that set, past the window, as a gap in the audit surface and alert accordingly. Step 1's full-payload size check is what keeps an ordinary oversized-payload caller error out of this path entirely.
- **Clock source for cadence and purge.** After the [Purge Eligible] delegation, the composition owns `now` in exactly **one** place: the `seal_cadence` timer. That `now` is a host-injected input read at the composition's I/O seam before the action's transition runs (per `execution-contract.md` §Logic Confinement), so the orchestration transition is a pure function of injected `now`. Purge eligibility is *not* evaluated here — [Purge Eligible] delegates to Retention Window's own read-time `purge_eligible` projection (Retention Window Invariant 11), so the eligibility clock is that atom's injected `now`, one reading per [Purge Eligible] call. **That reading is not shared with a later `RetentionWindow.purge`.** The projection and the purge are two independent readings at two moments — Retention Window's Invariant 8 single-reading discipline is scoped to *within* its own Purge action, where the eligibility guard and the `purged_at` stamp read the same value — and the gap between them is harmless because eligibility is **monotone in `now`**: once `now ≥ retention_until` holds it keeps holding under a non-decreasing clock, so nothing on the list can become ineligible before the cascade reaches it. The residual disagreement is the benign one Retention Window's own *Clock semantics* edge case names: two readings near the boundary may differ by one sweep, and no write is at stake in the projection. The authoritative source is deployment-shaped in both cases: the deployer configures the clock (system clock, GPS — Global Positioning System — disciplined clock, NTP — Network Time Protocol — synchronized cluster clock) and must ensure it is monotonically non-decreasing. Clock skew across nodes in a distributed deployment can cause non-deterministic eligibility results and inconsistent cadence firing; the deployer owns the monotonicity guarantee. For deployments composing a Trusted Timestamping pattern *(forthcoming)*, the TSA's anchored time may serve as the authoritative source; that composing pattern owns the clock-authority contract.

Where the composition breaks down: when the four constituent stores share an adversary with write access to all of them and external anchoring is absent; when the host environment cannot supply a stable, reproducibly-addressable record set at verify time (mutable event payloads under non-versioned references); when the retention policy and the integrity-coverage cadence are mismatched (events purged before their covering seal is verified against them); when the actor registry's historical public material is not retained and old attestations begin failing verification under a new key.

#### Record Action

The composition's core action: it validates the caller's primitives, attests the actor via Actor Identity, appends the event to the Event Log, places the event under Retention Window, links the two in the composition's derived indexes, and (under per-event cadence) fires a seal over **the range that firing actually cuts** — `[sealed_through + 1 .. tail]`, typically just this new event but wider whenever a prior seal failed and left the tail undrained. Returns the new `event_id`, or [Recording Failure] when a constituent store refuses — `recording-failure(step)`: at step 2 nothing is committed at all, at step 3 or 4 a partial state is left after attestation — or `invalid-credential` / `invalid-request` before anything is recorded — the latter covering an `action_ref` inside the **reserved `audit.*` namespace** from any caller whose `actor_ref` is not the configured `reconciliation_operator`, since only the composition's own reconciliation path may write under it. Which partial state a [Recording Failure] leaves depends on where the sequence stopped: an append failing after the attestation leaves an **orphan attestation**, a retention placement failing after the append leaves an **unretained event**, and the two are found by different halves of the reconciliation scan. A seal failure at the final step does not reject the call — the record exists and is attributed; the event stays in the unsealed tail and the seal retries.

Kind: Operation

#### Seal Now

The composition action that seals the current unsealed tail under interval or on-demand cadence, and that [Purge Event] step 0 invokes to cover a tail event before cascading over it — it delegates to Tamper Evidence's `seal` over the slice `[sealed_through + 1 .. tail]`, where `tail` is the highest `sequence_number` an open-upper-bound sequence-range read returns, records the coverage as a sequence-number range, and advances `sealed_through`. It cuts **new** coverage only: the composition has no surface that seals a range some seal already covers, which is what makes each range's seal the one seal over it for good. Returns the `evidence_id`, or one of four distinct refusals: [Nothing To Seal] when the tail is empty, [Mechanism Failure] carrying the mechanism's own reason when the mechanism could not perform, `invalid-request` when the mechanism refuses how it was asked (a deployment misconfiguration that every retry reproduces), and [Recording Failure] when the seal store would not persist the proof. Under all of them the events stay in the tail and the next cadence firing retries.

Kind: Operation

#### Read Record

The composition's consolidated read: given an `event_id`, it resolves the id to a `sequence_number` through `event_to_sequence`, then joins the event (its payload where content is still present), its attestation reference, its retention record (state and deadlines), and its seal-coverage status — including the covering seal's full range, whether this event's own sequence number is in that seal's `purged_events` set, and whether that seal is partly purged around a still-live event — into one [Audit Record], or returns `not-known` for an id no constituent knows. **For a purged event it reads the *who / what / when* from the attestation record rather than from the payload** — the cascade takes Event Log's `data` field in its entirety, so the payload's own `action_ref`, `actor_ref`, and `attestation_id` go with it, and the surviving route is the destruction record's `(event_id, attestation_id)` pair into the attestation store's still-readable fields. Returning the range is what tells a [Verify Record] caller what to present. A pure projection — it changes no state, produces no audit event, and carries no permissions layer at this composition; callers gate it. It presents; [Verify Record] proves.

Kind: Operation

#### Verify Record

The composition's four-way verification query: given an `event_id` and the re-presented record set the covering seal commits to, it checks the retention state first, then log presence, then the attestation (Actor Identity) and the covering seal (Tamper Evidence). Returns `verified`, a prefixed `failed-verification(reason)` (including `purged` for a lawfully destroyed record), [Unverifiable] when verification could not be performed, or `not-known` for a fabricated id. Any of the first three may additionally carry the qualifier `(compensation-window)` on a **separate channel** — it reports that the event is inside Invariant 2's compensation window with its retention side not yet landed, which is orthogonal to the integrity finding and is therefore neither a fourth outcome nor a reason folded under one. The record set must be re-presented: the rule is always *the payloads of every event in the covering seal's range*, which [Read Record] returns, and the cadence moves only that range's extent. The retention check running first is also what keeps a purged event's attestation out of the Actor Identity call: the cascade destroyed that attestation's `proof`, so `verify` could only fail on it, and the `purged` outcome anticipates exactly that.

Kind: Operation

#### Purge Eligible

The derived read query returning the list of `event_id`s eligible for the cascade. It does not compute eligibility itself: it delegates to Retention Window's own read-time `purge_eligible` projection and maps each eligible retention back to its event. **The name is shared with the projection it delegates to, so keep the two apart:** *this* card is the composition's own action over `event_id`s, which is what a caller invokes and what the nightly sweep drives the cascade from; `RetentionWindow.purge_eligible` is the constituent projection over `retention_id`s that actually evaluates `now ≥ retention_until`, and it is the atom's Invariant 11 — not this composition — that guarantees eligibility is derived at read time and never stored. This action adds the id mapping and nothing else; it evaluates no clock of its own and has no rejection arm.

Kind: Operation

#### Purge Event

The composition action that coordinates the cascade for a retention-elapsed event: it first ensures the event is sealed (invoking [Seal Now] where the event is still in the unsealed tail, since the destruction record has to live on a covering entry), then requires the event's retention to be resolved at all — an event with a log entry but no retention record is in Invariant 2's compensation window, and the action returns [Retention Unresolved] rather than cascading over a lifetime nothing ever placed — then purges the retention record (the one constituent purge surface, and even that one deletes nothing — `purge` is a state transition and the record survives in *Purged*), writes a destruction record adding the event's `sequence_number` to the covering `seal_coverage` entry's `purged_events` set and capturing that event's `(event_id, attestation_id)` binding before the delegation destroys it, and delegates destruction of **Event Log's `data` field in its entirety** (the whole appended `{action_ref, actor_ref, attestation_id, data}` object, not merely the caller's `data` sub-field) and of the attestation's `proof` to the deployment's declared **shredding-class** `erasure_mechanism` — a class boundary argued in full in the cascade-on-purge rule, *Boundary one*, and cited rather than restated here. The mechanism reports `destroyed` or `destruction-failed(reason)`, and the cascade branches on which: a reported failure is [Cascade Failure] as `cascade-failure(step-3)`, leaving retention *Purged* over readable content and re-driven by the reconciliation scan until a `destroyed` outcome lands. The attestation's `attestation_id`, `action_ref`, `actor_ref`, and `attested_at` survive readable, so the regulator's *who / what / when* outlives the purge — read from the attestation store through the destruction record's pair, since the payload's copies went with the field. It destroys no constituent content itself, disposes of no seal, and re-seals nothing: the partly-purged seal stays the seal over its range and its live members answer `unverifiable(partially-purged-coverage)` thereafter. Returns `ok`, `not-known`, [Not Eligible] when retention has not elapsed, [Retention Unresolved] when there is no retention record to purge, or [Cascade Failure] carrying the step at which a store refused (including `seal` for the precondition seal); where a Legal Hold is composed, it additionally surfaces [Under Legal Hold].

Kind: Operation

#### Audit Record

The composition's emergent output, produced by [Read Record]: the single consolidated structure the four atoms together present for one event — the event bound to its attestation (who), its retention (how long), and its seal coverage (integrity) — observable, attributable, tamper-evident, and lifetime-bounded. The one structure the regulator actually queries; no constituent presents it alone.

Kind: Type

#### Recording Failure

The composition's own rejection for *a store refused the write* — returned from [Record Action] on any constituent `storage-failure`, carrying the step that refused (`recording-failure(step)`): at step 2 the attestation store itself refused and **nothing was committed at all** — no attestation exists and there is nothing to compensate; at step 3 or step 4 a store failed **after** the actor was attested, and the implementation must resolve the resulting partial state — an orphan attestation or an unretained event, per the step carried (Edge cases). Also returned from [Seal Now] when Tamper Evidence computed a proof but the **seal store** would not persist it, in which case nothing landed, `sealed_through` does not advance, and the same slice is re-sealed at the next cadence firing. The common thread is what distinguishes it from [Mechanism Failure]: the surface that failed is a *store*, not a mechanism.

Kind:      Member
Member of: the record-action and seal rejections
Role:      Rejection
Projects:  recording-failure

#### Nothing To Seal

The composition's own rejection from [Seal Now] — returned when the unsealed tail is empty (no events since `sealed_through`).

Kind:      Member
Member of: the seal rejection
Role:      Rejection
Projects:  nothing-to-seal

#### Mechanism Failure

The composition's own rejection from [Seal Now] — returned when the underlying Tamper Evidence seal mechanism **could not compute a proof**, **carrying the constituent's own `(reason)` through unchanged** so the operator reads the cause rather than an unnamed failure. The carried reason splits the arm's two worlds: a transient outage (signing hardware down, TSA unreachable under anchored mode, HSM session lost), which the next cadence firing may clear, and a standing preconditions failure (keying material of the wrong shape for the mechanism actually running — Tamper Evidence routes wrong-shape credentials *here*, not to `invalid-request`), which every firing reproduces until Configuration changes. What it is *not* is the other two arms: a request malformed at the atom's own guard — a whitespace-only slice reference or an **absent** credential, both the composition's own construction — surfaces as `invalid-request`; a **store** that will not persist a computed proof surfaces as [Recording Failure]. Under every occurrence no coverage entry is written and `sealed_through` does not move, so the failure is a no-op rather than a gap; where the seal was [Purge Event]'s step-0 precondition, the cascade rejects `cascade-failure(seal)` and nothing else changes either.

Kind:      Member
Member of: the seal rejection
Role:      Rejection
Projects:  mechanism-failure

#### Cascade Failure

The composition's own rejection from [Purge Event] — returned when a store or the seal mechanism refuses mid-cascade, and **carrying the step at which it refused**, because the halves leave materially different worlds. `cascade-failure(seal)`: the step-0 precondition seal did not land, so the event is still in the unsealed tail with nowhere to record a destruction — **nothing changed anywhere**, the retention is still *Retained*, and the event stays on [Purge Eligible]'s list while the operator reads [Seal Now]'s own three-way diagnosis. `cascade-failure(step-1)`: `RetentionWindow.purge` failed, **nothing changed anywhere**, the retention is still *Retained*, and the event stays on [Purge Eligible]'s list, so an ordinary retry is the whole remedy. `cascade-failure(step-2)` or `(step-3)`: the retention already moved to *Purged* and then the destruction-record write or the erasure delegation failed — where *the delegation failed* covers both the delegation that could not be issued and the one the mechanism answered with `destruction-failed(reason)`, since both leave the same world — **retention Purged, cascade incomplete**, which is the dangerous half, because the record now says the content was lawfully destroyed while the evidence of that destruction is missing or the content is still readable. That state is invisible to [Purge Eligible] (whose projection is false for a *Purged* retention), so it can never be retried off the eligible list: **the reconciliation scan is its retry trigger**, and until the scan closes it the entry stands as a compliance alert bounded by the `compensation_window`. Naming the step is what tells an operator which of the two remedies applies.

Kind:      Member
Member of: the purge rejection
Role:      Rejection
Projects:  cascade-failure

#### Not Eligible

The composition's own rejection from [Purge Event] — returned when the event's retention has not elapsed (`now < retention_until`), so the no-early-purge gate refuses the cascade.

Kind:      Member
Member of: the purge rejection
Role:      Rejection
Projects:  not-eligible

#### Retention Unresolved

The composition's own rejection from [Purge Event] — returned at step 0½ when the `event_id` resolves to a **log entry but to no retention record at all**. That is the compensation-window state Invariant 2's liveness arm admits: an appended event whose retention placement has not landed yet. There is nothing for the cascade to purge — `RetentionWindow.purge` takes a `retention_id` and none exists — and placing one here in order to expire it would be the composition asserting a lifetime no policy ever set. **The remedy is the reconciliation path, not a retry**: the compensating `place_under_retention` must land first, after which the event is purgeable, or not, on the same terms as any other. No step of the cascade proper runs; only step 0's precondition seal may already have landed, which is not undone and destroys nothing. It is distinct from `not-known` (which means *no constituent has heard of this id* and is false here — the log entry proves the event is real) and from [Not Eligible] (which means *a retention exists and its period has not elapsed*).

Kind:      Member
Member of: the purge rejection
Role:      Rejection
Projects:  retention-unresolved

#### Under Legal Hold

The **conditional** rejection from [Purge Event] — present in the action's contract exactly when the deployment composes a [Legal Hold](../atoms/legal-hold.md) pattern, and absent otherwise. Returned when a preservation order over the record intercepts the cascade before its first step, so no step runs: the retention stays *Retained*, nothing joins the covering seal's `purged_events` set, and no erasure delegation is issued. Legal Hold owns the gate; this composition names where the gate lands.

Kind:      Member
Member of: the purge rejection
Role:      Rejection
Projects:  under-legal-hold

#### Unverifiable

The composition's own [Verify Record] outcome for *verification could not be performed*. Three reasons carry on it. Two are availability: a constituent verification surface is unavailable — Actor Identity's `registry-unavailable`, Tamper Evidence's `mechanism-verification-unavailable` — and the correct response is to retry when the surface returns. The third is `partially-purged-coverage`, returned for a **live** event whose covering seal has had *other* members lawfully destroyed: the record set the seal commits to can no longer be re-presented, so no integrity claim over that event is obtainable from that seal. It is the one reason here that is not transient — the canonical composition has no re-sealing surface, so it is the standing answer for the rest of that event's retained lifetime, and it resolves only if the deployment composes the forthcoming Seal Lifecycle pattern (Edge cases). All three share this arm rather than being reasons under `failed-verification(...)` for one reason: *nothing is known to be wrong with the record*. Reporting a registry outage — or the composition's own lawful cascade — as a failed record would manufacture a finding against a trail that is, as far as anyone can tell, intact.

Member of: the verify-record outcome
Role:      Outcome
Projects:  unverifiable
