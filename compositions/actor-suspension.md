## Summary

Actor Suspension is a regulated composition — a specification that wires several freestanding patterns together — that does one job thoroughly: when you need to cut off an actor (a departing employee, a hacked account, a contractor who's done), it closes *every* door at once and leaves proof.

The problem it solves is the classic security gap where someone's access is pulled in one place but not another — their login is disabled but a permission grant still works, or their role is removed but an open session keeps going. This composition makes that gap visible and short-lived by treating suspension as one ordered, completed act: it records what it is about to close, finds every active permission grant the actor holds and every active login session they have, revokes each of them, marks the actor as suspended, and writes one tamper-proof audit record that lists every grant and session it revoked.

If any part of that can't complete, nothing is undone — a revoked grant or session cannot be un-revoked — but nothing is hidden either: the actor is marked as *suspending*, the record shows exactly which doors are still open, and a mandated completion sweep closes them within a declared window. A half-suspended actor is dangerous only when everyone wrongly believes they are locked out; this composition never lets the records say that. Optionally, it also revokes the actor's login credential so they can't sign back in.

It is the exact mirror of the Login pattern: Login wires up "check the credential, then start a session" on the way in; this wires up "suspend the actor, then revoke every grant and every session" on the way out. The composition's defining emergent guarantee — a property that exists only when the patterns are combined — is that single multi-surface revocation: after a successful suspension, the actor provably holds zero active grants and zero active sessions, and the audit record names every one that was revoked. Its common uses are exactly the moments compliance regimes care about most: offboarding a terminated employee (the access must be removed promptly and provably), responding to a compromised account, and ending a third-party's access. Any system that must deactivate an actor across multiple access surfaces at once — and prove from the records that nothing was missed — is a candidate for this composition.

## Intent

Every system that distinguishes actors eventually has to *un*-distinguish one. An employee leaves; a credential is found in a paste dump; a contractor's engagement ends; an account shows the signature of a takeover. The obligation that follows is deceptively simple to state and notoriously easy to botch: cut off *every* way that actor can still act, *now*, and be able to prove afterward that you did. The botch is always the same shape — access is revoked on one surface but not another. The session cookie is invalidated but the API token's permission grant is still active; the role is removed but a long-lived session keeps working; the login credential is disabled but a service grant the actor still holds quietly authorizes a scheduled job. Each gap is a half-open door, and a half-suspended actor is more dangerous than an un-suspended one, because the system and its operators believe the actor is locked out.

This is the friction this composition exists to resolve, and naming it precisely keeps the composition honest: **this composition is not an "account deactivation feature"; it is a multi-surface de-authorization, ordered across durability boundaries and completed rather than undone, with a records-alone completeness proof.** An actor's ability to act is spread across two independent authorization surfaces that no single atom relates: **Permissions** holds the grants that say *what the actor may do*, and **Session** holds the live authenticated channels that say *the actor is currently acting*. Revoking one without the other leaves a door open. Worse, each surface has its own multiplicity — an actor may hold many grants (issued at different times, by different grantors) and many sessions (one per device) — so closure on each surface is itself an *enumerate-then-revoke-each* operation, exactly the mass-revocation pattern Permissions names as the composing system's responsibility (*"enumerate all active grants where `grant.subject_ref` = departing_subject, then call `revoke(grant_id)` for each"*) and Session supports through its `principal_ref`-queryable store (*"revoke all sessions for this account"*). The structure that snapshots both surfaces, revokes every member of both, sets the actor's suspension state, and seals the whole act as one attributed, tamper-evident record belongs to no single constituent. It belongs to the composition, and this composition is that structure.

this composition is Login's mirror image, and the symmetry is load-bearing. Login is the *inbound* wiring: `Credential.verify → Session.issue`, with the emergent **cascade** that revoking a credential terminates the derived sessions. This composition is the *outbound* wiring: `suspend → revoke every grant + revoke every session`, with the emergent guarantee that suspension closes *all* surfaces at once. Login's cascade walks an active-session set under a snapshot and tolerates partial completion (a per-session storage failure is logged and the cascade continues — partial revocation beats none). This composition makes a stronger choice than Login's, and the difference is deliberate: a suspension that half-completes leaves an ambiguous security state, so this composition never leaves one *ambiguous* — an earlier revision promised that by rolling the whole cascade back under one transaction, which no constituent could honor (a revoked grant, session, or credential is terminal by its own atom's invariant, and the substrate's audit append cannot be withdrawn), so what it promises now is **completion**: the intent is recorded before anything is revoked, every revocation that commits stands, an actor whose cascade stopped is visibly `Suspending` with the open doors named, and a mandated sweep closes them within a declared window. The same TOCTOU (time-of-check-to-time-of-use) race Login surfaced as finding Final Critique 1 in its Final Critique 4 — a concurrent terminal transition during the cascade — recurs here and is resolved the same way: a grant or session the cascade targets that another process already revoked is found already-terminal and counted, not re-revoked.

This is a composition, not a new primitive. Actor Identity, Permissions, Session, and Audit Trail are unchanged; this composition is the wiring that makes them coherent as one suspension surface. It introduces emergent actions — [Suspend Actor], the read-only [Suspension Report], and the thin [Reinstate Actor] — and an emergent state machine the constituents do not carry: the actor's **Active → Suspended** lifecycle. Actor Identity is the attestation atom, with a single `Attested` state and *no* actor lifecycle (it explicitly defers actor registration, deactivation, and suspension to a forthcoming Actor Registry / Identity Provisioning pattern); the Active/Suspended state is therefore a **composition-introduced surface** This composition owns, not a state read from any constituent.

What the composition is *not*: it is not the issuance surface — it revokes grants and sessions but never issues them (`Permissions.grant` and `Session.issue` are called elsewhere), so it cannot, by itself, prevent a *new* grant or session being created for a suspended actor after the cascade snapshot (a named composing-layer obligation, exactly as Login's cascade is snapshot-scoped); it is not the actor registry that provisions actor identities; it is not an authorization-policy engine deciding *whether* an actor should be suspended (that is wired ahead of [Suspend Actor] at the administrative / Multi-Party Approval layer); and it does not restore access on reinstatement — [Reinstate Actor] lifts the suspension state but never un-revokes a terminal grant or session (re-provisioning is the issuance layer's job). Each is named explicitly in Edge cases.

## Composes

- **[Actor Identity](../atoms/actor-identity.md)** — the identity of the actor being suspended. This composition introduces the actor's **Active → Suspended** lifecycle as a composition-introduced state machine, because Actor Identity carries no lifecycle state of its own (a single `Attested` state, with actor registration / deactivation / suspension explicitly deferred to a forthcoming Actor Registry / Identity Provisioning pattern). The `actor_ref` is the suspension subject and the join key across the two authorization surfaces. This composition does **not** mutate any attestation (they are immutable — Actor Identity Invariant 1); the attestation surface enters via the Audit Trail substrate, which attests the `actor.suspended` event under the *suspending* operator's credential.

- **[Permissions](../atoms/permissions.md)** — the grant surface, used in **both directions**. *Read:* [Suspend Actor] enumerates the actor's active grants by querying the grant store for `{grant_id}` where `subject_ref = actor_ref` and `status = Active` (the grant store is queryable; Permissions' Behavior section exposes per-grant fields to composing systems). *Write:* the cascade calls `Permissions.revoke(grant_id) → ok | rejected(not-known | not-active | storage-failure)` for **each** enumerated grant — Permissions provides no bulk-revoke surface, and naming the per-grant enumerate-then-revoke as the composing system's responsibility is Permissions' own *Mass revocation on subject deprovisioning* edge case. This composition is that composing system. A `revoke` reaching an already-`not-active` grant (a concurrent revocation) is the benign TOCTOU case (below), not an error.

- **[Session](../atoms/session.md)** — the live-channel surface, used in **both directions**. *Read:* [Suspend Actor] enumerates the actor's active sessions by querying the session store for `{session_token}` where `principal_ref = actor_ref` and `status = Active` (Session's store is `principal_ref`-queryable precisely to support *"show all active sessions for this account"* and *"revoke all sessions for this account"* administrative operations). *Write:* the cascade calls `Session.revoke(session_token, revoked_by_ref = suspended_by_ref, reason) → revoked | rejected(invalid-request | already-terminal | not-known | storage-failure)` for **each** active session. A session that became terminal between the snapshot and the revoke returns `already-terminal` — the benign TOCTOU case, counted as skipped exactly as Login's Final Critique 4 (finding Final Critique 1) handles it.

- **[Audit Trail](./audit-trail.md)** — the regulated-audit **substrate**: this composition names it the way an atom is named and reaches **Event Log, Actor Identity, Tamper Evidence, and Retention Window transitively through it** (per the *Compositions of compositions* convention — see [`spec-format.md`](../spec-format.md)), maintaining one Audit Trail instance for all suspension events. This composition records its events by calling **`AuditTrail.record_action(action_ref, actor_ref, credential, data) → event_id | rejected(invalid-credential | invalid-request | recording-failure(step))` directly on the one Audit Trail instance the substrate carries** — the `(step)` payload carried on every transcription, since the substrate's `step-4` arm means the event is already appended (Action wiring) — the established substrate-composition pattern (Multi-Party Approval and Resolve a Person's Data Rights record their own events on the Audit Trail their substrate carries). The capability is Audit Trail's own declared `record_action` and its Invariant 1 (attribution coverage), reached through the named substrate — not an ambient reach-through. The event's `data` carries the **complete enumeration** of every revoked `grant_id` and `session_token` (Invariant 2). The substrate seals the event (Tamper Evidence) and governs its retention (Retention Window) so the suspension record outlives the access it closed.

- **[Credential](../atoms/credential.md)** *(optional fifth constituent)* — when composed, the cascade additionally calls `Credential.revoke(credential_id, revoked_by_ref = suspended_by_ref, reason) → revoked | rejected(...)` on the actor's authentication credential so the suspended actor cannot re-authenticate to establish a *new* session. This closes the re-entry door the grant/session revocation alone leaves open (revoking current sessions does not stop a new login). When Credential is **not** composed, closing that door is a named composing obligation (the deployment revokes the credential through Login's surface, or wires this composition with Credential). Including Credential makes this composition the full outbound counterpart to Login's inbound `Credential.verify → Session.issue`.

The Permissions grant store, the Session store, and (when composed) the Credential store are owned by their respective constituent instances; the Event Log, Actor Identity attestation store, Tamper Evidence seal store, and Retention Window store are owned by the Audit Trail substrate. This composition owns no constituent state — it owns the emergent `actor_suspension_state` map, a derived index over its own intent and outcome events inside the audit horizon and truth-bearing past it (Composition state), and the `suspension_log`.

A surface this composition needs that crosses the two authorization surfaces is the **unified actor namespace**: the `actor_ref` must be the same identity under which the actor holds Permissions grants (as `subject_ref`) and Session sessions (as `principal_ref`). This unification is the precise concept the [Authenticated Actor](./authenticated-actor.md) composition establishes as a `principal_ref ↔ actor_ref` bijection, and the precise gap `demos/attributed-permissions-admin/CORNERS.md` §Cross-atom identity surface aliasing flags when it diverges. This composition names the unification as a deployment precondition (Configuration) — satisfied by Authenticated Actor as a named composing peer or by deployment convention — rather than composing Authenticated Actor directly; the enumeration's correctness is conditional on it, an externally-clearable check (Generation acceptance).

## Composition logic

### Composition state

The composition owns emergent state that wires the constituents into one suspension surface. None of this state belongs to a single constituent.

- **`actor_suspension_state`** — map from `actor_ref` to `{state, invocation_id?, intent_event_id?, suspended_at?, suspended_by_ref?, reason?, suspension_event_id?}` where `state ∈ {Active, Suspending, Suspended}`. An `actor_ref` absent from the map is treated as **Active** (the default — no suspension has been recorded). `Suspending` is the declared in-cascade state: the [Suspend Actor] intent event has landed and its outcome event has not, so some of the actor's enumerated access may be closed and some may not; it is a gate state exactly as `Suspended` is (Invariant 3 — the issuance layer refuses both), and it is never silent, since [Suspension Report] shows it with the open members named. This is the composition-introduced actor lifecycle the constituents do not carry. The `suspension_event_id` binds the Suspended state to the sealed `actor.suspended` Audit Trail event that enumerates what was revoked. **Contract classification: derived index** over this composition's own audit events, per [`execution-contract.md`](../execution-contract.md) §Composition state — *rebuild procedure:* enumerate the substrate through its declared open-upper-bound sequence-range read, filter in composition code to this composition's `action_ref`s, and for each `actor_ref` take the latest of: an `actor.suspension_intended` with no `actor.suspended` naming its `invocation_id` → `Suspending` (with the intent's `invocation_id`, `intent_event_id`, operator and reason); an `actor.suspended` → `Suspended` (with its `event_id`); an `actor.reinstated` later than both → `Active`. The three Contract obligations attach — outside every atomicity surface, rebuild-on-miss, no consistency claim. **The classification splits at the audit horizon:** past it the payloads the rebuild reads are destroyed, the entry is the only surviving carrier of the actor's lifecycle state, and it is **truth-bearing** under a durability obligation (Configuration, `state_durability`) — **extraction-pending** against the forthcoming Actor Registry / Identity Provisioning atom that Actor Identity itself names as the home of the actor lifecycle this composition introduces.

- **`suspension_log`** — append-only record of every [Suspend Actor] and [Reinstate Actor] call, whether it transitioned, was a no-op, or failed. Each entry: `entry_id` (opaque, system-generated), `invocation_id`, `actor_ref`, `operation` (`suspend` | `reinstate`), [Outcome] (`suspended` | `reinstated` | `already-suspended` | `already-active` | `revocation-failure(surface)` | `recording-failure` | `invalid-request`), [Revoked Grants] (the `grant_id` set, on success), [Revoked Sessions] (the `session_token` set, on success), `suspension_event_id?`, `attempted_at`. This is the composition's composition-layer query surface; the **Audit Trail substrate is the tamper-evident external-auditor record** of the same suspension events. **Contract classification, in two halves.** The entries that mirror an audit event (`suspended`, `reinstated`, and a `revocation-failure` or `recording-failure` whose intent event landed) are a **derived index** over those events — the same enumerate-and-filter rebuild as above, one entry per intent or outcome event. The entries no constituent replays — `already-suspended`, `already-active`, `invalid-request`, and a refusal that stopped before its intent record — are **extraction-pending** against the **Failed-Attempt Log** *(forthcoming)* pattern the substrate names for attempted-but-not-committed actions; until it lands, they are held here under the same `state_durability` obligation, and Generation acceptance check 3 reads them as evidence of refusals, never as evidence of acts. There is no composition-owned middle: every entry is one of those two things.

The actor's grants live in the Permissions store, its sessions in the Session store, its (optional) credential in the Credential store, and the suspension events in the Audit Trail substrate. This composition duplicates none of them; it transitions the suspension state and binds each suspension to the sealed event that records the revoked set.

The **enumerated active set** for an `actor_ref` — its active grants and active sessions — is not stored as standing state; it is computed at suspension time by querying the Permissions and Session stores. The `actor.suspension_intended` event carries that set as the **plan**, and the sealed `actor.suspended` event's enumerated `{revoked_grants, revoked_sessions}` *is* the durable, records-alone record of what the cascade closed — the two are separately recorded because their divergence is diagnostic: a planned member the outcome does not name is an owed revocation, and an outcome member the plan did not name is a conformance failure.

### Configuration

- **`unified_actor_namespace`** — a deployment-declared precondition (default `enforced`) asserting that the `actor_ref` this composition suspends is the same identity value under which the actor holds Permissions grants (as `subject_ref`) and Session sessions (as `principal_ref`). This is the **declaring source** (capability provenance) for the enumeration's correctness: this composition enumerates `subject_ref = actor_ref` grants and `principal_ref = actor_ref` sessions, which is complete *only* if the namespaces coincide. A deployment satisfies this via the [Authenticated Actor](./authenticated-actor.md) `principal_ref ↔ actor_ref` binding (a named composing peer) or by convention; whether the namespaces actually coincide is an externally-clearable check (Generation acceptance). A deployment whose namespaces diverge under-enumerates and produces an incomplete suspension — the audit-gap the CORNERS finding flags.
- **`revoke_credential_on_suspend`** — whether the cascade also revokes the actor's authentication credential (requires the optional Credential constituent). Default `true` when Credential is composed; absent otherwise. When `false` or Credential is uncomposed, closing the re-authentication door is a named composing obligation (Edge cases — *Re-authentication after suspension*).
- **`audit_trail_retention_policy`** — *inherited, not re-configured.* this composition records its `actor.suspended` / `actor.reinstated` events on the Audit Trail substrate, which carries the host's regulatory retention policy; `record_action` takes the policy via the substrate's configured value. The suspension events should persist at least as long as the longest retention obligation over the access they closed — often longer, for post-offboarding dispute defensibility (the access is gone; the proof it was lawfully closed must outlive it).
- **`application_actor_ref` and `application_credential`** — the composition's **service identity**: a registered actor in the substrate's Actor Identity registry under which the completion sweep attests every record it writes and performs every revocation it makes outside the invocation that owed them — the `actor.recovery_intended` record, the completing `Permissions.revoke` / `Session.revoke` / `Credential.revoke` calls (as `revoked_by_ref`, with the original operator and `intent_event_id` in the `reason`), and the compensating `actor.suspended` event, which carries the operator as `suspended_by`. The operator's credential is not in hand when the sweep runs, and attesting under it would be a false attribution. Every write an action makes inside its own invocation is attested by the calling operator; only the sweep uses this identity.
- **`suspension_completion_bound`** — the deployment-declared maximum duration of a [Suspend Actor] invocation between its intent record and its outcome record, read against the seam-injected `now`. It is the **lower edge** of the completion sweep: a `Suspending` actor whose intent is younger than it may belong to an invocation still revoking, and a sweep that completed it then would revoke beside the invocation and append a second outcome; the sweep examines nothing younger. The **upper edge** is `audit_trail_retention_policy`'s horizon: past it the intent event — the plan — is destroyed, and a `Suspending` entry there is the truth-bearing half of the index, completed from the live stores rather than from a plan the sweep can no longer read. *Default:* none.
- **`completion_window`** — the deployment-declared duration within which a `Suspending` actor must reach `Suspended` — every planned member closed and the outcome event landed — or be escalated as an unresolved compliance finding. Invariant 1's **liveness arm is a claim about this window and is unstatable without it**; it is the declaring source for the window Invariant 1 and Generation acceptance check 6 spend. No default is prescribed: under the offboarding regimes this composition serves, an actor half-closed for longer than the window is a control failure, and how long is a compliance determination.
- **`reconciliation_cadence`** — the interval at which the completion sweep the *Cross-store consistency under partial failure* edge case mandates is run, in addition to its mandatory run at process restart. Cadence bounds *detection*, the window bounds *repair*, and a cadence longer than the window makes the window unmeetable by construction.
- **`state_durability`** — the durability the deployment owes the two composition stores, stated as an ordering: **at least as durable as the Permissions and Session stores they gate**. Inside the audit horizon `actor_suspension_state` is derived and a lost entry is a rebuild trigger; past it an entry is the only carrier of the actor's lifecycle state, and a lost entry there is a finding — an actor the issuance gate reads as Active who was suspended — rather than a miss to repair. The `suspension_log`'s refusal entries are held under the same obligation until the Failed-Attempt Log pattern owns them.

### Primitive policies

- **`actor_ref`** — opaque reference to the actor being suspended; the key of `actor_suspension_state` and the join key (`= subject_ref = principal_ref` under `unified_actor_namespace`). Must contain at least one non-whitespace character (`invalid-request`). Byte-identity equality; never normalized.
- **`suspended_by_ref`** — non-empty reference to the operator performing the suspension; flows to `Session.revoke` (as `revoked_by_ref`), `Credential.revoke` (as `revoked_by_ref`), and `AuditTrail.record_action` (as `actor_ref`), so the records attribute the suspension to one operator. Whether `suspended_by_ref` is *authorized* to suspend actors is a named composing concept (Edge cases — *Suspension authorization*), exactly as Login leaves logout authorization to a composing layer.
- **`credential`** — the suspending operator's opaque credential, consumed by `AuditTrail.record_action` (and the Actor Identity reached through the substrate) — first at the intent record, which is where it is verified before anything irreversible happens (Invariant 5), and again at the outcome record; never persisted by this composition.
- **`invocation_id`** — one fresh identifier per state-changing invocation, injected at the composition's I/O seam alongside `now` (the Logic Confinement Principle — minted nowhere inside a transition) and written into every audit event, every `actor_suspension_state` entry, and every `suspension_log` entry the invocation produces. It is the key the completion sweep pairs an intent to its outcome by, and the key its pre-check reads the trail with, so a stale index never causes a second outcome event or a second revocation.
- **`reason`** — non-empty string naming why the actor is suspended; carried into each `Session.revoke` / `Credential.revoke` reason and the `actor.suspended` event. Mandatory (a suspension without a stated reason is a compliance finding — Permissions, Session, and Credential each require a non-empty reason on revoke).
- **`grant_id`** / **`session_token`** / **`credential_id`** — opaque constituent handles surfaced by the enumeration; passed to the respective `revoke`. Opaque; never normalized.

No primitive is case-sensitivity-normalized at the composition layer; deployments wanting normalization wire it at the calling layer before invoking composition actions.

### Action wiring

The composition exposes three actions: one emergent multi-surface suspension ([Suspend Actor]), one read-only query ([Suspension Report]), and one thin lifecycle-reversal ([Reinstate Actor]).

**Uniform constituent-rejection mapping, and the one ordering every action follows.** No audit write on this page is inside any transaction, and no constituent revocation is undone once it commits: [Suspend Actor] runs **durable intent → irreversible domain mutations → durable outcome → derived index**. A `Permissions.revoke → not-active` or `Session.revoke → already-terminal` is **benign** — the target is already terminal, which is the cascade's goal (the TOCTOU already-terminal case), counted as skipped, never a failure. A non-benign revoke failure (`storage-failure`, or a `not-known` on a member the snapshot returned) does not stop the cascade and undoes nothing: the cascade continues over the remaining members, the failed member is named, and the actor stays `Suspending` for the completion sweep. The substrate's taxonomy is `invalid-credential | invalid-request | recording-failure(step)`, mapped per step, and **the `(step)` payload is load-bearing**: `recording-failure(step-4)` means the event is appended and attested with only its retention placement failed — the invocation reads the event's id back by the declared enumerate-and-filter read selecting this `invocation_id`, and proceeds as landed with a hard alert; `recording-failure(step-2 | step-3)` means the event is not in the log. `invalid-request` is a deployment fault — a mis-derived payload cap, or the substrate's own retention-configuration source, on which the event **is** appended — and the same read-back decides which.

#### `suspend_actor`

```
suspend_actor(actor_ref, suspended_by_ref, credential, reason) →
  {suspended, revoked_grants, revoked_sessions, revoked_credential?, event_id}
 | rejected(
   invalid-request
  | invalid-credential
  | already-suspended
  | revocation-failure(surface, open_members)
  | recording-failure
  )
```

Suspends an actor by recording the plan, revoking every active Permissions grant and every active Session (and, optionally, the actor's Credential) one by one, transitioning the actor to Suspended, and sealing the complete revoked set into one attributed Audit Trail event. Steps:

1. Validate: `actor_ref`, `suspended_by_ref`, `credential`, `reason` each non-empty. Any violation → append `suspension_log` `{operation: suspend, outcome: invalid-request}`; return `rejected(invalid-request)`. Stop.
2. **Suspension-state gate (the cascade ordering, Invariant 3).** Read `actor_suspension_state[actor_ref]` (rebuild-on-miss). `Suspended` → append `suspension_log` `{outcome: already-suspended}`; return [Already Suspended] and change no state — the cascade does **not** re-run. `Suspending` → the actor's earlier cascade did not complete: this call **resumes** it rather than starting a second — skip to step 6 with the members the records show still open (the intent's plan less the outcome's absence; re-snapshot for the `Active` members among them), under the earlier invocation's `intent_event_id`, and write no second intent; a resume is what a retry after `revocation-failure` or a crash lawfully is. An `actor_ref` absent from the map is **Active** and proceeds.
3. **Snapshot the active set (read-only).** Enumerate the actor's active grants `G = {grant_id : subject_ref = actor_ref ∧ status = Active}` from the Permissions store and active sessions `S = {session_token : principal_ref = actor_ref ∧ status = Active}` from the Session store, and (where Credential is composed and `revoke_credential_on_suspend`) the actor's active `credential_id`. The snapshot and steps 4–8 run under one **per-`actor_ref` serialization** (*Concurrency*) — not a transaction: nothing below is undone by anything. A grant or session created *after* the snapshot is not in `G`/`S` and is not covered by this suspension (snapshot-scoped — Invariant 1's stated bound, and the *Suspension is snapshot-scoped* edge case). An empty `G` and `S` is a valid suspension.
4. **Intent record — the plan, and the credential gate.** `AuditTrail.record_action(action_ref = actor.suspension_intended, actor_ref = suspended_by_ref, credential, data = {invocation_id, suspended_actor: actor_ref, planned_grants: G, planned_sessions: S, planned_credential?, reason, intended_at = now})` → `intent_event_id`. This is where the substrate verifies the operator's credential against the actor registry — **before any revocation**, so an unverified caller never closes anyone's access (Invariant 5). Nothing has committed, so every arm is a clean pre-state rejection: `invalid-credential` → `rejected(invalid-credential)`; `invalid-request` → `rejected(invalid-request)`, a deployment fault (its retention source leaves this intent standing with no outcome, which step 2's `Suspending` reading and the sweep then treat as a cascade with every member open — harmless, since the plan is the snapshot and re-snapshotting finds the same members); `recording-failure(step)` → `rejected(recording-failure)`, retryable as a fresh call. Stop on any. The plan is carried because the outcome enumerates separately and their divergence is diagnostic (Composition state).
5. **Transition to `Suspending`** — `actor_suspension_state[actor_ref] = {state: Suspending, invocation_id, intent_event_id, suspended_by_ref, reason}`, an index write over the intent event just landed (rebuildable from it; a lost write is a rebuild trigger).
6. **Cascade — revoke every member, continuing past failures.** Each call is a committed, irreversible write on its atom's store; none is enlisted in any transaction and none is undone:
  - For each `grant_id ∈ G` still Active: `Permissions.revoke(grant_id)`. `ok` → add to [Revoked Grants]. `not-active` (a concurrent revocation already terminalized it) → benign, add to [Revoked Grants]. `not-known` (a grant the query returned but the store no longer knows — structurally near-impossible, since Permissions never deletes grants, Invariant 10) → a **non-benign** failure: add to `open_members` with `permissions`, surface a high-priority store-inconsistency finding in `suspension_log`, and **continue**. `storage-failure` → add to `open_members` and continue.
  - For each `session_token ∈ S` still Active: `Session.revoke(session_token, revoked_by_ref = suspended_by_ref, reason)`. `revoked` → add to [Revoked Sessions]. `already-terminal` → benign, add to [Revoked Sessions]. `not-known` → non-benign as above (Session is durable, Invariant 9), added to `open_members` and continue. `storage-failure` → add to `open_members` and continue.
  - If `revoke_credential_on_suspend` and Credential is composed: `Credential.revoke(credential_id, revoked_by_ref = suspended_by_ref, reason)`. `revoked` → set `revoked_credential = credential_id`. `already-terminal` → benign. `storage-failure` → add to `open_members` and continue.
  If `open_members` is non-empty when the cascade ends: append `suspension_log` `{outcome: revocation-failure(<surfaces>), open_members}`; the actor stays `Suspending` with every revocation that committed standing; return `rejected(revocation-failure(surface, open_members))` — the caller retries (step 2's resume arm) or the completion sweep closes the rest within `completion_window`. **Nothing is rolled back, and the rejection says exactly which doors are still open.**
7. **Seal the act — `record_action` carrying the complete enumeration (Invariant 2), after every revocation has committed.** `AuditTrail.record_action(action_ref = actor.suspended, actor_ref = suspended_by_ref, credential, data = {invocation_id, intent_event_id, suspended_actor: actor_ref, revoked_grants, revoked_sessions, revoked_credential?, reason, suspended_at = now})` → `event_id`. The `data` carries the **complete revoked set** (or, for a very large set, a cryptographic digest of it with its cardinality — mechanism-neutral, and re-derivable from the plan and the constituent records), so the enumeration is sealed and tamper-evident. **The ordering is the guarantee**: because the append follows every revocation, an `actor.suspended` event that exists always has its revocations behind it — the direction an auditor reads, and the direction the earlier rollback wiring made violable (a `step-4` failure inside a transaction that then rolled the revokes back left a sealed event enumerating revocations that had been undone — a false record nothing could withdraw). Arms: `recording-failure(step-4)`, and `invalid-request` whose read-back finds the event → the event is appended; take its id and proceed to step 8 with a hard alert; `recording-failure(step-2 | step-3)`, `invalid-credential` (a rotation between the two writes), and `invalid-request` whose read-back finds nothing → the revocations stand and the outcome is owed: append `suspension_log` `{outcome: recording-failure}`, leave the actor `Suspending`, return `rejected(recording-failure)`; the completion sweep lands the outcome (*Cross-store consistency under partial failure*).
8. **Transition to `Suspended`** — `actor_suspension_state[actor_ref] = {state: Suspended, invocation_id, suspended_at: now, suspended_by_ref, reason, suspension_event_id: event_id}` (an index write over the outcome event). Append `suspension_log` `{operation: suspend, outcome: suspended, revoked_grants, revoked_sessions, suspension_event_id: event_id}`. Return `{suspended, revoked_grants, revoked_sessions, revoked_credential?, event_id}`. The actor is now **Suspended** with zero active grants and zero active sessions over the snapshot (Invariant 1).

#### `suspension_report` (read-only)

```
suspension_report(actor_ref) →
  {state, suspended_at?, suspended_by_ref?, reason?, revoked_grants?, revoked_sessions?, suspension_event_id?}
 | rejected(invalid-request)
```

The read-only surface an auditor, operator, or the actor themselves uses to read an actor's suspension status and, if Suspended, the complete set that was revoked. Resolves `actor_suspension_state[actor_ref]`: absent or `Active` → `{state: Active}`; `Suspended` → the full suspension record including `suspended_at`, `suspended_by_ref`, `reason`, the `suspension_event_id`, and the enumerated [Revoked Grants] / [Revoked Sessions] (read from the sealed event the `suspension_event_id` binds, the authoritative record). **No Audit Trail event is produced** — a pure read. Logging *who read a suspension report* belongs to a composing access-log concept, named rather than absorbed.

#### `reinstate_actor`

```
reinstate_actor(actor_ref, reinstated_by_ref, credential, reason) →
  {reinstated, event_id}
 | rejected(invalid-request | invalid-credential | already-active | recording-failure)
```

Lifts an actor's suspension by transitioning Suspended → Active and recording an attributed `actor.reinstated` event. **It does not restore any revoked grant or session** — those are terminal in their constituents (Permissions Invariant 3, Session Invariant 4) and cannot be un-revoked; re-authorizing the actor means issuing *fresh* grants and sessions through the issuance layer, which is not this composition's surface. Steps: validate inputs (`invalid-request`); if `state ≠ Suspended` → [Already Active] (no-op); else record `AuditTrail.record_action(action_ref = actor.reinstated, actor_ref = reinstated_by_ref, credential, data = {invocation_id, reinstated_actor: actor_ref, reason, reinstated_at = now})` → `event_id` — the single load-bearing write, which is also where the credential is verified, so no state depends on an unverified claim (Invariant 5's first answer) — mapping `invalid-credential` → `rejected(invalid-credential)`; `recording-failure(step-4)` and the retention-source `invalid-request` → the event is appended, read back by `invocation_id` and proceed; `recording-failure(step-2 | step-3)` and the payload-source `invalid-request` → `rejected(recording-failure)` / `rejected(invalid-request)`, nothing changed; then set `actor_suspension_state[actor_ref] = {state: Active}` — an index write over the event just landed, rebuildable from it, so a failure here is a rebuild trigger and never a partial; append `suspension_log`; return `{reinstated, event_id}`. A `Suspending` actor cannot be reinstated: [Reinstate Actor] returns [Already Active] with the reason `suspending` for it — its cascade is completed first, then lifted. Reinstatement is the state-machine's reverse edge only — re-provisioning access is the composing layer's job (Edge cases — *Reinstatement does not restore access*).

### The load-bearing wiring decision — multi-surface revocation is complete by construction or by completion, never undone; suspension gates the cascade

The composition's structural reason to exist has two halves.

**Half 1 — every active grant and every active session is revoked, and a cascade that stops is finished, not reversed.** *[Suspend Actor] records its plan, snapshots both authorization surfaces, revokes every member of both (and optionally the credential), transitions the actor to Suspended, and seals the complete revoked set — ordered across durability boundaries; on any non-benign failure the revocations that committed stand, the actor is visibly `Suspending`, and a mandated sweep closes the rest.*

*Principle.* An actor's ability to act is spread across two independent surfaces (grants and sessions), each with its own multiplicity; closing the actor out requires closing *both surfaces completely*, and a partial close is dangerous exactly when it is invisible. *Likely objection:* why not revoke grants and sessions independently — let an admin call `Permissions.revoke` per grant and `Session.revoke` per session — rather than wrapping them in one composition action? *Mechanism that resolves it:* because independent revocation has no completeness guarantee and no record of incompleteness — an admin who revokes the grants but forgets a session, or whose session-revocation step fails midway, leaves a half-open actor and no record that the closure was incomplete. This composition snapshots both surfaces, records the plan under the operator's verified credential, revokes every enumerated member, and seals what it closed. **An earlier revision promised more — that on any failure the whole cascade would roll back under one transaction, so the actor was either fully de-authorized or unchanged — and the promise was empty:** a revoked grant is terminal (Permissions Invariant 3), a revoked session is absorbing (Session Invariant 4), a revoked credential is terminal, and the substrate's audit append cannot be withdrawn; no host transaction could enlist any of them, and the wiring that claimed to made a *false record* reachable — a sealed `actor.suspended` event enumerating revocations the rollback had undone. What replaces it is the honest form: the intent event names the plan; every revocation that commits stands; the actor whose cascade stopped is `Suspending`, which the issuance gate treats as closed and [Suspension Report] shows with the open members named; and the completion sweep closes the remainder within `completion_window`. A benign already-terminal target (a concurrent revocation) is counted toward the goal, not treated as a failure. *Result:* after [Suspend Actor] returns success, the actor provably holds zero active grants and zero active sessions over the snapshot; after it returns `revocation-failure`, the records say exactly which doors are still open and the sweep is what closes them — the multi-surface completeness no single constituent provides, without a rollback no constituent could have honored.

**Half 2 — the Active→Suspended transition gates the cascade, and the sealed event records exactly what was closed.** *The cascade runs only on the Active→Suspending edge (a second suspension is a no-op, a call against a `Suspending` actor is a resume), and the `actor.suspended` event enumerates every revoked `grant_id` and `session_token`.*

*Principle.* The suspension must be idempotent (running it twice must not double-revoke or produce two conflicting records) and its completeness must be provable from the records alone. *Likely objection:* doesn't recording the whole revoked set duplicate what the Permissions and Session stores already show (each revoked grant/session carries its own `revoked_at`)? *Mechanism that resolves it:* the per-constituent revocation records show *that* each grant/session was revoked, but only the composition knows they were revoked *as one suspension act* and which set constituted "the actor's access at suspension time." The state gate (step 2) makes the cascade start exactly once — a second [Suspend Actor] sees `Suspended` and returns `already-suspended`, or sees `Suspending` and resumes under the same intent — and the sealed `actor.suspended` event binds the complete `{revoked_grants, revoked_sessions}` enumeration into one tamper-evident record, so an auditor reads one event and sees the whole closure, not a scatter of independently-revoked records they must reassemble and hope is complete. The intent event's plan is the second record the auditor reads: a member the plan named that the outcome does not is an owed closure, and the pair is what makes *complete* checkable rather than asserted. *Result:* the suspension is idempotent (gated on the state), its completeness is records-alone provable (plan against sealed enumeration), resting on the substrate's Tamper Evidence so a later attempt to shrink the recorded set to hide a missed surface breaks the seal.

## Composition-level invariants

These invariants emerge from the composition; none belongs to a single constituent. Each invariant's *Rests on:* clause is its **capability-provenance record** (see [`pressure-testing.md`](../pressure-testing.md) §Capability provenance): every clause traces to a declared source — a named constituent invariant or action, a deployment-declared configuration capability, or a composition-introduced surface this composition owns.

- **Invariant 1 — Completeness of multi-surface revocation (load-bearing; safety plus liveness).** *Safety — no `Suspended` actor with open access:* after `suspend_actor(actor_ref, …)` returns success, the actor holds **zero active grants** (no `Active` Permissions grant with `subject_ref = actor_ref`) and **zero active sessions** (no `Active` Session with `principal_ref = actor_ref`) over the snapshot taken in step 3, and the `actor.suspended` event is appended only after every one of those revocations has committed, so there is no reachable state in which the records say Suspended while an enumerated grant or session remains Active. *Safety — no unsurfaced partial:* a cascade that stops — a non-benign revoke failure that returns, or a crash between the intent and the outcome — leaves the actor `Suspending`, never `Active` and never `Suspended`: the intent event is trail-resident, the index reads it, [Suspension Report] names the open members, and the issuance gate treats `Suspending` as closed. *Liveness — every `Suspending` actor reaches `Suspended` within the declared `completion_window`*, the remaining members closed and the outcome landed by the caller's resume or by the completion sweep, with a completed cascade distinguishable from a clean one by `cascade_recovery = true` on its outcome event. **Snapshot-scoped, modulo concurrent issuance:** a grant or session created *after* the step-3 snapshot is not covered by this suspension (the composition revokes but never issues, so it cannot block a concurrent `Permissions.grant` / `Session.issue`); closing that gap is the composing-layer obligation to gate issuance on `actor_suspension_state` — `Suspending` and `Suspended` alike (Edge cases — *Suspension is snapshot-scoped*). **What is deliberately not claimed:** that a failed [Suspend Actor] leaves the actor unchanged. It does not and cannot — every revocation that committed is terminal by its atom's own invariant — and an earlier revision's claim that it did rested on a rollback no constituent declares. *This is the load-bearing claim and the formal-model subject; the model owes re-derivation over the ordered cascade with the `Suspending` state and the completion sweep (the Ledger's `formal:` line), and its twin is the retired rollback wiring, which reaches a sealed outcome event over undone revocations.* *Rests on:* Permissions **`revoke`** + **Invariant 2 (status monotonicity Active→Revoked)** + **Invariant 3 (revocation terminal)** + **Invariant 8 (revoked grants confer no permission)**; Session **`revoke`** + **Invariant 4 (revocation absorbing)**; the Permissions **`subject_ref`-queryable grant store** and Session **`principal_ref`-queryable session store** (the enumeration capabilities both atoms declare); **Audit Trail's append-only contract — that an appended event cannot be withdrawn — the declared source of the durability boundary this invariant is organized around**; the composition-introduced **intent → revoke → outcome ordering** (Action wiring), the **`Suspending` state**, the **completion sweep**, and the deployment-declared **`completion_window`**, **`suspension_completion_bound`** and **`reconciliation_cadence`** (Configuration), which make the liveness arm a bounded claim rather than an "eventually"; and the **`unified_actor_namespace`** configuration capability (the enumeration's join correctness).

- **Invariant 2 — Audit completeness.** Every successful [Suspend Actor] produces exactly one `actor.suspended` Audit Trail event whose `data` enumerates **every** `grant_id` in [Revoked Grants] and **every** `session_token` in [Revoked Sessions] (and `revoked_credential` when applicable) — the complete set the cascade closed, with no revoked surface omitted from the record. The enumeration is part of the sealed event payload, so an auditor can confirm the suspension's completeness from one tamper-evident record. The event is paired to its intent by `invocation_id` and `intent_event_id`, and the intent's plan is what the enumeration is checked against: every planned member is either in the enumeration or benignly terminal, and every enumerated member was planned. *Defended in-line:* step 7 carries the complete `{revoked_grants, revoked_sessions}` (or its digest) into the `record_action` `data`, appended after every revocation has committed (step 6). *Rests on:* Audit Trail **`record_action`** and **Invariant 1 (attribution coverage)** reached through the named substrate, the substrate's **Tamper Evidence** sealing (so the enumeration cannot be silently shrunk), and the composition-introduced cascade that assembles the complete set before sealing.

- **Invariant 3 — Suspension cascade ordering.** The cascade **starts** only on the Active → Suspending transition: [Suspend Actor] against an actor already in `Suspended` returns `already-suspended` and changes no state (no second cascade, no second event, no double-revoke); against an actor in `Suspending` it **resumes** the earlier cascade under its intent, writing no second intent and no second outcome (the resume and the completion sweep pre-check the trail by `invocation_id`, so an outcome that already landed is never re-appended); and the suspension state is monotonic on the suspend edge (an actor in `Suspending` or `Suspended` stays there until [Reinstate Actor] explicitly reverses a completed one). The benign TOCTOU case — a grant/session the cascade targets that a concurrent process already terminalized — is counted toward completion (`not-active`/`already-terminal` → skipped, not aborted), mirroring Login's Final Critique 4 (finding Final Critique 1) already-terminal handling. *Defended in-line:* step 2's state gate rejects an already-Suspended actor and resumes a Suspending one before any new intent; step 6 treats already-terminal targets as benign. *Rests on:* the composition-introduced **`actor_suspension_state` gate** (Action wiring step 2) and the benign-already-terminal mapping (step 4), with the terminal-target semantics resting on Permissions **Invariant 3 (revocation terminal)** and Session **Invariant 5 (terminal-state absorbing)**.

- **Invariant 4 — Constituent invariants preserved.** All Permissions invariants (1–10) hold over the grant store, all Session invariants (1–11) hold over the session store, all Actor Identity invariants (1–9) hold over the attestation store, all Audit Trail composition-level invariants (1–8) hold over the substrate (and transitively all Event Log, Retention Window, and Tamper Evidence invariants), and — when composed — all Credential invariants (1–11) hold over the credential store. This composition weakens no constituent invariant; it revokes through each constituent's own `revoke` and records through the substrate's own `record_action`. *Rests on:* each constituent's own Generation acceptance bar over its store instance.

- **Invariant 5 — Authentication precedes commitment.** At [Suspend Actor] no constituent `revoke` — each an irreversible write — is reached on any path before the operator's credential has validated against the actor registry's public material for `suspended_by_ref`: the intent record (step 4) is the mechanism, standing before the first revocation, so an actor's access is never closed on an unverified claim, and `invalid-credential` there is a pre-state refusal with nothing revoked. At [Reinstate Actor] the single `record_action` is itself the load-bearing write, so nothing commits ahead of the verification and no second mechanism is needed. **What this does and does not establish:** a successful validation establishes that material matching the operator's registered verifier was presented at that instant — not that the presenter *is* that operator, not that the presentation is bound to a channel, and nothing about whether the operator was *authorized* to suspend this actor (Edge cases — *Suspension authorization*). *Rests on:* Audit Trail's `record_action` and the Actor Identity attestation reached through it. *Defended in-line:* the ordering is visible in [Suspend Actor]'s step list, and Generation acceptance check 6 tests it from the records alone.

Invariant 1 (complete multi-surface revocation) is the emergent guarantee the composition exists to provide; Invariant 2 (audit completeness) is what makes that guarantee provable from one sealed record against one recorded plan; Invariant 3 (cascade ordering) makes the suspension idempotent and orders the cascade behind the state transition; Invariant 5 makes the operator a verified one before anything is closed. Invariant 4 preserves every constituent guarantee underneath.

## Examples

### Walkthrough — offboarding a departing employee, end to end

A bank deploys this composition over its access surfaces with `unified_actor_namespace = enforced` (the `actor_ref` is the employee's directory identity, used as `subject_ref` for grants and `principal_ref` for sessions, bound via Authenticated Actor), `completion_window = 15m`, Credential composed with `revoke_credential_on_suspend = true`, and the substrate's `audit_trail_retention_policy = sox_7_year`.

1. **Suspension trigger.** An employee resigns; HR's offboarding workflow calls `suspend_actor(actor_ref = "emp_4821", suspended_by_ref = "hr_offboard_svc", credential = <svc-cred>, reason = "employment-ended-2026-06-10")`.
2. **State gate.** `actor_suspension_state["emp_4821"]` is absent → Active → proceed (Invariant 3).
3. **Snapshot and plan.** The Permissions store yields three active grants for `subject_ref = "emp_4821"`: `g_finread`, `g_wireinit`, `g_reports`. The Session store yields two active sessions for `principal_ref = "emp_4821"`: `tok_laptop`, `tok_phone`. `AuditTrail.record_action(actor.suspension_intended, "hr_offboard_svc", <svc-cred>, data = {invocation_id, suspended_actor: "emp_4821", planned_grants: {g_finread, g_wireinit, g_reports}, planned_sessions: {tok_laptop, tok_phone}, planned_credential: cred_emp4821, reason})` → `ev_susp_int_001` — the service credential is verified here, before anything is revoked; `actor_suspension_state["emp_4821"] = {Suspending, …}`.
4. **Cascade.** `Permissions.revoke(g_finread) → ok`; `revoke(g_wireinit) → ok`; `revoke(g_reports) → ok` (`revoked_grants = {g_finread, g_wireinit, g_reports}`). `Session.revoke(tok_laptop, "hr_offboard_svc", "employment-ended-2026-06-10") → revoked`; `revoke(tok_phone, …) → revoked` (`revoked_sessions = {tok_laptop, tok_phone}`). `Credential.revoke(cred_emp4821, "hr_offboard_svc", …) → revoked` (`revoked_credential = cred_emp4821`).
5. **Seal + transition.** `AuditTrail.record_action(actor.suspended, "hr_offboard_svc", <svc-cred>, data = {invocation_id, intent_event_id: ev_susp_int_001, suspended_actor: "emp_4821", revoked_grants: {g_finread, g_wireinit, g_reports}, revoked_sessions: {tok_laptop, tok_phone}, revoked_credential: cred_emp4821, reason})` → `ev_susp_001`, appended after every revocation committed; then `actor_suspension_state["emp_4821"] = {Suspended, suspended_at, suspended_by_ref: "hr_offboard_svc", reason, suspension_event_id: ev_susp_001}`.
6. **Result.** Returns `{suspended, revoked_grants (3), revoked_sessions (2), revoked_credential, event_id: ev_susp_001}`. `emp_4821` now holds zero active grants and zero active sessions (Invariant 1); `Permissions.permitted("emp_4821", "wire:initiate") → denied`; `Session.validate(tok_laptop) → invalid(revoked)`; and the revoked credential blocks re-authentication.

### Domain example — compromised account, a cascade that stops and is completed

A security team suspends a compromised account: `suspend_actor("svc_8830", "soc_analyst_k", <cred>, "credential-in-paste-dump")`. The snapshot finds five grants and one long-lived session; the intent event lands with that plan, and `svc_8830` is `Suspending`. Mid-cascade, `Session.revoke(tok_svc)` returns `storage-failure` (the session store is briefly unreachable). **Nothing is undone** — the five grant revocations that committed are terminal and stand. The cascade finishes its remaining members, `suspension_log` records `{outcome: revocation-failure(session), open_members: {tok_svc}}`, and [Suspend Actor] returns `rejected(revocation-failure(session, {tok_svc}))`. The account is now *mostly* closed and the records say exactly which door is still open: [Suspension Report] shows `Suspending` with `open_members = {tok_svc}`, and the issuance gate refuses new access for it. The SOC (Security Operations Center) retries once the session store recovers — step 2 finds `Suspending` and **resumes** under the same intent, revokes `tok_svc`, seals the complete enumeration, and the account is `Suspended`; had no one retried, the completion sweep would have done the same within `completion_window`, under the service identity, with `cascade_recovery = true` on the outcome. An earlier revision of this example rolled the five grant revocations back and left the account untouched — a rollback the Permissions atom never offered, whose only real effect would have been a sealed record disagreeing with the store.

### Rejection path — already-suspended no-op, and the benign TOCTOU race

- **Already suspended (idempotent no-op).** A retry of the offboarding call: `suspend_actor("emp_4821", …)` → step 2 finds `state = Suspended` → `rejected(already-suspended)`; no second cascade, no double-revoke, no second event (Invariant 3). The single suspension from the first call stands.
- **Benign TOCTOU — concurrent logout during the cascade.** While `suspend_actor("emp_4821", …)` is mid-cascade, the employee's laptop client fires its own `Session.revoke(tok_laptop)` (a logout). The cascade's `Session.revoke(tok_laptop, …)` then returns `already-terminal` — the session is already terminal, which is the cascade's goal. This composition counts `tok_laptop` in [Revoked Sessions] (the surface is closed) and does **not** abort. This mirrors Login's Final Critique 4 (finding Final Critique 1) already-terminal handling: a concurrent terminal transition during the cascade is benign, not a failure.

### Regulated adversarial scenarios

Three scenarios the composition must survive in regulated contexts.

**Regulator audit — "prove this terminated employee's access was fully and promptly removed."** A SOX (Sarbanes-Oxley Act) auditor examines `emp_4821`'s offboarding. `suspension_report("emp_4821")` returns `Suspended` with `suspended_at`, `suspended_by_ref`, and the enumerated [Revoked Grants] (3) and [Revoked Sessions] (2). The auditor reads the sealed `actor.suspended` event `ev_susp_001` and confirms, via `AuditTrail.verify_record`, that the enumeration was not altered after the fact (Invariant 2, resting on the substrate's Tamper Evidence). For each enumerated `grant_id`, the Permissions store shows `status = Revoked` with `revoked_at` at suspension time; for each `session_token`, the Session store shows `Revoked`. Invariant 1 is the structural answer: a Suspended actor has zero active grants and zero active sessions. The examiner consults no source code — every claim is verified from the records.

**Disputed action — "this employee couldn't have done that; we suspended them."** An action attributed to `emp_4821` is dated after `suspended_at`. The investigator reads `ev_susp_001`: the suspension revoked every active grant and session in the plan, with the outcome sealed only after every revocation committed (Invariant 1). If the disputed action used a session, `Session.validate` on that token returns `invalid(revoked)` with `revoked_at` at suspension time — the session could not have authorized an action afterward. If it used a grant, `Permissions.permitted` returns `denied`. The only way the action could post-date the suspension is a *new* grant/session issued after the snapshot — which [Suspension Report] plus the issuance records would show, and which is the named snapshot-scoped gap (the composing layer's obligation to gate issuance on the suspension state). The records bound the question precisely.

**Breach investigation — "during the incident, was any suspended actor's access incompletely closed?"** An investigator suspects a suspension half-completed under load. They walk the `actor.suspended` events in the incident window and, for each, cross-check that every enumerated `grant_id`/`session_token` is terminal in its store *and* that no active grant/session for that `actor_ref` exists outside the enumerated set as of `suspended_at`. Invariant 1 forecloses a `Suspended`-with-active-access state: the outcome event is appended only after every revocation committed. A half-closed actor is a `Suspending` one, and the investigator reads it directly — the intent's plan against the store, the `suspension_log`'s `revocation-failure(surface)` entry naming the open members, and, once completed, the outcome's `cascade_recovery = true` — the honest record of a surface that did not close on the first pass and when it did. Because the enumeration is in the sealed event payload, an attempt to shrink it to hide a missed surface breaks the seal.

## Generation acceptance

A derived implementation of this composition is *acceptable* — in the regulator-acceptance sense — when an external auditor, given the composition's emergent state (`actor_suspension_state`, `suspension_log`) plus the Permissions, Session, (optional) Credential, and Audit Trail substrate stores, can do all of the following without recourse to source code, runbooks, or developer narration.

### Audit-Trail-traversal-clearable checks

These checks an auditor answers by reading the composition's records (including the Audit Trail substrate reached through it).

1. **Suspension completeness (the multi-surface guarantee).** For every actor in `actor_suspension_state` with `state = Suspended` (rebuilt from the trail, not read from the index alone), confirm that **no** Permissions grant with `subject_ref = actor_ref` is `Active` and **no** Session with `principal_ref = actor_ref` is `Active` — except grants/sessions whose `granted_at`/`issued_at` post-dates `suspended_at` (the snapshot-scoped concurrent-issuance gap, a composing-layer obligation, not a defect of this composition). A Suspended actor with an active grant or session issued *before* `suspended_at` is a conformance failure (Invariant 1).
2. **Audit enumeration completeness, against the plan.** For every `Suspended` actor, confirm exactly one `actor.suspended` event carries its `suspension_event_id`, that `AuditTrail.verify_record` returns `verified` for it (over the covering range `read_record` names), that its `intent_event_id` resolves to an `actor.suspension_intended` event with the same `invocation_id` preceding it in the log's own order, and that the outcome's enumerated [Revoked Grants] / [Revoked Sessions] equals the intent's `planned_grants` / `planned_sessions` — every planned member either enumerated or terminal in its store by a concurrent act before `suspended_at`, and no enumerated member unplanned — and that every enumerated member resolves to a Revoked record. A revoked-at-suspension grant or session absent from the sealed enumeration is a conformance failure (Invariant 2). The inverse: every `grant_id`/`session_token` in the enumeration resolves to a Revoked record.
3. **Idempotence and ordering.** Confirm the trail shows at most one `actor.suspended` per `actor_ref` between reinstatements and at most one `actor.suspension_intended` per cascade (a resume writes none), that any [Suspend Actor] against an already-Suspended actor recorded `already-suspended` in the `suspension_log` with no second event (Invariant 3), and that `actor_suspension_state` rebuilt from the trail transitions only Active→Suspending→Suspended (via [Suspend Actor] and its completion) and Suspended→Active (via [Reinstate Actor]), each edge bound to a sealed event. The `suspension_log`'s refusal entries are evidence of refusals, never of acts.
4. **Attribution and reason completeness.** For every `actor.suspended` event, confirm it is attributed (via the substrate's Actor Identity) to the `suspended_by_ref` and carries a non-empty `reason`, and that each revoked Session and (optional) Credential record carries the same `suspended_by_ref` and `reason`. A suspension with no attributed operator or no reason is a conformance failure.
5. **Constituent Generation acceptance bars.** Verify Permissions' five checks over the grant store, Session's five checks over the session store, Audit Trail's six checks over the substrate (transitively clearing Event Log, Actor Identity, Tamper Evidence, Retention Window), and — when composed — Credential's six checks. This composition's invariants depend on the correctness of the constituents' invariants (Invariant 4).
6. **Authentication precedence, and unfinished cascades surfaced and completed within the window.** For every `actor.suspended` event, its intent event precedes it and names the same operator (or, for a compensated outcome carrying `cascade_recovery = true` and attested under `application_actor_ref`, the operator as `data.suspended_by`, with an `actor.recovery_intended` record naming the same `invocation_id` preceding it) — the records-alone proof that the operator was verified before any revocation (Invariant 5). Then, **between two edges** — intents older than `suspension_completion_bound` and inside the audit horizon — for every `actor.suspension_intended` with no `actor.suspended` naming its `invocation_id`, confirm **either** the actor is `Suspending` with the open members named in the `suspension_log`, **or** a completing outcome landed within `completion_window` of the intent's `intended_at`, **or** an open compliance finding names the actor. Neither is a conformance failure; *none of the three* is — the partial was silent. An intent younger than the bound is inconclusive; one past the horizon is answered by the index's truth-bearing half, not by this check. Confirm `completion_window`, `suspension_completion_bound` and `reconciliation_cadence` are all declared, with the cadence no longer than the window.

### Externally-clearable checks

These questions arise around this composition but cannot be answered from its records alone — they are the composition's named audit-gaps, each routed to the evidence that owns it.

- **Whether the `actor_ref` namespace truly coincides with the grant `subject_ref` and session `principal_ref` namespaces.** This composition's enumeration (and hence Invariant 1's completeness) is complete only under `unified_actor_namespace`; whether the deployment actually unified them — via Authenticated Actor's binding or convention — requires the deployment's identity-namespace evidence, not this composition's records. This is the load-bearing enumeration-completeness audit-gap (Edge cases — *Unified actor namespace is a precondition*); a divergent namespace under-enumerates silently.
- **Whether a new grant or session was issued after the suspension snapshot.** Invariant 1 is snapshot-scoped; whether the composing layer correctly gated `Permissions.grant` / `Session.issue` on the suspension state (so a suspended actor acquired no new access) requires the issuance layer's records, not this composition's (Edge cases — *Suspension is snapshot-scoped*).
- **Whether the `suspended_by_ref` was authorized to suspend the actor.** This composition attributes *who* suspended but does not gate *whether* they were permitted to; that requires a Permissions instance scoped to suspension authority (Edge cases — *Suspension authorization*).
- **Whether re-authentication was actually blocked.** When Credential is uncomposed or `revoke_credential_on_suspend = false`, whether the deployment closed the re-authentication door requires the credential/Login records, not this composition's (Edge cases — *Re-authentication after suspension*).

## Edge cases and explicit non-goals

- **Suspension is snapshot-scoped; concurrent issuance is a composing-layer gap.** Invariant 1's completeness holds over the active set enumerated at the step-3 snapshot. This composition revokes but never issues; it cannot prevent a concurrent `Permissions.grant` or `Session.issue` from creating *new* access for the actor after the snapshot. This is the exact analog of Login's snapshot-scoped cascade (Login Invariant 2): the composing layer that issues grants and sessions must gate issuance on `actor_suspension_state` (refuse to issue for a `Suspending` or `Suspended` actor — both are closed states), which this composition exposes as a queryable surface ([Suspension Report]). This composition closes the actor's *existing* access completely; keeping it closed against new issuance is the issuance layer's obligation, and whether it did so is an externally-clearable check. A deployment that wants the suspension to be self-enforcing wires the issuance gate; this composition provides the state the gate reads.
- **Unified actor namespace is a precondition, not a guarantee.** This composition enumerates `subject_ref = actor_ref` grants and `principal_ref = actor_ref` sessions; this is complete only if the three namespaces coincide. The composition names this as a deployment precondition (`unified_actor_namespace`, satisfied by Authenticated Actor's `principal_ref ↔ actor_ref` binding or by convention) rather than verifying it — verifying that two opaque namespaces coincide is not a records-alone check at this composition's layer. A deployment whose namespaces diverge under-enumerates and produces a suspension that misses the actor's access under the other namespace — the precise gap `demos/attributed-permissions-admin/CORNERS.md` §Cross-atom identity surface aliasing flags, here named as an externally-clearable check rather than papered over.
- **Completion, not undo — why this composition promises more than Login and less than it once claimed.** Login's cascade is best-effort (a per-session storage failure is logged and the cascade continues; partial revocation beats none). This composition's earlier revisions inverted that into `all-or-nothing`: any non-benign failure would roll the whole cascade back so the actor was either fully de-authorized or unchanged. **That posture was never available.** A revoked grant is terminal (Permissions Invariant 3), a revoked session absorbing (Session Invariant 4), a revoked credential terminal, and the substrate's audit append un-withdrawable — none can be enlisted in a host transaction, so the rollback the posture rested on was an undeclared capability of every store it named, and the wiring that claimed it could leave a sealed `actor.suspended` event enumerating revocations that had been undone: a false record, which is the one partial nothing can repair. What the composition promises now is the stronger *honest* form: the intent is recorded before anything is revoked, every revocation that commits stands, an actor whose cascade stopped is `Suspending` — a closed state for the issuance gate, a named set of open members for the operator — and the completion sweep closes the remainder within a declared window. The `partial_failure_posture` knob is gone with the posture; there is one behavior.
- **Cross-store consistency under partial failure.** Two partials are reachable and both are completed, never repaired by fabrication. **(a) The stopped cascade.** The intent landed and one or more revocations did not (a non-benign arm that returned, or a crash mid-cascade): the actor is `Suspending`, the plan is on the intent event, the committed revocations are in their stores. **(b) The unsealed cascade.** Every revocation committed and the `actor.suspended` append did not land (a `step-2 | step-3` failure, or a crash between step 6 and step 7): the actor is `Suspending` with no open members. A **completion sweep** runs at process restart and on `reconciliation_cadence` (Configuration), **between two edges and as the composition**: it examines `Suspending` entries — rebuilt from the trail, never read from the index alone — whose intent is older than `suspension_completion_bound` (a younger one may be an invocation still revoking, and completing it beside that invocation would revoke twice and append a second outcome) and inside the audit horizon (past it the plan is destroyed and the entry is the index's truth-bearing half; the sweep then completes from the live stores, re-snapshotting the actor's Active members, and says so on the outcome as `plan_unavailable = true`). For each, it **pre-checks the trail** by `invocation_id` — an `actor.suspended` already naming this intent means a completion landed and the index is stale, a rebuild trigger and nothing to emit — then writes an **`actor.recovery_intended`** record under `application_actor_ref` / `application_credential` naming the `invocation_id`, the intent, and the members it is about to close; revokes each still-Active planned member under the service identity (`revoked_by_ref = application_actor_ref`, `reason = "suspension-completion:" + intent_event_id + ": " + <the operator's reason>`, so the constituent's own record traces to the operator's intent); and appends the `actor.suspended` outcome with `cascade_recovery = true`, the operator as `suspended_by`, `intent_event_id`, and the complete enumeration — re-derived from the plan and the constituent records, never remembered from the process that died. A `Suspending` actor not completed within `completion_window` escalates as an unresolved compliance finding rather than remaining an open retry, and under the offboarding regimes this composition serves is a hard alerting condition. The sweep and every action take the per-`actor_ref` serialization (*Concurrency*), so a completion and a resume never interleave on one actor.
- **Reinstatement does not restore access.** [Reinstate Actor] lifts the Suspended state but never un-revokes a grant or session — those are terminal in Permissions (Invariant 3) and Session (Invariant 4) and cannot return to Active. Re-authorizing a reinstated actor means issuing *fresh* grants (`Permissions.grant`) and letting them establish *new* sessions (`Login`), which is the issuance layer's surface, not this composition's. Reinstatement is the state-machine's reverse edge only — it re-opens the actor to *future* provisioning; it does not reach back into the constituents' terminal records. A deployment that needs the old access back re-grants it explicitly, which is the correct, auditable behavior (the new grants carry new `granted_at` timestamps and their own attributions).
- **Suspension authorization is a composing concept.** Whether the `suspended_by_ref` is *authorized* to suspend a given actor is an inward-authorization question this composition does not gate — it composes no Permissions instance scoped to suspension authority (the cut is Actor Identity + Permissions-as-revocation-target + Session + Audit Trail). The operator is attributed cryptographically via the substrate's Actor Identity, so an auditor can always answer *who* suspended the actor; whether they were *permitted* to requires a Permissions check (or a Multi-Party Approval gate for high-stakes suspensions) wired ahead of [Suspend Actor], exactly as Resolve a Person's Data Rights leaves operator authorization to a composing pattern.
- **Re-authentication after suspension.** Revoking the actor's active grants and sessions closes their *current* access but does not, by itself, stop a *new* login that would establish a fresh session. Closing that door is the optional Credential constituent's job (`revoke_credential_on_suspend = true` revokes the authentication credential so re-authentication fails — Credential Invariant 4). When Credential is uncomposed, the deployment must revoke the credential through Login's surface (`Credential.revoke` then optionally `revoke_sessions_for_credential`) in parallel — a named composing obligation, with whether it did so an externally-clearable check.
- **No bulk constituent surfaces — the cascade enumerates and revokes each.** Permissions provides no bulk-revoke (its *Mass revocation* edge case names per-grant enumerate-then-revoke as the composing system's job) and Session's bulk revocation is likewise an enumerate-and-revoke over its `principal_ref`-queryable store. This composition is the composing pattern that performs both enumerations and the per-member revocations under one per-`actor_ref` serialization, recording the plan before and the outcome after — which is exactly why the completeness guarantees (Invariants 1 and 2) live at the composition layer and not in either atom.
- **The actor lifecycle is composition-introduced.** Actor Identity has a single `Attested` state and no actor lifecycle; the Active/Suspended state machine is this composition's own (a composition-introduced surface). This composition therefore does not — and cannot — read a "suspended" flag from Actor Identity; it owns `actor_suspension_state`. A deployment that also wants the actor's *attestation registry* entry deactivated (so the actor cannot produce new attestations) composes that through the forthcoming Actor Registry / Identity Provisioning pattern, or — for the attest surface specifically — through [Authenticated Actor](./authenticated-actor.md), whose revocation cascade closes the attest surface when the credential is revoked. This composition + Authenticated Actor + Credential-revocation together close the full outbound surface (grants, sessions, attestation, re-authentication); this composition alone closes grants and sessions.
- **Suspending an actor with no active access.** An `actor_ref` with no active grants and no active sessions (a typo, or an actor already de-provisioned) is a valid no-op suspension: the cascade revokes nothing, the actor transitions to Suspended, and the `actor.suspended` event records empty [Revoked Grants]/[Revoked Sessions]. This is meaningful ("we suspended an actor who held no active access") and harmless; it is distinct from a failure. This composition does not reject it, mirroring Login's `revoke_sessions_for_credential` returning `{revoked: 0}` for a credential with no sessions.
- **Concurrency.** Two concurrent [Suspend Actor] calls for the same `actor_ref` are serialized per `actor_ref` — a host serialization obligation over steps 3–8, the resume arm, and the completion sweep, not a transaction — and the state gate: the first transitions and cascades; the second observes `Suspending` or `Suspended` and resumes or returns `already-suspended` (Invariant 3). The benign TOCTOU race — a concurrent `Session.revoke`/`logout` or `Permissions.revoke` terminalizing a target the cascade also targets — is counted as skipped (already-terminal/not-active), not aborted, mirroring Login's Final Critique 4 (finding Final Critique 1). A concurrent `Permissions.grant`/`Session.issue` after the snapshot is the snapshot-scoped gap above.
- **Clock semantics.** `suspended_at`, `reinstated_at`, and `attempted_at` are best-effort wall-time annotations read from the substrate's clock authority at record time (injected by the Audit Trail substrate, not sampled inside this composition's transition — logic-confinement, inherited from the substrate). The Event Log `sequence_number` (via the substrate) is the authoritative order source for the suspension events. The cascade's correctness depends on the per-`actor_ref` serialization, not on clock comparison; `invocation_id` is seam-injected alongside `now` (*Primitive policies*).

#### Suspend Actor

The composition's load-bearing emergent action: under a per-`actor_ref` serialization, it snapshots the actor's active grants and sessions, records the plan under the operator's verified credential, revokes every one (and, optionally, the actor's Credential), seals the complete revoked set into one attributed Audit Trail event, and transitions the actor Active → Suspending → Suspended. Returns `{suspended, revoked_grants, revoked_sessions, revoked_credential?, event_id}`, or [Already Suspended] (idempotent no-op), [Revocation Failure] (a non-benign constituent revoke failed — the actor stays Suspending with the open members named, and a retry resumes), `invalid-credential`, or `recording-failure`. Neither constituent carries this multi-surface cascade.

Kind: Operation

#### Suspension Report

The composition's read-only query: resolves `actor_suspension_state[actor_ref]` (rebuild-on-miss), returning `{state: Active}` for an unsuspended actor, `{state: Suspending, open_members, intent_event_id, …}` for one whose cascade has not completed, or the full Suspended record — `suspended_at`, `suspended_by_ref`, `reason`, and the enumerated [Revoked Grants] / [Revoked Sessions] read from the sealed event. Changes no state; the queryable surface the issuance layer gates new access on.

Kind: Operation

#### Reinstate Actor

The composition's thin lifecycle-reversal: transitions Suspended → Active and records an attributed `actor.reinstated` event. It does **not** restore any revoked grant or session (those are terminal in their constituents) — re-provisioning is the issuance layer's job. Returns `{reinstated, event_id}`, or [Already Active] (no-op) / `recording-failure`.

Kind: Operation
