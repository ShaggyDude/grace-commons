## Summary

Actor Suspension is a regulated composition — a specification that wires several freestanding patterns together — that does one job thoroughly: when you need to cut off an actor (a departing employee, a hacked account, a contractor who's done), it closes *every* door at once and leaves proof.

The problem it solves is the classic security gap where someone's access is pulled in one place but not another — their login is disabled but a permission grant still works, or their role is removed but an open session keeps going. This composition makes that gap visible and short-lived by treating suspension as one ordered, completed act: it records what it is about to close, finds every active permission grant the actor holds and every active login session they have, revokes each of them, marks the actor as suspended, and writes one tamper-proof audit record that lists every grant and session it revoked.

If any part of that can't complete, nothing is undone — a revoked grant or session cannot be un-revoked — but nothing is hidden either: the actor is marked as *suspending*, the record shows exactly which doors are still open, and a mandated completion sweep closes them within a declared window. A half-suspended actor is dangerous only when everyone wrongly believes they are locked out; this composition never lets the records say that. Optionally, it also revokes every login credential the actor holds — a password and a hardware key are two, and closing one would leave the other working — so they can't sign back in.

It is the exact mirror of the Login pattern: Login wires up "check the credential, then start a session" on the way in; this wires up "suspend the actor, then revoke every grant and every session" on the way out. The composition's defining emergent guarantee — a property that exists only when the patterns are combined — is that single multi-surface revocation: after a successful suspension, the actor provably holds zero active grants and zero active sessions, and the audit record names every one that was revoked. Its common uses are exactly the moments compliance regimes care about most: offboarding a terminated employee (the access must be removed promptly and provably), responding to a compromised account, and ending a third-party's access. Any system that must deactivate an actor across multiple access surfaces at once — and prove from the records that nothing was missed — is a candidate for this composition.

## Intent

Every system that distinguishes actors eventually has to *un*-distinguish one. An employee leaves; a credential is found in a paste dump; a contractor's engagement ends; an account shows the signature of a takeover. The obligation that follows is deceptively simple to state and notoriously easy to botch: cut off *every* way that actor can still act, *now*, and be able to prove afterward that you did. The botch is always the same shape — access is revoked on one surface but not another. The session cookie is invalidated but the API token's permission grant is still active; the role is removed but a long-lived session keeps working; the login credential is disabled but a service grant the actor still holds quietly authorizes a scheduled job. Each gap is a half-open door, and a half-suspended actor is more dangerous than an un-suspended one, because the system and its operators believe the actor is locked out.

This is the friction this composition exists to resolve, and naming it precisely keeps the composition honest: **this composition is not an "account deactivation feature"; it is a multi-surface de-authorization, ordered across durability boundaries and completed rather than undone, with a records-alone completeness proof.** An actor's ability to act is spread across two independent authorization surfaces that no single atom relates: **Permissions** holds the grants that say *what the actor may do*, and **Session** holds the live authenticated channels that say *the actor is currently acting*. Revoking one without the other leaves a door open. Worse, each surface has its own multiplicity — an actor may hold many grants (issued at different times, by different grantors) and many sessions (one per device) — so closure on each surface is itself an *enumerate-then-revoke-each* operation, exactly the mass-revocation pattern Permissions names as the composing system's responsibility (*"enumerate all active grants where `grant.subject_ref` = departing_subject, then call `revoke(grant_id)` for each"*) and Session supports through its `principal_ref`-queryable store (*"revoke all sessions for this account"*). The structure that snapshots both surfaces, revokes every member of both, sets the actor's suspension state, and seals the whole act as one attributed, tamper-evident record belongs to no single constituent. It belongs to the composition, and this composition is that structure.

this composition is Login's mirror image, and the symmetry is load-bearing. Login is the *inbound* wiring: `Credential.verify → Session.issue`, with the emergent **cascade** that revoking a credential terminates the derived sessions. This composition is the *outbound* wiring: `suspend → revoke every grant + revoke every session`, with the emergent guarantee that suspension closes *all* surfaces at once. Login's cascade walks an active-session set under a snapshot and tolerates partial completion (a per-session storage failure is logged and the cascade continues — partial revocation beats none). This composition makes a stronger choice than Login's, and the difference is deliberate: a suspension that half-completes leaves an ambiguous security state, so this composition never leaves one *ambiguous* — an earlier revision promised that by rolling the whole cascade back under one transaction, which no constituent could honor (a revoked grant, session, or credential is terminal by its own atom's invariant, and the substrate's audit append cannot be withdrawn), so what it promises now is **completion**: the intent is recorded before anything is revoked, every revocation that commits stands, an actor whose cascade stopped is visibly `Suspending` with the open doors named, and a mandated sweep closes them within a declared window. The same TOCTOU (time-of-check-to-time-of-use) race Login surfaced as finding Final Critique 1 in its Final Critique 4 — a concurrent terminal transition during the cascade — recurs here and is resolved the same way: a grant or session the cascade targets that another process already revoked is found already-terminal and counted, not re-revoked.

This is a composition, not a new primitive. Actor Identity, Permissions, Session, and Audit Trail are unchanged; this composition is the wiring that makes them coherent as one suspension surface. It introduces emergent actions — [Suspend Actor], the read-only [Suspension Report], and the thin [Reinstate Actor] — and an emergent state machine the constituents do not carry: the actor's **Active → Suspending → Suspended** lifecycle. Actor Identity is the attestation atom, with a single `Attested` state and *no* actor lifecycle (it explicitly defers actor registration, deactivation, and suspension to a forthcoming Actor Registry / Identity Provisioning pattern); the Active/Suspended state is therefore a **composition-introduced surface** This composition owns, not a state read from any constituent.

What the composition is *not*: it is not the issuance surface — it revokes grants and sessions but never issues them (`Permissions.grant` and `Session.issue` are called elsewhere), so it cannot, by itself, prevent a *new* grant or session being created for a suspended actor after the cascade snapshot (a named composing-layer obligation, exactly as Login's cascade is snapshot-scoped); it is not the actor registry that provisions actor identities; it is not an authorization-policy engine deciding *whether* an actor should be suspended (that is wired ahead of [Suspend Actor] at the administrative / Multi-Party Approval layer); and it does not restore access on reinstatement — [Reinstate Actor] lifts the suspension state but never un-revokes a terminal grant or session (re-provisioning is the issuance layer's job). Each is named explicitly in Edge cases.

## Composes

- **[Actor Identity](../atoms/actor-identity.md)** — the identity of the actor being suspended. This composition introduces the actor's **Active → Suspending → Suspended** lifecycle as a composition-introduced state machine, because Actor Identity carries no lifecycle state of its own (a single `Attested` state, with actor registration / deactivation / suspension explicitly deferred to a forthcoming Actor Registry / Identity Provisioning pattern). The `actor_ref` is the suspension subject and the join key across the two authorization surfaces. This composition does **not** mutate any attestation (they are immutable — Actor Identity Invariant 1); the attestation surface enters via the Audit Trail substrate, which attests the `actor.suspended` event under the *suspending* operator's credential.

- **[Permissions](../atoms/permissions.md)** — the grant surface, used in **both directions**. *Read:* [Suspend Actor] enumerates the actor's active grants by querying the grant store for `{grant_id}` where `subject_ref = actor_ref` and `status = Active` (the grant store is queryable; Permissions' Behavior section exposes per-grant fields to composing systems). *Write:* the cascade calls `Permissions.revoke(grant_id) → ok | rejected(not-known | not-active | storage-failure)` for **each** enumerated grant — Permissions provides no bulk-revoke surface, and naming the per-grant enumerate-then-revoke as the composing system's responsibility is Permissions' own *Mass revocation on subject deprovisioning* edge case. This composition is that composing system. A `revoke` reaching an already-`not-active` grant (a concurrent revocation) is the benign TOCTOU case (below), not an error.

- **[Session](../atoms/session.md)** — the live-channel surface, used in **both directions**. *Read:* [Suspend Actor] enumerates the actor's active sessions by querying the session store for `{session_token}` where `principal_ref = actor_ref` and `status = Active` (Session's store is `principal_ref`-queryable precisely to support *"show all active sessions for this account"* and *"revoke all sessions for this account"* administrative operations). *Write:* the cascade calls `Session.revoke(session_token, revoked_by_ref = suspended_by_ref, reason) → revoked | rejected(invalid-request | already-terminal | not-known | storage-failure)` for **each** active session. A session that became terminal between the snapshot and the revoke returns `already-terminal` — the benign TOCTOU case, counted as skipped exactly as Login's Final Critique 4 (finding Final Critique 1) handles it.

- **[Audit Trail](./audit-trail.md)** — the regulated-audit **substrate**: this composition names it the way an atom is named and reaches **Event Log, Actor Identity, Tamper Evidence, and Retention Window transitively through it** (per the *Compositions of compositions* convention — see [`spec-format.md`](../spec-format.md)), maintaining one Audit Trail instance for all suspension events. This composition records its events by calling **`AuditTrail.record_action(action_ref, actor_ref, credential, data) → event_id | rejected(invalid-credential | invalid-request | recording-failure(step))` directly on the one Audit Trail instance the substrate carries** — the `(step)` payload carried on every transcription, since the substrate's `step-4` arm means the event is already appended (Action wiring) — the established substrate-composition pattern (Multi-Party Approval and Resolve a Person's Data Rights record their own events on the Audit Trail their substrate carries). The capability is Audit Trail's own declared `record_action` and its Invariant 1 (attribution coverage), reached through the named substrate — not an ambient reach-through. The event's `data` carries the **complete enumeration** of every revoked `grant_id` and `session_token` (Invariant 2). The substrate seals the event (Tamper Evidence) and governs its retention (Retention Window) so the suspension record outlives the access it closed.

- **[Credential](../atoms/credential.md)** *(optional fifth constituent)* — when composed, the cascade additionally revokes **every effective-Active credential the actor holds**, used in **both directions**. *Read:* [Suspend Actor] enumerates `{credential_id : principal_ref = actor_ref ∧ effective-Active}` from the Credential store — declared queryable, each record's derived effective status observable to administrative surfaces — across **every** `credential_type`, because Credential Invariant 2 bounds effective-Active *per `(principal_ref, credential_type)` pair*, not per principal: a password and a hardware key are two effective-Active credentials, and revoking one leaves the other authenticating (Credential Invariant 4 is pair-scoped too). *Write:* `Credential.revoke(credential_id, revoked_by_ref = suspended_by_ref, reason) → revoked | rejected(invalid-request | already-terminal | not-known | storage-failure)` for **each**, so the suspended actor cannot re-authenticate on any type to establish a *new* session. This closes the re-entry door the grant/session revocation alone leaves open (revoking current sessions does not stop a new login). When Credential is **not** composed, closing that door is a named composing obligation (the deployment revokes every credential through Login's surface, or wires this composition with Credential). Including Credential makes this composition the full outbound counterpart to Login's inbound `Credential.verify → Session.issue`.

The Permissions grant store, the Session store, and (when composed) the Credential store are owned by their respective constituent instances; the Event Log, Actor Identity attestation store, Tamper Evidence seal store, and Retention Window store are owned by the Audit Trail substrate. This composition owns no constituent state — it owns the emergent `actor_suspension_state` map, a derived index over its own intent and outcome events inside the audit horizon and truth-bearing past it (Composition state), the `index_high_water` mark that bounds the trail reads the map's miss rule needs, and the `suspension_log`.

A surface this composition needs that crosses the authorization surfaces is the **unified actor namespace**: the `actor_ref` must be the same identity under which the actor holds Permissions grants (as `subject_ref`), Session sessions (as `principal_ref`), and — where composed — Credential credentials (as Credential's own `principal_ref`, a third namespace). This unification is the precise concept the [Authenticated Actor](./authenticated-actor.md) composition establishes as a `principal_ref ↔ actor_ref` bijection, and the precise gap `demos/attributed-permissions-admin/CORNERS.md` §Cross-atom identity surface aliasing flags when it diverges. This composition names the unification as a deployment precondition (Configuration) — satisfied by Authenticated Actor as a named composing peer or by deployment convention — rather than composing Authenticated Actor directly; the enumeration's correctness is conditional on it, an externally-clearable check (Generation acceptance).

## Composition logic

### Composition state

The composition owns emergent state that wires the constituents into one suspension surface. None of this state belongs to a single constituent.

- **`actor_suspension_state`** — map from `actor_ref` to `{state, invocation_id?, intent_event_id?, suspended_at?, suspended_by_ref?, reason?, suspension_event_id?}` where `state ∈ {Active, Suspending, Suspended}`. **An `actor_ref` absent from the map is a miss, not an answer.** The index is written after the intent record is durable ([Suspend Actor] step 5 after step 4), so a crash between the two leaves a plan in the trail and no entry; a read that took absence for `Active` would let a second intent be written for the actor and let the issuance gate re-open one whose plan is already recorded. **The `index_high_water` mark is the line, and which side of it a fact lies on decides who answers.** *Above the mark* the trail answers: every read of the index — [Suspend Actor]'s gate, [Suspension Report], [Reinstate Actor]'s state read — also runs the tail read step 2 states (the trail above the mark, filtered to this actor) and takes the trail's answer wherever it is later than the entry's; an absent key with no open intent above the mark, and no entry below it, is **Active** (no suspension has been recorded). *Below the mark* the index answers, and it answers **as a truth-bearing store under `state_durability`** (Configuration): the sweep has indexed every event of this composition at or below the mark, so an entry lost there is not a miss the read can observe and repair — it is a durability breach, an actor the issuance gate reads as Active who was suspended, surfaced by check 3's trail-to-index comparison as a finding. The alternative — a keyed read that enumerates the whole retained trail below the mark on every absent key — would keep the miss rule total at the cost of a full enumeration per issuance-gate read of every never-suspended actor; this composition takes the durability obligation instead, and says so here rather than leaving the mark to convert absence silently back into an answer. `Suspending` is the declared in-cascade state: the [Suspend Actor] intent event has landed and its outcome event has not, so some of the actor's enumerated access may be closed and some may not; it is a gate state exactly as `Suspended` is (Invariant 3 — the issuance layer refuses both), and it is never silent, since [Suspension Report] shows it with the open members named. This is the composition-introduced actor lifecycle the constituents do not carry. The `suspension_event_id` binds the Suspended state to the sealed `actor.suspended` Audit Trail event that enumerates what was revoked. **Contract classification: derived index** over this composition's own audit events, per [`execution-contract.md`](../execution-contract.md) §Composition state — *rebuild procedure:* enumerate the substrate through its declared open-upper-bound sequence-range read, filter in composition code to this composition's `action_ref`s, and for each `actor_ref` take the latest of: an `actor.suspension_intended` with no `actor.suspended` naming its `invocation_id` → `Suspending` (with the intent's `invocation_id`, `intent_event_id`, operator and reason); an `actor.suspended` → `Suspended` (with its `event_id`); an `actor.reinstated` later than both → `Active`; an `actor.resume_intended` or `actor.recovery_intended` changes no state. The three Contract obligations attach — outside every atomicity surface, rebuild-on-miss, no consistency claim — where a **miss** is an absent key *or* an entry the trail contradicts (a `Suspending` entry whose outcome has landed, a `Suspended` entry whose reinstatement has), the keyed lookup that observes it is the tail read above `index_high_water`, never an enumeration of the index (§*A derived index is trustworthy only where a miss is observable*), and the rebuild is **additive**: it writes and overwrites entries from events it reads and never evicts an entry it has no event for, since past the horizon the entry is the only record. **The classification splits twice — at the mark and at the horizon.** Above the mark the element is derived and rebuild-on-miss as stated. Below the mark and inside the horizon it is complete by the sweep's construction and truth-bearing for *absence* under `state_durability`, though every entry there is still re-derivable from the retained trail (check 3 does exactly that). Past the horizon the payloads the rebuild reads are destroyed, the entry is the only surviving carrier of the actor's lifecycle state, and it is **truth-bearing** under a durability obligation (Configuration, `state_durability`) — **extraction-pending** against the forthcoming Actor Registry / Identity Provisioning atom that Actor Identity itself names as the home of the actor lifecycle this composition introduces.

- **`index_high_water`** — the log position (a `sequence_number`) through which the completion sweep has reconciled `actor_suspension_state` with the trail: every `actor.suspension_intended` at or below it has an index entry or an `actor.suspended` naming its `invocation_id`. The sweep advances it at the end of each run to the top of the range it read, held back to just below the lowest intent it could not index (an actor whose section it could not take). **Maintenance:** on every run the sweep reads the trail above the mark — the substrate's declared open-upper-bound sequence-range read, filtered in composition code to this composition's `action_ref`s — and indexes **every** event it reads, young ones included (an intent younger than `suspension_completion_bound` is indexed as `Suspending` even though the sweep will not complete it; an outcome as `Suspended`; a reinstatement as `Active`), each write made under the actor's section, since the index write is idempotent over the event and needs no bound; it then advances the mark to the top of the range read, **held back to just below the lowest event it could not index** (an actor whose section it could not take). Every read of the index, and the read-backs of steps 4 and 7, enumerate the trail only above the mark, which is what keeps the miss rule a tail read rather than a full enumeration; below the mark the index answers under `state_durability` (the `actor_suspension_state` entry above). **Contract classification: derived index** — a lost mark reads as the retained log's first position, and every tail read degrades to a full enumeration inside the horizon: slower, never wrong. Past the horizon it has nothing to say; the entries there are the truth-bearing half.

- **`suspension_log`** — append-only record of every [Suspend Actor] and [Reinstate Actor] call, whether it transitioned, was a no-op, or failed. Each entry: `entry_id` (opaque, system-generated), `invocation_id`, `actor_ref`, `operation` (`suspend` | `reinstate`), [Outcome] (`suspended` | `reinstated` | `already-suspended` | `already-active` | `revocation-failure(surface)` | `recording-failure(intent | outcome)` | `invalid-request`), [Revoked Grants] (the `grant_id` set, on success), [Revoked Sessions] (the `session_token` set, on success), `revoked_credentials` (the `credential_id` set, on success, where Credential is composed), `suspension_event_id?`, `attempted_at`. This is the composition's composition-layer query surface; the **Audit Trail substrate is the tamper-evident external-auditor record** of the same suspension events. **Contract classification, in two halves.** The entries that mirror an audit event (`suspended`, `reinstated`, and a `revocation-failure` or `recording-failure(outcome)`, each with its intent event behind it) are a **derived index** over those events — the same enumerate-and-filter rebuild as above, one entry per intent or outcome event. The entries no constituent replays — `already-suspended`, `already-active`, `invalid-request`, `recording-failure(intent)`, and any refusal that stopped before its intent record — are **extraction-pending** against the **Failed-Attempt Log** *(forthcoming)* pattern the substrate names for attempted-but-not-committed actions; until it lands, they are held here under the same `state_durability` obligation, and Generation acceptance check 3 reads them as evidence of refusals, never as evidence of acts. There is no composition-owned middle: every entry is one of those two things.

The actor's grants live in the Permissions store, its sessions in the Session store, its (optional) credential in the Credential store, and the suspension events in the Audit Trail substrate. This composition duplicates none of them; it transitions the suspension state and binds each suspension to the sealed event that records the revoked set.

The **enumerated active set** for an `actor_ref` — its active grants and active sessions — is not stored as standing state; it is computed at suspension time by querying the Permissions and Session stores. The `actor.suspension_intended` event carries that set as the **plan**, and the sealed `actor.suspended` event's enumerated `{revoked_grants, revoked_sessions, revoked_credentials}` *is* the durable, records-alone record of what the cascade closed — the two are separately recorded because their divergence is diagnostic: a planned member the outcome does not name is an owed revocation, and an outcome member the plan did not name is a conformance failure.

### Configuration

- **`unified_actor_namespace`** — a deployment-declared precondition (default `enforced`) asserting that the `actor_ref` this composition suspends is the same identity value under which the actor holds Permissions grants (as `subject_ref`), Session sessions (as Session's `principal_ref`), and — where Credential is composed — credentials (as Credential's `principal_ref`, a third namespace the knob covers). This is the **declaring source** (capability provenance) for the enumeration's correctness: this composition enumerates `subject_ref = actor_ref` grants, `principal_ref = actor_ref` sessions, and `principal_ref = actor_ref` credentials, which is complete *only* if the namespaces coincide. A deployment satisfies this via the [Authenticated Actor](./authenticated-actor.md) `principal_ref ↔ actor_ref` binding (a named composing peer) or by convention; whether the namespaces actually coincide is an externally-clearable check (Generation acceptance). A deployment whose namespaces diverge under-enumerates and produces an incomplete suspension — the audit-gap the CORNERS finding flags.
- **`revoke_credential_on_suspend`** — whether the cascade also revokes **every** effective-Active authentication credential the actor holds, across every `credential_type` (requires the optional Credential constituent; Credential Invariant 2 is per `(principal_ref, credential_type)` pair, so "the credential" is a set). Default `true` when Credential is composed; absent otherwise. When `false` or Credential is uncomposed, closing the re-authentication door is a named composing obligation (Edge cases — *Re-authentication after suspension*).
- **`audit_trail_retention_policy`** — *inherited, not re-configured.* this composition records its `actor.suspension_intended`, `actor.resume_intended`, `actor.recovery_intended`, `actor.suspended` and `actor.reinstated` events on the Audit Trail substrate, which carries the host's regulatory retention policy; `record_action` takes the policy via the substrate's configured value. The suspension events should persist at least as long as the longest retention obligation over the access they closed — often longer, for post-offboarding dispute defensibility (the access is gone; the proof it was lawfully closed must outlive it).
- **`application_actor_ref` and `application_credential`** — the composition's **service identity**: a registered actor in the substrate's Actor Identity registry under which the completion sweep attests every record it writes and performs every revocation it makes outside the invocation that owed them — the `actor.recovery_intended` record, the completing `Permissions.revoke` / `Session.revoke` / `Credential.revoke` calls (as `revoked_by_ref`, with the original operator and `intent_event_id` in the `reason`), and the compensating `actor.suspended` event, which carries the operator as `suspended_by`. The operator's credential is not in hand when the sweep runs, and attesting under it would be a false attribution. Every write an action makes inside its own invocation is attested by the calling operator; only the sweep uses this identity.
- **`suspension_completion_bound`** — the deployment-declared maximum duration of a [Suspend Actor] invocation between its intent record and its outcome record, read against the seam-injected `now`. It is the **lower edge** of the completion sweep: a `Suspending` actor whose intent is younger than it may belong to an invocation still revoking, and a sweep that completed it then would revoke beside the invocation and append a second outcome; the sweep examines nothing younger. The **upper edge** is `audit_trail_retention_policy`'s horizon: past it the intent event — the plan — is destroyed, and a `Suspending` entry there is the truth-bearing half of the index, completed from the live stores rather than from a plan the sweep can no longer read. The bound is also where the **invocation's own writing stops**: an invocation whose outcome append fails returns `recording-failure(outcome)` and yields the act — it does not retry the outcome — and an invocation that outlives the bound (a stalled process whose section lapsed) adopts an outcome the sweep landed rather than writing beside it (step 7's pre-check; §*A compensator is exclusive*). *Default:* none — and a deployment that declares none refuses to start, since the sweep's lower edge and the inequality below are unstatable without it.
- **`completion_window`** — the deployment-declared duration within which a `Suspending` actor must reach `Suspended` — every planned member closed and the outcome event landed — or be escalated as an unresolved compliance finding. Invariant 1's **liveness arm is a claim about this window and is unstatable without it**; it is the declaring source for the window Invariant 1 and Generation acceptance check 6 spend. No default is prescribed: under the offboarding regimes this composition serves, an actor half-closed for longer than the window is a control failure, and how long is a compliance determination.
- **`reconciliation_cadence`** — the interval at which the completion sweep the *Cross-store consistency under partial failure* edge case mandates is run, in addition to its mandatory run at process restart. Cadence bounds *detection*, the window bounds *repair*, and **the inequality that makes the window meetable is `suspension_completion_bound + reconciliation_cadence + outcome_write_latency < completion_window`, strictly**: a `Suspending` actor orphaned at `t` is invisible to the sweep until `t + suspension_completion_bound`, the next run is at most a cadence later, and the completing outcome lands a latency after that. *Cadence no longer than the window* is satisfied by a deployment that breaches on every orphan — a fifteen-minute bound, a ten-minute cadence and a ten-minute window pass it and close nothing in time. The hold term needs no fourth knob: a stalled-but-alive invocation can keep the sweep off an actor for at most its lease, and the lease is the bound (`per_actor_serialization`), so hold time is already inside the first term. The three terms are **checked at instance start**, and a deployment whose knobs fail the inequality refuses to start rather than starting to breach; Generation acceptance check 6 confirms the same inequality, not a fragment of it (§*Liveness is arithmetic*).
- **`outcome_write_latency`** — the deployment's disclosed bound on one `AuditTrail.record_action` landing, from the sweep's compensating call to the event's append; the third term of the inequality above. *Default:* none.
- **`per_actor_serialization`** — an **instance capability requirement**: a host-supplied mutual exclusion keyed by `actor_ref`, taken by [Suspend Actor] at its state gate (step 2) and held through its index write (step 8), by the resume arm, and by the completion sweep for every actor it examines. It is the declaring source for every serialization claim on this page — the constituents serialize their own calls, and none declares a section spanning several of them — **Its semantics, stated:** the section is released on the invocation's return (success or rejection) or on its death; where the host implements it as a **lease**, the lease is exactly `suspension_completion_bound` long — at least the bound, so an invocation inside its bound is never evicted, and no longer, so the time a stalled-but-alive holder can keep the sweep off the actor is bounded by the bound the liveness inequality already carries — and **its expiry is the invocation's terminus**: an invocation whose lease expired has yielded the act. Every write after the invocation's first write (each revocation at step 6, the outcome at step 7, the index at step 8) is made only while the section is held; an invocation that finds its section lost re-takes it before its pre-check and makes no write it cannot take the section for. Whether the host's section actually excludes across nodes, and honours the lease length, is an externally-clearable check (Generation acceptance). *Default:* not supplied — and the composition does not start without it.
- **`planned_set_cap`** — the most members `|G| + |S| + |C|` one suspension may plan, set so that the **largest record the act can write** — the sweep's compensating `actor.suspended`, carrying the full `{revoked_grants, revoked_sessions, revoked_credentials}` plus `cascade_recovery`, `suspended_by`, `plan_unavailable`, `intent_event_id` and `invocation_id` — fits the substrate's `payload_cap`. Step 3 sizes the plan against it before the intent is written, so an outcome that could not be sealed is refused before anything is revoked rather than discovered with every revocation committed (§*An outcome is sized before the intent*). The deployment sets it above the largest access set any actor may lawfully hold; an actor over it is a control failure surfaced before the act, not after. The maximal envelope is constructed and checked against `payload_cap` at instance start. *Default:* none.
- **`clock_skew_allowance`** — the declared allowance under which a stamp this composition wrote from its seam (`suspended_at`, `intended_at`) may be compared with a stamp a constituent wrote from its own (`granted_at`, `issued_at`, `registered_at`, `revoked_at`). The comparison may narrow a check's verdict, never decide one: a member whose stamp falls inside the allowance of `suspended_at` is reported *inconclusive by skew*, not as a failure (Generation acceptance, standing rule). No write on this page rests on a cross-seam comparison; the sweep's lower edge compares `intended_at` with the sweep's own seam-injected `now`, two readings of one clock (§*A stamp from another seam never decides a write alone*). *Default:* none.
- **`state_durability`** — the durability the deployment owes the two composition stores, stated as an ordering: **at least as durable as the Permissions and Session stores they gate**. Above `index_high_water` `actor_suspension_state` is derived and a lost entry is a rebuild trigger the tail read fires; below the mark — inside the horizon, where the sweep has indexed every event, and past it, where an entry is the only carrier of the actor's lifecycle state — a lost entry is a finding, an actor the issuance gate reads as Active who was suspended, rather than a miss to repair (Composition state states why the mark rather than the horizon is the line). The `suspension_log`'s refusal entries are held under the same obligation until the Failed-Attempt Log pattern owns them; `index_high_water` needs none (a lost mark degrades a read, never an answer).

### Primitive policies

- **`actor_ref`** — opaque reference to the actor being suspended; the key of `actor_suspension_state` and the join key (`= subject_ref = principal_ref` under `unified_actor_namespace`). Must contain at least one non-whitespace character (`invalid-request`). Byte-identity equality; never normalized.
- **`suspended_by_ref`** — non-empty reference to the operator performing the suspension; flows to `Session.revoke` (as `revoked_by_ref`), `Credential.revoke` (as `revoked_by_ref`), and `AuditTrail.record_action` (as `actor_ref`), so the records attribute the suspension to one operator. Whether `suspended_by_ref` is *authorized* to suspend actors is a named composing concept (Edge cases — *Suspension authorization*), exactly as Login leaves logout authorization to a composing layer.
- **`credential`** — the suspending operator's opaque credential, consumed by `AuditTrail.record_action` (and the Actor Identity reached through the substrate) — first at the intent record, which is where it is verified before anything irreversible happens (Invariant 5), and again at the outcome record; never persisted by this composition.
- **`invocation_id`** — one fresh identifier per state-changing invocation, injected at the composition's I/O seam alongside `now` (the Logic Confinement Principle — minted nowhere inside a transition) and written into every audit event, every `actor_suspension_state` entry, and every `suspension_log` entry the invocation produces. It is the key the completion sweep pairs an intent to its outcome by, and the key its pre-check reads the trail with, so a stale index never causes a second outcome event or a second revocation. A resume (step 2) and the sweep write under the **cascade's** `invocation_id` — the act's key, taken from the intent — and the resume carries its own seam-injected id as `resume_invocation_id` on its `actor.resume_intended` record, so every outcome pairs to its intent on one field.
- **`reason`** — non-empty string naming why the actor is suspended; carried into each `Session.revoke` / `Credential.revoke` reason and the `actor.suspended` event. Mandatory (a suspension without a stated reason is a compliance finding — Permissions, Session, and Credential each require a non-empty reason on revoke).
- **`grant_id`** / **`session_token`** / **`credential_id`** — opaque constituent handles surfaced by the enumeration (`credential_id` one per effective-Active credential, across every `credential_type`); passed to the respective `revoke`. Opaque; never normalized. The three enumerations together are bounded by `planned_set_cap` (Configuration), sized at step 3 before the intent.

No primitive is case-sensitivity-normalized at the composition layer; deployments wanting normalization wire it at the calling layer before invoking composition actions.

### Action wiring

The composition exposes three actions: one emergent multi-surface suspension ([Suspend Actor]), one read-only query ([Suspension Report]), and one thin lifecycle-reversal ([Reinstate Actor]).

**Uniform constituent-rejection mapping, and the one ordering every action follows.** No audit write on this page is inside any transaction, and no constituent revocation is undone once it commits: [Suspend Actor] runs **durable intent → irreversible domain mutations → durable outcome → derived index**. A `Permissions.revoke → not-active` or `Session.revoke → already-terminal` is **benign** — the target is already terminal, which is the cascade's goal (the TOCTOU already-terminal case), counted as skipped, never a failure. A non-benign revoke failure (`storage-failure`, or a `not-known` on a member the snapshot returned) does not stop the cascade and undoes nothing: the cascade continues over the remaining members, the failed member is named, and the actor stays `Suspending` for the completion sweep. The substrate's taxonomy is `invalid-credential | invalid-request | recording-failure(step)`, mapped per step, and **the `(step)` payload is load-bearing at every position, the intent's included**: `recording-failure(step-4)` means the event is appended and attested with only its retention placement failed — the invocation reads the event's id back through the substrate's declared sequence-range read over the log's tail (from `index_high_water`, Composition state, to the open upper bound), **filtered in composition code** to this composition's `action_ref` carrying this `invocation_id` (the substrate indexes no payload field; the selection is the composition's own), and proceeds as landed with a hard alert; `recording-failure(step-2 | step-3)` means the event is not in the log. `invalid-request` is a deployment fault — a mis-derived payload cap, or the substrate's own retention-configuration source, on which the event **is** appended — and the same read-back decides which. **What the composition exports carries the position, not the step**: `recording-failure(intent)` at [Suspend Actor]'s boundary means nothing of this call is committed and it may be re-run as a fresh call; `recording-failure(outcome)` means the revocations stand, the outcome is owed, and a re-run resumes rather than restarts (the signature block, and step 7).

#### `suspend_actor`

```
suspend_actor(actor_ref, suspended_by_ref, credential, reason) →
  {suspended, revoked_grants, revoked_sessions, revoked_credentials, event_id, resumed_by?}
 | rejected(
   invalid-request
  | invalid-credential
  | already-suspended
  | revocation-failure(surface, open_members)
  | recording-failure(intent | outcome)
  )
```

Suspends an actor by recording the plan, revoking every active Permissions grant and every active Session (and, where Credential is composed, every effective-Active Credential the actor holds) one by one, transitioning the actor to Suspended, and sealing the complete revoked set into one attributed Audit Trail event. Steps:

1. Validate: `actor_ref`, `suspended_by_ref`, `credential`, `reason` each non-empty. Any violation → append `suspension_log` `{operation: suspend, outcome: invalid-request}`; return `rejected(invalid-request)`. Stop.
2. **Suspension-state gate (the cascade ordering, Invariant 3) — under the per-`actor_ref` section.** Take the per-`actor_ref` serialization here (`per_actor_serialization`, Configuration; *Concurrency*), ahead of the read, and hold it through step 8 — released on every return, success or rejection, and on the invocation's death; where the host leases it, the lease's expiry is this invocation's terminus and every later write below is made only while the section is held: two calls that both read the gate before either wrote its intent would both write one. Read `actor_suspension_state[actor_ref]`. **The miss rule (Composition state): the trail answers above the mark, the index below it.** Whatever the entry says, also run the tail read — the substrate's open-upper-bound sequence-range read from `index_high_water`, filtered in composition code to this composition's `action_ref`s carrying `suspended_actor = actor_ref` — and take the trail's answer wherever it is later than the entry's: an `actor.suspension_intended` there with no `actor.suspended` naming its `invocation_id` is a `Suspending` actor whose index write was lost (a crash between step 4 and step 5) and whose entry the sweep has not yet written; an `actor.suspended` or `actor.reinstated` there is a landed transition whose index write was lost. Write the entry now (the rebuild) and proceed on it. An absent key with no open intent above the mark is **Active** — below the mark the index is complete by the sweep's construction and answers under `state_durability` — and proceeds. `Suspended` → append `suspension_log` `{outcome: already-suspended}`; return [Already Suspended] and change no suspension state — the cascade does **not** re-run. `Suspending` → the actor's earlier cascade did not complete: this call **resumes** it rather than starting a second. Compute the open members as the intent's plan less the members the stores show closed (re-read each planned member by its handle: one still `Active` — or, for a credential, still effective-Active — is open; every other planned member is closed or benignly terminal), then **record the resume under the resumer's credential before touching any of them**: `AuditTrail.record_action(action_ref = actor.resume_intended, actor_ref = suspended_by_ref, credential, data = {invocation_id: <the cascade's, from the intent>, resume_invocation_id: <this call's seam-injected id>, intent_event_id, suspended_actor: actor_ref, resumed_by: suspended_by_ref, open_members, reason: <the intent's reason>, resumed_at = now})` → `resume_event_id`. This is where the substrate verifies the **resumer's** credential — the earlier intent verified the operator who planned the cascade, not whoever calls to finish it, and a resume that revoked first and attested at the outcome would close access on an unverified claim, with the constituent records naming a principal nobody had checked (Invariant 5). Its arms are the intent record's (step 4): `invalid-credential` → `rejected(invalid-credential)`, nothing touched; `recording-failure(step-4)` and `invalid-request` whose read-back finds the record → proceed as landed with a hard alert; `recording-failure(step-2 | step-3)` and `invalid-request` whose read-back finds nothing → `rejected(recording-failure(intent))` / `rejected(invalid-request)`, nothing touched by this call. The cascade then continues at step 6 over the open members under the earlier invocation's `intent_event_id` and `invocation_id`, with `revoked_by_ref = suspended_by_ref` (the resumer, whose credential the resume record verified) and `reason = "suspension-resume:" + intent_event_id + ": " + <the intent's reason>` on every constituent revocation, so each constituent record traces to the plan; it writes no second `actor.suspension_intended`. A resume is what a retry after `revocation-failure`, after `recording-failure(outcome)`, or after a crash lawfully is.
3. **Snapshot the active set (read-only), and size the act.** Enumerate the actor's active grants `G = {grant_id : subject_ref = actor_ref ∧ status = Active}` from the Permissions store, active sessions `S = {session_token : principal_ref = actor_ref ∧ status = Active}` from the Session store, and (where Credential is composed and `revoke_credential_on_suspend`) **every effective-Active credential** `C = {credential_id : principal_ref = actor_ref ∧ effective-Active}` from the Credential store — across **every** `credential_type`, because Credential Invariant 2 bounds effective-Active *per `(principal_ref, credential_type)` pair*, not per principal: an actor holding a password and a hardware key holds two effective-Active credentials, and revoking "the" credential leaves the other authenticating (Credential Invariant 4 is pair-scoped too). Each read is the constituent's own declared queryable surface — Permissions' full grant set, Session's `principal_ref`-queryable store, Credential's queryable store with its derived effective status — under `unified_actor_namespace`. The snapshot runs inside the per-`actor_ref` section taken at step 2 — not a transaction: nothing below is undone by anything. A grant, session, or credential created *after* the snapshot is not in `G`/`S`/`C` and is not covered by this suspension (snapshot-scoped — Invariant 1's stated bound, and the *Suspension is snapshot-scoped* edge case). An empty `G`, `S` and `C` is a valid suspension. **Then size the largest record this act can write, before the intent:** if `|G| + |S| + |C|` exceeds `planned_set_cap` (Configuration — the cardinality under which the sweep's compensating `actor.suspended`, the largest payload the act can produce, fits the substrate's `payload_cap`), append `suspension_log` `{operation: suspend, outcome: invalid-request, detail: plan-exceeds-cap}`, raise a hard alert, and return `rejected(invalid-request)` with nothing written and nothing revoked: a plan the outcome could never seal is refused here, not discovered at step 7 with every revocation committed and the binding un-writable for as long as the set stays over the cap (§*An outcome is sized before the intent*).
4. **Intent record — the plan, and the credential gate.** `AuditTrail.record_action(action_ref = actor.suspension_intended, actor_ref = suspended_by_ref, credential, data = {invocation_id, suspended_actor: actor_ref, planned_grants: G, planned_sessions: S, planned_credentials: C, reason, intended_at = now})` → `intent_event_id`. This is where the substrate verifies the operator's credential against the actor registry — **before any revocation**, so an unverified caller never closes anyone's access (Invariant 5). The arms follow the uniform mapping above, and **not every arm is a clean pre-state rejection: the substrate's `step-4` arm and its retention-source `invalid-request` arrive with the intent already appended**, and a caller told otherwise retries as a fresh call and lands a second plan for one actor — two intents for the sweep to pair, then two completions, a check-3 breach. So: `invalid-credential` → `rejected(invalid-credential)`, nothing appended. `recording-failure(step-4)`, and `invalid-request` whose read-back finds the intent → the intent **is** in the log: take its `event_id` as `intent_event_id` and proceed to step 5 as landed, with a hard alert on the retention configuration. `recording-failure(step-2 | step-3)`, and `invalid-request` whose read-back finds nothing → nothing is in the log: `rejected(recording-failure(intent))` / `rejected(invalid-request)`, retryable as a fresh call. The read-back is the one the preamble names — the substrate's sequence-range read over the tail above `index_high_water`, filtered in composition code to an `actor.suspension_intended` carrying this `invocation_id`. The plan is carried because the outcome enumerates separately and their divergence is diagnostic (Composition state).
5. **Transition to `Suspending`** — `actor_suspension_state[actor_ref] = {state: Suspending, invocation_id, intent_event_id, suspended_by_ref, reason}`, an index write over the intent event just landed (rebuildable from it; a lost write is a rebuild trigger — the one step 2's tail read and the sweep's rebuild fire, since an absent key is never read as `Active` while this intent is open).
6. **Cascade — revoke every member, continuing past failures.** Each call is a committed, irreversible write on its atom's store; none is enlisted in any transaction and none is undone. On a resume (step 2) `revoked_by_ref` is the resumer and `reason` the prefixed form step 2 states — the principal the constituent record names is always the one whose credential a record in the trail verified. Each revocation is made only while the section is held: an invocation whose lease has expired has yielded, and it re-takes the section before making any further write — or, failing that, returns `rejected(recording-failure(outcome))` with the members it closed standing and the rest the sweep's:
  - For each `grant_id ∈ G` still Active: `Permissions.revoke(grant_id)`. `ok` → add to [Revoked Grants]. `not-active` (a concurrent revocation already terminalized it) → benign, add to [Revoked Grants]. `not-known` (a grant the query returned but the store no longer knows — structurally near-impossible, since Permissions never deletes grants, Invariant 10) → a **non-benign** failure: add to `open_members` with `permissions`, surface a high-priority store-inconsistency finding in `suspension_log`, and **continue**. `storage-failure` → add to `open_members` and continue.
  - For each `session_token ∈ S` still Active: `Session.revoke(session_token, revoked_by_ref = suspended_by_ref, reason)`. `revoked` → add to [Revoked Sessions]. `already-terminal` → benign, add to [Revoked Sessions]. `not-known` → non-benign as above (Session is durable, Invariant 9), added to `open_members` and continue. `storage-failure` → add to `open_members` and continue.
  - If `revoke_credential_on_suspend` and Credential is composed, for each `credential_id ∈ C` still effective-Active: `Credential.revoke(credential_id, revoked_by_ref = suspended_by_ref, reason)`. `revoked` → add to `revoked_credentials`. `already-terminal` (a concurrent revocation or rotation, or a credential that lapsed since the snapshot — Credential returns it by derivation, writing nothing) → benign, add to `revoked_credentials`. `not-known` (the store no longer knows a record the query just returned) → non-benign as above, add to `open_members` with `credential` and continue. `invalid-request` (Credential refused the reference or reason this composition passed — a deployment fault, since step 1 validated both non-empty) → non-benign, add to `open_members` and continue. `storage-failure` → add to `open_members` and continue.
  If `open_members` is non-empty when the cascade ends: append `suspension_log` `{outcome: revocation-failure(<surfaces>), open_members}`; the actor stays `Suspending` with every revocation that committed standing; return `rejected(revocation-failure(surface, open_members))` — the caller retries (step 2's resume arm) or the completion sweep closes the rest within `completion_window`. **Nothing is rolled back, and the rejection says exactly which doors are still open.**
7. **Seal the act — `record_action` carrying the complete enumeration (Invariant 2), after every revocation has committed.** First, still inside the per-`actor_ref` section, **re-read the trail for an outcome already naming this `invocation_id`** — the same tail read as step 4's read-back. The section may be a host lease, and a lease can expire under an invocation that stalled; an invocation that finds its section lost has yielded the act, and it **re-takes the section before this pre-check** — never between the sweep's pre-check and the sweep's append — and makes no write it cannot take the section for. Under the section, an outcome already naming this `invocation_id` (the sweep's, landed while the lease was lost) is **adopted as this invocation's own** — take its `event_id` and proceed to step 8 as landed — rather than appended beside, which the seal would then protect (§*A compensator is exclusive*: one writer per act, and where the invocation holds no clock to yield at its bound, its outcome step runs the pre-check under the act's key). Otherwise `AuditTrail.record_action(action_ref = actor.suspended, actor_ref = suspended_by_ref, credential, data = {invocation_id, intent_event_id, suspended_actor: actor_ref, suspended_by: <the intent's operator>, revoked_grants, revoked_sessions, revoked_credentials, reason, suspended_at = now, resumed_by?, resume_event_id?, cascade_recovery?})` → `event_id`, where `resumed_by`, `resume_event_id` and `cascade_recovery = true` are present exactly when this invocation resumed an earlier cascade at step 2 — the attesting `actor_ref` is then the resumer, `data.suspended_by` still names the operator whose intent planned the act, and a completed cascade is distinguishable from a clean one on its outcome whoever completed it (Invariant 1). The `data` carries the **complete revoked set** — never a digest of it: check 2 compares the enumeration to the plan member by member, and nothing that reads a set can read a digest; the set is bounded at step 3 so the record fits the cap — so the enumeration is sealed and tamper-evident. **The ordering is the guarantee**: because the append follows every revocation, an `actor.suspended` event that exists always has its revocations behind it — the direction an auditor reads, and the direction the earlier rollback wiring made violable (a `step-4` failure inside a transaction that then rolled the revokes back left a sealed event enumerating revocations that had been undone — a false record nothing could withdraw). Arms: `recording-failure(step-4)`, and `invalid-request` whose read-back finds the event → the event is appended; take its id and proceed to step 8 with a hard alert; `recording-failure(step-2 | step-3)`, `invalid-credential` (a rotation between the two writes), and `invalid-request` whose read-back finds nothing → the revocations stand and the outcome is owed: append `suspension_log` `{outcome: recording-failure(outcome)}`, leave the actor `Suspending`, and return `rejected(recording-failure(outcome))` — **the invocation does not retry the outcome write**; it yields the act here, and the completion sweep lands the outcome once the intent is older than `suspension_completion_bound` (*Cross-store consistency under partial failure*). The position rides the exported code, `recording-failure(intent | outcome)` on the signature: `intent` tells the caller nothing was committed and the whole action may be re-run as a fresh call; `outcome` tells the caller the revocations **stand** and a re-run resumes rather than restarts (§*A composition's own rejection arm carries the retry bit*).
8. **Transition to `Suspended`** — `actor_suspension_state[actor_ref] = {state: Suspended, invocation_id, suspended_at: now, suspended_by_ref, reason, suspension_event_id: event_id}` (an index write over the outcome event). Append `suspension_log` `{operation: suspend, outcome: suspended, revoked_grants, revoked_sessions, revoked_credentials, suspension_event_id: event_id}`. Release the section. Return `{suspended, revoked_grants, revoked_sessions, revoked_credentials, event_id, resumed_by?}`. The actor is now **Suspended** with zero active grants and zero active sessions over the snapshot (Invariant 1).

#### `suspension_report` (read-only)

```
suspension_report(actor_ref) →
  {state, intent_event_id?, open_members?, suspended_at?, suspended_by_ref?, reason?, revoked_grants?, revoked_sessions?, revoked_credentials?, suspension_event_id?}
 | rejected(invalid-request)
```

The read-only surface an auditor, operator, or the actor themselves uses to read an actor's suspension status and, if Suspended, the complete set that was revoked. Resolves `actor_suspension_state[actor_ref]` under step 2's miss rule — the tail read above `index_high_water` runs on every read and its answer wins where later than the entry's; below the mark the index answers under `state_durability` — and returns: no open intent above the mark and no entry, or `Active`, → `{state: Active}`; `Suspending` → `{state: Suspending, intent_event_id, suspended_by_ref, reason, open_members}` with the open members re-derived from the plan against the live stores — the state the issuance gate refuses on and the operator reads the open doors from; `Suspended` → the full suspension record including `suspended_at`, `suspended_by_ref`, `reason`, the `suspension_event_id`, and the enumerated [Revoked Grants] / [Revoked Sessions] / `revoked_credentials` (read from the sealed event the `suspension_event_id` binds, the authoritative record). **No Audit Trail event is produced** — a pure read. Logging *who read a suspension report* belongs to a composing access-log concept, named rather than absorbed.

#### `reinstate_actor`

```
reinstate_actor(actor_ref, reinstated_by_ref, credential, reason) →
  {reinstated, event_id}
 | rejected(invalid-request | invalid-credential | already-active | recording-failure)
```

Lifts an actor's suspension by transitioning Suspended → Active and recording an attributed `actor.reinstated` event. **It does not restore any revoked grant or session** — those are terminal in their constituents (Permissions Invariant 3, Session Invariant 4) and cannot be un-revoked; re-authorizing the actor means issuing *fresh* grants and sessions through the issuance layer, which is not this composition's surface. Steps: validate inputs (`invalid-request`); read `actor_suspension_state[actor_ref]` under step 2's miss rule (the tail read above `index_high_water` runs here too, so a `Suspended` entry whose reinstatement already landed unindexed is read as `Active`, and a lost `Suspended` entry above the mark is rebuilt before it is judged); if `state ≠ Suspended` → [Already Active] (no-op); else record `AuditTrail.record_action(action_ref = actor.reinstated, actor_ref = reinstated_by_ref, credential, data = {invocation_id, reinstated_actor: actor_ref, reason, reinstated_at = now})` → `event_id` — the single load-bearing write, which is also where the credential is verified, so no state depends on an unverified claim (Invariant 5's first answer) — mapping `invalid-credential` → `rejected(invalid-credential)`; `recording-failure(step-4)` and the retention-source `invalid-request` → the event is appended, read back by `invocation_id` and proceed; `recording-failure(step-2 | step-3)` and the payload-source `invalid-request` → `rejected(recording-failure)` / `rejected(invalid-request)`, nothing changed; then set `actor_suspension_state[actor_ref] = {state: Active}` — an index write over the event just landed, rebuildable from it, so a failure here is a rebuild trigger and never a partial; append `suspension_log`; return `{reinstated, event_id}`. A `Suspending` actor cannot be reinstated: [Reinstate Actor] returns [Already Active] with the reason `suspending` for it — its cascade is completed first, then lifted. Reinstatement is the state-machine's reverse edge only — re-provisioning access is the composing layer's job (Edge cases — *Reinstatement does not restore access*).

### The load-bearing wiring decision — multi-surface revocation is complete by construction or by completion, never undone; suspension gates the cascade

The composition's structural reason to exist has two halves.

**Half 1 — every active grant and every active session is revoked, and a cascade that stops is finished, not reversed.** *[Suspend Actor] records its plan, snapshots both authorization surfaces, revokes every member of both (and, where Credential is composed, every effective-Active credential the actor holds), transitions the actor to Suspended, and seals the complete revoked set — ordered across durability boundaries; on any non-benign failure the revocations that committed stand, the actor is visibly `Suspending`, and a mandated sweep closes the rest.*

*Principle.* An actor's ability to act is spread across two independent surfaces (grants and sessions), each with its own multiplicity; closing the actor out requires closing *both surfaces completely*, and a partial close is dangerous exactly when it is invisible. *Likely objection:* why not revoke grants and sessions independently — let an admin call `Permissions.revoke` per grant and `Session.revoke` per session — rather than wrapping them in one composition action? *Mechanism that resolves it:* because independent revocation has no completeness guarantee and no record of incompleteness — an admin who revokes the grants but forgets a session, or whose session-revocation step fails midway, leaves a half-open actor and no record that the closure was incomplete. This composition snapshots both surfaces, records the plan under the operator's verified credential, revokes every enumerated member, and seals what it closed. **An earlier revision promised more — that on any failure the whole cascade would roll back under one transaction, so the actor was either fully de-authorized or unchanged — and the promise was empty:** a revoked grant is terminal (Permissions Invariant 3), a revoked session is absorbing (Session Invariant 4), a revoked credential is terminal, and the substrate's audit append cannot be withdrawn; no host transaction could enlist any of them, and the wiring that claimed to made a *false record* reachable — a sealed `actor.suspended` event enumerating revocations the rollback had undone. What replaces it is the honest form: the intent event names the plan; every revocation that commits stands; the actor whose cascade stopped is `Suspending`, which the issuance gate treats as closed and [Suspension Report] shows with the open members named; and the completion sweep closes the remainder within `completion_window`. A benign already-terminal target (a concurrent revocation) is counted toward the goal, not treated as a failure. *Result:* after [Suspend Actor] returns success, the actor provably holds zero active grants and zero active sessions over the snapshot; after it returns `revocation-failure`, the records say exactly which doors are still open and the sweep is what closes them — the multi-surface completeness no single constituent provides, without a rollback no constituent could have honored.

**Half 2 — the Active→Suspending transition gates the cascade, and the sealed event records exactly what was closed.** *The cascade runs only on the Active→Suspending edge (a second suspension is a no-op, a call against a `Suspending` actor is a resume under its own attested resume record), and the `actor.suspended` event enumerates every revoked `grant_id`, `session_token`, and `credential_id`.*

*Principle.* The suspension must be idempotent (running it twice must not double-revoke or produce two conflicting records) and its completeness must be provable from the records alone. *Likely objection:* doesn't recording the whole revoked set duplicate what the Permissions and Session stores already show (each revoked grant/session carries its own `revoked_at`)? *Mechanism that resolves it:* the per-constituent revocation records show *that* each grant/session was revoked, but only the composition knows they were revoked *as one suspension act* and which set constituted "the actor's access at suspension time." The state gate (step 2) makes the cascade start exactly once — a second [Suspend Actor] sees `Suspended` and returns `already-suspended`, or sees `Suspending` and resumes under the same intent — and the sealed `actor.suspended` event binds the complete `{revoked_grants, revoked_sessions, revoked_credentials}` enumeration into one tamper-evident record, so an auditor reads one event and sees the whole closure, not a scatter of independently-revoked records they must reassemble and hope is complete. The intent event's plan is the second record the auditor reads: a member the plan named that the outcome does not is an owed closure, and the pair is what makes *complete* checkable rather than asserted. *Result:* the suspension is idempotent (gated on the state), its completeness is records-alone provable (plan against sealed enumeration), resting on the substrate's Tamper Evidence so a later attempt to shrink the recorded set to hide a missed surface breaks the seal.

## Composition-level invariants

These invariants emerge from the composition; none belongs to a single constituent. Each invariant's *Rests on:* clause is its **capability-provenance record** (see [`pressure-testing.md`](../pressure-testing.md) §Capability provenance): every clause traces to a declared source — a named constituent invariant or action, a deployment-declared configuration capability, or a composition-introduced surface this composition owns.

- **Invariant 1 — Completeness of multi-surface revocation (load-bearing; safety plus liveness).** *Safety — no `Suspended` actor with open access:* after `suspend_actor(actor_ref, …)` returns success, the actor holds **zero active grants** (no `Active` Permissions grant with `subject_ref = actor_ref`) and **zero active sessions** (no `Active` Session with `principal_ref = actor_ref`) — and, where Credential is composed with `revoke_credential_on_suspend`, **zero effective-Active credentials** on any `credential_type` — over the snapshot taken in step 3, and the `actor.suspended` event is appended only after every one of those revocations has committed, so there is no reachable state in which the records say Suspended while an enumerated grant or session remains Active. *Safety — no unsurfaced partial:* a cascade that stops — a non-benign revoke failure that returns, or a crash between the intent and the outcome — leaves the actor `Suspending`, never `Active` and never `Suspended`: the intent event is trail-resident, the index reads it, [Suspension Report] names the open members, and the issuance gate treats `Suspending` as closed. *Liveness — every `Suspending` actor reaches `Suspended` within the declared `completion_window`*, the remaining members closed and the outcome landed by the caller's resume or by the completion sweep — **one writer per act**: the invocation yields the outcome at `recording-failure(outcome)` and never retries it, the sweep examines nothing younger than `suspension_completion_bound`, both serialize on the actor's key, and a stalled invocation adopts a landed outcome rather than writing beside it — with a completed cascade distinguishable from a clean one by `cascade_recovery = true` on its outcome event, whoever completed it, and the window meetable only under the inequality `reconciliation_cadence` states. **Snapshot-scoped, modulo concurrent issuance:** a grant or session created *after* the step-3 snapshot is not covered by this suspension (the composition revokes but never issues, so it cannot block a concurrent `Permissions.grant` / `Session.issue`); closing that gap is the composing-layer obligation to gate issuance on `actor_suspension_state` — `Suspending` and `Suspended` alike (Edge cases — *Suspension is snapshot-scoped*). **What is deliberately not claimed:** that a failed [Suspend Actor] leaves the actor unchanged. It does not and cannot — every revocation that committed is terminal by its atom's own invariant — and an earlier revision's claim that it did rested on a rollback no constituent declares. *This is the load-bearing claim and the formal-model subject; the model owes re-derivation over the ordered cascade with the `Suspending` state and the completion sweep (the Ledger's `formal:` line), and its twin is the retired rollback wiring, which reaches a sealed outcome event over undone revocations.* *Rests on:* Permissions **`revoke`** + **Invariant 2 (status monotonicity Active→Revoked)** + **Invariant 3 (revocation terminal)** + **Invariant 8 (revoked grants confer no permission)**; Session **`revoke`** + **Invariant 4 (revocation absorbing)**; the Permissions **`subject_ref`-queryable grant store** and Session **`principal_ref`-queryable session store** (the enumeration capabilities both atoms declare); **Audit Trail's append-only contract — that an appended event cannot be withdrawn — the declared source of the durability boundary this invariant is organized around**; the composition-introduced **intent → revoke → outcome ordering** (Action wiring), the **`Suspending` state**, the **completion sweep**, and the deployment-declared **`completion_window`**, **`suspension_completion_bound`**, **`reconciliation_cadence`** and **`outcome_write_latency`** (Configuration), which make the liveness arm a bounded claim rather than an "eventually" — the strict inequality over the four is the claim; the host **`per_actor_serialization`** section (the one-writer rule's declaring source); **`planned_set_cap`** (what makes the outcome writable under the substrate's `payload_cap`); and the **`unified_actor_namespace`** configuration capability (the enumeration's join correctness, across all three constituent namespaces).

- **Invariant 2 — Audit completeness.** Every successful [Suspend Actor] produces exactly one `actor.suspended` Audit Trail event whose `data` enumerates **every** `grant_id` in [Revoked Grants], **every** `session_token` in [Revoked Sessions], and — where Credential is composed — **every** `credential_id` in `revoked_credentials`, across every `credential_type` — the complete set the cascade closed, with no revoked surface omitted from the record. The enumeration is part of the sealed event payload, so an auditor can confirm the suspension's completeness from one tamper-evident record. The event is paired to its intent by `invocation_id` and `intent_event_id`, and the intent's plan is what the enumeration is checked against: every planned member is either in the enumeration or benignly terminal, and every enumerated member was planned. *Defended in-line:* step 7 carries the complete `{revoked_grants, revoked_sessions, revoked_credentials}` — never a digest, the set being bounded by `planned_set_cap` at step 3 — into the `record_action` `data`, appended after every revocation has committed (step 6). *Rests on:* Audit Trail **`record_action`** and **Invariant 1 (attribution coverage)** reached through the named substrate, the substrate's **Tamper Evidence** sealing (so the enumeration cannot be silently shrunk), and the composition-introduced cascade that assembles the complete set before sealing.

- **Invariant 3 — Suspension cascade ordering.** The cascade **starts** only on the Active → Suspending transition: [Suspend Actor] against an actor already in `Suspended` returns `already-suspended` and changes no state (no second cascade, no second event, no double-revoke); against an actor in `Suspending` it **resumes** the earlier cascade under its intent, writing its own `actor.resume_intended` under the resumer's credential, no second `actor.suspension_intended` and no second outcome (the resume, the outcome step and the completion sweep pre-check the trail by `invocation_id` under the actor's section, so an outcome that already landed is never re-appended); and the suspension state is monotonic on the suspend edge (an actor in `Suspending` or `Suspended` stays there until [Reinstate Actor] explicitly reverses a completed one). The benign TOCTOU case — a grant/session the cascade targets that a concurrent process already terminalized — is counted toward completion (`not-active`/`already-terminal` → skipped, not aborted), mirroring Login's Final Critique 4 (finding Final Critique 1) already-terminal handling. *Defended in-line:* step 2's state gate rejects an already-Suspended actor and resumes a Suspending one before any new intent; step 6 treats already-terminal targets as benign. *Rests on:* the composition-introduced **`actor_suspension_state` gate** (Action wiring step 2) and the benign-already-terminal mapping (step 6), with the terminal-target semantics resting on Permissions **Invariant 3 (revocation terminal)** and Session **Invariant 5 (terminal-state absorbing)**.

- **Invariant 4 — Constituent invariants preserved.** All Permissions invariants (1–10) hold over the grant store, all Session invariants (1–11) hold over the session store, all Actor Identity invariants (1–9) hold over the attestation store, all Audit Trail composition-level invariants (1–8) hold over the substrate (and transitively all Event Log, Retention Window, and Tamper Evidence invariants), and — when composed — all Credential invariants (1–11) hold over the credential store. This composition weakens no constituent invariant; it revokes through each constituent's own `revoke` and records through the substrate's own `record_action`. *Rests on:* each constituent's own Generation acceptance bar over its store instance.

- **Invariant 5 — Authentication precedes commitment.** At [Suspend Actor] no constituent `revoke` — each an irreversible write — is reached on any path before the credential of the principal that revocation will be attributed to has validated against the actor registry's public material: the intent record (step 4) is the mechanism for a fresh cascade and the `actor.resume_intended` record (step 2) for a resumed one, each standing before the first revocation its invocation makes, so an actor's access is never closed on an unverified claim, the constituent's own `revoked_by_ref` always names a principal a record in the trail verified, and `invalid-credential` at either is a refusal with nothing revoked by that call. At [Reinstate Actor] the single `record_action` is itself the load-bearing write, so nothing commits ahead of the verification and no second mechanism is needed. **What this does and does not establish:** a successful validation establishes that material matching the operator's registered verifier was presented at that instant — not that the presenter *is* that operator, not that the presentation is bound to a channel, and nothing about whether the operator was *authorized* to suspend this actor (Edge cases — *Suspension authorization*). *Rests on:* Audit Trail's `record_action` and the Actor Identity attestation reached through it. *Defended in-line:* the ordering is visible in [Suspend Actor]'s step list, and Generation acceptance check 6 tests it from the records alone.

Invariant 1 (complete multi-surface revocation) is the emergent guarantee the composition exists to provide; Invariant 2 (audit completeness) is what makes that guarantee provable from one sealed record against one recorded plan; Invariant 3 (cascade ordering) makes the suspension idempotent and orders the cascade behind the state transition; Invariant 5 makes the operator a verified one before anything is closed. Invariant 4 preserves every constituent guarantee underneath.

## Examples

### Walkthrough — offboarding a departing employee, end to end

A bank deploys this composition over its access surfaces with `unified_actor_namespace = enforced` (the `actor_ref` is the employee's directory identity, used as `subject_ref` for grants and `principal_ref` for sessions, bound via Authenticated Actor), `completion_window = 15m` with `suspension_completion_bound = 5m`, `reconciliation_cadence = 5m` and `outcome_write_latency = 30s` (five plus five plus a half is strictly under fifteen — the instance-start check passes), `planned_set_cap = 500`, Credential composed with `revoke_credential_on_suspend = true`, and the substrate's `audit_trail_retention_policy = sox_7_year`.

1. **Suspension trigger.** An employee resigns; HR's offboarding workflow calls `suspend_actor(actor_ref = "emp_4821", suspended_by_ref = "hr_offboard_svc", credential = <svc-cred>, reason = "employment-ended-2026-06-10")`.
2. **State gate.** The section for `emp_4821` is taken; `actor_suspension_state["emp_4821"]` is absent, and the tail read above `index_high_water` finds no open `actor.suspension_intended` for it → Active → proceed (Invariant 3).
3. **Snapshot and plan.** The Permissions store yields three active grants for `subject_ref = "emp_4821"`: `g_finread`, `g_wireinit`, `g_reports`. The Session store yields two active sessions for `principal_ref = "emp_4821"`: `tok_laptop`, `tok_phone`. The Credential store yields two effective-Active credentials for `principal_ref = "emp_4821"` — a `password` and a `public-key` hardware token: `cred_emp4821_pw`, `cred_emp4821_key`. Seven members, under the cap. `AuditTrail.record_action(actor.suspension_intended, "hr_offboard_svc", <svc-cred>, data = {invocation_id, suspended_actor: "emp_4821", planned_grants: {g_finread, g_wireinit, g_reports}, planned_sessions: {tok_laptop, tok_phone}, planned_credentials: {cred_emp4821_pw, cred_emp4821_key}, reason})` → `ev_susp_int_001` — the service credential is verified here, before anything is revoked; `actor_suspension_state["emp_4821"] = {Suspending, …}`.
4. **Cascade.** `Permissions.revoke(g_finread) → ok`; `revoke(g_wireinit) → ok`; `revoke(g_reports) → ok` (`revoked_grants = {g_finread, g_wireinit, g_reports}`). `Session.revoke(tok_laptop, "hr_offboard_svc", "employment-ended-2026-06-10") → revoked`; `revoke(tok_phone, …) → revoked` (`revoked_sessions = {tok_laptop, tok_phone}`). `Credential.revoke(cred_emp4821_pw, "hr_offboard_svc", …) → revoked`; `revoke(cred_emp4821_key, …) → revoked` (`revoked_credentials = {cred_emp4821_pw, cred_emp4821_key}` — had only the password been revoked, the hardware key would still authenticate).
5. **Seal + transition.** `AuditTrail.record_action(actor.suspended, "hr_offboard_svc", <svc-cred>, data = {invocation_id, intent_event_id: ev_susp_int_001, suspended_actor: "emp_4821", suspended_by: "hr_offboard_svc", revoked_grants: {g_finread, g_wireinit, g_reports}, revoked_sessions: {tok_laptop, tok_phone}, revoked_credentials: {cred_emp4821_pw, cred_emp4821_key}, reason})` → `ev_susp_001`, appended after every revocation committed and after the pre-check found no outcome already naming this `invocation_id`; then `actor_suspension_state["emp_4821"] = {Suspended, suspended_at, suspended_by_ref: "hr_offboard_svc", reason, suspension_event_id: ev_susp_001}`.
6. **Result.** Returns `{suspended, revoked_grants (3), revoked_sessions (2), revoked_credentials (2), event_id: ev_susp_001}`. `emp_4821` now holds zero active grants and zero active sessions (Invariant 1); `Permissions.permitted("emp_4821", "wire:initiate") → denied`; `Session.validate(tok_laptop) → invalid(revoked)`; and both revoked credentials block re-authentication on either type.

### Domain example — compromised account, a cascade that stops and is completed

A security team suspends a compromised account: `suspend_actor("svc_8830", "soc_analyst_k", <cred>, "credential-in-paste-dump")`. The snapshot finds five grants and one long-lived session; the intent event lands with that plan, and `svc_8830` is `Suspending`. Mid-cascade, `Session.revoke(tok_svc)` returns `storage-failure` (the session store is briefly unreachable). **Nothing is undone** — the five grant revocations that committed are terminal and stand. The cascade finishes its remaining members, `suspension_log` records `{outcome: revocation-failure(session), open_members: {tok_svc}}`, and [Suspend Actor] returns `rejected(revocation-failure(session, {tok_svc}))`. The account is now *mostly* closed and the records say exactly which door is still open: [Suspension Report] shows `Suspending` with `open_members = {tok_svc}`, and the issuance gate refuses new access for it. The SOC (Security Operations Center) retries once the session store recovers — step 2 finds `Suspending`, records `actor.resume_intended` under the analyst's credential naming `{tok_svc}` as the open member, and **resumes** under the same intent: it revokes `tok_svc` as `soc_analyst_k` with the prefixed reason, seals the complete enumeration with `resumed_by = soc_analyst_k` and `cascade_recovery = true`, and the account is `Suspended`; had no one retried, the completion sweep would have done the same within `completion_window`, under the service identity behind an `actor.recovery_intended` record, with `cascade_recovery = true` on the outcome. Either way the outcome says the cascade was completed, and by whom. An earlier revision of this example rolled the five grant revocations back and left the account untouched — a rollback the Permissions atom never offered, whose only real effect would have been a sealed record disagreeing with the store.

### Rejection path — already-suspended no-op, and the benign TOCTOU race

- **Already suspended (idempotent no-op).** A retry of the offboarding call: `suspend_actor("emp_4821", …)` → step 2 finds `state = Suspended` → `rejected(already-suspended)`; no second cascade, no double-revoke, no second event (Invariant 3). The single suspension from the first call stands.
- **Benign TOCTOU — concurrent logout during the cascade.** While `suspend_actor("emp_4821", …)` is mid-cascade, the employee's laptop client fires its own `Session.revoke(tok_laptop)` (a logout). The cascade's `Session.revoke(tok_laptop, …)` then returns `already-terminal` — the session is already terminal, which is the cascade's goal. This composition counts `tok_laptop` in [Revoked Sessions] (the surface is closed) and does **not** abort. This mirrors Login's Final Critique 4 (finding Final Critique 1) already-terminal handling: a concurrent terminal transition during the cascade is benign, not a failure.

### Regulated adversarial scenarios

Three scenarios the composition must survive in regulated contexts.

**Regulator audit — "prove this terminated employee's access was fully and promptly removed."** A SOX (Sarbanes-Oxley Act) auditor examines `emp_4821`'s offboarding. `suspension_report("emp_4821")` returns `Suspended` with `suspended_at`, `suspended_by_ref`, and the enumerated [Revoked Grants] (3), [Revoked Sessions] (2) and `revoked_credentials` (2). The auditor reads the sealed `actor.suspended` event `ev_susp_001` and confirms, via `AuditTrail.verify_record`, that the enumeration was not altered after the fact (Invariant 2, resting on the substrate's Tamper Evidence). For each enumerated `grant_id`, the Permissions store shows `status = Revoked` with `revoked_at` at suspension time; for each `session_token`, the Session store shows `Revoked`; for each of the two `credential_id`s, the Credential store shows `Revoked` — the password and the hardware key both, so no type re-authenticates. Invariant 1 is the structural answer: a Suspended actor has zero active grants, zero active sessions, and zero effective-Active credentials. The examiner consults no source code — every claim is verified from the records.

**Disputed action — "this employee couldn't have done that; we suspended them."** An action attributed to `emp_4821` is dated after `suspended_at`. The investigator reads `ev_susp_001`: the suspension revoked every active grant and session in the plan, with the outcome sealed only after every revocation committed (Invariant 1). If the disputed action used a session, `Session.validate` on that token returns `invalid(revoked)` with `revoked_at` at suspension time — the session could not have authorized an action afterward. If it used a grant, `Permissions.permitted` returns `denied`. The only way the action could post-date the suspension is a *new* grant/session issued after the snapshot — which [Suspension Report] plus the issuance records would show, and which is the named snapshot-scoped gap (the composing layer's obligation to gate issuance on the suspension state). The records bound the question precisely.

**Breach investigation — "during the incident, was any suspended actor's access incompletely closed?"** An investigator suspects a suspension half-completed under load. They walk the `actor.suspended` events in the incident window and, for each, cross-check that every enumerated `grant_id`/`session_token` is terminal in its store *and* that no active grant/session for that `actor_ref` exists outside the enumerated set as of `suspended_at`. Invariant 1 forecloses a `Suspended`-with-active-access state: the outcome event is appended only after every revocation committed. A half-closed actor is a `Suspending` one, and the investigator reads it directly — the intent's plan against the store, the `suspension_log`'s `revocation-failure(surface)` entry naming the open members, and, once completed, the outcome's `cascade_recovery = true` — the honest record of a surface that did not close on the first pass and when it did. Because the enumeration is in the sealed event payload, an attempt to shrink it to hide a missed surface breaks the seal.

## Generation acceptance

A derived implementation of this composition is *acceptable* — in the regulator-acceptance sense — when an external auditor, given the composition's emergent state (`actor_suspension_state`, `suspension_log`) plus the Permissions, Session, (optional) Credential, and Audit Trail substrate stores, can do all of the following without recourse to source code, runbooks, or developer narration.

### Audit-Trail-traversal-clearable checks

These checks an auditor answers by reading the composition's records (including the Audit Trail substrate reached through it). **Standing rule for every cross-seam comparison below:** `suspended_at` and `intended_at` are this composition's seam stamps; `granted_at`, `issued_at`, `registered_at` and `revoked_at` are the constituents'. Wherever a check compares one with the other it runs under `clock_skew_allowance` (Configuration), and a member whose stamp falls inside the allowance of the composition's stamp is reported *inconclusive by skew*, never as a failure and never as a pass.

1. **Suspension completeness (the multi-surface guarantee).** For every actor in `actor_suspension_state` with `state = Suspended` — rebuilt from the trail above `index_high_water`, re-derived from the retained trail below it, and taken from the index's **truth-bearing half** past the horizon, which the rebuild never evicts — confirm that **no** Permissions grant with `subject_ref = actor_ref` is `Active`, **no** Session with `principal_ref = actor_ref` is `Active`, and — where Credential is composed with `revoke_credential_on_suspend` — **no** credential with `principal_ref = actor_ref` is effective-Active on **any** `credential_type` — except grants/sessions/credentials whose `granted_at`/`issued_at`/`registered_at` post-dates `suspended_at` beyond the skew allowance (the snapshot-scoped concurrent-issuance gap, a composing-layer obligation, not a defect of this composition). A Suspended actor with an active grant, session, or credential issued *before* `suspended_at` is a conformance failure (Invariant 1).
2. **Audit enumeration completeness, against the plan.** For every `Suspended` actor, confirm exactly one `actor.suspended` event carries its `suspension_event_id`, that `AuditTrail.verify_record` returns `verified` for it (over the covering range `read_record` names), that its `intent_event_id` resolves to an `actor.suspension_intended` event with the same `invocation_id` preceding it in the log's own order, and that the outcome's enumerated [Revoked Grants] / [Revoked Sessions] / `revoked_credentials` equals the intent's `planned_grants` / `planned_sessions` / `planned_credentials` member by member — every planned member either enumerated or terminal in its store by a concurrent act before `suspended_at` (under the skew allowance), and no enumerated member unplanned — and that every enumerated member resolves to a Revoked record. A revoked-at-suspension grant, session, or credential absent from the sealed enumeration is a conformance failure (Invariant 2). The inverse: every `grant_id`/`session_token`/`credential_id` in the enumeration resolves to a Revoked record. **Inside the audit horizon only:** a `Suspended` entry whose outcome event the substrate has lawfully destroyed answers `failed-verification(purged)` from `verify_record` before any payload is examined, the enumeration is unavailable rather than missing, and the entry is answered by the index's truth-bearing half — reported as *purged*, counted as consistent, never as a failure of this check.
3. **Idempotence and ordering.** Confirm the trail shows at most one `actor.suspended` per `actor_ref` between reinstatements and at most one `actor.suspension_intended` per cascade (a resume writes an `actor.resume_intended`, never a second suspension intent; a `step-4` arm at the intent is read back and proceeded on, never re-run), that any [Suspend Actor] against an already-Suspended actor recorded `already-suspended` in the `suspension_log` with no second event (Invariant 3), and that `actor_suspension_state` rebuilt from the trail transitions only Active→Suspending→Suspended (via [Suspend Actor] and its completion) and Suspended→Active (via [Reinstate Actor]), each edge bound to a sealed event. The `suspension_log`'s refusal entries are evidence of refusals, never of acts.
4. **Attribution and reason completeness — three writers, each landed.** For every `actor.suspended` event, confirm it carries a non-empty `reason` and that `data.suspended_by` names the actor its intent event was attested under, and then confirm its attesting `actor_ref` (via the substrate's Actor Identity) is exactly one of: **(i)** that operator — a clean cascade, with no `cascade_recovery`; **(ii)** the `resumed_by` the event names, with `cascade_recovery = true` and an `actor.resume_intended` attested under that same actor naming this `invocation_id` preceding it — a caller's resume; **(iii)** `application_actor_ref`, with `cascade_recovery = true` and an `actor.recovery_intended` naming this `invocation_id` preceding it — the sweep. Then, for each revoked Session and (optional) Credential record the enumeration names, confirm its `revoked_by_ref` is the writer that closed it — the operator, a resumer named on a resume record for this `invocation_id`, or `application_actor_ref` — and its reason is the operator's reason, or that reason under the `suspension-resume:` / `suspension-completion:` prefix carrying this `intent_event_id`. A suspension with no attributed operator, no reason, an attesting actor outside the three, or a constituent record whose `revoked_by_ref` no record in the trail verified, is a conformance failure.
5. **Constituent Generation acceptance bars.** Verify Permissions' five checks over the grant store, Session's five checks over the session store, Audit Trail's six checks over the substrate (transitively clearing Event Log, Actor Identity, Tamper Evidence, Retention Window), and — when composed — Credential's six checks. This composition's invariants depend on the correctness of the constituents' invariants (Invariant 4).
6. **Authentication precedence, and unfinished cascades surfaced and completed within the window.** For every `actor.suspended` event, its intent event precedes it in the log's own order and was attested under the operator `data.suspended_by` names; and where the outcome carries `cascade_recovery = true`, the record that verified its completer precedes every revocation the completer made — an `actor.resume_intended` attested under `resumed_by`, or an `actor.recovery_intended` attested under `application_actor_ref`, each naming the same `invocation_id` — so that no constituent record's `revoked_at` precedes the record that verified its `revoked_by_ref` (under the skew allowance): the records-alone proof that every principal who closed a door was verified before closing it (Invariant 5). Then, **between two edges** — intents older than `suspension_completion_bound` and inside the audit horizon — for every `actor.suspension_intended` with no `actor.suspended` naming its `invocation_id`, confirm **either** the actor is `Suspending` with the open members named in the `suspension_log`, **or** a completing outcome landed within `completion_window` of the intent's `intended_at`, **or** an open compliance finding names the actor. Any one of the three clears the check; an intent with *none of the three* is a conformance failure — the partial was silent. An intent younger than the bound is inconclusive; one past the horizon is answered by the index's truth-bearing half, not by this check. Confirm `completion_window`, `suspension_completion_bound`, `reconciliation_cadence` and `outcome_write_latency` are all declared and that **`suspension_completion_bound + reconciliation_cadence + outcome_write_latency < completion_window`, strictly** — all three terms, not the cadence alone: a deployment whose cadence fits the window while the bound does not breaches on every orphan with the fragment passing (§*Liveness is arithmetic*). And confirm that for every `invocation_id` exactly one `actor.suspended` exists — two is a second writer, which the section and the pre-checks foreclose in a conforming deployment.

### Externally-clearable checks

These questions arise around this composition but cannot be answered from its records alone — they are the composition's named audit-gaps, each routed to the evidence that owns it.

- **Whether the `actor_ref` namespace truly coincides with the grant `subject_ref`, session `principal_ref`, and credential `principal_ref` namespaces.** This composition's enumeration (and hence Invariant 1's completeness) is complete only under `unified_actor_namespace`; whether the deployment actually unified them — via Authenticated Actor's binding or convention — requires the deployment's identity-namespace evidence, not this composition's records. This is the load-bearing enumeration-completeness audit-gap (Edge cases — *Unified actor namespace is a precondition*); a divergent namespace under-enumerates silently.
- **Whether a new grant or session was issued after the suspension snapshot.** Invariant 1 is snapshot-scoped; whether the composing layer correctly gated `Permissions.grant` / `Session.issue` on the suspension state (so a suspended actor acquired no new access) requires the issuance layer's records, not this composition's (Edge cases — *Suspension is snapshot-scoped*).
- **Whether the `suspended_by_ref` was authorized to suspend the actor.** This composition attributes *who* suspended but does not gate *whether* they were permitted to; that requires a Permissions instance scoped to suspension authority (Edge cases — *Suspension authorization*).
- **Whether re-authentication was actually blocked.** When Credential is uncomposed or `revoke_credential_on_suspend = false`, whether the deployment closed the re-authentication door — on every credential type the actor holds — requires the credential/Login records, not this composition's (Edge cases — *Re-authentication after suspension*).
- **Whether the host's per-actor section excludes.** `per_actor_serialization` is an instance capability the deployment supplies; whether it actually serializes the invocation, a resume, and the sweep across every node — and how its lease lapses — is the host's evidence, not this composition's records. The records can show a second outcome for one `invocation_id` (check 6) after the fact; they cannot show the section held.

## Edge cases and explicit non-goals

- **Suspension is snapshot-scoped; concurrent issuance is a composing-layer gap.** Invariant 1's completeness holds over the active set enumerated at the step-3 snapshot. This composition revokes but never issues; it cannot prevent a concurrent `Permissions.grant` or `Session.issue` from creating *new* access for the actor after the snapshot. This is the exact analog of Login's snapshot-scoped cascade (Login Invariant 2): the composing layer that issues grants and sessions must gate issuance on `actor_suspension_state` (refuse to issue for a `Suspending` or `Suspended` actor — both are closed states), which this composition exposes as a queryable surface ([Suspension Report]). This composition closes the actor's *existing* access completely; keeping it closed against new issuance is the issuance layer's obligation, and whether it did so is an externally-clearable check. A deployment that wants the suspension to be self-enforcing wires the issuance gate; this composition provides the state the gate reads.
- **Unified actor namespace is a precondition, not a guarantee.** This composition enumerates `subject_ref = actor_ref` grants, `principal_ref = actor_ref` sessions, and — where Credential is composed — `principal_ref = actor_ref` credentials; this is complete only if the namespaces coincide with the `actor_ref`'s. The composition names this as a deployment precondition (`unified_actor_namespace`, satisfied by Authenticated Actor's `principal_ref ↔ actor_ref` binding or by convention) rather than verifying it — verifying that two opaque namespaces coincide is not a records-alone check at this composition's layer. A deployment whose namespaces diverge under-enumerates and produces a suspension that misses the actor's access under the other namespace — the precise gap `demos/attributed-permissions-admin/CORNERS.md` §Cross-atom identity surface aliasing flags, here named as an externally-clearable check rather than papered over.
- **Completion, not undo — why this composition promises more than Login and less than it once claimed.** Login's cascade is best-effort (a per-session storage failure is logged and the cascade continues; partial revocation beats none). This composition's earlier revisions inverted that into `all-or-nothing`: any non-benign failure would roll the whole cascade back so the actor was either fully de-authorized or unchanged. **That posture was never available.** A revoked grant is terminal (Permissions Invariant 3), a revoked session absorbing (Session Invariant 4), a revoked credential terminal, and the substrate's audit append un-withdrawable — none can be enlisted in a host transaction, so the rollback the posture rested on was an undeclared capability of every store it named, and the wiring that claimed it could leave a sealed `actor.suspended` event enumerating revocations that had been undone: a false record, which is the one partial nothing can repair. What the composition promises now is the stronger *honest* form: the intent is recorded before anything is revoked, every revocation that commits stands, an actor whose cascade stopped is `Suspending` — a closed state for the issuance gate, a named set of open members for the operator — and the completion sweep closes the remainder within a declared window. The `partial_failure_posture` knob is gone with the posture; there is one behavior.
- **Cross-store consistency under partial failure.** Two partials are reachable and both are completed, never repaired by fabrication. **(a) The stopped cascade.** The intent landed and one or more revocations did not (a non-benign arm that returned, or a crash mid-cascade): the actor is `Suspending`, the plan is on the intent event, the committed revocations are in their stores. **(b) The unsealed cascade.** Every revocation committed and the `actor.suspended` append did not land (a `step-2 | step-3` failure, or a crash between step 6 and step 7): the actor is `Suspending` with no open members, and the invocation that owed the outcome has **yielded it** — it returned `recording-failure(outcome)` and does not retry. A **completion sweep** runs at process restart and on `reconciliation_cadence` (Configuration), **between two edges and as the composition**. Its candidate set is the union of the index's `Suspending` entries and the open intents in the trail above `index_high_water` — the substrate's open-upper-bound sequence-range read, filtered in composition code to this composition's `action_ref`s — so an intent whose index write was lost is found by the sweep and not only by the next caller. It indexes **every** event it reads above the mark — intents young and old, outcomes, reinstatements, each under the actor's section, the index write being idempotent over its event — and advances the mark to the top of the range read, held back below any event it could not index; the mark bounds the **sweep's** reading and the tail reads, and nothing else (Composition state). It examines only candidates whose intent is older than `suspension_completion_bound` (a younger one may be an invocation still revoking, and completing it beside that invocation would revoke twice and append a second outcome) and inside the audit horizon (past it the plan is destroyed and the entry is the index's truth-bearing half; the sweep then completes from the live stores, re-snapshotting the actor's Active members, and says so on the outcome as `plan_unavailable = true`). **One writer per act.** For each candidate the sweep takes the actor's section (`per_actor_serialization`) and holds it across its pre-check and its writes; a section it cannot take — the invocation is alive and inside its lease, or a caller's resume is running — it skips until its next run, holding `index_high_water` back below that intent (the hold is bounded: the lease is `suspension_completion_bound` long, and an invocation past it has yielded — `per_actor_serialization`), so the invocation, a resume, the restart run and the cadence run, on one node or several, never both land an outcome for one `invocation_id`. Under the section it **pre-checks the trail** — the same tail read, filtered to an `actor.suspended` naming this `invocation_id` — an outcome already there means a completion landed and the index is stale, a rebuild trigger and nothing to emit; the pre-check is re-read under the section, never before it (§*A compensator is exclusive*). Then it writes an **`actor.recovery_intended`** record under `application_actor_ref` / `application_credential` naming the `invocation_id`, the intent, and the members it is about to close; revokes each still-Active planned member under the service identity (`revoked_by_ref = application_actor_ref`, `reason = "suspension-completion:" + intent_event_id + ": " + <the operator's reason>`, so the constituent's own record traces to the operator's intent); and appends the `actor.suspended` outcome with `invocation_id` (the cascade's — the key every check pairs on), `intent_event_id`, `cascade_recovery = true`, the operator as `suspended_by`, and the complete enumeration `{revoked_grants, revoked_sessions, revoked_credentials}` — re-derived from the plan and the constituent records, never remembered from the process that died. The compensating outcome is the largest record this composition writes, and `planned_set_cap` is what keeps it inside the substrate's `payload_cap`. A `Suspending` actor not completed within `completion_window` escalates as an unresolved compliance finding rather than remaining an open retry, and under the offboarding regimes this composition serves is a hard alerting condition; the window is meetable only under the inequality `reconciliation_cadence` states.
- **Reinstatement does not restore access.** [Reinstate Actor] lifts the Suspended state but never un-revokes a grant or session — those are terminal in Permissions (Invariant 3) and Session (Invariant 4) and cannot return to Active. Re-authorizing a reinstated actor means issuing *fresh* grants (`Permissions.grant`) and letting them establish *new* sessions (`Login`), which is the issuance layer's surface, not this composition's. Reinstatement is the state-machine's reverse edge only — it re-opens the actor to *future* provisioning; it does not reach back into the constituents' terminal records. A deployment that needs the old access back re-grants it explicitly, which is the correct, auditable behavior (the new grants carry new `granted_at` timestamps and their own attributions).
- **Suspension authorization is a composing concept.** Whether the `suspended_by_ref` is *authorized* to suspend a given actor is an inward-authorization question this composition does not gate — it composes no Permissions instance scoped to suspension authority (the cut is Actor Identity + Permissions-as-revocation-target + Session + Audit Trail). The operator is attributed cryptographically via the substrate's Actor Identity, so an auditor can always answer *who* suspended the actor; whether they were *permitted* to requires a Permissions check (or a Multi-Party Approval gate for high-stakes suspensions) wired ahead of [Suspend Actor], exactly as Resolve a Person's Data Rights leaves operator authorization to a composing pattern.
- **Re-authentication after suspension.** Revoking the actor's active grants and sessions closes their *current* access but does not, by itself, stop a *new* login that would establish a fresh session. Closing that door is the optional Credential constituent's job (`revoke_credential_on_suspend = true` revokes **every** effective-Active credential the actor holds, so re-authentication fails on every type — Credential Invariant 4 is absorbing per `(principal_ref, credential_type)` pair, and Invariant 2 admits one effective-Active credential per pair, so an actor with a password and a hardware key holds two and revoking one leaves the other authenticating). When Credential is uncomposed, the deployment must revoke every such credential through Login's surface (`Credential.revoke` then optionally `revoke_sessions_for_credential`) in parallel — a named composing obligation, with whether it did so an externally-clearable check.
- **No bulk constituent surfaces — the cascade enumerates and revokes each.** Permissions provides no bulk-revoke (its *Mass revocation* edge case names per-grant enumerate-then-revoke as the composing system's job) and Session's bulk revocation is likewise an enumerate-and-revoke over its `principal_ref`-queryable store. This composition is the composing pattern that performs both enumerations and the per-member revocations under one per-`actor_ref` serialization, recording the plan before and the outcome after — which is exactly why the completeness guarantees (Invariants 1 and 2) live at the composition layer and not in either atom.
- **The actor lifecycle is composition-introduced.** Actor Identity has a single `Attested` state and no actor lifecycle; the Active/Suspended state machine is this composition's own (a composition-introduced surface). This composition therefore does not — and cannot — read a "suspended" flag from Actor Identity; it owns `actor_suspension_state`. A deployment that also wants the actor's *attestation registry* entry deactivated (so the actor cannot produce new attestations) composes that through the forthcoming Actor Registry / Identity Provisioning pattern, or — for the attest surface specifically — through [Authenticated Actor](./authenticated-actor.md), whose revocation cascade closes the attest surface when the credential is revoked. This composition + Authenticated Actor + Credential-revocation together close the full outbound surface (grants, sessions, attestation, re-authentication); this composition alone closes grants and sessions.
- **Suspending an actor with no active access.** An `actor_ref` with no active grants and no active sessions (a typo, or an actor already de-provisioned) is a valid no-op suspension: the cascade revokes nothing, the actor transitions to Suspended, and the `actor.suspended` event records empty [Revoked Grants]/[Revoked Sessions]. This is meaningful ("we suspended an actor who held no active access") and harmless; it is distinct from a failure. This composition does not reject it, mirroring Login's `revoke_sessions_for_credential` returning `{revoked: 0}` for a credential with no sessions.
- **Concurrency.** Two concurrent [Suspend Actor] calls for the same `actor_ref` are serialized per `actor_ref` — `per_actor_serialization` (Configuration), a host mutual-exclusion obligation keyed by `actor_ref` and held from the state gate (step 2) through the index write (step 8), by the resume arm, and by the completion sweep for every actor it examines; not a transaction — and the state gate: the first takes the section, transitions and cascades; the second waits, then observes `Suspending` or `Suspended` and resumes or returns `already-suspended` (Invariant 3). The gate is inside the section because two calls that both read it before either wrote its intent would both write one. The section is released on return or death; where the host leases it, the lease is `suspension_completion_bound` long and its expiry is the invocation's terminus — an invocation whose lease expired has yielded, re-takes the section before any further write, and adopts under it an outcome the sweep landed rather than appending beside it (step 7); it makes no write it cannot take the section for. The benign TOCTOU race — a concurrent `Session.revoke`/`logout` or `Permissions.revoke` terminalizing a target the cascade also targets — is counted as skipped (already-terminal/not-active), not aborted, mirroring Login's Final Critique 4 (finding Final Critique 1). A concurrent `Permissions.grant`/`Session.issue` after the snapshot is the snapshot-scoped gap above.
- **Clock semantics.** `intended_at`, `resumed_at`, `suspended_at`, `reinstated_at`, and `attempted_at` are best-effort wall-time annotations stamped from the **composition's own seam-injected `now`** — one reading per invocation, taken at the I/O seam and passed through into the payloads, never sampled inside a transition (the Logic Confinement Principle, *Primitive policies*). The substrate exports no clock: the one stamp that is the substrate's is `recorded_at`, which Event Log writes at its own seam on append and this composition never supplies or compares against a write. The Event Log `sequence_number` (via the substrate) is the authoritative order source for the suspension events. The cascade's correctness depends on the per-`actor_ref` serialization, not on clock comparison; `invocation_id` is seam-injected alongside `now` (*Primitive policies*).

#### Suspend Actor

The composition's load-bearing emergent action: under a per-`actor_ref` serialization, it snapshots the actor's active grants and sessions (and, where Credential is composed, every effective-Active credential the actor holds), sizes the plan against the cap, records the plan under the operator's verified credential, revokes every one, seals the complete revoked set into one attributed Audit Trail event, and transitions the actor Active → Suspending → Suspended. Returns `{suspended, revoked_grants, revoked_sessions, revoked_credentials, event_id, resumed_by?}`, or [Already Suspended] (idempotent no-op), [Revocation Failure] (a non-benign constituent revoke failed — the actor stays Suspending with the open members named, and a retry resumes under its own attested `actor.resume_intended` record), `invalid-credential`, or `recording-failure(intent | outcome)` — the position telling the caller whether the revocations stand. Neither constituent carries this multi-surface cascade.

Kind: Operation

#### Suspension Report

The composition's read-only query: resolves `actor_suspension_state[actor_ref]` (rebuild-on-miss), returning `{state: Active}` for an unsuspended actor, `{state: Suspending, open_members, intent_event_id, …}` for one whose cascade has not completed, or the full Suspended record — `suspended_at`, `suspended_by_ref`, `reason`, and the enumerated [Revoked Grants] / [Revoked Sessions] read from the sealed event. Changes no state; the queryable surface the issuance layer gates new access on.

Kind: Operation

#### Reinstate Actor

The composition's thin lifecycle-reversal: transitions Suspended → Active and records an attributed `actor.reinstated` event. It does **not** restore any revoked grant or session (those are terminal in their constituents) — re-provisioning is the issuance layer's job. Returns `{reinstated, event_id}`, or [Already Active] (no-op) / `recording-failure`.

Kind: Operation

#### Revoked Grants

The set of `grant_id`s the suspension cascade revoked, carried in the sealed `actor.suspended` event's `data` (Invariant 2 — audit completeness). Its completeness — every active grant for the actor, no surface omitted — is what makes the suspension provable from one tamper-evident record.

#### Revoked Sessions

The set of `session_token`s the suspension cascade revoked, carried in the sealed `actor.suspended` event's `data` (Invariant 2). Together with [Revoked Grants] it is the complete, sealed record of everything the suspension closed.

Kind:      Field
Field of:  the suspension event
Role:      the revoked session enumeration
Projects:  revoked_sessions

#### Outcome

The `suspension_log` entry's classification of a [Suspend Actor] / [Reinstate Actor] call: `suspended`, `reinstated`, [Already Suspended], [Already Active], `revocation-failure(surface)`, `recording-failure(intent | outcome)`, or `invalid-request`. The composition's own records-alone query surface alongside the tamper-evident Audit Trail.

Kind:      Field
Field of:  the suspension-log entry
Role:      the call classification
Projects:  outcome

#### Already Suspended

The composition's own idempotence rejection from [Suspend Actor] — returned when the actor is already Suspended: the cascade does not re-run (no second revoke, no second event; Invariant 3). The state gate that fires the cascade exactly once, on the Active → Suspending edge.

Kind:      Member
Member of: the suspend rejection
Role:      Rejection
Projects:  already-suspended

#### Revocation Failure

The composition's own rejection from [Suspend Actor] — returned when a non-benign constituent revoke (or store inconsistency) fails: every revocation that committed stands, the actor is `Suspending`, and the rejection names the surface and the open members; a retry resumes the cascade under the resumer's own verified credential, and the completion sweep closes it otherwise (Invariant 1). A benign already-terminal target (a concurrent revocation) is *not* this — it is counted toward completion.

Kind:      Member
Member of: the suspend rejection
Role:      Rejection
Projects:  revocation-failure

#### Already Active

The composition's own no-op rejection from [Reinstate Actor] — returned when the actor is not Suspended, so there is nothing to reinstate; carries the reason `suspending` where the actor's cascade has not yet completed, which is finished before it can be lifted.

Kind:      Member
Member of: the reinstate rejection
Role:      Rejection
Projects:  already-active

an absent `actor_suspension_state` key read as a miss and resolved through the trail's tail above a new `index_high_water` mark the sweep advances, so a crash between the durable intent and the index write cannot let a second intent or a fresh grant through; the intent record's `recording-failure(step-4)` and retention-source `invalid-request` read back and proceeded on as landed, `recording-failure(intent | outcome)` on the signature, and the invocation yielding the act at the outcome position instead of retrying beside the sweep; the per-`actor_ref` section taken at the gate and declared as `per_actor_serialization`, with step 7 adopting a landed outcome under a lapsed lease; `C`, every effective-Active credential across every `credential_type`, planned and revoked as a set; `clock_skew_allowance` under every cross-seam comparison the checks make; and, on the closure check, the mark made the line — the trail answers above `index_high_water` on every index read, the index answers below it as a truth-bearing store under `state_durability` because the sweep indexes every event it reads (young intents included) and holds the mark back below any it could not — with the section's lease semantics stated: released on return or death, `suspension_completion_bound` long, its expiry the invocation's terminus, every later write made only under a re-taken section. *Over:* a resume that revoked under the resumer's reference and verified nobody until the outcome;
