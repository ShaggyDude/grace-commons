## Summary

Execute Gated Workflow freezes a declared process map (states, allowed transitions, and which transitions require human approval) and enforces permissions on who may start or advance it.

A guarded transition is gated by an Approval Step that an authorized actor opens on demand and assigns to the named approver's in-tray; the transition cannot fire until that step is Approved.

The Audit Trail substrate attributes every transition and gate decision to the acting actor's identity, governs retention, and seals the record tamper-evident — a records-alone forensic proof.

Together these produce what no constituent provides alone: the process followed only its declared path, every gate was cleared by a real human decision (not a bare assertion), and the full history is attributed, sealed, and reconstructible.

This composition is the structural form of FDA (US Food and Drug Administration) 21 CFR (Code of Federal Regulations) Part 11 electronic records workflows, SOX (Sarbanes-Oxley Act — US law on corporate financial reporting and records integrity) §404 process-control records, ISO (International Organization for Standardization) 9001 §8.5.1 production-process documentation, and any regulated domain that must prove compliant execution from records alone.

## Intent

Multi-step regulated processes share a common structure: an entity moves through a declared sequence of states, some transitions require explicit human approval before they may fire, every transition must be attributed and tamper-evidenced, and the entire history must be reconstructible from the records alone. A pharmaceutical batch-release process requires declared quality-control steps with a qualified-person approval gate before release. A financial journal-entry promotion requires controller sign-off at the booking gate. A clinical-trial protocol change requires investigator and sponsor approvals before the change may proceed. A software change request requires a review approval before it can be merged to production. In every case the structure is the same: a declared state machine governing the process instance, a set of gates (transitions that may not fire until a named approver has actually decided), an authorization surface governing who may advance the process at all, a work-tracking surface that keeps each pending gate in the right person's in-tray, and a regulated-audit substrate that makes every advancement and every gate decision attributed, sealed, and retention-governed.

The constituent atoms address each of these concepts freestanding. [State Machine](../atoms/state-machine.md) enforces declared-transition discipline — only declared transitions fire, exactly one current state at all times, the full transition history is append-only, total-ordered, and replay-deterministic. [Approval Step](../atoms/approval-step.md) is the per-gate primitive — one named approver, one subject, one scope, one outcome (Pending → Approved | Rejected | Withdrawn), with approver exclusivity enforced by its Invariant 4. [Permissions](../atoms/permissions.md) governs standing authorization — who holds the `workflows:start`, `workflows:open-gate`, `workflows:fire`, and `workflows:read` grants that permit process-level actions. [Assignment](../atoms/assignment.md) binds each open gate to the named approver's in-tray and recalls the binding when the gate is decided. [Audit Trail](./audit-trail.md) is the regulated-audit substrate that supplies Event Log, Actor Identity, Retention Window, and Tamper Evidence as a single composition.

What the constituent atoms cannot answer alone is the central regulated question: *did this guarded transition fire only after its named approval was genuinely recorded?* State Machine's guard model (Invariant 8 — guard-gating without evaluation) deliberately does not evaluate guard predicates; it trusts a caller-asserted `guard_satisfied = true` and records the assertion. Its Edge cases name this as a calling-system obligation and explicitly flag approval-gate evaluation as the concept this composition re-converges. This composition is where that re-convergence happens: it binds each guarded transition to an Approval Step (`gate_binding`), reads the bound step's state before any `fire` call, and asserts `guard_satisfied = true` to `WorkflowStateMachine.fire` only when the bound step is in Approved. The caller of this composition never supplies `guard_satisfied` directly for a guarded transition; the composition derives it from the gate's actual approval record.

This is not a new primitive. The four constituent atoms and the Audit Trail substrate are unchanged. The composition is the wiring that makes their concepts coherent — one consolidated multi-actor regulated-workflow surface rather than five parallel record stores the auditor must correlate by hand.

The composition addresses what State Machine's EOS (Essence of Software — Daniel Jackson's framework for specifying software concepts as freestanding, composable units) extraction correctly deferred — guard evaluation, non-repudiable attribution, retention governance, and Permissions gating — and adds the in-tray binding for pending approvals, a need the gate mechanism itself creates rather than one the atom deferred. The composition resolves those obligations without re-deriving the underlying primitives.

## Composes

- **[State Machine](../atoms/state-machine.md)** — the orchestrating spine, called throughout the Action wiring by the instance handle **`WorkflowStateMachine`** (this composition's single named State Machine instance; the handle is introduced here once and used unqualified below). Provides the declared-state-machine enforcement: only declared transitions fire; exactly one current state at all times; the full transition history is append-only, total-ordered by sequence number, and replay-deterministic. The composition instantiates one State Machine instance per workflow run, supplies it the deployment-declared process declaration, and calls `fire` on it as the sole write path — the composition never bypasses `fire`.

- **[Approval Step](../atoms/approval-step.md)** — the per-gate human-approval primitive. Provides the gate record: one Approval Step per guarded transition that becomes relevant, each carrying its own `step_id`, `subject_ref`, `approver_ref`, `submitter_ref`, `scope`, lifecycle (Pending → Approved | Rejected | Withdrawn), and Invariant 4 enforcement that only the named `approver_ref` may transition to Approved or Rejected. The composition submits one Approval Step when a gate is opened, reads its state when the corresponding transition is to be fired, and supplies `guard_satisfied = true` to `WorkflowStateMachine.fire` only when the step is in Approved.

- **[Permissions](../atoms/permissions.md)** — the authorization surface for workflow-level actions. Provides `grant`, `revoke`, and `permitted`. Every workflow-level state-changing action ([Start Workflow], [Open Gate], [Fire Transition]) and every workflow-level read query ([Read Workflow]) is gated by a `permitted` check before reaching any constituent store. Step-level gate decisions ([Decide Gate]) are enforced by Approval Step's Invariant 4 (named-approver exclusivity) directly — no redundant chain-layer permission check, mirroring Multi-Party Approval's scope-vocabulary discipline.

- **[Assignment](../atoms/assignment.md)** — the in-tray binding for open gates. On gate opening, one Assignment record is created (`task_ref = step_id`, `assignee_ref = approver_ref` from the `gate_spec`) so the named approver can ask *"which approval gates are currently in my in-tray?"* — answered by **enumerate-and-filter over Assignment's declared read surface** (enumerate the composition's Assignment instance's records and filter by `assignee_ref` and Active state in the caller's own code, the same declared-read discipline the Audit Trail traversals use); Assignment declares no dedicated assignee-keyed in-tray query, and none is assumed. When the gate is decided (Approved, Rejected, or Withdrawn), the corresponding Assignment is recalled — the responsibility is discharged.

- **[Audit Trail](./audit-trail.md)** — the regulated-audit substrate. Every workflow-level action (`workflow_started`, `gate_opened`, `gate_decided`, `transition_fired`) is recorded as one `record_action` call on the Audit Trail instance, producing an Event Log entry, an Actor Identity attestation, a Retention Window record, and (per the deployment's seal cadence) a Tamper Evidence seal. The composition maintains exactly one Audit Trail instance configured with the deployment's regulatory retention policy. **Consumed read capability, declared:** beyond `record_action`, the composition consumes the substrate's declared pass-through list read — a query by sequence-number range (here always with an **open upper bound**) passes through the substrate to its Event Log's `read` unchanged, per the substrate's own list-query split — and performs every event selection **in composition code** over the returned sequence: filtering by `action_ref` and payload fields is the enumerate-and-filter move the substrate itself uses for its own rebuilds, never a payload-predicate *query* (that lookup shape is the forthcoming **Reverse Index** pattern's, which a deployment may compose over the trail as an instance optimization without changing this contract). Every "traverse the Audit Trail for X events" in Composition state, the binding-consumption rule's `transition_fired` lookup, Invariant 3's forward reconstruction, and Generation acceptance's trail-walking checks name this capability and no other. The Event Log and Actor Identity atoms that the roadmap entry for this composition listed as prerequisites are reached transitively through Audit Trail; the composition does not maintain separate Event Log or Actor Identity instances.

## Composition logic

### Composition state

The composition owns emergent state — the workflow store, the gate binding maps, and the cross-atom traversal maps — that wires the constituent atoms into one queryable regulated-workflow surface. None of these state elements belongs to any single constituent atom — and none holds truth the records lack: every element carries its Contract classification per [`execution-contract.md`](../execution-contract.md) §Composition state, and all four are **derived indexes** over this composition's own audit events, each with a named rebuild procedure — every "traverse the Audit Trail" below is the declared enumerate-and-filter read (*Composes*), never a payload-predicate query — and the Contract's three obligations (the element sits **outside the actions' atomicity surface**; a missing or lost entry is a **rebuild trigger, not data loss** — reads consult it with rebuild-on-miss semantics; and it claims no cross-constituent transactional consistency).

- **`workflow_store`** — the set of workflow instance records. Each record carries: `instance_id` (the State Machine instance id, assigned by the constituent at [Start Workflow]), `subject_ref` (an opaque reference to the entity whose lifecycle this workflow governs), `initiator_ref` (the actor who started the workflow), `declaration_ref` (the optional caller-supplied opaque reference naming the deployment's process-definition identity, taken verbatim from [Start Workflow]'s `declaration_ref` parameter; null when none was supplied), [Gate Spec] (the map from each guarded transition's guard label to `{approver_ref, scope}` naming the approval required for that gate; set at [Start Workflow], immutable), and `started_at`. The workflow record is immutable on every field from the moment [Start Workflow] returns; the State Machine constituent owns `current_state` and the transition history. **Contract classification: derived index.** *Rebuild procedure:* traverse the Audit Trail for `workflow_started` events — [Start Workflow]'s outcome record (step 5) writes the full record (`instance_id`, `subject_ref`, `initiator_ref`, `declaration_ref`, the complete [Gate Spec], `started_at`) into the event payload; `initiator_ref` rides **the payload**, never the event's `actor_ref`, because a `recovery = true` re-emission is composition-attributed (Action wiring preamble) and the actor field would then name the service identity — so every field, the load-bearing [Gate Spec] included, is immutable audit content and the rebuild is total from the payload alone **for as long as that payload survives — which is a bound, and *total from the payload alone* without it was the overstatement**. The Audit Trail substrate destroys an event's payload in its entirety at the audit instance's horizon; what survives is the event's `event_id`, `sequence_number` and `recorded_at`, plus the attestation's `action_ref`, `actor_ref` and `attested_at` through the destruction record's `(event_id, attestation_id)` pair — so a purged `workflow_started` event stays *identifiable* while its payload does not survive, and the payload is where every field of the workflow record lived. The classification therefore splits: *derived index* while the instance's `workflow_started` event retains its payload, **not rebuildable** beyond it. **No second source covers the loss**: State Machine holds the instance, its history and its current state, but the [Gate Spec] is a composition-introduced surface and lives in no constituent's store — which is precisely why the composition writes it into the event, and precisely why the horizon takes it. Generation acceptance check 1's dependence on [Gate Spec] is records-alone rather than map-resident, and is bounded by the same horizon.

**None of the four intent event kinds (`workflow_start_intended`, `gate_open_intended`, `gate_decision_intended`, `transition_intended`) is a rebuild source for any map below.** Each carries only what existed before its action's load-bearing call, so none names a minted `instance_id`, `step_id` or `assignment_id`; the rebuild procedures traverse the outcome kinds alone, and an implementation that sourced a map from an intent event would resolve identifiers for invocations that committed nothing.

- **`gate_binding`** — map from **`(instance_id, from_state, action)`** to the `step_id` of the Approval Step **currently** gating that declared transition. The key carries `from_state` because State Machine's transition uniqueness is per `(from_state, action)` — a legal declaration may guard the same action name out of two different states with different approvers, and a key that dropped `from_state` would let one state's approval clear the other state's differently-guarded transition and let one state's Pending gate spuriously block the other's. Set when [Open Gate] opens a gate for the transition matched from the *current* state; since gates are opened only from the current state and the moot-gate cascade withdraws Pending gates when the workflow leaves it, at most one binding per `(instance_id, action)` holds a Pending step at any moment **at quiescence** (a mooted step whose cascade withdrawal is still landing in the recovery window is Pending a moment longer — [Fire Transition] step 7) — but the key, and every traversal below, still carries `from_state`. This is the load-bearing traversal from a guarded transition to its approval record: at [Fire Transition], the composition reads `gate_binding[(instance_id, current_state, action)]` to find the step, then reads the step's state to determine whether `guard_satisfied` may be asserted. *Binding lifecycle, declared:* the binding **history** is append-only — it lives in the `gate_opened` events (each carrying `from_state` in its data) and is never erased — and the map holds the *current* (latest) binding per key. **Consumption is part of the lifecycle:** a binding is *consumed* by the one guarded firing it authorized. A binding's step is consumed **iff** a `transition_fired` event carries its `step_id` — read through the declared enumerate-and-filter (*Composes*; the map may cache the flag) — **or** the key holds a committed-but-unrecorded firing: the fire committed and its record is still landing, the pending-record state [Fire Transition] step 6's retry obligation already tracks. At quiescence the two arms coincide and the event is the truth; inside the step-6 retry window the pending record is, so a committed firing can never read as unconsumed and re-fire on one approval. A key may be re-bound to a fresh step **only when its currently bound step is in a terminal non-Approved state** (`Rejected` or `Withdrawn`) **or is Approved-and-consumed** (the return-path case: the prior approval authorized exactly one firing, and a re-entry needs a fresh one); re-binding is refused while the bound step is `Pending` ([Already Open]) and while it is `Approved`-and-unconsumed — an unconsumed approval is never silently discarded, and there is **no discard path**: Approval Step's `withdraw` is Pending-only (its Invariant 3) and submitter-exclusive (its Invariant 5), so an Approved step can be neither withdrawn nor re-bound over — it is consumed by firing, or it stands. **No entry is ever deleted** — the moot-gate cascade withdraws the bound step and recalls its Assignment but leaves the binding in place, so the traversal Generation acceptance checks 1 and 4 walk is never severed. **Contract classification: derived index.** *Rebuild procedure:* traverse the Audit Trail for `gate_opened` events and take, per `(instance_id, from_state, action)`, the latest event's `step_id` in Event Log order; a binding's consumed flag rebuilds from the `transition_fired` events' `step_id` fields.

- **`gate_to_assignment`** — map from `step_id` (a gate's Approval Step id) to the `assignment_id` of its in-tray binding. Set when [Open Gate] creates the Assignment. Used at [Decide Gate] to recall the in-tray binding when the gate is decided, and during moot-gate cascade when the workflow leaves a transition's `from_state` by firing a different transition out of it; entries persist after recall (the Assignment's own `Recalled` state carries the lifecycle). **Contract classification: derived index.** *Rebuild procedure:* traverse the Audit Trail for `gate_opened` events and take `{step_id, assignment_id}` from each payload — **bounded by the audit instance's horizon**, past which the payload is destroyed in its entirety and the traversal cannot identify a `gate_opened` event at all. **A second source exists and covers only part of the range**: Assignment's declared `active_for(task_ref)` query answers for a still-**Active** assignment, since `task_ref` is the `step_id`, but entries here deliberately persist after recall and a `Recalled` assignment is not returned by that surface. So the fallback recovers exactly the entries whose lifecycle is still open, and loses the historical ones — the opposite of the direction an audit wants, and worth stating rather than presenting the query as a general escape. Entries whose `gate_opened` event has been purged are therefore **not rebuildable**, and the classification splits accordingly.

- **`transition_to_event`** — map from a fired transition's `transition_id` (the State Machine history entry id) to the Audit Trail `event_id` that attributes and seals the firing. Set at [Fire Transition] after `WorkflowStateMachine.fire` succeeds and `AuditTrail.record_action` returns an `event_id`. The traversal backbone for the forensic query: from a transition in the State Machine history to its Audit Trail attestation. **Contract classification: derived index.** *Rebuild procedure:* traverse the Audit Trail for `transition_fired` events and key each event's own `event_id` by the `transition_id` its payload carries.

*Relations (cardinality and modality, declared):* one workflow instance has **zero or more** gate bindings (one *current* binding per `(instance_id, from_state, action)` key; the key's full binding history lives in the `gate_opened` events); each bound Approval Step has **exactly one** Assignment (`gate_to_assignment`, mandatory at [Open Gate] success); each fired transition has **exactly one** Audit Trail event at quiescence (`transition_to_event` — the [Fire Transition] step-6 window before a retried audit write lands is the recovery-bounded exception, and the same window is why the binding-consumption rule reads the pending record as well as the events — *Binding lifecycle*); and each workflow instance has exactly one `workflow_store` record. A lost index entry on any of these is a rebuild trigger, never a relation violation — the relations hold in the events.

### Configuration

- **`audit_trail_retention_policy`** — the `retention_policy` **configured on this composition's single Audit Trail instance** at deployment (a policy reference, or a content-derived policy selector). Audit Trail's `record_action(action_ref, actor_ref, credential, data)` takes **no per-call retention argument** — retention is a property of the instance, per that composition's own Configuration — so every audit write this composition makes inherits this configured policy; no call site passes it. Typically a regulatory policy id (`sox_7_year`, `fda_part_11_predicate_rule`, `hipaa_6_year`, `ich_e6_tmf`). **It carries a declared ordering obligation, and the obligation is not about defensibility.** This composition's `workflow_store`, `gate_to_assignment` and `gate_binding` all rebuild by traversing event payloads, and the substrate destroys a payload in its entirety at this instance's horizon — leaving `event_id` and `sequence_number` and not even the `action_ref` a traversal filters on. So: **the horizon must outlast the longest-lived workflow instance the deployment admits.** Violating it does not merely thin an audit. It takes the [Gate Spec] out of `workflow_store`'s rebuild, where no constituent holds it because the gate specification is a composition-introduced surface; and it takes entries out of the moot-gate plan's anchored derivation ([Fire Transition] step 4a), whose completeness is what makes Generation acceptance check 7's *exact* comparison meaningful — so a long-running instance past the horizon silently reacquires the very under-reporting the anchoring removed. Generation acceptance check 8 tests it. The choice is deployment policy; regulated deployments must configure it explicitly, and the audit record of a workflow should persist at least as long as the process records it attests, and often longer for litigation defensibility.

- **`application_actor_ref` and `application_credential`** — the deployment-provisioned actor reference and credential the composition uses when emitting Audit Trail entries outside a human actor's own invocation: the `moot_gate_recalled` event produced when the composition cascade-recalls a gate that has become unreachable because the workflow left its `from_state` by firing a different transition, and every **recovery re-emission** — an owed audit record the implementation lands outside the invocation that constructed it, or one whose human actor's credential is rejected `invalid-credential` before it lands (Action wiring preamble); each recovery re-emission carries the original human actor in its event `data` and `recovery = true`. The composition actor is a first-class registered actor in the Audit Trail's underlying Actor Identity registry — not a special-cased nil — and its attestations verify under the same rules as human-actor attestations. The deployment is responsible for issuing, rotating, and retiring this credential under the same Permissions and operational discipline applied to any privileged service identity. An auditor querying the Audit Trail sees `moot_gate_recalled` events attributed to the composition actor and can verify them against the registry's public material exactly as for human-actor events. The forgery defense for composition-actor events rests on the composition's own structural records: a forged `moot_gate_recalled` event claiming a gate was recalled for a transition that the State Machine history shows was actually fired (rather than superseded by an alternate transition) is detectable by cross-referencing the event against the transition history. The distinguishing rule: an event carries `actor_ref = application_actor_ref` exactly when the composition emits it outside a human actor's own invocation — the `moot_gate_recalled` cascade and the `recovery = true` re-emissions, which name the human actor in their `data`; all other workflow events use the human actor's reference.

### Scope vocabulary

Permissions treats action scopes as opaque. This composition defines the canonical scope vocabulary for its Permissions instance:

| Scope | Permits |
|-------|---------|
| [Workflows Start] | Call [Start Workflow] to instantiate a new workflow run |
| [Workflows Open Gate] | Call [Open Gate] to open an approval gate for a guarded transition |
| [Workflows Fire] | Call [Fire Transition] to advance the workflow through a transition |
| [Workflows Read] | Read workflow records and their composed gate, assignment, and attestation surface |

Gate decisions ([Decide Gate], which wraps Approval Step's `approve`/`reject`/`withdraw`) are *not* additionally permission-gated at the workflow layer: Approval Step's Invariant 4 (only the named `approver_ref` may transition Pending to Approved or Rejected) and Invariant 5 (only the named `submitter_ref` may transition Pending to Withdrawn) are the structural enforcement for who may decide each gate. Adding a second permission check at the workflow layer would be redundant and risks the two checks drifting out of sync. The workflow composition relies on Approval Step's enforcement and surfaces an `unauthorized` rejection from the underlying atom unchanged, mirroring Multi-Party Approval's scope-vocabulary discipline.

The vocabulary is deployment-configurable. A deployment that distinguishes initiator roles (e.g., `workflows:start:pharma-batch`, `workflows:start:financial-journal`) introduces finer-grained scopes and adjusts the wiring accordingly; the canonical vocabulary above is the minimum useful set.

### Logic confinement (clock and id)

The clock is an **injected input at the composition's single I/O seam**, never read inside a guard or a transition and never threaded through a caller signature. Per the Logic Confinement Principle ([`execution-contract.md`](../execution-contract.md)), the host reads the clock once per invocation and injects `now` (`clock_t`) at the seam before the orchestration runs; the actions below are pure functions of their inputs, the constituent records, and that injected `now`. Because the clock enters at the seam rather than as a parameter, the action signatures below carry **no** `now` argument. Where a constituent's own contract accepts an explicit timestamp — `instantiated_at` on `WorkflowStateMachine.instantiate`, `fired_at` on `WorkflowStateMachine.fire`, `submitted_at` on `ApprovalStep.submit`, `decided_at` / `withdrawn_at` on Approval Step's decisions — the composition passes that seam-injected reading rather than leaving the constituent to take a second, independent clock reading.

`now` is consumed for exactly one purpose at this layer: **stamping timestamps on committed writes.** There are no derived deadlines — the composition computes no TTL (time-to-live), expiry, or due date — and **no clock-bearing guard**: the load-bearing gate in [Fire Transition] is *state*-valued, not time-valued (it reads whether the bound Approval Step is in Approved), so no guard in this composition consults the clock at all. The stamps are:

- `instantiated_at` on `WorkflowStateMachine.instantiate` and `started_at` on the `workflow_store` record, at [Start Workflow];
- `submitted_at` on `ApprovalStep.submit`, at [Open Gate];
- `decided_at` on `ApprovalStep.approve` / `ApprovalStep.reject` and `withdrawn_at` on `ApprovalStep.withdraw`, at [Decide Gate] and on the moot-gate cascade;
- `fired_at` on `WorkflowStateMachine.fire`, at [Fire Transition];
- `intended_at` on each action's intent record (`workflow_start_intended`, `gate_open_intended`, `gate_decision_intended`, `transition_intended`) — payload provenance only, since `record_action` accepts no timestamp parameter.

Ordering is never derived from those stamps: the State Machine's `sequence_number` is the authoritative order of the transition history and the Audit Trail's own sequence is the authoritative order of the event record (Edge cases — *Clock source for `fired_at` and `decided_at`*). Nor is any eligibility, expired, or overdue flag stored anywhere in the composition's emergent state — `workflow_store`, `gate_binding`, `gate_to_assignment`, and `transition_to_event` carry identity, binding, and immutable stamps only — so nothing at this layer can lag the clock.

**One injected `now` per invocation, shared — where a contract accepts it; payload provenance where none does.** Within a single [Start Workflow] the same reading is both `instantiated_at` and `started_at`, so the constituent's instance record and the composition's workflow record name one instant rather than two adjacent ones — these are explicit timestamp parameters the constituents' contracts accept. The Audit Trail's `record_action(action_ref, actor_ref, credential, data)` accepts **no** timestamp: the substrate stamps its events at its own seam, and this composition never claims that stamp equals its own reading. What it does instead is **payload provenance**: the invocation's reading is carried *inside the event `data`* (`started_at` on `workflow_started`, `submitted_at` on `gate_opened`, `decided_at` / `withdrawn_at` on `gate_decided`, `fired_at` on `transition_fired`), so the audit payload names the same instant the constituent record does, while the event's own `recorded_at` remains the substrate seam's independent reading. Within a single [Open Gate] the same reading is the Approval Step's `submitted_at` and the `gate_opened` payload's `submitted_at` — which matters because Approval Step's Invariant 7 (`decided_at ≥ submitted_at`) is enforced before commit. Within a single [Decide Gate] the same reading is the Approval Step's `decided_at` / `withdrawn_at` and the `gate_decided` payload's copy. Within a single [Fire Transition] the same reading is `fired_at`, the `transition_fired` payload's `fired_at`, and the moot-gate cascade the firing triggers (step 7) — the cascade is part of that invocation, not a second one with a clock read of its own. Each action's `intended_at` is that same single reading, so an invocation's intent event and its outcome event carry **one instant** in their payloads. **The consequence must be stated rather than discovered:** their order cannot be read off those stamps, and no check may try to — ordering between an intent event and its outcome is the Audit Trail's own sequence, which is already this composition's authoritative order source for the event record. This costs nothing, because ordering was never derived from the stamps here.

**The inter-seam skew arm is named, not wished away:** the constituents bound their explicit timestamps (`fired_at` ≥ `instantiated_at` and not in the future; `decided_at` / `withdrawn_at` ≥ `submitted_at` and not in the future), each judged against records stamped from *earlier invocations'* readings — under skewed injections a fresh reading can fall below a stored bound or read as future, and the constituent rejects `invalid-request`. Because this layer validates all caller inputs first, a constituent `invalid-request` on these timestamp-accepting calls is that cross-seam case (or a validation disagreement), never the caller's fault: it is mapped to `recording-failure(pre-commit)` — nothing committed; the caller may retry, and the retry's later reading clears the bound. Ids are allocated by the constituents at their own seams: `instance_id` by State Machine's `instantiate`, `step_id` by Approval Step's `submit`, `assignment_id` by Assignment's `assign`, `transition_id` by the appended State Machine history entry, and `event_id` by Audit Trail's `record_action`. This composition mints no id inside a transition and generates no cryptographic material — attestations and seals belong to the Audit Trail substrate, produced at its own seam through Actor Identity and Tamper Evidence.

### Primitive policies

These input-validation rules apply at the composition boundary before any constituent atom is invoked. They close the *"but what does X mean exactly?"* class of Pass 3 findings up front.

- **`actor_ref`, `subject_ref`, `initiator_ref`, `approver_ref`, `submitter_ref`** — must each contain at least one non-whitespace character. Null, empty, or whitespace-only values are `invalid-request` at the composition boundary before any Permissions or constituent call.
- **`credential`** — must be a parseable, non-null value of the deployment's declared credential type. Malformed credentials are `invalid-request` before any constituent call.
- **`reason`** — optional on [Start Workflow], [Open Gate], [Fire Transition]. Required as the `reason` argument passed into [Decide Gate] for gate decisions of type Rejected or Withdrawn (propagated from Approval Step's `reject`/`withdraw` mandatory-reason rule). If supplied in any action, must contain at least one non-whitespace character.
- **`gate_spec`** — at [Start Workflow], the composition validates that `gate_spec` covers exactly the set of guarded transitions in the supplied declaration: for every transition in the declaration carrying a `guard` label, `gate_spec` must contain an entry keyed by that guard label, and each entry must carry a non-whitespace `approver_ref` and a non-whitespace `scope`. A `gate_spec` with entries for guard labels not present in the declaration is `invalid-request` (the deployment named a gate that will never be used). A declaration with guarded transitions for which `gate_spec` has no entry is `invalid-request` (a guarded transition with no named approver cannot be governed). Each entry's guard label, `approver_ref`, and `scope` — and the entry count — are additionally validated against **budget-derived caps**: the deployment derives, from its Audit Trail instance's payload budget and reference caps, the per-field and per-count bounds under which the largest event this composition constructs (`workflow_started`, which embeds the complete [Gate Spec]) is guaranteed within budget; the same caps bound every free-text field the composition embeds in any event payload (`subject_ref`, `declaration_ref`, `reason`, the scope strings), and **minted-id width bounds** are declared for `instance_id`, `step_id`, `assignment_id`, `transition_id`, and `event_id` (the substrate's `attestation_id_width` move) so id-carrying payloads are sizable before any id is minted. An over-cap value is `invalid-request` here, before any constituent call — this is what forecloses the **payload-size** source of `record_action`'s `invalid-request` arm by construction (Action wiring preamble). It does not foreclose the arm outright: the substrate routes its own retention-configuration faults onto the same code, which no cap here can bound. The `gate_spec` validation is part of [Start Workflow] step 2; no `WorkflowStateMachine.instantiate` call is made until `gate_spec` passes.
- **`instance_id`** — must contain at least one non-whitespace character for all actions that take it. Non-whitespace enforcement follows State Machine's own `instance_id` rule.
- **`action`** — must contain at least one non-whitespace character for every action that takes it ([Open Gate], [Decide Gate], [Fire Transition]). The transition-name vocabulary is the declaration's own: a well-formed but undeclared `action` lands on the declared `invalid-transition` arms, never `invalid-request`.
- **`decision`** — at [Decide Gate], must be one of the string literals `"approve"`, `"reject"`, or `"withdraw"`; any other value is `invalid-request`.

### Action wiring

Every state-changing action follows the same four-part shape: **Permissions check** (where the action has one), **intent record**, **load-bearing constituent call(s)**, **outcome record**. The intent record is an `AuditTrail.record_action` written before anything commits; it is where the substrate validates the caller's credential, and it is what makes the constituent calls that follow acts of a verified principal (Invariant 8). [Decide Gate] differs in exactly one respect and no other: it has **no Permissions check**, because structural authorization there is Approval Step's Invariants 4 and 5 rather than a scope grant — its order is intent record, decision, outcome record. Each action lists the full rejection taxonomy; every constituent rejection is either propagated, renamed, or surfaced as a new code at the composition boundary.

**`recording-failure` carries a stage discriminator, and it is load-bearing.** Every `recording-failure` this composition returns is `recording-failure(stage)` with `stage ∈ {pre-commit, post-commit}`: `pre-commit` means the action's **load-bearing write did not commit** (the State Machine transition for [Fire Transition]; the Approval Step decision for [Decide Gate]; the Approval Step submit for [Open Gate]; the constituent instantiate for [Start Workflow]) — the caller may safely retry the whole action. **Every action's intent record is a `pre-commit` write by construction**: it precedes the load-bearing call, so a failure there leaves nothing committed and the whole action is retryable, and the composition-actor re-emission rule below never applies to it — that rule lands a record *owed* for an act that already happened, and a refused intent record means the act does not happen. `post-commit` means the load-bearing write **committed** and a subsequent write failed — and what happens next **splits by action class, stated here once**. For the two **mutation actions** ([Fire Transition], [Decide Gate]): the caller must **not** re-invoke (a re-invocation would re-fire a non-idempotent transition or re-decide a decided gate; see Edge cases — *[Fire Transition] non-idempotency*); recovery is the implementation's, retrying the *failed write* per *Cross-store consistency under partial failure*, and the caller confirms the committed outcome through [Read Workflow]. For the two **creation actions**, whose load-bearing write minted fresh ids, the two are opposites: [Start Workflow]'s orphan instance is referenced by nothing (its audit record never landed, and its map entry is written only after the record), so the orphan is quarantined and the caller **re-invokes**, minting fresh ids — re-invocation is safe by construction; [Open Gate]'s committed step and Assignment are live in the approver's in-tray, so the implementation **completes the remainder** (the assign where that is what failed, then the `gate_opened` record) under retry-until-lands as a hard alerting condition, the gate staying structurally quarantined until the record lands — no map binding exists yet, so [Decide Gate] answers [Gate Not Open] and [Fire Transition] answers [Gate Not Cleared] — and the caller must **not** re-invoke (a re-invoke before the maps land would pass step 5's no-binding check and double-submit a second gate). Map writes appear in no stage at all: they follow the audit record and are derived-index writes outside the atomicity surface — a failed map write is a rebuild trigger, never a failure arm. Each step's failure arm above maps to its stage by its position relative to the load-bearing write.

**Retries assume transience only where transience holds — every retried `record_action`'s rejection arms are partitioned into transient and deterministic, with a landing for each deterministic arm.** Each retry-until-lands obligation above loops only on the substrate's transient failure (`recording-failure(step)` — a storage or attestation fault a later attempt can clear). The two deterministic arms never loop: `invalid-request` has **two sources and neither loops**. Its *payload-size* source is foreclosed by construction — Primitive policies bounds every field the composition embeds in an event payload (the `gate_spec` caps, the guarded-transition count that bounds `moot_gate_plan`, and minted-id width bounds) — so an over-budget payload can only mean a mis-derived deployment cap, a pageable fault. Its *retention-configuration* source is not foreclosed and cannot be: Audit Trail routes its own Retention Window rejections onto this arm and names them a deployment fault rather than a caller fault, and because the substrate places retention after it appends, this arm can leave an appended, unretained event that the substrate's reconciliation owns. Also pageable, also never retried; and `invalid-credential` is **landed by attribution** — an owed record emitted outside the original invocation (an implementation retry after the invocation returned, or a record whose human actor's credential was revoked mid-flight) is emitted under `application_actor_ref` / `application_credential` with the original human actor in its event `data` and `recovery = true` (Configuration), so a revoked-mid-flight caller cannot orphan a committed act's audit trail, and an `invalid-credential` on such a composition-attributed emission is the deployment's own service-credential fault — a pageable alert, never a loop. A recovery re-emission carries the payload the original construction carried, plus the human actor and the `recovery = true` marker; its `actor_ref` is the composition actor — which is why no rebuild in this composition reads a human identity off an event's `actor_ref`.

#### `start_workflow(actor_ref, declaration, subject_ref, gate_spec, credential, [declaration_ref], [reason]) → {instance_id} | rejected(permission-denied | invalid-credential | invalid-declaration | invalid-request | recording-failure)`

`declaration_ref` is an optional caller-supplied opaque reference naming the deployment's process-definition identity for this declaration (typically a Definition Registry key — Edge cases, *Declaration versioning*); it is stored and recorded verbatim, never dereferenced by the composition, and null when the deployment names none.

1. `Permissions.permitted(actor_ref, workflows:start)` → if `denied`, return `rejected(permission-denied)`.
2. Validate `gate_spec` against `declaration` per Primitive policies: every guarded transition in the declaration has a matching entry in `gate_spec`; every `gate_spec` entry names a real guard label; every entry carries non-whitespace `approver_ref` and `scope`; and the **count of guarded transitions** is within its budget-derived cap — the cap that bounds [Fire Transition]'s `moot_gate_plan`, which carries one entry per bound gate and therefore scales with guarded transitions rather than with `gate_spec` entries (several transitions may share one guard label). Validate `subject_ref` per Primitive policies (non-whitespace). Any violation is `rejected(invalid-request)`. No State Machine call is made until this passes.
3. **Intent record.** `AuditTrail.record_action(action_ref=workflow_start_intended, actor_ref, credential, data={subject_ref, declaration_ref, gate_spec, intended_at=now})` → `event_id_intent`. This is where the substrate validates the caller's credential against the actor registry for the supplied `actor_ref`, so no State Machine instance is minted on an unverified claim (*Authentication precedes commitment*; Invariant 8). Nothing has committed, so every arm is a clean pre-state rejection and the caller may safely retry the whole action: `invalid-credential` → `rejected(invalid-credential)`; `invalid-request` → `rejected(invalid-request)` — the budget caps foreclose its **payload-size** source only, so in practice this is either a mis-derived deployment cap or the substrate's **retention-configuration** source (Audit Trail routes its Retention Window `invalid-request` / `invalid-policy` / `policy-not-found` onto this arm, a deployment fault its own contract names as such); both are pageable, neither is retried. **The retention source is the one arm where "nothing committed" needs its qualifier:** the substrate places retention *after* it appends, so an intent event can be in the Event Log unretained while this composition returns a refusal — nothing committed **at this composition's layer**, and the stranded unretained event is owned by the substrate's own reconciliation, never by a compensating write here. `recording-failure(step)` → `rejected(recording-failure(pre-commit))`. **The composition-actor re-emission rule does not apply here and must not be reached for:** that rule lands a record *owed* for an act that already committed, and an intent record precedes the act, so there is nothing owed — a refused intent record means the action does not happen, which is the entire point. The payload carries **no `instance_id`** — none exists yet, and `workflow_store`'s rebuild traverses `workflow_started` events alone; an intent event naming an instance would manufacture a rebuild source for an instance that may never have been created.
4. Call `WorkflowStateMachine.instantiate(declaration, actor_ref, instance_metadata={subject_ref, gate_spec_labels}, instantiated_at=now)` → `instance_id | rejected(invalid-declaration)`, where `instantiated_at` is the **seam-injected `now`** for this invocation (see *Logic confinement*). Propagate `invalid-declaration` unchanged. `storage-failure` from the constituent surfaces as `recording-failure`.
5. Call `AuditTrail.record_action(action_ref=workflow_started, actor_ref, credential, data={intent_event_id, instance_id, subject_ref, initiator_ref=actor_ref, declaration_ref, gate_spec, started_at, reason?})` → `event_id | rejected(...)`, where `started_at` is the **same** seam-injected `now` step 4 passed as `instantiated_at` — one reading, not two, so the instance record and the event payload name the same instant. The payload carries the **complete** workflow record, the full [Gate Spec] included (not merely its labels), so `workflow_store` is rebuildable from this event alone and check 1's gate-spec dependence is records-alone (Composition state). **The record lands before the map write, deliberately:** the map is a derived index over this event, and writing it first would manufacture a store record with no backing event — a ghost [Read Workflow] would serve and a rebuild would drop. A **transient** Audit Trail failure here surfaces as `recording-failure(post-commit)` under the creation-action rule (preamble): the orphan instance — referenced by no event and no map entry — is quarantined, and the caller re-invokes. The deterministic arms never reach that rule: `invalid-request` is foreclosed by the budget caps (Primitive policies), and on `invalid-credential` at *this* write — reachable only where the actor's registration changed between the step-3 intent record and this one — the composition completes this invocation's record by composition-actor re-emission (`recovery = true`, the initiator in `data` — Action wiring preamble) and the action proceeds — no orphan, and no re-invoke stream minting one quarantined instance per deterministic repeat. (Note: gates are opened lazily — see [Open Gate] — not at start. The event proves the workflow began and under which actor's authority.)
6. Write the `workflow_store` record: `{instance_id, subject_ref, initiator_ref=actor_ref, declaration_ref, gate_spec, started_at}` — a **derived-index write over the just-landed event**, outside the atomicity surface: a failure here is a rebuild trigger, never a failure arm, and the action still returns.
7. Return `{instance_id}`.

**Partial-failure recovery.** One case exists: step 4 succeeded and step 5's record failed. The State Machine instance then exists in its constituent store with no audit event and no map entry — referenced by nothing, invisible to every rebuild, and never served by [Read Workflow] (the map write follows the record, so no ghost record can precede its event). The composition does not issue the `instance_id`; the orphaned instance is quarantined for the deployment's store-maintenance process, and the caller re-invokes [Start Workflow], minting fresh ids — safe by construction (the creation-action rule, Action wiring preamble). The deterministic arms never produce the orphan: `invalid-request` is foreclosed and `invalid-credential` completes by composition-actor re-emission (step 5). A step-6 map failure is not a recovery case at all: the truth landed at step 5, and the map rebuilds from it.

#### `open_gate(actor_ref, instance_id, action, credential) → {step_id, assignment_id} | rejected(permission-denied | invalid-credential | invalid-request | not-known | invalid-transition | not-guarded | gate-not-available | already-open | recording-failure)`

This action opens an approval gate for a guarded transition whose `from_state` equals the workflow instance's current state. Gates are opened lazily — the composition does not open all gates at start, only when the workflow has reached the state from which the guarded transition departs and an actor with `workflows:open-gate` authority decides to open it.

1. `Permissions.permitted(actor_ref, workflows:open-gate)` → if `denied`, return `rejected(permission-denied)`.
2. Look up `workflow_store[instance_id]` → `not-known` if absent.
3. Call `WorkflowStateMachine.current(instance_id)` → `current_state`, and `WorkflowStateMachine.read_declaration(instance_id)` for the immutable declaration. **Terminal is checked first, matching the constituent's own rejection priority (`not-known` → `terminal` → `invalid-transition`):** if `current_state ∈ terminal_states`, return [Gate Not Available] — a terminal instance cannot have gates opened, and `WorkflowStateMachine.fire` would likewise return `terminal`. (Checked before the transition match because a terminal state never has a matching outgoing transition — the reversed order would shadow this arm into dead code.)
4. Identify the declared transition matching `(current_state, action)`. If no declared transition has `(from_state = current_state, action = action)`, return `invalid-transition`. If the matched transition has no `guard` label, return [Not Guarded] (unguarded transitions are fired directly without opening a gate).
5. Check the current binding: look up `gate_binding[(instance_id, current_state, action)]`. If a binding exists, read its bound step's state (`ApprovalStep.read({step_id})` — the singleton query; take the single returned step; an **empty sequence** is the internal-consistency anomaly [Fire Transition] step 5b names — the binding resolved a step the store does not know — surfaced as `recording-failure` for the store-maintenance process). If the step is `Pending` → return [Already Open] (a live gate is already bound; call [Decide Gate] to resolve it). If the step is `Approved` and **unconsumed** (no `transition_fired` event carries its `step_id` and no committed-but-unrecorded firing holds it — the consumption rule's two arms, Composition state, *Binding lifecycle*) → return [Already Open] as well, with the distinct meaning stated to the caller in the rejection data: the gate is *cleared, unconsumed* — fire the transition rather than re-opening; an unconsumed approval is never silently discarded by a re-open, and no path can discard it (Approval Step's `withdraw` is Pending-only). If the step is `Rejected`, `Withdrawn`, or **`Approved`-and-consumed** (the return-path case — the prior approval authorized exactly the one firing that consumed it) → proceed: the key may be **re-bound** to a fresh gate; the new `gate_opened` event appends to the key's binding history and the map repoints to the new `step_id` at step 10 (Composition state — *Binding lifecycle*).
6. **Intent record.** `AuditTrail.record_action(action_ref=gate_open_intended, actor_ref, credential, data={instance_id, from_state=current_state, action, guard_label, approver_ref=gate_spec[guard_label].approver_ref, scope=gate_spec[guard_label].scope, intended_at=now})` → `event_id_intent`, written before the `ApprovalStep.submit` that commits. **This record** is where the substrate validates the opener's credential — `ApprovalStep.submit` accepts no credential and verifies nothing — so no Approval Step is created on an unverified claim (*Authentication precedes commitment*; Invariant 8). Nothing has committed, so every arm is a clean pre-state rejection and the caller may safely retry the whole action: `invalid-credential` → `rejected(invalid-credential)`; `invalid-request` → `rejected(invalid-request)` — the budget caps foreclose its **payload-size** source only, so in practice this is either a mis-derived deployment cap or the substrate's **retention-configuration** source (Audit Trail routes its Retention Window `invalid-request` / `invalid-policy` / `policy-not-found` onto this arm, a deployment fault its own contract names as such); both are pageable, neither is retried. **The retention source is the one arm where "nothing committed" needs its qualifier:** the substrate places retention *after* it appends, so an intent event can be in the Event Log unretained while this composition returns a refusal — nothing committed **at this composition's layer**, and the stranded unretained event is owned by the substrate's own reconciliation, never by a compensating write here. `recording-failure(step)` → `rejected(recording-failure(pre-commit))`. **The composition-actor re-emission rule does not apply here and must not be reached for:** that rule lands a record *owed* for an act that already committed, and an intent record precedes the act, so there is nothing owed — a refused intent record means the action does not happen, which is the entire point. The payload carries **no `step_id`** and **no `assignment_id`** — neither exists yet, and `gate_binding` rebuilds from `gate_opened` events keyed by `(instance_id, from_state, action)`; an intent event carrying a `step_id` would put a second, non-authoritative binding source into the trail the binding-history rebuild reads.
7. Call `ApprovalStep.submit(subject_ref=workflow_store[instance_id].subject_ref + ":" + action, approver_ref=gate_spec[guard_label].approver_ref, submitter_ref=workflow_store[instance_id].initiator_ref, scope=gate_spec[guard_label].scope, submitted_at=now)` → `step_id | rejected(invalid-request | storage-failure)`. Propagate failures as `recording-failure` (a temporal-bound `invalid-request` here is the cross-seam skew arm — *Logic confinement* — mapped `recording-failure(pre-commit)`, retryable). The concatenated gate subject carries a declared non-parsing rule: the composition **never parses it back** — the transition↔step binding truth is `gate_binding` and the `gate_opened` event, not the string — so no delimiter escaping is required here; a deployment that chooses to parse gate subjects in its own tooling must pick an injective encoding for its `subject_ref` vocabulary, a deployment obligation named rather than assumed. **The gate's `submitter_ref` is the workflow's `initiator_ref`, not the [Open Gate] caller** — so the withdrawal authority over the gate (Approval Step Invariant 5: only the submitter may withdraw) belongs to the workflow initiator, which is what makes both `decide_gate(withdraw)` and the composition's moot-gate cascade (which withdraw with `withdrawn_by = initiator_ref`) authorized. The [Open Gate] caller is recorded as the actor on the `gate_opened` Audit Trail event (step 9), preserving the attribution of who opened the gate; the *withdrawal* authority is the initiator's, mirroring Multi-Party Approval's initiator-owns-withdrawal discipline.
8. Call `Assignment.assign(task_ref=step_id, assignee_ref=gate_spec[guard_label].approver_ref)` → `assignment_id | rejected(...)`. A failure here is `recording-failure(post-commit)` under the creation-action rule (preamble): step 7's submit committed, so the implementation completes the remainder under retry-until-lands — the assign retried (an `already-assigned` on retry means the first attempt actually committed and is treated as idempotent success, the fresh `step_id` guaranteeing no other assignment can own the `task_ref`), then step 9's record — as a hard alerting condition; the caller must not re-invoke.
9. Call `AuditTrail.record_action(action_ref=gate_opened, actor_ref, credential, data={intent_event_id, instance_id, from_state=current_state, action, step_id, assignment_id, approver_ref, scope, submitted_at})` — `from_state` makes the binding-history rebuild key-exact, and `submitted_at` is the payload-provenance copy of the seam reading step 7 passed (*Logic confinement*). **The record lands before the map writes, deliberately** — the binding is a derived index over this event, and a binding that preceded its event would make an unaudited gate decidable and would vanish on rebuild under an already-fired transition. A failure here is `recording-failure(post-commit)`: the implementation retries this record until it lands as a hard alerting condition — looping only on the transient `recording-failure(step)`; an `invalid-credential` lands by composition-actor re-emission and `invalid-request` is foreclosed by the budget caps (the retry partition, Action wiring preamble) — and until it does the gate is **structurally quarantined** — no map binding exists, so [Decide Gate] answers [Gate Not Open] and [Fire Transition] answers [Gate Not Cleared]; the approver's in-tray item exists, but the gate accepts no decision until its opening is audited. The caller must not re-invoke (the creation-action rule, preamble — a re-invoke before the maps land would pass step 5's no-binding check and double-submit).
10. Write: `gate_binding[(instance_id, current_state, action)] = step_id`; `gate_to_assignment[step_id] = assignment_id` — **derived-index writes over the just-landed event**, outside the atomicity surface: a failure is a rebuild trigger, never a failure arm, and the action still returns.
11. Return `{step_id, assignment_id}`.

**Partial-failure recovery.** Both cases follow the creation-action rule (Action wiring preamble) and neither leaves a decidable unaudited gate. A step-6 (intent record) or step-7 (submit) failure is `pre-commit` — nothing committed; the caller retries the whole action. After step 7 commits, the implementation owns completion: a step-8 failure retries the assign (idempotent per the `already-assigned` rule) and proceeds; a step-9 failure retries the `gate_opened` record until it lands, with the gate structurally quarantined meanwhile (no binding → [Gate Not Open] / [Gate Not Cleared]) — so the once-possible orphan states (an unaudited gate the approver could decide; a binding a rebuild would drop) are unreachable by ordering. Map-write failures at step 10 are rebuild triggers, not recovery cases.

#### `decide_gate(actor_ref, instance_id, action, decision, reason?, credential) → approved | rejected_outcome | withdrawn | rejected(not-known | gate-not-open | not-pending | unauthorized | invalid-credential | invalid-request | recording-failure)`

`reason` is optional for `decision = "approve"` and **required** for `"reject"` and `"withdraw"` (Approval Step's mandatory-reason rule, validated at Primitive policies before any constituent call) — the one statement of its optionality; the per-arm notes below apply it.

This action wraps the Approval Step's `approve`/`reject`/`withdraw` on the bound gate for the transition matched from the current state (`gate_binding[(instance_id, current_state, action)]`). Structural authorization (only the named `approver_ref` may approve or reject; only `submitter_ref` may withdraw) is enforced by Approval Step's Invariant 4 and Invariant 5 directly; no redundant Permissions check is added.

1. Look up `workflow_store[instance_id]` → `not-known` if absent.
2. Call `WorkflowStateMachine.current(instance_id)` → `current_state`, and look up `gate_binding[(instance_id, current_state, action)]` → `step_id`. If no binding exists, return [Gate Not Open] (there is no Approval Step open for this transition from the current state; call [Open Gate] first). Resolving through the current state is sound by construction: gates open only for transitions departing the current state, and the moot-gate cascade withdraws Pending gates when the workflow leaves it — a binding under another `from_state` holds a terminal step [Decide Gate] could only answer `not-pending` for (at quiescence — a mooted step whose cascade withdrawal is still landing is Pending a moment longer, but this step's current-state resolution never reaches it).
3. Validate `decision` is one of `{"approve", "reject", "withdraw"}` → `invalid-request` otherwise.
4. **Intent record.** `AuditTrail.record_action(action_ref=gate_decision_intended, actor_ref, credential, data={instance_id, from_state=current_state, action, step_id, decision, reason?, intended_at=now})` → `event_id_intent`, written before the Approval Step call that commits the decision. **This action has no Permissions check by design** — structural authorization is Approval Step's Invariants 4 and 5, which compare the supplied `actor_ref` against the step's named `approver_ref` or `submitter_ref` — so before this record the composition's load-bearing claim, *the named approver approved*, was being decided against an actor reference nobody had verified. The intent record is where that reference is validated against the actor registry, and it is therefore what turns Approval Step's exclusivity check from a comparison of two strings into a statement about a verified principal (*Authentication precedes commitment*; Invariant 8, and its binding half). Nothing has committed, so every arm is a clean pre-state rejection and the caller may safely retry the whole action: `invalid-credential` → `rejected(invalid-credential)`; `invalid-request` → `rejected(invalid-request)` — the budget caps foreclose its **payload-size** source only, so in practice this is either a mis-derived deployment cap or the substrate's **retention-configuration** source (Audit Trail routes its Retention Window `invalid-request` / `invalid-policy` / `policy-not-found` onto this arm, a deployment fault its own contract names as such); both are pageable, neither is retried. **The retention source is the one arm where "nothing committed" needs its qualifier:** the substrate places retention *after* it appends, so an intent event can be in the Event Log unretained while this composition returns a refusal — nothing committed **at this composition's layer**, and the stranded unretained event is owned by the substrate's own reconciliation, never by a compensating write here. `recording-failure(step)` → `rejected(recording-failure(pre-commit))`. **The composition-actor re-emission rule does not apply here and must not be reached for:** that rule lands a record *owed* for an act that already committed, and an intent record precedes the act, so there is nothing owed — a refused intent record means the action does not happen, which is the entire point.
5. Dispatch on `decision` — each constituent call below carries its `decided_at` / `withdrawn_at` stamped from the **seam-injected `now`** for this invocation, the same reading step 7's `gate_decided` event carries in its payload (payload provenance — see *Logic confinement*). A temporal-bound `invalid-request` from these calls (the resolved stamp below the step's `submitted_at`, or read as future — possible only under cross-seam skew, since this layer validated the caller's inputs first) is the skew arm *Logic confinement* names: mapped `recording-failure(pre-commit)`, retryable with a later reading; a *caller*-caused `invalid-request` (a whitespace reason on approve, malformed inputs) is caught at Primitive policies before any call.
   - `"approve"`: call `ApprovalStep.approve(step_id, decided_by=actor_ref, reason?, decided_at=now)` → `approved | rejected(invalid-request | not-known | not-pending | unauthorized | storage-failure)`. Propagate `not-pending` and `unauthorized` unchanged; map `storage-failure` to `recording-failure`; map the constituent's `not-known` to `recording-failure` — an internal-consistency anomaly (the composition's own binding named a step the Approval Step store does not know; step 2 resolved the binding), distinct from step 1's caller-supplied unknown-instance `not-known`, surfaced for the store-maintenance process. `invalid-request` is the temporal skew arm per this step's preamble — `recording-failure(pre-commit)` — since caller-caused malformations were caught at Primitive policies.
   - `"reject"`: call `ApprovalStep.reject(step_id, decided_by=actor_ref, reason, decided_at=now)` → `rejected_outcome | rejected(...)`. `reason` is required; a null or whitespace-only `reason` is caught at the Primitive policies layer and returned as `invalid-request` before this call is made. Propagate rejections as above.
   - `"withdraw"`: call `ApprovalStep.withdraw(step_id, withdrawn_by=actor_ref, reason, withdrawn_at=now)` → `withdrawn | rejected(...)`. `reason` is required. Gate withdrawal is the workflow initiator's act: because the gate's Approval Step carries `submitter_ref = initiator_ref` (set at [Open Gate] step 7), Approval Step Invariant 5 admits a withdrawal only when `actor_ref` is the workflow initiator; a non-initiator withdrawal attempt surfaces `unauthorized` unchanged. Propagate rejections as above.
6. On atom-level success, recall the in-tray binding: `Assignment.recall(gate_to_assignment[step_id])` — every rejection arm mapped, none failing the action (the decision committed at step 5; the recall is discharge bookkeeping). `not-active` (a moot-gate cascade already discharged it) is idempotent no-op success. `storage-failure` is transient: the action proceeds to step 7 and returns the decision outcome, and the implementation retries the recall until it lands as a hard alerting condition — the recovery-bounded window Invariant 4 and check 4 name. `not-known` is a stale `gate_to_assignment` entry, a derived-index miss: rebuild the map and retry; a `not-known` that survives the rebuild is a conformance fault (an [Open Gate]-committed Assignment cannot be absent from its own store), surfaced as a hard alert for the store-maintenance process, never a retry loop.
7. Call `AuditTrail.record_action(action_ref=gate_decided, actor_ref, credential, data={intent_event_id, instance_id, from_state=current_state, action, step_id, decision, reason?, decided_at | withdrawn_at})` — the stamp is the payload-provenance copy of the step-5 reading. Failure is `recording-failure(post-commit)`. If this `record_action` fails after the Approval Step transition succeeded, the recovery path in Edge cases *Cross-store consistency under partial failure* applies.
8. Return the outcome token from step 5 (`approved`, `rejected_outcome`, or `withdrawn`).

#### `fire_transition(actor_ref, instance_id, action, credential) → new_state | rejected(permission-denied | invalid-credential | invalid-request | not-known | terminal | invalid-transition | gate-not-cleared | recording-failure)`

This is the composition's load-bearing wiring action. For unguarded transitions, it calls `WorkflowStateMachine.fire` directly. For guarded transitions, it reads the bound gate's Approval Step state and asserts `guard_satisfied = true` only when the step is in Approved.

1. `Permissions.permitted(actor_ref, workflows:fire)` → if `denied`, return `rejected(permission-denied)`.
2. Look up `workflow_store[instance_id]` → `not-known` if absent.
3. Call `WorkflowStateMachine.read_declaration(instance_id)` and `WorkflowStateMachine.current(instance_id)` to determine the matched transition and whether it is guarded. **Terminal first, matching the constituent's rejection priority (`not-known` → `terminal` → `invalid-transition`):** if `current_state ∈ terminal_states`, return `terminal` — checked before the transition match, because a terminal state never has a matching outgoing transition and the reversed order would make this arm unreachable. Then, if no declared transition matches `(current_state, action)`, return `invalid-transition` (`WorkflowStateMachine.fire` would return the same).
4. **For an unguarded transition** (the matched transition has no `guard` label):
   a. **Intent record.** Derive the **moot-gate plan** — read-only: the matched transition's declared `to_state`, and every gate on this `instance_id` whose key `from_state` ≠ that `to_state` and whose bound Approval Step reads Pending; this is the set step 7's cascade will withdraw.

  **The derivation is anchored in the `gate_opened` events, not in an enumeration of `gate_binding`, and the distinction is load-bearing rather than stylistic.** `gate_binding` is a **derived index** whose contract is *rebuild-on-miss* — and rebuild-on-miss is a **keyed-lookup** contract: it repairs a gap because a lookup that finds nothing is an observable signal. An **enumeration** of the same map has no miss to observe; it cannot distinguish a key whose entry was lost from a gate that was never opened, so a lost entry would drop a gate from the plan **silently and permanently** — step 7 would never withdraw it, and check 7 keys on the plan, so nothing downstream could notice. A human approval that this firing made moot would simply survive, Pending, and stand ready to clear the guarded transition if the workflow ever returns to that `from_state`, which is the precise harm the cascade exists to prevent. So the derivation runs the other way round: **enumerate this instance's `gate_opened` events** through the declared enumerate-and-filter surface (*Composes*) — the binding history is append-only and lives in those events, each carrying its `from_state` — take the latest event per `(instance_id, from_state, action)` key, and for each such key resolve the current binding through the **keyed** `gate_binding` lookup with rebuild-on-miss before reading the bound step's state. The index is still used, and still a cache; what changed is that its completeness is no longer what the plan's completeness rests on. Only a miss that survives the rebuild is a miss. (In-corpus exemplar: [Audit Trail](./audit-trail.md)'s unretained-event sweep, which anchors in the log and probes `event_to_retention` per event for exactly this reason.)

  **The anchor's own bound, stated rather than assumed.** The `gate_opened` events carry `from_state` in their payloads, so this derivation is a payload-sourced read and inherits the substrate's retention horizon: past it, the cascade's own retention policy governs what remains enumerable. The bound is benign for the ordinary case — a workflow instance's gates are contemporary with its own execution, far inside any audit retention horizon a regulated deployment sets — but it is a real bound and it is now a **declared deployment obligation** rather than a caution: `audit_trail_retention_policy`'s horizon must outlast the longest-lived workflow instance the deployment admits (Configuration; Generation acceptance check 9). Past that point the plan's completeness degrades to whatever `gate_binding` still holds, which is exactly the silent under-reporting the anchoring was introduced to remove — **the two classes of this debt meeting on one line, where the second class's repair is undone by the third class's gap unless the deployment closes it.** Then `AuditTrail.record_action(action_ref=transition_intended, actor_ref, credential, data={instance_id, action, from_state=current_state, to_state, guarded=<bool>, step_id if guarded, moot_gate_plan, intended_at=now})` → `event_id_intent`, written before the `WorkflowStateMachine.fire` that commits. **This record** is where the substrate validates the firer's credential — `WorkflowStateMachine.fire` takes no credential at all — so a declared transition never commits on an unverified actor's asserted authority (*Authentication precedes commitment*; Invariant 8). Nothing has committed, so every arm is a clean pre-state rejection and the caller may safely retry the whole action: `invalid-credential` → `rejected(invalid-credential)`; `invalid-request` → `rejected(invalid-request)` — the budget caps foreclose its payload-size source only (see the plan-bound note below); the substrate's retention-configuration source remains reachable and is a pageable deployment fault, and it is the one arm on which an intent event may already be appended-but-unretained, so "nothing committed" means nothing committed *at this composition's layer*. `recording-failure(step)` → `rejected(recording-failure(pre-commit))`. The composition-actor re-emission rule does not apply: it lands a record *owed* for a committed act, and nothing has committed here. **The `moot_gate_plan` is a bounded field, and the bound is not the `gate_spec`'s.** It carries one entry per currently-bound gate on this instance — that is, per *guarded transition*, since `gate_binding` is keyed `(instance_id, from_state, action)`. The `gate_spec` is keyed by **guard label**, and nothing forbids several transitions sharing one label, so a `gate_spec` entry cap does not bound the number of bindings. The bound is the **count of guarded transitions in the supplied declaration**, which [Start Workflow] step 2 validates against its own budget-derived cap alongside the `gate_spec` caps (Primitive policies). That is what keeps this payload inside the budget.

  **Why this record carries the plan when [Open Gate]'s and [Start Workflow]'s deliberately do not.** The cascade withdraws *human approvals* under the composition's own service identity, in a burst the caller never sees and whose per-gate `moot_gate_recalled` events are the only other trace. Naming the plan before the fire makes *what this firing was about to moot* a recorded fact independent of whether the cascade completed — which is precisely what the cascade's own retry-until-lands window leaves in doubt. **Under the declared serialization the plan is exact, not an upper bound** — the serialization domain is the `instance_id` across all three state-changing actions (Edge cases — *Concurrent [Fire Transition] calls*), so no [Decide Gate] can interleave between this derivation and step 7's cascade, and nothing else in the invocation changes a bound step's state. The plan is therefore precisely the set step 7 will attempt, and **both directions of divergence are findings, of different severities**. A planned gate with **no** `moot_gate_recalled` event means the withdrawal is still owed — the cascade's retry-until-lands window is open, or the cascade did not run at all — and is an alerting condition, resolved only by confirming that gate's bound step reached a terminal state; it must not be read as ordinary. A `moot_gate_recalled` event naming a gate **no plan named** is a conformance failure: the cascade withdrew a human approval this firing had no basis to moot. Neither direction is checkable without the plan on the record. Contrast [Open Gate] and [Start Workflow], whose intent events carry no minted identifier: there the datum would have a *rival authoritative carrier* (the `gate_opened` event that `gate_binding` rebuilds from; the `workflow_started` event that `workflow_store` rebuilds from), and a second copy in the trail would be a rebuild hazard rather than a cross-check.

   b. Call `WorkflowStateMachine.fire(instance_id, action, actor_ref, fired_at=now)` → `new_state | rejected(...)`, where `fired_at` is the **seam-injected `now`** for this invocation (see *Logic confinement*). Propagate constituent rejections: `not-known` → `not-known`; `terminal` → `terminal`; `invalid-transition` → `invalid-transition`; `storage-failure` → `recording-failure(pre-commit)`; a temporal-bound `invalid-request` (the resolved `fired_at` below the instance's `instantiated_at`, or read as future — the cross-seam skew arm, *Logic confinement*) → `recording-failure(pre-commit)`, retryable with a later reading. (The `guard-not-satisfied` rejection from the constituent is not reachable here because the transition is unguarded and the composition does not pass `guard_satisfied`.)
   c. On constituent success, proceed to step 6, which records the firing and writes `transition_to_event`.

5. **For a guarded transition** (the matched transition carries a `guard` label):
   a. Look up `gate_binding[(instance_id, current_state, action)]` to find the bound `step_id`. If no binding exists, the gate has not been opened — the composition refuses to fire: return [Gate Not Cleared] (the guarded transition cannot be asserted until its gate is opened and resolved).
   b. Read the Approval Step state for `step_id` via `ApprovalStep.read({step_id})` — the constituent's declared query surface returns an **ordered sequence of steps**; the singleton `step_id` query yields at most one, and the composition takes that single step (an empty sequence is an internal-consistency anomaly — the binding names a step the store does not know — surfaced as `recording-failure` for the store-maintenance process, never as a fireable state). If the step is not in Approved state, return `gate-not-cleared`. **If the step is Approved but already consumed** — a `transition_fired` event carries its `step_id`, **or a committed-but-unrecorded firing holds it** (the step-6 retry window's pending record — the consumption rule's second arm, Composition state, *Binding lifecycle*) — return `gate-not-cleared` likewise: one approval authorizes exactly one firing; a firing whose record is still landing already spent this one (the caller retries once the record lands); and a return-path re-firing needs a fresh gate through [Open Gate]'s Approved-and-consumed re-binding arm. The composition evaluates the guard by reading the gate state; it does not delegate this check to the caller. A gate in Pending, Rejected, or Withdrawn state — or consumed — prevents the guarded transition from firing. The caller cannot override this: there is no surface by which the caller supplies `guard_satisfied` for a guarded transition in this composition.
   c. **Intent record** — the same record step 4a specifies, with `guarded = true` and the bound `step_id` carried, so the event names *which approval* this firing is about to consume. Steps 5a and 5b are read-only in their entirety and precede it deliberately: the gate resolution must be settled before the intent is recorded, or the record would name a firing whose authority had not yet been established, and a `gate-not-cleared` refusal — the ordinary outcome of polling a gate that is still Pending — would leave an intent event behind on every poll.

   d. The step is in Approved and the intent record has landed. Call `WorkflowStateMachine.fire(instance_id, action, actor_ref, guard_satisfied=true, fired_at=now)` → `new_state | rejected(...)`, where `fired_at` is the **seam-injected `now`** for this invocation (see *Logic confinement*). The composition asserts `guard_satisfied = true` here because it has just verified, from the bound Approval Step's actual state, that the named approver approved — a *state*-valued check, not a timestamp comparison, so the gate evaluation itself consults no clock. Propagate constituent rejections as in step 4b (the temporal `invalid-request` arm included).
   e. On constituent success, proceed to step 6, which records the firing and writes `transition_to_event`.

6. On constituent `fire` success (either path): **retrieve the appended history entry's `transition_id`** — `fire` returns only `new_state`, so the composition calls `WorkflowStateMachine.history(instance_id)` and takes the highest-`sequence_number` entry, which under the per-instance serialization (Edge cases — *Concurrent [Fire Transition] calls*) is exactly the entry this fire appended; its `transition_id` is the id used below. Then call `AuditTrail.record_action(action_ref=transition_fired, actor_ref, credential, data={intent_event_id, instance_id, action, from_state=prior current_state, new_state, transition_id, guarded=<bool>, step_id if guarded, fired_at})` → `event_id` — `fired_at` in the payload is the provenance copy of the reading step 4b/5d passed, naming the same instant the history entry carries; the event's own `recorded_at` is the substrate seam's reading and is never claimed equal (*Logic confinement*). Write `transition_to_event[transition_id] = event_id`. Failure is `recording-failure(post-commit)`. If `record_action` fails after `WorkflowStateMachine.fire` succeeded, see *Cross-store consistency under partial failure* in Edge cases; the composition surfaces `recording-failure(post-commit)` but the State Machine transition has committed.

7. **Moot-gate cascade**: the cascade acts on **the `moot_gate_plan` recorded at step 4a/5c**, not on a fresh enumeration of `gate_binding`. This matters twice: the plan and the cascade must be one derivation, or a rebuild landing between them would make the cascade act on a set the record does not name (which check 7 would correctly report as a conformance failure); and the recorded plan is what an auditor holds, so acting on anything else would make the record a description of a different act. Each planned entry is a gate whose key's `from_state` ≠ the transition's `to_state` and whose bound Approval Step read Pending at derivation time — now moot — the workflow has left the state from which that guarded transition departs, so the gate will not be evaluated unless the workflow returns to that state (which is only possible if the declaration includes a return path, per the State Machine's declared-transition model). For each moot gate identified:
   - Call `ApprovalStep.withdraw(step_id, withdrawn_by=initiator_ref, reason="Gate moot: workflow left the gate's from_state by firing a different transition", withdrawn_at=now)` — the same injected reading as the firing that mooted it, since the cascade is part of that invocation → if `not-pending`, the gate was already decided — no action needed.
   - Call `Assignment.recall(gate_to_assignment[step_id])` → treat `not-active` as idempotent success.
   - Call `AuditTrail.record_action(action_ref=moot_gate_recalled, actor_ref=application_actor_ref, credential=application_credential, data={intent_event_id, transition_id, instance_id, from_state=<the moot key's from_state>, moot_action=<the moot key's action — the mooted gate's own transition name, not the one that fired>, fired_action=action, step_id, reason="Gate moot: workflow left the gate's from_state by firing a different transition", withdrawn_at})` — the stamp the payload carries is the cascade's shared seam reading.
   - **Cascade call failures do not fail the action** — the fired transition stands. An `ApprovalStep.withdraw` `storage-failure`, a temporal-bound `invalid-request` (the skew arm — the shared reading below the moot step's later `submitted_at`; retried with a later reading), an `Assignment.recall` `storage-failure`, or a failed `moot_gate_recalled` record is retried by the implementation under *Cross-store consistency under partial failure* — the retry partition (Action wiring preamble) governing: the transient arm loops, the deterministic arms land — and treated as a hard alerting condition until it lands; the caller's `new_state` return is unaffected.
   - The maps are **not** touched: `gate_binding[(instance_id, from_state, moot_action)]` and `gate_to_assignment[step_id]` remain in place — the withdrawn step's terminal state is what marks the gate discharged, the binding history stays traversable for Generation acceptance checks 1 and 4, and a return-path re-entry re-binds through [Open Gate] step 5's terminal-non-Approved rule rather than through a deletion.

8. Return `new_state`.

#### `read_workflow(actor_ref, query) → workflow_view | rejected(permission-denied | invalid-request | invalid-query)`

1. Validate `actor_ref` per Primitive policies → `invalid-request` on failure. `Permissions.permitted(actor_ref, workflows:read)` → if `denied`, return `rejected(permission-denied)` — **always the explicit rejection, never a silently empty result**: an empty list is reserved for a well-formed, permitted query matching nothing, and overloading it with denial would make the two indistinguishable to the caller. A deployment preferring deny-by-empty (to avoid confirming instance existence to unauthorized callers) wires that translation above this composition.
2. Query `workflow_store` on the supported filter axes: `instance_id`, `subject_ref`, `initiator_ref`, `started_at` range (using `{after: <timestamp>, before: <timestamp>}` sub-key form). An unrecognized filter key is `invalid-query`. An empty result for a well-formed query is an empty list, not a rejection.
3. For each workflow instance in the result set, the response carries a composed view: the workflow record fields (`instance_id`, `subject_ref`, `initiator_ref`, `declaration_ref`, `gate_spec`, `started_at`); the State Machine's `current_state` and `transition_history` (via `WorkflowStateMachine.current` and `WorkflowStateMachine.history`); the full transition history enriched with each transition's Audit Trail `event_id` (via `transition_to_event`); and each gate's Approval Step record (via `gate_binding` joined against the Approval Step store) with its Assignment status (via `gate_to_assignment` joined against the Assignment store). The composition does not surface the underlying Audit Trail event payloads on this query; auditors querying the full audit evidence call `AuditTrail.verify_record` directly with the relevant `event_id`s.

### The load-bearing wiring decision — approval-gated transition (guard evaluation via Approval Step)

**Principle.** A regulated multi-actor workflow requires that a guarded transition cannot fire until its named human approval is genuinely recorded (attributed, sealed, and retention-governed) and that the process moved only through declared transitions. These two properties together constitute a records-alone-provable gated process.

**Likely objection.** "State Machine already has a guard-gating mechanism — it accepts `guard_satisfied = true` from the caller. Why not let the caller assert it, or let the composition simply trust the caller's word?"

**Mechanism that resolves it.** State Machine deliberately extracted guard evaluation as an EOS boundary (its Edge cases explicitly name this composition as the re-convergence point): guards recur across many forms — approval records, threshold evaluations, quorum counts, external conditions — and each has its own state machine. The atom correctly does not absorb any of them. For approval-type guards, this composition is the designated evaluation layer. The mechanism: the composition binds each guarded transition to an Approval Step (`gate_binding`) at gate-opening time, and at [Fire Transition] reads the bound step's actual state. `guard_satisfied = true` is asserted to `WorkflowStateMachine.fire` if and only if the bound step is in Approved. The caller of this composition supplies no `guard_satisfied` argument; the composition's own read of the Approval Step state is the evaluation. The gate is structurally closed: an actor with `workflows:fire` and a guarded transition they want to advance cannot assert the guard themselves — the approval record must exist.

**Result.** A records-alone-provable gated workflow: the process moved only through declared transitions (State Machine Invariants 3 and 8, preserved by the composition); each guarded transition cleared its named approval by the authorized actor (Approval Step Invariant 4, enforced at gate-decision time, verified by the composition at fire time); all transition firings and all gate decisions are attributed, sealed, and retention-governed (Audit Trail substrate). An auditor reconstructing the process from the records alone sees: every transition is declared; every guarded transition's history entry corresponds to a bound Approval Step in Approved state; every action is attributed to a real registered actor under Actor Identity attestation. No transition fired on a bare caller assertion.

## Composition-level invariants

These invariants (conditions that must always hold) emerge from the composition. None belongs to a single constituent atom; each requires two or more constituents working together to hold. Each invariant names the constituent invariants it depends on and the action wiring step that establishes it.

- **Invariant 1 — Approval-gated transition (load-bearing), one firing per approval.** A guarded transition's history entry exists in the State Machine instance only if its bound Approval Step (via `gate_binding[(instance_id, from_state, action)]`) was in Approved state — and **unconsumed** — at the moment [Fire Transition] called `WorkflowStateMachine.fire` with `guard_satisfied = true`. Consumption is records-alone at quiescence: each Approved gate authorizes exactly one firing, so every guarded `transition_fired` event's `step_id` appears in exactly one such event — a `step_id` carried by two guarded firings is a conformance failure (one human decision cannot authorize repeated regulated firings; a return path re-binds a fresh gate). Inside [Fire Transition]'s step-6 retry window the committed-but-unrecorded firing is itself the consumption (the pending record the retry obligation tracks — Composition state, *Binding lifecycle*), so the window admits no second firing on the same approval. The composition evaluates the gate (reads the Approval Step state at [Fire Transition] step 5b) and asserts `guard_satisfied = true` only on a confirmed Approved step; a guarded transition never fires on a bare caller assertion. Rests on State Machine Invariants 3 (only-declared-transitions) and 8 (guard-gating without evaluation) plus Approval Step Invariant 4 (approver exclusivity). This invariant is the composition's foundational record-alone-provable claim: a history entry with `guard_satisfied = true` means the composition verified an Approved gate, not merely trusted a caller — and, since the authentication-precedence discipline, the *approver* whose Approved state is being read was themselves verified before that state was written (Invariant 8's binding half), so the claim reaches a principal and not merely a matching actor reference.

- **Invariant 2 — Permission-gated process advancement.** No transition fires by an actor who did not hold `workflows:fire` at the invocation's Permissions check; no workflow is started by an actor who did not hold `workflows:start` at the check; no gate is opened by an actor who did not hold `workflows:open-gate` at the check — the at-check-time reading is the claim (grants may change between the check and any later read). `Permissions.permitted` takes an actor reference and no credential, so this invariant's subject is anchored by Invariant 8: the actor whose grant was checked is validated at the invocation's intent record before anything commits. Gate decisions are enforced by Approval Step Invariants 4 and 5 (named-approver exclusivity and submitter exclusivity) directly — no redundant chain-layer Permissions check for gate decisions. Rests on [Fire Transition] step 1, [Start Workflow] step 1, and [Open Gate] step 1 Permissions checks, plus Approval Step Invariants 4 and 5.

- **Invariant 3 — Attributed and sealed history.** Every fired transition (both guarded and unguarded), every gate decision (opened, decided), every workflow start, and every moot-gate cascade withdrawal (`moot_gate_recalled`) produces exactly one **outcome** `AuditTrail.record_action` call, each returning an `event_id`. Each of the four state-changing actions additionally makes an **intent** `record_action` call *before* its load-bearing constituent call (Invariant 8); intent events are not counted by this invariant's exactly-one claim, since a refused-and-retried invocation legitimately produces several for one eventual act. The full workflow and gate lifecycle is reconstructible forward via Audit Trail — the declared enumerate-and-filter read (*Composes*), ordered by the substrate's sequence — and reverse via the workflow records (via `transition_to_event` and `gate_binding`). Inherits Audit Trail's atomicity surface: `record_action` is treated as atomic from this composition's perspective, modulo Audit Trail's own *Partial attestation on step failure* edge case (Actor Identity.attest succeeds but EventLog.append fails), whose recovery is inherited from the substrate and not re-derived here. Rests on each action's outcome record — [Start Workflow] step 5, [Open Gate] step 9, [Decide Gate] step 7, [Fire Transition] step 6 — and the Audit Trail substrate's composition-level invariants.

- **Invariant 4 — Gate assignment coverage and moot-gate cascade.** Every open gate (a submitted, undecided Approval Step bound via `gate_binding` to a transition reachable from the current state) has exactly one Active Assignment with `task_ref = step_id` and `assignee_ref = approver_ref` from the `gate_spec`. Assignments are recalled in two cases: (a) when the gate is decided ([Decide Gate]'s recall step, step 6), the Assignment is recalled in the same composition-level action; (b) when the workflow fires a transition that leaves the gate's `from_state` by an alternate path (moot-gate cascade in [Fire Transition] step 7), every still-Active Assignment for moot gates is recalled and the bound Approval Steps are withdrawn. After either case, no gate has a lingering Active Assignment **at quiescence** — the recovery-bounded window in which a committed decision's failed `Assignment.recall` is still being retried ([Decide Gate]'s recall step, step 6 — a hard alerting condition until it lands) is the one admitted exception. An `ApprovalStep.withdraw` that returns `not-pending` (the gate was already decided) and an `Assignment.recall` that returns `not-active` (the binding was already discharged) are treated as idempotent no-op successes. **The cascade's coverage rests on how the plan is derived, and that is a capability-provenance dependency rather than a detail of the wiring.** *Every still-Active Assignment for moot gates* quantifies over a set that has to be computed, and computing it by enumerating `gate_binding` would rest the invariant on a **derived index read at a shape its contract does not cover**: rebuild-on-miss repairs a keyed lookup, because a lookup that finds nothing is observable, and an enumeration has no miss to observe. A lost entry would then be indistinguishable from a gate never opened, and this invariant would be false in a way no check could detect. The plan is therefore derived from the **`gate_opened` events** — the append-only binding history — with the map consulted per key under rebuild-on-miss ([Fire Transition] step 4a). *Rests on:* [Open Gate] steps 8–10; [Decide Gate]'s recall step (step 6); [Fire Transition]'s moot-gate cascade (step 7) and its step-4a **anchored plan derivation**; the substrate's declared enumerate-and-filter read surface (*Composes*), which is what makes the `gate_opened` events enumerable at all; and, for the derivation's own totality, the substrate's `audit_trail_retention_policy` — a payload-sourced read is bounded by the horizon that governs the payloads (step 4a).

- **Invariant 5 — Only-declared-transitions and replay determinism preserved.** The composition never bypasses `WorkflowStateMachine.fire`; every transition in the State Machine history is a declared transition; `current_state` replays deterministically from the history. This is the constituent invariant of State Machine (Invariants 3, 7) preserved and surfaced at the composition level because it is load-bearing for the audit proof. The composition adds no alternate write path to the State Machine's transition history. Guarded transitions fire through the same `fire` surface as unguarded transitions; the only difference is whether `guard_satisfied` is asserted (and if so, the composition asserts it, not the caller).

- **Invariant 6 — Records-alone process proof (forensic completability).** [Read Workflow] (combined with `AuditTrail.verify_record` on the `event_id`s from `transition_to_event`) lets an auditor prove from the records alone: (a) the process moved only through declared transitions; (b) each guarded transition's history entry corresponds to a bound Approval Step in Approved state (via `gate_binding`); (c) the approving actor on the Approval Step matched `approver_ref` from the `gate_spec` (Approval Step Invariant 4); (d) the `workflows:fire` grant was held by the actor who fired each transition (the Permissions store's point-in-time grant reconstruction, with the Actor Identity attestation proving who the actor was — Generation acceptance check 5's two-source split); (e) the complete history is attributed and sealed. This is the emergent guarantee no single constituent provides: State Machine provides (a); Approval Step provides (b) and (c); Permissions provides (d); Audit Trail provides attribution and sealing for the combined record. The composition provides all five in one queryable surface.

- **Invariant 7 — Constituent invariants preserved.** All invariants of every constituent atom hold over its respective instance. State Machine's invariants (declaration immutability, exactly one current state, only-declared-transitions, terminal absorption, history append-only and complete, history total order, replay determinism, guard-gating without evaluation, transition attribution completeness, instance store durability) hold per workflow instance. Approval Step's invariants hold per gate step. Permissions' invariants hold over the workflow-store-scoped Permissions instance. Assignment's invariants hold over the composition's Assignment instance. Audit Trail's composition-level invariants hold over its instance.

- **Invariant 8 — Authentication precedes commitment at the four state-changing actions.** At [Start Workflow], [Open Gate], [Decide Gate] and [Fire Transition] — every action that accepts a `credential` and acts on a named actor's authority — no constituent call that commits is reached on any path before that actor's credential has validated against the actor registry's public material for the supplied `actor_ref`. Each opens with an **intent record** whose `AuditTrail.record_action` performs that validation: `workflow_start_intended` before `WorkflowStateMachine.instantiate`, `gate_open_intended` before `ApprovalStep.submit`, `gate_decision_intended` before the Approval Step decision, and `transition_intended` before `WorkflowStateMachine.fire`. `invalid-credential` is a `pre-commit` refusal with nothing committed and the whole action retryable; each outcome event carries `intent_event_id` back for an exact per-invocation join. [Read Workflow] is outside the invariant: it commits nothing.

  **The binding half, which is where this composition's load-bearing claim actually rested.** Invariant 1 says a guarded transition fires only when the *named approver* approved. That approval is made by [Decide Gate], which carries **no Permissions check by design** — Approval Step's Invariants 4 and 5 enforce approver and submitter exclusivity by comparing the supplied `actor_ref` against the step's recorded `approver_ref` / `submitter_ref`. Before the intent record, both sides of that comparison were strings and neither had been verified: an actor reference is an assertion, and the composition committed the decision on it, verifying it only afterwards at the `gate_decided` record. The intent record does not change the comparison; it establishes that the caller **is** the actor being compared, before the decision commits — which is what makes Invariant 1's *named approver approved* a claim about a principal rather than about a value. The same holds for Invariant 2 at the other three actions, whose `Permissions.permitted(actor_ref, …)` likewise consults an actor reference that carries no credential.

  **What a validation does not establish.** It establishes that material matching the actor's registered verifier was presented at that instant. It does *not* establish that the presenter **is** that actor (a stolen credential validates), that the presentation is bound to a channel or session, or that it cannot be replayed — so the disputed-transition scenario's forensic boundary is unchanged in kind and moved only in time: the records now show the approver was verified *before* the gate cleared rather than after. It establishes nothing about whether the named approver was the *correct* authority for the decision, which stays an externally-clearable check. *Rests on:* Audit Trail's `record_action` and the Actor Identity attestation reached through it. *Defended in-line:* the ordering is visible in all four actions' step lists, and Generation acceptance check 6 tests it from the records alone.

## Examples

### Walkthrough — FDA 21 CFR Part 11 / ISO 9001 pharmaceutical batch release

A pharmaceutical manufacturer's batch-release system uses this composition to govern the lifecycle of batch `BR-2026-0412` under FDA 21 CFR Part 211 (Current Good Manufacturing Practice for Finished Pharmaceuticals) and FDA 21 CFR Part 11 (Electronic Records; Electronic Signatures). The deployment configures `audit_trail_retention_policy = fda_part_11_predicate_rule`.

**Declaration.** The deployment declares the following process:

```
states: {sampled, testing, qp-review, released, rejected}
transitions: [
  {from: sampled,    action: begin-testing,  to: testing},
  {from: testing,    action: complete-tests, to: qp-review},
  {from: qp-review,  action: release,        to: released, guard: "QP-sign-off"},
  {from: qp-review,  action: reject-batch,   to: rejected, guard: "QP-rejection"},
  {from: testing,    action: fail-tests,     to: rejected}
]
initial_state: sampled
terminal_states: {released, rejected}
```

The `gate_spec` maps `"QP-sign-off"` to `{approver_ref: "qp_director_santos", scope: "pharma:batch-release:qp-sign-off"}` and `"QP-rejection"` to `{approver_ref: "qp_director_santos", scope: "pharma:batch-release:qp-rejection"}`.

1. **QA (quality assurance) manager starts the workflow.** `start_workflow(actor_ref=qa_manager, declaration, subject_ref="br-2026-0412", gate_spec, credential=qa_credential)` → Permissions returns permitted (qa_manager holds `workflows:start`); `gate_spec` validates (two guarded transitions, two matching entries, both within the guarded-transition cap) — **validation precedes the intent record deliberately**, because the intent event's payload carries the whole `gate_spec` and it is this validation that keeps that payload inside the budget; then — **before any instance is minted** — Audit Trail records `workflow_start_intended`, which is where `qa_credential` is checked against the actor registry (had it not validated, the call would have returned `rejected(invalid-credential)` with no State Machine instance created); `WorkflowStateMachine.instantiate(declaration)` → `instance_id="wf-batch-br-2026-0412"`; Audit Trail records `workflow_started`; the `workflow_store` record is written over the landed event (record before map). Returns `{instance_id="wf-batch-br-2026-0412"}`.

2. **Lab technician begins testing.** `fire_transition(actor_ref=lab_tech_rivera, instance_id, action="begin-testing", credential)` → Permissions permitted; `begin-testing` is an unguarded transition; the moot-gate plan is empty (no gate is bound yet) and Audit Trail records `transition_intended` carrying it, verifying the technician's credential; `WorkflowStateMachine.fire(instance_id, "begin-testing", actor_ref=lab_tech_rivera)` → `new_state="testing"`; Audit Trail records `transition_fired`; `transition_to_event` updated. Returns `"testing"`.

3. **Tests complete; QA manager advances to QP (qualified person — the named releaser role under EU/FDA pharmaceutical manufacturing rules) review.** `fire_transition(actor_ref=qa_manager, instance_id, action="complete-tests", credential)` → unguarded; `transition_intended` records first (empty plan), verifying the credential; fires; Audit Trail records `transition_fired` carrying `intent_event_id`. `current_state = "qp-review"`.

4. **QA manager opens the release gate.** `open_gate(actor_ref=qa_manager, instance_id, action="release", credential)` → Permissions permitted (qa_manager holds `workflows:open-gate`); matched transition has guard `"QP-sign-off"`; not already open; Audit Trail records `gate_open_intended` — verifying the manager's credential before any Approval Step exists, and carrying no `step_id`, since none has been minted; `ApprovalStep.submit(subject_ref="br-2026-0412:release", approver_ref="qp_director_santos", submitter_ref="qa_manager", scope="pharma:batch-release:qp-sign-off")` → `step_id="step-qp-0412-release"`; `Assignment.assign(task_ref=step_id, assignee_ref="qp_director_santos")` → `assignment_id="asgn-qp-0412"`; Audit Trail records `gate_opened`; maps written over the landed event (record before maps). Returns `{step_id, assignment_id}`.

5. **QP Director reviews and approves.** `decide_gate(actor_ref=qp_director_santos, instance_id, action="release", decision="approve", reason="Batch specification limits met; COA (Certificate of Analysis) reviewed; QP sign-off granted under 21 CFR 211.68", credential=qp_credential)` → no Permissions check (Approval Step Invariant 4 is the enforcement); `gate_binding[(instance_id, "qp-review", "release")]` → `step_id`; then Audit Trail records `gate_decision_intended` → `ev_dec_int`, **which is where `qp_credential` is validated against the actor registry — so the sign-off that follows is made by a verified principal, not by whoever supplied the string `"qp_director_santos"`**; `ApprovalStep.approve(step_id, decided_by="qp_director_santos", reason=...)` → `approved`; `Assignment.recall(asgn-qp-0412)`; Audit Trail records `gate_decided`. Returns `approved`.

6. **QA manager fires the release transition.** `fire_transition(actor_ref=qa_manager, instance_id, action="release", credential)` → Permissions permitted; matched transition is guarded; `gate_binding[(instance_id, "qp-review", "release")]` → `step_id="step-qp-0412-release"`; `ApprovalStep.read({step_id})` → step state is Approved and unconsumed — steps 5a and 5b are read-only and settle the gate before anything is recorded; then Audit Trail records `transition_intended` carrying `{guarded: true, step_id: "step-qp-0412-release", to_state: "released", moot_gate_plan: []}` → `ev_fire_int` — the plan is **empty** because the only gate ever opened on this instance is the one this firing consumes, and no other gate is bound and Pending; an empty plan is a positive claim, not a missing field, and check 7 reads it as *this firing was to moot nothing*, verifying `qa_manager`'s credential before the batch is released; composition asserts `guard_satisfied = true`; `WorkflowStateMachine.fire(instance_id, "release", actor_ref="qa_manager", guard_satisfied=true)` → `new_state="released"`; Audit Trail records `transition_fired` with `guarded=true, step_id`; `transition_to_event` updated. Check moot-gate cascade: `current_state` is now `"released"` (terminal); the `"QP-rejection"` gate was never opened, so no cascade is needed. Returns `"released"`.

7. **Attempt to fire after terminal state.** `fire_transition(actor_ref=qa_manager, instance_id, action="release", credential)` → Permissions permitted; `WorkflowStateMachine.current(instance_id)` → `"released"` ∈ `terminal_states`; returns `terminal`.

8. **Two years later — FDA Part 11 inspection.** Inspector queries `read_workflow({instance_id: "wf-batch-br-2026-0412"})`. The composed view returns: workflow record; State Machine history (3 entries — `begin-testing`, `complete-tests`, `release` — tracing sampled → testing → qp-review → released); each transition's Audit Trail `event_id`; the `"QP-sign-off"` gate's Approval Step (Approved, `decided_by=qp_director_santos`, `decided_at`, `decision_reason`). The inspector calls `AuditTrail.verify_record(event_id, payload)` for the `transition_fired` event on the release transition and receives `verified`. The inspector confirms: (a) the batch moved only through declared states (Invariant 5); (b) the release transition fired with `guard_satisfied=true` and the bound step is in Approved state (Invariant 1); (c) the QP's identity is attested under Part 11 §11.50 (Audit Trail Actor Identity). Control evidence is complete from the records alone.

### Rejected gate and recovery — re-binding after a Rejected step

A journal-entry workflow's `post` transition is guarded; its gate is open and bound to `step-je-091`. The controller rejects: `decide_gate(..., decision="reject", reason="Supporting docs incomplete — resubmit with invoice attached")` → `gate_decision_intended` records first, which is where the controller's credential is validated — this action has no Permissions check, so that record is the only thing standing between an asserted `actor_ref` and a committed rejection — then `ApprovalStep.reject` → `rejected_outcome`; the Assignment is recalled; `gate_decided` is recorded carrying `intent_event_id`. A subsequent `fire_transition(..., action="post")` reads the bound step, finds `Rejected`, and returns `rejected(gate-not-cleared)` — the rejection stands as the gate's outcome, but it does not brick the transition: after the preparer attaches the invoice, `open_gate(..., action="post")` finds the bound step in `Rejected` (a terminal non-Approved state — [Open Gate] step 5's re-binding rule), records `gate_open_intended`, binds a fresh Approval Step `step-je-114`, repoints `gate_binding`, and assigns it to the controller's in-tray. The controller approves the fresh gate (its own intent record first); `fire_transition` now reads `Approved` on the current binding and fires. Both gates' histories persist — two `gate_opened` events for the pair, the Rejected step immutable in the Approval Step store with its stated reason — so the auditor sees the rejection, the rework, and the eventual approval as three records, not a mutated one.

### Failure path — transition fired, audit write fails

A `fire_transition` commits its `WorkflowStateMachine.fire` (the load-bearing write) and the step-6 `record_action` fails: the caller receives `rejected(recording-failure(post-commit))` — the stage discriminator says the transition **did** fire, so the caller must not re-invoke (a retry would fire again; the transition is non-idempotent) and instead confirms the new state via [Read Workflow]. The implementation retries the audit write until it lands, the landed entry — emitted under the composition actor when the retry completes outside the original invocation, the firing actor in its `data` and `recovery = true` (an ordinary payload field — the substrate offers no marker surface, and none is needed) — letting the auditor distinguish the recovered attestation from a clean one; until it lands, the missing entry is a gap in Invariant 3 surfaced as a hard alerting condition (Edge cases — *Cross-store consistency under partial failure*).

### Moot-gate cascade — alternate transition leaves a gate unreachable

A purchase-order workflow has `current_state = "awaiting-approval"` with a guarded `approve` transition (guard `"finance-sign-off"`) and an unguarded `cancel` transition, both departing from `awaiting-approval`. The finance director's approval gate has been opened (`gate_binding[(instance_id, "awaiting-approval", "approve")]` set, Approval Step in Pending). A process manager calls `fire_transition(action="cancel")`. The `cancel` transition is unguarded, so step 4a derives the moot-gate plan read-only — the declared `to_state` is `"cancelled"`, and the one bound gate on this instance keys from `"awaiting-approval"` with its step still Pending, so the plan is `[{from_state: "awaiting-approval", action: "approve", step_id}]` — and records `transition_intended` carrying it, verifying the process manager's credential before the cancellation commits. The fire then succeeds; `new_state = "cancelled"`. The moot-gate cascade (step 7): the `"approve"` gate's `from_state = "awaiting-approval"` ≠ `"cancelled"` = `new_state`; the gate is moot. The composition calls `ApprovalStep.withdraw(step_id, withdrawn_by=initiator_ref, reason="Gate moot: ...")`, `Assignment.recall(assignment_id)`, and `AuditTrail.record_action(action_ref=moot_gate_recalled, actor_ref=application_actor_ref, ...)`. The finance director's in-tray no longer shows the approval task. The audit trail records the withdrawal and the reason.

### Regulated adversarial scenarios

Three adversarial reads this composition must survive in regulated contexts:

#### Regulator audit — SOX §404 / FDA 21 CFR Part 11: prove declared-path and gate-cleared

An FDA inspector or SOX auditor demands evidence that process instance `wf-batch-br-2026-0412` moved only through its declared states and that the qualified-person gate was genuinely cleared before the release transition fired. The auditor queries `read_workflow({instance_id: "wf-batch-br-2026-0412"})` and receives the composed view. The auditor confirms: (a) every transition in the State Machine history corresponds to a declared transition (`read_declaration` returns the immutable declaration; Invariant 5 — constituent State Machine Invariant 3 — guarantees no undeclared transition could have produced a history entry); (b) the `"release"` history entry carries `guard_satisfied: true`, and its `transition_fired` event — reached via `transition_to_event` — carries the `step_id` pointing to the bound Approval Step (the history entry itself carries no `step_id`; the binding lives in the composition's records); (c) `ApprovalStep.read({step_id})` returns `state: Approved`, `decided_by: "qp_director_santos"`, `decided_at`, and `decision_reason`; (d) Approval Step Invariant 4 guarantees `decided_by` matched `approver_ref` at the time of the approve call; (e) `AuditTrail.verify_record(event_id, payload)` for the `transition_fired` event returns `verified`. The auditor's structural questions — *did the batch move only through declared states?* and *was the release gate cleared by the named QP?* — are answered from the records alone under Invariants 1, 5, and 6. No recourse to source code, runbooks, or developer narration is needed.

#### Disputed transition — party claims a guarded transition fired without its approval

An external party (an auditor, a counterparty, an investigator) claims that the `"release"` transition fired in instance `wf-batch-br-2026-0412` without QP approval, or that an unauthorized actor advanced the process. The structural rebuttal:

For the "no approval" claim: the State Machine history entry for the release transition carries `guard_satisfied: true` (Invariant 8 of the constituent — the atom records whether the caller asserted the guard). The composition's `gate_binding[(instance_id, "qp-review", "release")]` maps to `step_id="step-qp-0412-release"`. `ApprovalStep.read({step_id})` returns `state: Approved`. Invariant 1 of this composition states that `guard_satisfied = true` was asserted by the composition only because the bound step was in Approved at the time of [Fire Transition] step 5b. A forgery would require either (a) fabricating an Approved Approval Step record for a step whose `approver_ref` is `qp_director_santos` — foreclosed by Approval Step Invariant 4 (only the named approver may decide) and by the Audit Trail's tamper-evident sealing — or (b) fabricating a State Machine history entry with `guard_satisfied: true` — foreclosed by the State Machine's Invariant 5 (history append-only — no `fire` writes an entry unless it succeeded; every one of its rejection arms writes nothing) and by Tamper Evidence sealing. Invariants 1, 3, and 5 together constitute the structural rebuttal.

For the "unauthorized actor advanced the process" claim: `AuditTrail.verify_record` for the `transition_fired` event returns the Actor Identity attestation, binding the actor reference to the action at the attested timestamp. Invariant 2 (Permission-gated process advancement) guarantees the firing actor held `workflows:fire` at the time of the Permissions check. An auditor confirms both.

#### Breach or incident investigation — reconstruct transitions and gate decisions during anomaly window

During a security incident (suspected credential compromise from 2026-05-01T00:00:00Z to 2026-05-03T23:59:59Z), the incident response team must determine which workflow transitions fired and which gates were cleared during the window. The team queries `read_workflow({started_at: {after: "2026-04-25T00:00:00Z"}})` for all recently started workflows. For each instance, the team inspects the State Machine history and — via `transition_to_event` — the corresponding Audit Trail `event_id`s. For each `transition_fired` event in the anomaly window, the team calls `AuditTrail.verify_record(event_id, payload)` to confirm integrity. Any `failed-verification(seal-proof-invalid)` or `failed-verification(seal-record-set-mismatch)` response — the substrate's actual tamper-indicating reason tokens — indicates a post-hoc modification, a forensic finding. For guarded transitions in the window, the team cross-references the bound Approval Step via `gate_binding`: `ApprovalStep.read({step_id})` returns `decided_by` and `decided_at`, and the Audit Trail `gate_decided` event confirms the gate decision under the Actor Identity attestation. The team also queries the Permissions store for actors who held `workflows:fire` during the window; any transition fired by an actor whose grant was not active at the firing timestamp is a finding. Invariants 3 and 5 bound the forensic window precisely: the transition history is the complete ordered record of every step that advanced the process; the Audit Trail provides the integrity attestation for each.

## Generation acceptance

A derived implementation of this composition is *acceptable* — in the regulator-acceptance sense — when an external auditor, given the composition's emergent state plus the constituent stores, can do all of the following without recourse to source code, runbooks, or developer narration. Every trail-walking step below reads the substrate through the declared enumerate-and-filter capability (*Composes*) — the pass-through sequence-range read, filtered in the auditor's own code — assuming no payload-index surface:

### Audit-Trail-traversal-clearable checks

1. **Verify the approval-gated-transition claim.** For every State Machine history entry whose matched declared transition carries a `guard` label (i.e., history entries with `guard_satisfied: true`): follow `transition_to_event[transition_id]` to the Audit Trail `event_id` and confirm it carries `step_id` in its data; follow `gate_binding[(instance_id, from_state, action)]` — the entry's `from_state` matching the history entry's — to find the bound `step_id` (the binding history in the `gate_opened` events resolves the binding that was current at the firing, where a later re-bind has repointed the map); confirm the singleton `ApprovalStep.read({step_id})` query's returned step carries `state: Approved`; confirm its `decided_by` matches the `gate_spec`'s `approver_ref` for that guard label (Approval Step Invariant 4); and confirm **one-firing-per-approval** — the `step_id` appears in exactly one guarded `transition_fired` event across the trail (an at-quiescence check: a firing whose record is still landing is tracked by the pending record, not the trail — Composition state, *Binding lifecycle*). A guarded history entry whose bound step is not in Approved, whose `decided_by` does not match `approver_ref`, or whose `step_id` recurs across two guarded firings is a conformance failure under Invariant 1.

2. **Verify only-declared-transitions and replay determinism.** For every workflow instance: call `WorkflowStateMachine.read_declaration(instance_id)` and `WorkflowStateMachine.history(instance_id)`. Confirm every `{from_state, action, to_state}` triple in the history corresponds to a declared transition. Replay the history in `sequence_number` order from `initial_state` and confirm it arrives at `current_state`. A history entry with a triple not in the declaration, or a replay that does not converge to `current_state`, is a conformance failure under Invariant 5 (constituent State Machine Invariants 3 and 7). Confirm the declaration has not changed since [Start Workflow]: State Machine Invariant 1 (declaration immutability) is the structural guarantee, and the runnable form is cross-snapshot — `read_declaration(instance_id)` taken at two audit times must return byte-identical declarations (the `workflow_started` payload carries `declaration_ref`, not the declaration itself, so the event alone cannot re-derive the declared content).

3. **Verify audit completeness.** For every workflow start, gate opening, gate decision, and transition firing recorded in the workflow store and Approval Step store: exactly one corresponding **outcome** `record_action` event exists in the Audit Trail (`action_ref ∈ {workflow_started, gate_opened, gate_decided, transition_fired, moot_gate_recalled}`). The reverse direction holds **for the outcome kinds only**: every event of one of those kinds corresponds to a record in the workflow store, `gate_binding`, or Approval Step store. **The four intent kinds are deliberately excluded from both directions of this check** — the exactly-one direction because an action refused after its intent record and retried leaves several intent events for one eventual act, which is the discipline working rather than failing; the reverse direction because an invocation refused *at* its load-bearing call leaves an intent event that corresponds to no record anywhere, by design. Intent events are checked by check 6, which is where their residue is triaged. No workflow action is invisible to the Audit Trail; no Audit Trail entry **of an outcome kind** refers to an instance or gate that does not exist. Invariant 3 is the contract. Inherits Audit Trail's atomicity surface (modulo the substrate's *Partial attestation on step failure* edge case; the auditor alerts on orphan attestations referencing this composition's `action_ref` values).

4. **Verify gate assignment coverage.** **Enumerate the gates from the `gate_opened` events for each instance — not from `gate_binding` — taking the latest event per `(instance_id, from_state, action)` key and resolving each key through the map with rebuild-on-miss.** An auditor who enumerated the map instead would be quantifying over a derived index whose losses are invisible to that read shape, so a lost entry would present as a gate that was never opened and the check would pass over exactly the gate whose Assignment it exists to verify. The events are the authoritative binding history (Composition state); the map is the cache. Then, for every gate whose bound Approval Step is in Pending state: confirm exactly one Active Assignment exists with `task_ref = step_id` and `assignee_ref = approver_ref`. For every `gate_binding` entry whose bound step is in a terminal state: confirm no Active Assignment exists for that step — at quiescence; the recovery-bounded window of a committed decision's still-retrying recall ([Decide Gate]'s recall step, step 6 — Invariant 4's admitted exception) is a hard alerting condition, not a conformance failure. Confirm moot gates (where the workflow's current state is not the gate's `from_state`) have been withdrawn and their Assignments recalled (or the gate was already decided before the workflow left the state). Invariant 4 is the contract.

5. **Verify permission enforcement.** For every `workflow_started`, `gate_opened`, and `transition_fired` Audit Trail entry, two separate verifications with two separate sources: *who acted* is proven by the Audit Trail's Actor Identity attestation (the attestation binds the actor to the act — it says nothing about authorization; for a `recovery = true` entry the attestation binds the **composition actor** to the record's emission, and who acted is read from the named human actor in the event `data`, corroborated by the constituent store's own attribution — the State Machine history entry's actor, the Approval Step's `decided_by`); *whether that actor held the corresponding scope* (`workflows:start`, `workflows:open-gate`, `workflows:fire`) *at the time of the action* is reconstructed from the **Permissions store's own grant records** — its point-in-time reconstruction (a grant with `granted_at ≤ t` and not revoked before `t`, Permissions' own Generation-acceptance discipline) evaluated at the event's timestamp. The reconstruction inherits the timestamp caveat: absent a composed Trusted Timestamping pattern, event timestamps are advisory — and Permissions mutations are not events in this composition's trail (grant and revoke land in the Permissions store, not the Audit Trail), so no shared event order spans the two sources and the at-time-of-action claim is best-effort in wall time; a deployment needing an exact interleaving records its Permissions mutations to the same Audit Trail (a composing enrichment) and inherits the substrate's sequence. Invariant 2 is the contract.

6. **Verify authentication precedence.** For every `workflow_started`, `gate_opened`, `gate_decided` and `transition_fired` event, take its `data.intent_event_id`, resolve it to the matching intent event (`workflow_start_intended` / `gate_open_intended` / `gate_decision_intended` / `transition_intended`), and confirm that event precedes it in the Audit Trail's own sequence and names the same `actor_ref`. Because the substrate validates the caller's credential against the actor registry inside every `record_action`, the intent event *is* the records-alone proof that the actor was authenticated **before** the instance was minted, the gate submitted, the decision committed, or the transition fired — which is what makes Invariant 8 verifiable rather than asserted, and what anchors check 5's *who acted* to a moment before the act rather than after it. **Read the order from the Audit Trail's sequence, never from the stamps:** an invocation's `intended_at` and its outcome payload's stamp are one injected reading and are equal by construction (*Logic confinement*). **Join by `intent_event_id`**, never by `{actor_ref, instance_id, action}` — a return path legitimately re-fires the same action on the same instance, so a stale intent event would satisfy this check for a later firing it never authorized.

  **The two exceptions are both `recovery = true` emissions and both are expected.** A record re-emitted outside its original invocation carries the composition actor as `actor_ref` and the human in its `data` (Action wiring preamble); for such an event, compare the intent event's `actor_ref` against the **named human in the outcome event's `data`**, not against its `actor_ref`, which is the composition's. `moot_gate_recalled` events are outside **this** check's scope — it quantifies over the four outcome kinds — and are covered by check 7 instead. They are emitted under the composition's service identity rather than a caller's, so there is no caller credential for this check to have preceded; what they do carry is the firing's `intent_event_id` and `transition_id`, which is how check 7 joins each recall to the plan that authorized it. Without those keys the join would fall back to `{instance_id, fired_action, from_state}`, which a return path makes ambiguous for exactly the reason this check forbids that shape of join.

  **An intent event with no outcome event is not a failure.** It names an invocation that committed nothing, and every arm that produces one is a `pre-commit` refusal the caller may retry — so the expected population is *large* and dominated by ordinary refusals rather than by orphans. Triage by action, and note that the four actions are not equally triageable.

  **[Decide Gate] and guarded [Fire Transition]** intents carry a `step_id`: read that Approval Step's state and the State Machine history. A committed effect with no outcome event is the `post-commit` orphan the *Cross-store consistency* edge case already owns, recovered by its own retry-until-lands obligation; no committed effect means the constituent refused and nothing is owed. **An unguarded [Fire Transition]** intent carries no `step_id`, so it joins only on `{instance_id, action, from_state}` — sufficient unless the declaration has a return path that re-fires the same action from the same state, where the auditor must fall back to counting firings against outcome events for that key rather than matching them one to one.

  **[Open Gate]** intents carry no minted identifier, but the residue is still derivable: an Approval Step whose `step_id` appears in no `gate_opened` event is the committed-but-unrecorded gate, reachable through Approval Step's own read surface. **[Start Workflow]** intents are the one arm this check cannot close. An orphaned State Machine instance is unreachable through that constituent's declared surface — `current`, `history` and `read_declaration` are all keyed by an `instance_id` that was never issued to anyone, and the atom declares no enumeration — so a [Start Workflow] intent with no `workflow_started` event tells an auditor *that* an instantiation may have been attempted and never *which* instance resulted. **That arm is an externally-clearable question** (check 11), not a records-alone one; a deployment that needs it closed must require an enumerate-all-instances read on its wired State Machine instance, the same instance-capability form the Audit Trail substrate declares for its own open-upper-bound read.

  **Never write a compensating outcome event for an intent whose effect you cannot find** — a compensating record for an act that did not happen is worse than the missing record it replaces.

7. **Verify the moot-gate cascade against its declared plan.** For every `transition_intended` event that **resolves to a `transition_fired` outcome** (by `intent_event_id` — an intent event with no firing is check 6's residue and is not this check's input), take its `moot_gate_plan` and gather the `moot_gate_recalled` events carrying the same `intent_event_id`. Compare the two sets. Under the declared per-`instance_id` serialization the plan is **exact**, not an upper bound, so both directions are findings. **Exactness rests on the plan's derivation being anchored in the `gate_opened` events rather than in an enumeration of `gate_binding`** ([Fire Transition] step 4a): a plan derived by enumerating a derived index could silently under-report, and this check — which keys on the plan — would have no way to tell an under-reported plan from a correct one, since the gate it omitted appears in neither set being compared. The anchoring is what makes *exact* a claim rather than an assumption, and it is why the two directions below can both be findings. A `moot_gate_recalled` event naming a gate the plan did not name is a **conformance failure**: the cascade withdrew a human approval this firing had no basis to moot. A planned gate with no recall event is an **alerting condition, not an ordinary outcome**: the withdrawal is still owed — the cascade's retry window is open, or the cascade never ran — and it clears only by confirming that gate's bound Approval Step reached a terminal state. Neither direction is checkable without the plan on the record, which is the reason it is recorded before the fire rather than derived after it.

8. **The audit horizon outlasts the workflows it records.** Compare the horizon of `audit_trail_retention_policy` against the longest-lived workflow instance the deployment admits — for a long-running regulated lifecycle (a clinical trial master file, a multi-year change-control record) that is the instance's own expected duration, not an average. **The horizon must be the longer**, and a violation is a conformance failure rather than an alerting condition, because what it costs cannot be recovered from any other store. Three consequences follow, and an auditor should test for each. `workflow_store`'s rebuild loses the **[Gate Spec]**, which lives in no constituent store — State Machine holds the instance, its history and its current state, but the gate specification is a composition-introduced surface, which is why the composition writes it into the event and why the horizon takes it; check 1's records-alone dependence on [Gate Spec] is bounded by exactly this. `gate_to_assignment`'s rebuild loses the historical entries and keeps only those still Active through Assignment's `active_for(task_ref)` — the opposite of the direction an audit wants. And the moot-gate plan's **anchored derivation** ([Fire Transition] step 4a) degrades back to whatever `gate_binding` still holds, silently reacquiring the under-reporting that anchoring was introduced to remove, and with it the ability of **check 7** to treat the plan as exact. Where the deployment's instance lifetimes are a business fact this composition cannot read, the comparison is **externally-clearable** and is listed there; the classifications' split by retention state remains checkable here.

### Externally-clearable checks

8. **Whether an orphaned State Machine instance exists behind a [Start Workflow] intent event.** The creation-action rule quarantines an instance whose `workflow_started` record never landed; check 6 can see the intent event but cannot reach the instance, because State Machine's declared read surface is keyed by an `instance_id` this composition never issued and the atom declares no enumeration. Resolving it requires either the deployment's own State Machine store-maintenance surface or an enumerate-all-instances instance capability declared on the wired instance. The composition's records prove the attempt; they cannot enumerate its residue.

9. **Whether the deployment's longest workflow instance outlives the audit horizon.** Generation acceptance check 8 states the obligation and can be answered from the records wherever both durations are readable. Instance lifetimes, though, are a business fact — how long a trial stays open, how long a change-control record remains live — and this composition holds no expectation of them. A violation is not a reporting defect: it removes the [Gate Spec] from the records-alone rebuild, where no constituent store holds it, and returns the moot-gate plan's anchored derivation to the silent under-reporting the enumeration-anchoring round removed. The composition's defence is to state the bound and split the classifications by retention state; the ordering itself is the deployment's.

10. **Whether the `gate_spec` correctly maps to the regulatory requirements.** The composition records the `gate_spec` declared at [Start Workflow] and validates it structurally (every guarded transition named; every entry non-whitespace). It does not verify that the declared `approver_ref` for each guard label was the *correct authority* under the deployment's regulatory policy. *"Was `qp_director_santos` the required qualified person for batch `BR-2026-0412` under 21 CFR Part 211?"* is a calling-system policy question; the composition records the answer the calling system declared. Verification of the policy mapping (transition guard → required approver identity) requires the deployment's role-authorization registry — typically a Permissions instance scoped to scope-approval grants, cross-referenced at the `started_at` timestamp. This parallels Multi-Party Approval's *Audit gaps* for approver-set policy.

10. **Whether the declared state machine itself matches the regulation's required process.** The composition records the declaration as supplied and enforces declared-transition discipline. It does not verify that the declared states and transitions are the correct process under the applicable regulation. *"Does this declaration correctly represent the five-step batch-release process required by Part 211?"* is a regulatory-process-design question answered by the deployment's process documentation. Auditors verify this by reading the declaration alongside the relevant regulatory standard.

11. **Whether the named approver was authorized to hold the approver role.** The composition enforces that `decided_by` matched `approver_ref` (Approval Step Invariant 4) and that `approver_ref` was named in the `gate_spec` (checked at [Start Workflow]). It does not verify that the named approver was the *correct authority* for the scope at the time of the gate decision. Cross-referencing `approver_ref` and `scope` against the deployment's standing-authorization registry at `started_at` is an external audit check.

## Edge cases and explicit non-goals

- **Multi-approver gates.** A guarded transition requiring N approvers (all-of-N, M-of-N, one-of-N quorum) uses a [Multi-Party Approval](./multi-party-approval.md) chain in place of a single Approval Step as the gate. The composition reads the chain's terminal Approved state the same way it reads a single step's Approved state — via the `gate_binding` pointing to the chain id rather than a `step_id`. The `gate_spec` entry for such a guard label names the chain's `chain_id` (or a stable chain reference) rather than a single `approver_ref` — a different entry shape, so the enrichment adjusts Primitive policies' `gate_spec` validation (which, in the core model stated above, requires a non-whitespace `approver_ref` and `scope` per entry) along with the wiring; the [Open Gate] action initiates a Multi-Party Approval chain rather than a single Approval Step. This multi-approver-gate variant is a composing enrichment of this composition, not part of the core single-approver-gate model. Deployments requiring multi-approver gates on any guarded transition should layer [Multi-Party Approval](./multi-party-approval.md) for those gates.

- **Non-approval guard evaluation.** This composition evaluates only approval-type guards — guards whose evaluation consists of checking whether a named actor has approved a specific subject under a specific scope. Guards whose evaluation requires a threshold check (an account balance exceeds a limit), a quorum count (N of M sensors reported above threshold), an external condition (an external service returned a green status), or a rules-engine decision are still the caller's responsibility per State Machine's guard-evaluation edge case. The composition does not provide a generic guard-evaluation surface; it evaluates exactly one class: approval-step-based guards. A deployment that declares a guarded transition with a non-approval guard must supply `guard_satisfied = true` to `WorkflowStateMachine.fire` via a separate orchestration layer that is not this composition. Mixing approval and non-approval guards in the same declaration is a deployment-design choice; the composition owns only the approval-type ones.

- **Parallel / concurrent active states and fork-join.** This composition has exactly one current state at all times, inherited from State Machine's Invariant 2 (exactly one current state). Parallel workflows — where an instance is in multiple active states simultaneously, with fork and join transitions — are out of scope. A Parallel Workflow fork-join composition (not yet in the library) handles that concept; this composition is the single-active-state declared-machine primitive.

- **Declaration versioning and sharing declarations across instances.** The composition takes the declaration as a value at [Start Workflow] and fixes it immutably per instance via State Machine's Invariant 1 (declaration immutability). Sharing one canonical declaration template across many instances — updating the template for new instances without re-supplying it at each `instantiate` — is handled by a Definition Registry (named in State Machine's edge cases). The calling system retrieves the current template from a registry and supplies it at [Start Workflow]; this composition receives a value declaration.

- **Moot-gate cascade and return paths.** The moot-gate cascade (step 7 of [Fire Transition]) withdraws open gates for transitions whose `from_state` the workflow has left. If the declaration includes a return path to that `from_state` (e.g., a transition from `released` back to `qp-review` for a re-review), a gate that was moot when the workflow left may become relevant again. In this case, [Open Gate] must be called again for the re-relevant transition; the prior gate has been withdrawn and cannot be re-used (Approval Step's Invariant 3 — terminal absorption). The re-open is admitted by [Open Gate] step 5's binding rule: the key's bound step is in `Withdrawn`, a terminal non-Approved state, so a fresh step is bound and `gate_binding` repoints while the prior binding stays in the `gate_opened` event history — no deletion is involved. (The same rule's Approved-and-consumed arm covers the other return-path case: a gate that was fired before the workflow looped back — its approval is consumed, and the re-entry binds a fresh gate rather than reusing the spent one.) The deployment must design its process declaration and gate-opening logic accordingly.

- **Cross-store consistency under partial failure.** [Start Workflow] writes to three stores in sequence (State Machine constituent, then Audit Trail, then the derived-index `workflow_store` — record before map). [Open Gate] writes to four (Approval Step, Assignment, Audit Trail, then the derived-index maps — record before maps). [Decide Gate] writes to three (Approval Step, the Assignment recall, Audit Trail). [Fire Transition] writes to two or more (State Machine constituent, Audit Trail, plus the moot cascade's Approval Step withdrawals, Assignment recalls, and `moot_gate_recalled` events — the composition maps are not mutated by the cascade). A failure mid-sequence leaves partial state. The recovery rules are stated per action under each action's *Partial-failure recovery* note, and the caller's recourse splits by action class per the preamble's stated rule — mutation actions never re-invoke; [Start Workflow] re-invokes over a quarantined orphan; [Open Gate] is completed by the implementation with the gate structurally quarantined until its record lands. The general principle: the load-bearing write (the constituent atom transition for [Fire Transition]; the Approval Step submit for [Open Gate]) is the write that determines whether the action semantically committed, and the `recording-failure(stage)` discriminator (Action wiring preamble) tells the caller which side of it the failure fell on. Subsequent Audit Trail writes are recovery-amenable; map writes follow the record and are rebuild triggers, not failure arms. An Audit Trail `record_action` failure after a load-bearing write surfaces as `recording-failure(post-commit)` to the caller — who must **not** re-invoke the action (a [Fire Transition] re-invocation would re-fire; the caller confirms the committed state through [Read Workflow]) — and it is the **implementation** that retries the failed `record_action` until it lands, with the landed entry emitted under the composition actor when the retry completes outside the original invocation — the human actor in its `data` and `recovery = true`, an ordinary payload field (the substrate offers no marker surface) — so an auditor distinguishes a clean record from a recovered one, the retry looping only on the transient arm per the retry partition (Action wiring preamble); the Audit Trail entry will be missing until that retry succeeds, a gap in Invariant 3 the implementation must resolve and treat as a hard alerting condition.

- **[Fire Transition] non-idempotency.** `WorkflowStateMachine.fire` is not idempotent (two `fire` calls with the same `(instance_id, action)` both succeed if the transition is still available from the resulting state). Callers requiring at-most-once semantics under retry conditions must supply their own idempotency key at the orchestration layer; this composition does not provide one. This is exactly why the `recording-failure(stage)` discriminator exists (Action wiring preamble): `post-commit` tells the caller the fire committed and a blind retry would fire *again* — the caller reads the committed state through [Read Workflow] instead, and the implementation retries only the failed audit write.

- **Audit Trail records of failed authorization attempts.** A [Fire Transition], [Start Workflow], or [Open Gate] call rejected at the Permissions check (`permission-denied`) leaves no Audit Trail entry by default. High-assurance deployments where failed authorization attempts are themselves auditable compose a Failed-Attempt Log pattern; the canonical composition's audit surface is committed workflow actions, not attempted actions. This parallels Multi-Party Approval's equivalent edge case.

- **Guard evaluation for non-approval guards is still the caller's / Rules Engine's obligation.** Named explicitly in *Non-approval guard evaluation* above and in State Machine's edge cases. The composition evaluates only approval-type guards; all other guard evaluation paths remain the calling system's or a composing Rules Engine's responsibility.

- **Clock source for `fired_at` and `decided_at`.** These stamps — together with `instantiated_at`, `started_at`, and `withdrawn_at` — are set from the injected `clock_t` supplied at the composition's **single I/O seam** (see *Logic confinement*): read once per invocation by the host, shared across that invocation's constituent calls and Audit Trail event, and passed to any constituent whose contract accepts an explicit timestamp rather than left to default to a second reading. No action reads a wall clock internally and no signature carries a `now` parameter. The deployment supplies that reading — typically from the receiving node's wall clock, which names the *source* of the injected value, not a clock call inside a transition. Clock quality therefore remains a deployment matter: the honesty of the injected reading, its monotonicity, and skew across distributed nodes, which can still produce `fired_at` values that are not strictly monotonic across history entries even though each is a single seam reading. This is why ordering never rests on the stamps: `sequence_number` is the authoritative order source (inherited from State Machine's clock-semantics edge case) and `fired_at` is best-effort. For deployments requiring adversarially defensible timestamps on each transition, the Trusted Timestamping composing pattern is the resolution; the residual risk under seam injection is a deployment that injects a dishonest `now`, not an internal race.

- **Concurrent [Fire Transition] calls on the same instance.** Two callers concurrently calling [Fire Transition] on the same `instance_id` must be serialized; the implementation must hold a per-instance mutex at the composition layer as well as at the State Machine constituent layer. A concurrent [Fire Transition] and [Decide Gate] on the same instance must also be serialized to prevent the composition from reading an Approval Step state that changes between the state read and the `WorkflowStateMachine.fire` call. **An [Open Gate] racing a [Fire Transition] on the same instance must be serialized too:** a fire that leaves the gate's `from_state` while [Open Gate] is mid-flight can otherwise land a fresh gate (and its Assignment) for a transition that is already moot — a gate the cascade that ran before the binding existed never saw. Under serialization the late [Open Gate] re-derives `current_state` at its step 3 and returns `invalid-transition` instead. The serialization domain is the `instance_id`, across all three state-changing actions.

- **Non-repudiation, tamper-evidence, and retention.** These properties are inherited via the Audit Trail substrate. Non-repudiable attribution is provided by Actor Identity; tamper-evidence of the event log is provided by Tamper Evidence; retention governance is provided by Retention Window. This composition does not re-derive any of these properties; it names them as substrate obligations and inherits the substrate's composition-level invariants.

- **"Which approvals a transition should require" is the deployment's business-rule layer.** The `gate_spec` correctness — whether the declared approver for a guard label is the correct authority under the deployment's regulatory policy — is not this composition's contract to enforce. The composition enforces that `gate_spec` is structurally complete (every guarded transition named; every entry non-whitespace) and that the gate decision is made by the named approver (Approval Step Invariant 4). The policy question — whether the named approver was the right person — is an external audit check (Generation acceptance check 6).

## Standards references

This composition is the structural form of what every multi-actor regulated workflow requires:

- **FDA 21 CFR Part 11 (Electronic Records; Electronic Signatures)** — every transition in a Part 11 electronic records system is an electronic record; guarded transitions driven by approval gates constitute electronic signatures. Part 11 §11.50 requires signatures be attributable; §11.70 requires they be linked to records to prevent removal, substitution, or falsification. The composition's Audit Trail substrate (Actor Identity providing cryptographic binding; Tamper Evidence providing the linking) satisfies both. The only-declared-transitions enforcement (Invariant 5) and the approval-gated-transition claim (Invariant 1) are the records-alone proof that §11.10 system access controls and §11.70 record integrity requirements were honored.

- **FDA 21 CFR Part 211 (Current Good Manufacturing Practice for Finished Pharmaceuticals)** — batch release requires authorization by a Qualified Person or equivalent designated authority. A State Machine-governed batch lifecycle with a QP approval gate on the release transition is the structural form of the Part 211 batch-release control. The guarded-transition record (history entry with `guard_satisfied: true` and bound Approved Approval Step) is the per-batch control evidence.

- **SOX (Sarbanes-Oxley Act) §404 (15 U.S.C. (United States Code) §7262) — Internal control over financial reporting.** Process-control records for financial workflows (journal entry posting, purchase order approval, account reconciliation) must demonstrate that the required control steps occurred in the declared order and that each gate was cleared by an authorized actor. The composition's records-alone process proof (Invariant 6) is the structural form of the SOX §404 control evidence requirement. Composes with Audit Trail's SOX §802 retention obligation (7 years).

- **ISO 9001:2015 §8.5.1 (Control of production and service provision)** — production and service provision activities must be controlled by documented procedures with controlled transition points. **Enumeration-anchoring round — methodology debt #19, step (iii), second class, first instance — 2026-08-27 (landing touch; closes F10).** The class's load-bearing instance, taken first because its gap is the only one that is both **silent and harmful**: four of the five known instances fail silently, and this is the one where the silence costs a human approval.

**The finding, restated at the strength the sweep gave it.** `gate_binding` is classified a **derived index** with **rebuild-on-miss** semantics, and a reader is entitled to conclude that a lost entry is self-healing. It is — *at the shape the contract names*. Rebuild-on-miss is a **keyed-lookup** contract: it repairs a gap because a lookup that finds nothing is an observable signal. [Fire Transition] step 4a derived the moot-gate plan by **enumerating** the map, and an enumeration has no miss to observe. A lost entry is then indistinguishable from a gate that was never opened.

**What that costs, traced all the way down.** The gate is absent from the plan; step 7's cascade acts on the plan, so it never withdraws that gate; Generation acceptance check 7 compares the plan against the recall events, so an omission appears in **neither** set it compares and the check cannot see it. The moot Approval Step simply stays Pending. If the workflow later returns to that `from_state`, [Open Gate] finds a live binding — a key may be re-bound only when its bound step is terminal, and Pending is not — so the human's approval, given for an earlier visit to that state, is still the gate standing in front of the guarded transition. **That is precisely the harm the moot-gate cascade exists to prevent, reintroduced through the index.** No alarm fires anywhere along that path.

**And it undercut a claim this pattern had just strengthened.** The authentication-precedence round (2026-08-26) argued the `moot_gate_plan` is **exact** rather than an upper bound — the per-`instance_id` serialization forbids the interleaving that would make it a bound — and made check 7 report *both* directions as findings on that basis. The argument was right about serialization and silent about the enumeration. An under-reported plan is exact-looking and unfalsifiable: check 7 has nothing to compare an omission against. **A correctness argument is only as strong as the completeness of the set it quantifies over**, and this round is the second time in two days that a claim strengthened by one campaign turned out to rest on something a different class had already undermined.

**The fix: anchor the read in the authoritative record and use the index as a cache.** The binding history is append-only and lives in the `gate_opened` events, each carrying its `from_state`. The derivation now enumerates **those events** through the declared enumerate-and-filter surface, takes the latest per `(instance_id, from_state, action)` key, and resolves each key through `gate_binding` as a **keyed** lookup with rebuild-on-miss before reading the bound step's state. The index is still used and still a cache; what changed is that the plan's completeness no longer rests on the index's. **Only a miss that survives the rebuild is a miss.** Generation acceptance check 4 takes the same anchor — an auditor enumerating the map would pass over exactly the gate whose Assignment the check exists to verify — and check 7's exactness claim now names the derivation it depends on rather than resting on serialization alone. Invariant 4's *Rests on:* records the dependency, because *"every still-Active Assignment for moot gates"* quantifies over a computed set and where that set comes from is a capability-provenance fact, not a wiring detail.

**In-corpus exemplar: [Audit Trail](./audit-trail.md), and finding it corrected the campaign's own survey.** The pre-campaign survey had named Login's `login_event_log` bounded exception for this class and called it *"weaker than the other two exemplars and worth strengthening rather than copying verbatim."* It was looking in the wrong place. **The anchor's own bound is stated rather than left for the next class to find.** `gate_opened` payloads carry `from_state`, so the derivation is a payload-sourced read and inherits the substrate's retention horizon — the `Q-rebuild-bound` class, still open on this pattern. The bound is benign in the ordinary case, since a workflow instance's gates are contemporary with its own execution and far inside any regulated retention horizon; but it is real, and a deployment whose instances can outlive `audit_trail_retention_policy` must say so, because past that point the plan's completeness degrades to whatever the index still holds. **The two classes meet here, and an anchoring fix that ignored the horizon would simply move the silent failure one layer down.**
