## The framing: small pieces, loosely joined — and the joins are explicit

Grace Commons is **small pieces loosely joined** (David Weinberger, *Small Pieces Loosely Joined*, 2002 — the web's richness came not from the pages but from the loose joining of them by explicit links). The atoms are the small pieces; compositions are the loose joins. "Loosely joined" is load-bearing, not decorative: a join is an *explicit, minimal contract*, never deep coupling — which is exactly why an atom is never modified to compose it, and why an emergent guarantee can appear at a seam that neither piece owns.

An **outbound contract port** is a join that points *out of the system*: a declared point where the architecture **exports a guarantee it does not make internally**, to a named external fulfiller (deployment, the caller, the projector, or a future pattern), under a named contract. It is the **dual of the inbound four-destination routing** (`no-global-services.md`): inbound asks *what comes in and where does it sort* (value / concept / caller / obligation); outbound asks *what we deliberately do not promise internally, and to whom do we hand it, under what contract.*

The thesis is not "no boundaries." It is **every boundary is a named port with a contract** — the same explicit-over-implicit discipline the architecture applies everywhere, applied to its own edge. A seam labeled as a port, with its contract and status visible, is architecture; an unlabeled seam is a liability. This page exists so the ports are *findable in one place* rather than rediscovered as "weaknesses" each time someone probes the edge.

| **Auto-resolved / real-time semantic merge** (concurrent conflicting writes the *system* must settle) | system-decided convergence under genuine concurrent conflict | a **future pattern** (Conflict Reconciliation concept) + a CRDT/OT **engine** (off-the-shelf mechanism — Yjs / Automerge) | the **crossing, predicted**: the trigger is *auto-resolved + recorded* (the system decides and must remember the decision), **not** "multiplayer" per se. Opens a *Conflict Reconciliation* concept (recorded, attributed, auditable resolution = non-derivable truth); real-time syntactic convergence is the CRDT engine, never a concept. Narrow (collab-editor / canvas class). **Deferred frontier.** *Falsifiable fork:* ~1 reconciliation concept + the CRDT mechanism, or N bespoke merges | `no-global-services.md` §The git test, §Strange-pattern forecast |
| **Cross-atom visual atomicity** | the *appearance* of atomic multi-atom updates to a user | the presentation / projection layer | a presentation instance of *obligation realization* — the projector batches the render; the observable contract (no partial state through the declared read surface) is what's checked — **lower-tier, largely subsumed** by the obligation-realization port | `no-global-services.md` §Open items (known boundaries) |

Read the column that matters for the call: **Fulfilled by** is the honest answer to "who actually guarantees this?", and **Contract & status** distinguishes the *conceded-by-design* ports (most of them — and conceding cleanly is strength, not weakness) from the near-term tractable port (**offline-first** — high value, stays in the grammar) versus the one genuine deferred **frontier** (auto-resolved / real-time merge, which opens the Conflict Reconciliation concept) and the one **lower-tier** residual (visual atomicity). The split is deliberate: deferral is a *prediction* — the registry names the exact trigger that crosses the line (*auto-resolved + recorded*), so what's deferred is mapped, not vague.

## What is *not* a port

The **emergent-invariant / taxonomy-saturation metric** is not an outbound contract port — it exports no guarantee across a boundary. It is the **instrument that tests whether the architecture keeps the promises it *does* make internally** (does emergent-concept count stay bounded as the corpus grows?). It is the falsifier for the whole thesis, not a handoff. It stays in [`falsifiability-metric.md`](./falsifiability-metric.md); this registry only cross-refs it. (Noted because the three "remaining seams" a reviewer will name — liveness, co-editing, the metric — split exactly here: the first two are ports on this page; the third is the instrument that watches all of them.)

## Why this is the dual worth naming

The inbound routing answers Jackson's "where does this global service go?" with four destinations. The outbound registry answers the harder, more honest question — "what *won't* you promise, and who picks up the slack?" — with a fixed, labeled set of ports, each with a contract. Together they close the boundary in both directions: nothing ambient gets *in* (it sorts to one of four destinations), and nothing the architecture can't honor gets silently dropped *out* (it leaves through a named port with a named fulfiller). Small pieces, loosely joined — with every join, inbound and outbound, made explicit.
